# IT SAGs 2025 — full reference

**Source:** IEB *Information Technology Subject Assessment Guidelines*,
updated January 2024, implementation Grade 12 2025 — `20. Information
Technology SAGS 2025 (Updated Jan 2024).pdf` in this folder. Extracted with
`pdftotext -raw` (platform.md's local-tools note explains why `-raw`, not
`-layout`, for this particular PDF — Appendix G's multi-column table
confuses `-layout`'s position-based heuristic badly enough to interleave
unrelated grades' content; `-raw`'s content-stream order keeps each
column's own text together).

**What this file is for:** a token-cheap reference to check syllabus
wording, weighting, or a subtopic number, without re-opening or re-reading
the source PDF. It is a faithful, cleaned transcription of the SAGs' own
text — not a teaching document, not annotated with course decisions.

**Scope — what's in and what's deliberately left out:**

- **In:** sections 1–3 (means of assessment, requirements, taxonomies, SBA
  and PAT guidance), and the full Appendix G content list for **Topics
  1–3** (System Technologies, Internet & Communication Technologies,
  Social/Ethical Issues) across Grades 10–12.
- **Out, on purpose:** Topic 4 (Data and Information Management, Solution
  Development) is **not** duplicated here in full — it already has a
  dedicated, curated file, [sags-topic4-syllabus.md](sags-topic4-syllabus.md),
  built specifically for the Pascal course with a teaching-checklist layout
  or the Appendix G table. This file gives only Topic 4's subtopic index
  (numbers and one-line titles) for navigation, then points there.
- **Out entirely:** the administrative/moderation appendices — mark
  sheets (A, A.1, B, C, D, E), the referencing guide (F), and the PAT
  admin letters (H, I). None of that is useful when writing lesson content,
  which is this file's whole purpose. If a future need arises for those,
  re-extract from the PDF directly (see platform.md for the `pdftotext`
  path) rather than adding them here.

Subtopic numbers keep the SAGs' own scheme: `10.1.3` is "Grade 10, Topic 1,
subtopic 3." The same subtopic number is the same skill strand across all
three grades — e.g. `10.1.3` → `11.1.3` → `12.1.3` is one strand, deepening
each year.

## 1. Means of assessment

**Paper 1 (Practical), 3 hours, 150 marks → 100.** Databases (SQL),
algorithms, Object-Oriented Programming. Candidates use a **text-based
interface, not a GUI**, in the practical exam. No programmatic database
connection is required. Languages: **Delphi (Object Pascal), Java, or C#**
(check current IEB circulars for changes). Databases: MS Access, Java DB or
MySQL.

**Paper 2 (Theory), 3 hours, 150 marks → 100.** Covers all four topics.

**Weighting, Paper 2 (Theory):**

| Section | Marks |
|---|---|
| 1. Systems Technologies | 40 |
| 2. Internet and Communication Technologies | 45 |
| 3. Social Implications | 15 |
| 4. Data and Information Management and Solution Development | 50 |
| **Total** | **150 → 100** |

**Paper 1 (Practical)** is Topic 4 only, 150 marks → 100.

**PAT** — Topic 4 only, 100 marks, completed in Grade 12 (may start in
Grade 11).

**SBA (School Based Assessment) — 25% of the final NSC mark:**

| Component | Description | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| Test 1 | 1 Practical Test | 17.5 |
| Test 2 | 1 Theory Test | 17.5 |
| Task/Test | Alternative Assessment OR Test (Theory/Practical/Integrated) | 15 |
| Exam P1 | Grade 12 Preliminary Examination Paper 1 | 25 |
| Exam P2 | Grade 12 Preliminary Examination Paper 2 | 25 |
| **Total** | **SBA** | **100** |

An alternative task — the **Data Validation Task** (§3.4.7 below) — may
replace a Test; it is optional, not compulsory.

## 2. Taxonomies

### 2.1 Theory assessment (Paper 2)

| Level | Weight | Band | Question types | Hint verbs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30% | Knowledge, Comprehension | Definitions, matching columns, simple multiple choice, one-word answers | list, identify, show, name, state, define, what is, suggest, explain |
| 2 | 40% | Application, Analysis | Advantages/disadvantages, example from a scenario, compare & recommend, justified suggestions, complex multiple choice | describe, contrast/compare, distinguish, discuss, illustrate, show, classify |
| 3 | 30% | Computational Thinking (see Appendix G 10.4.1/11.4.1/12.4.1), Synthesis, Problem Solving, Evaluation | Comparison on multiple factors, suggest improvements, design from given code (or reverse), code an algorithm for an unseen problem, redesign class diagrams with inheritance, improve algorithm efficiency | modify, design, assess, recommend, explain, support, compare, arrange, combine, create, rank, conclude |

### 2.2 Practical assessment (Paper 1)

A concept-by-concept grid, Level 1 (30%) → Level 2 (40%) → Level 3 (30%):

**Concepts (syntax/structure/commands):**
- L1: syntax, structure, command; direct instruction — no deviation, total guidance given.
- L2: multi-step procedures; prescribed algorithm; guided instruction (some scaffolding present).
- L3: problem solving; adaptation of a prescribed algorithm; code efficiency (reuse of classes/inheritance); execution efficiency (no unnecessary processing); unguided use (little/no scaffolding — develop a solution from a desired outcome).

**Variables:**
- L1: declaration and assignment of primitive/built-in types (incl. string); constants; array declaration of primitive types, direct instruction.
- L2: declaration and assignment of objects, guided; declaration/assignment of an array of objects, guided.
- L3: unguided use; code/execution efficiency; declaration of inherited objects, guided instruction.

**Operations and usage:**
- L1: operators in calculations; constants and literals; simple string functions, direct instruction.
- L2: any combination of two control structures/methods (nested loop, loop+if, array+loop, method+loop).
- L3: any combination of three-or-more control structures/methods; unseen/unprepared problems requiring significant adaptation of prescribed algorithms, or a unique solution.

**Existing methods:**
- L1: importing/using additional functionality (Maths functions — round, sqrt, etc.); type casting and conversions.
- L2: application/adaption of prescribed algorithms to new situations requiring a combination of two-or-three control structures/methods (e.g. nested loop+if for a sort, loop+if for finding the highest).
- L3: problems focused on code/execution efficiency ("marks will be awarded for efficient solutions"); abstraction to reduce code (a method with parameters, or inheritance).

**Control structures:**
- L1: simple, non-nested selection and iteration; logical operations (AND, OR, NOT); simple non-nested if/switch/for/do…while/while.
- L2 / L3: see "Operations and usage" above — the same combination-of-structures ladder applies.

**I/O:**
- L1: simple I/O — keyboard input or screen output.
- L2: file reading/writing within loops (e.g. splitting and converting).
- L3: unguided use; code/execution efficiency.

**Own methods and class declaration (inner code classified separately):**
- L1: declaration of void methods/procedures with no parameters, direct instruction; declaration of a class under direct instruction; declaration of standard class methods (constructors, accessor, mutator, toString) under direct instruction.
- L2: declaration of methods with primitive or string parameters; guided declaration of static class variables and constants; guided declaration of a class; guided declaration of standard class methods.
- L3: declaration of methods involving any combination of arrays and objects; unguided OOP design from a problem definition; guided OOP design combining arrays and objects.

**SQL:**
- L1: single table, query with specified fields, and either a single restriction (no logical operators) or sorting.
- L2: simple queries on joined tables (WHERE); aggregate functions; functions in SQL; statements using two joined tables with no conditions/limitations/sorting; WHERE with more than two logical operators.
- L3: any combination of Level 2's SQL, joined tables, embedded queries, grouping, NOT IN, INNER/LEFT/RIGHT JOIN, GROUP BY, HAVING, SQL INSERT with SELECT.

## 3. Interpretation of requirements (condensed)

- **Paper 2 (Theory)** and **Paper 1 (Practical)** content are both drawn
  from Appendix G (§4 below).
- **Practical exam procedure:** candidates are supplied data files before
  the exam (teachers load them per accompanying instructions — check
  circulars for delivery details).
- **SBA suggested tests**, with topic and mode:

  | Test/Task | Topic | Mode |
  |---|---|---|
  | Theory Test | 1, 2 or 3 | Theory |
  | Normalisation Test | 4 | Theory |
  | OOP Test (Written) | 4 | Theory |
  | OOP Test (Programming) | 4 | Practical |
  | SQL Test | 4 | Practical |
  | Data Validation Task | 4 | Alternative Task |
  | Preliminary Theory Exam | 1, 2, 3, 4 | Theory |
  | Preliminary Practical Exam | 4 | Practical |

  All tests/exams must be set across the cognitive-level spread in §2
  above. Cluster-set tests are moderated and written on the same date
  where possible.

- **Theory Test** (§3.4.2): ~40–50 min, covers topics 1 OR 2 OR 3.
- **Normalisation Test** (§3.4.3): ~40–50 min. Normalise a single large
  table into more than one table, up to 3NF. Covers normal-form
  definitions, derived data, keys, anomalies, repeating groups. Candidates
  apply these to a given table.
- **OOP Test — Written** (§3.4.4): ~40–50 min. Suggested content: (A) OOP
  concepts — advantages over procedural coding, objects/classes, standard
  class methods and why they exist, data protection (private + get/set),
  data validation at class level, inheritance pros/cons, encapsulation,
  polymorphism (overloading/overriding). (B) Data structure design from a
  scenario — when to create a class, class diagrams with inheritance,
  user-defined objects as fields, sub/super class, instance vs class
  field/method, static vs non-static. (C) Understanding of code —
  constructor/accessor/mutator/toString, what's inherited, the function of
  `super`, parallel arrays vs array of objects.
- **OOP Test — Programming** (§3.4.5): ~40–50 min, practical. Suggested:
  (A) completing a partially coded solution — inherit from a given class,
  code a class with fields based on a given class (composition), code an
  array of a given class, code a UI. (B) Coding a solution from a JSON
  file — data structure to match it, read/store, manipulate (add/edit/
  delete/sort), write back.
- **SQL Test** (§3.4.6): ~40–45 min, practical, includes advanced SQL
  (multiple tables, JOINs, aggregate functions).
- **Data Validation Task** (§3.4.7, alternate to a test): a GUI accepting
  four data types (Numeric, Boolean, String, Date/Calendar) via the most
  appropriate component each, plus **any four** further validation checks
  via code — see the validation-type table below. Requires a Data
  Validation document: title page, GUI screenshot before input, a
  completed component-choice table (Appendix A.1), a test plan (standard/
  extreme/abnormal data), before/after test screenshots, commented code.

  | Validation type | How it works | Example |
  |---|---|---|
  | Check Digit | The last one or two digits check the others are correct | Barcodes, ISBN, credit card numbers |
  | Format Check | Data is in the right format | A number plate being "ABC 999 GP" |
  | Length Check | Data isn't too long or too short | A password is ten characters long |
  | Lookup Table | Value looked up against an acceptable-values table | The number of hours worked is 0–50 |
  | Presence Check | Data has been entered in a field | An ID number cannot be blank |
  | Range Check | Value falls within a specified range | A person in Grade 12 cannot have an age under 16 or over 19 |
  | Type Check | Data entered is the correct type | The value for a height is a real number |
  | Logic Check | Value is logically correct | There are only seven possible days in a week |

### 3.1 Performance Assessment Task (PAT)

- **Scope:** permanent data storage + a user interface (frontend) +
  classes to store/manipulate data (backend). More complex than a
  practical exam paper — not something buildable in a week. Bad examples:
  a calculator, Hangman, a currency converter.
- **Topic:** a game, a business system, or a real-world problem (e.g. a
  school tuck shop with ordering, POPI-compliant student data, profit
  calculations). Any hardware combination is allowed (desktop, mobile,
  Raspberry Pi/Arduino). External code must not exceed 20% of the
  solution and must be referenced. Candidates should research existing
  solutions to avoid reproducing one; NDP 2030 / UN SDGs problems are
  suggested topics.
- **Language:** an IEB-approved language (§1 above); an alternate language
  needs Portfolio Moderator permission by 28 February of matric year
  (Appendix I), and triggers National Moderation of the whole portfolio.
- **Criteria:** (A) Problem specification and motivation — solves a real
  problem for a real group of end-users, evaluated with at least one of
  them; investigate existing similar solutions. (B) User interface — GUI
  or text-based, task-appropriate; a non-GUI choice needs justification in
  the design document. (C) Other interfaces — sensors, network, cloud,
  specified. (D) Sequencing/data flow — clear, logical, easy to follow.
  (E) Storage/persistence — text/JSON files and/or a database, appropriate
  to the task (e.g. a game saves/loads and tracks high scores).
  (F) Algorithm complexity — Grade 12-level problem solving (Level 2/3 of
  §2.2), distributed across program operation, data processing and
  storage. (G) Separation of interface and engine — working code lives in
  separate classes/units, communicating via parameters and typed methods;
  UI code is only acceptable in small, justified amounts. (H) Good
  internal data structures — classes/arrays or a combination; logical,
  task-appropriate; classes are essential.
- **Five phases** (with suggested mark allocation): 1 — Specification
  Document (17); 2 — Design Document (30); 3 — Coding (38); 4.1 —
  Technical Document (8); 4.2 — Testing Document (7).
- **Moderation:** schools submit a rank-order list; the Regional Moderator
  selects 10% or a minimum of 5 PATs. Schools with no Regional Moderator,
  or non-compliant at regional level, submit all PATs for National
  Moderation.

## 4. Appendix G — content to be covered

Each subtopic below is: **number** — *skill header (the verb-phrase
heading SAGs itself uses)* — then its content bullets, verbatim from the
SAGs (bullet wording lightly cleaned of PDF extraction artefacts only,

### Topic 1: System Technologies — Hardware and Software

**10.1.1** — *differentiates between the concepts of hardware, software
and connectivity, data and information.*
- Definition/description of hardware/software
- Definition/description of ICT system
- Generic model/definition of a computer — Input Processing Output Model (IPO)
- Advantages and disadvantages of using computers
- Explanation of and differentiation between data and information

**10.1.2** — *identifies and distinguishes between computer types and
associated software.*
- System types: laptops, desktop, server, embedded computers, smart wear, tablets, smartphones, single board computer e.g. Raspberry Pi and Arduino
- Classification of computing devices: portable/mobility; processing power — server, super computer, desktop, mobile
- Operating systems associated with: desktop OS, mobile OS, embedded OS
- Application software: stand-alone applications, network applications
- Data transfer and synchronising between devices

**10.1.3** — *identifies the main hardware components of computing
devices.*
- Motherboard components: CPU (ALU, CU, registers); Primary Storage (BIOS, RAM, ROM); Secondary Storage (mechanical HDD, SSD, hybrid drive, flash drives, optical, SD cards)
- Input devices (pointing devices, keyboard types, scanners, microphones, biometric devices, sensors e.g. accelerometer)
- Output devices — monitors, printers, speakers, headphones
- Ports: USB, HDMI

**11.1.3** — *discusses how the various components of computers interact
with one another.*
- CPU design: parallel processing — hyper-threading and multi-processing; registers (effect of number of bits), ALU, CU
- Concepts of: difference in performance of CPU and RAM-speed, latency; SRAM (processor cache and registers); purpose and types of caching (processor, disk, browser, proxy/web)
- Motherboard function and connection of components: System Clock to synchronise events; over clocking and clock multiplication; Internal Bus/FSB — data bus, address bus and control bus and relationship to registers in the CPU; External Buses — speed vs. throughput: PCI express, SATA, USB

**12.1.3** — *analyses factors affecting overall performance of a
computer-based system.*
- Factors affecting performance and reliability: modular design (ports, cards vs onboard, buses); co-processors e.g. graphic processor, maths processor
- Techniques to improve processor speed: hyper-threading and multi-processing; increasing level 1/2/3 cache, register size (and its effect on data/address busses); changing a component's speed via clock multiplication and/or overclocking
- Increasing speed and size of RAM
- Improving other components/devices specific to the task: video card for 3D rendering; faster HDD/SSD for video editing

**11.1.4** — *compares primary and secondary storage in terms of speed,
bandwidth, capacity and reliability.*
- Categorises primary memory: registers, CPU cache, RAM
- Categorises secondary storage: flash memory, HDD, SSD, external hard drives, cloud storage

**12.1.4** — *makes recommendations for a hardware solution for a given
problem.*
- Motivate a typical computer system with respect to the hardware needed for a specific purpose computer system
- Motivate a computer-based solution to a specified problem as it relates to the needs of a specified user
- Describe mobile technologies and constraints: battery life, size, computing power, power consumption (see 11.2.6)

**10.1.5** — *identifies the functions of various types of operating
system.*
**10.1.6** — General functions of a generic operating system: user
interface; load and run programs; manage resources; interface between
hardware and application programs.

**11.1.5** — *discusses processing techniques and memory, storage and IO
management.*
- Machine Cycle: fetch, decode, execute and store
- Identify, analyse, compare and recommend processing techniques: software — multi-tasking, multi-threading related to hardware — hyper-threading and multi-processing
- UEFI vs BIOS and CMOS
- Interrupts: IRQs and IO Range
- Virtual memory: describe virtual memory including paging and swapping; effect of virtual memory on processing speed

**10.1.6** — *distinguishes between the types of system software.*
- Operating systems: utilities; drivers; programming tools and related applications; source code vs executable code/bytecode; distribution and licensing models
- Open source software: proprietary software; freeware; freemium software; creative commons

**11.1.6** — *discuss and analyse types of programming tools.*
- Compare and recommend language translators: high level languages; low level languages; compilers (one and two stage), interpreters and assemblers

**10.1.7** — *describes the purpose and effectiveness of operating system
tools and utilities to organise and manage the computer.* (Candidates
should already have these skills on local, mobile and remote computers.)
- Structuring data in secondary storage and cloud storage: management of desktop; management of files and folders including sharing and permissions; archive; backup; compress/decompress files
- System Management: installing/uninstalling software (custom and full installation, product keys, activation codes); adding devices and device drivers; scheduling/updating
- Security features: firewall, anti-malware

**10.1.8 / 11.1.8 / 12.1.8** — *states and discusses the implications of
the latest computer technologies* (same skill header at all three grades).
- Discusses possible impacts of the latest technologies on different societies, considering gender, race, culture, religion, ethnicity, environmental and economic factors.

### Topic 2: Internet and Communication Technologies

**10.2.1** — *displays a knowledge of networking in a LAN.*
- Overview of a network: describe a network; network devices/nodes (client/workstation, server, switch, router, firewall)
- Bounded Media — types of cabling (UTP and fibre); Unbounded Media — microwave, radio wave
- Classification of networks (PAN, LAN, WAN, GAN)
- Local area and smaller networks: general function of an ADSL router — wireless/hotspot, firewall, switch, connection to Internet; role of a server (any computer providing a service — authentication, file sharing, email); role of a client (any computer receiving a service — server and client could be the same machine); user profiles/rights/permissions to access network resources
- Reasons for using networks: communication; access to/sharing resources; centralisation; data transfer; productivity
- Advantages and disadvantages of networks

**11.2.1** — *discusses network configurations, devices and architectures
of a LAN and smaller networks.*
- LAN and smaller network concepts: bounded connection media (UTP, fibre); weaknesses of communication channels (eavesdropping, attenuation, cross talk, EMI); unbounded connection media (Bluetooth, wireless, radio waves); topology — star, bus, ring, hybrid, mesh; networking devices such as NIC, switch, router and bridge
- Network addressing — compare IPv4 and IPv6 (see 11.4.2), MAC address, DNS, DHCP, ARP
- Broadcast vs. point-to-point communication
- Format of Packets and Frames for Ethernet and IP addressing including headers (source, destination), payload, frame checks, VLAN tag related to network devices such as switch, bridge and router
- WLAN: devices (wireless access point, wireless bridge, wireless router); Wi-Fi and Hotspots; compare bounded (wired) vs wireless connection in terms of bandwidth and speed
- Extending a LAN: fibre optic backbone

**12.2.1** — *discusses distributed processing within a LAN.*
- Centralised vs distributed processing: thin clients; fat clients; smart clients

**11.2.2** — *discusses network configurations, devices and architectures
of a WAN.*
- WAN: gateways, Wi-Fi router; transmission — satellite radio waves and microwave; connection technologies (cellular technologies incl. latest; fibre vs ADSL); difference in usage and bandwidth
- Protocols: email protocols — POP3, SMTP, IMAP; internet protocols — TCP/IP (UDP vs TCP); IP frame vs Ethernet frame and associated devices (see 11.2.1); web protocols — HTTP, HTTPS; download protocols — FTP, WebDav

**12.2.2** — *evaluates the effectiveness of cloud computing as an
extension to a LAN.*
- Sharing concepts: peer-to-peer file sharing e.g. BitTorrent; compare FTP, WebDav and peer-to-peer file sharing
- Compare access to remote sites: remote access e.g. TeamViewer; VPN — to provide remote access; risks and advantages of all methods
- Internet of Things (IoT): concept of devices accessible on the Internet

**10.2.3** — *describes the Internet and connectivity options.*
- Describe the Internet: Internet as a global network; basic services on the network (mail, web sites)
- Connectivity Options: reason for an Internet Protocol (IP) address; hardware to connect — e.g. SIM card (cell), wireless router, personal hotspot, ADSL router

**11.2.3** — *discusses and compares the transmission of data over the
Internet.*
- Overview of multimedia as part of Internet technologies: download vs. streaming and effect on bandwidth; video on-demand; VOIP; podcast/vodcast
- Compression technology: lossy vs lossless; lossy compression of sound files; compression — quality vs. bandwidth and speed

**12.2.3** — *discusses the advantages and disadvantages of the deep web.*
- Concepts of: deep web and dark web
- Anonymous Browsing Tools: onion router — e.g. Tor; VPN — how it ensures privacy, anonymity

**10.2.4** — *makes effective use of Internet tools to communicate,
collaborate, organise and plan.* (Candidates should already have these
skills on local, mobile and remote computers.)
- Email: sends and receives emails; displays responsible communication styles — netiquette, spam, large file sizes, hoaxes; monitors emails frequently and organises email account
- Calendar: uses a calendar to schedule and share events
- Online storage: uses online tools to collaborate — notes, terminology lists, tasks (e.g. Google Docs, Office 365/MS Word)
- Responsible use of the Internet: social media

**10.2.5** — *describes the design and functional elements of a Web
site.*
- Classification of Web pages/sites: web page vs web site
- User Interface Design: compare usability issues — readability, navigation (three-click rule), consistency, layout, typography/colour, theme (see 10.4.12); user interface design as applied to desktop/mobile

**11.2.5** — *describes the evolution of Internet Service Technologies.*
- Overview of the evolution of the Internet: static and dynamic sites — Web 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0; client-side and server-side scripting; cookies; web-based applications; mobile applications — native, mobile web, hybrid; plug-ins and extensions
- Design factors for mobile technology: screen size, processing demands, storage demands, bandwidth requirements

**12.2.5** — *critically assesses Internet Service Technologies.*
- Overview of Internet Services technologies: Location Based Services (LBS); Global Positioning System (GPS)
- Discuss and analyse the purpose and effect of cloud computing: concepts of cloud computing (effect on local hardware needs); cloud services for application use (G-Suite, Office 365); cloud storage for data; advantages and disadvantages (permissions, security, bandwidth); cloud licensing vs other licencing models (10.1.6); ownership of data
- Security services: public and private key encryption and SSL; digital certificates; digital currency — block chain technology, distributed database (decentralisation), transaction and ledger

**10.2.6** — *navigates the Internet in order to retrieve information.*
- Describe the WWW: web address/uniform resource locator (URL)
- Searching Engines: defines a search engine with examples; applies Boolean logic to search criteria (see 10.4.4)

**11.2.6** — *efficiently searches the Internet for information.*
- Search Engines: performs searches for text, sound (e.g. Shazam) and images, reverse image lookup; performs searches using advanced search criteria using logical operators (see 10.4.4, 11.4.4)

**12.2.6** — *describes improving Web site reception for search engines.*
- Improve searching: Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)

**11.2.7** — *demonstrates an ability to identify sources of errors and
propose solutions.*
- Sources of errors: human error — GIGO, input errors; arithmetic errors — rounding, truncating, fixed number bits, overflow (see 11.4.2); data transmission errors — cable problems; programming errors — undetected logical errors
- Solutions for errors: verification vs validation; techniques for input — barcode scanner, QR codes, keyboard vs GUI design (drop down boxes, check boxes etc.), RFID, biometric input, Optical Character Recognition (OCR); types of checks for data validation — presence check, range check, uniqueness check, length check, type check, logical check, check digit, check sum (see 11.4.8, 11.4.14); data transmission check — parity

**11.2.8** — *demonstrates an understanding of data integrity and
protection.*
- Threats: hardware failure e.g. hard drive; power failure/surge; malware — ransomware, phishing, virus, spyware, pharming, spoofing; botnets, zombie armies; denial of service attacks; open ports; Wi-Fi vulnerabilities
- Solutions: RAID (mirroring, striping and parity, levels 1 and 5); backups (local vs remote); UPS; redundant power supplies; passwords including biometric input; user rights; encryption and digital signatures; network security — encryption, length of encryption key, TKIP; firewalls and port filtering; audit trails; anti-malware; updates and upgrades

**12.2.8** — *identify the risk and propose solutions to computer
security.*
- Identify potential threats; evaluate and recommend solutions

**10.2.9 / 11.2.9 / 12.2.9** — *states and discusses the implications of
the latest communication technologies* (same skill header at all three
grades).
- Analyses how communication technologies (cell phones, video calling, e-mail, messaging, social media) lead to human interaction, considering the benefits and risks of this interaction.

### Topic 3: Social and Ethical Issues

**10.3.1** — *describes the reasons for and effects of using computers.*
- Economic reasons for using computers: saving paper, labour, communication costs, efficiency, accuracy, reliability
- Digital divide: describes the digital divide; reasons for the digital divide
- Ergonomics, health issues: effects of RSI, eye strain, carpal tunnel
- Green computing issues: green use — minimise electrical consumption; green disposal — responsible disposal of computers, cartridges, repurposing and recycling

**11.3.1** — *examines the effects of the use of computers and
information across a range of application areas.*
- Effect on workplace and employment practices: Acceptable User Policy (AUP); decentralisation of the workplace; replacement of workforce — robotics, artificial intelligence, UAV such as drones
- Computer crime and its effects (see 11.2.8): social engineering — shoulder surfing, dumpster diving, phishing, trojan horse, reverse social engineering or role playing, use of social media for social engineering; hackers, crackers and virus authors; computer crimes such as theft of hardware, theft of software, theft of information, identity theft, bandwidth theft, theft of time and services
- Suggest safeguards against computer crimes, threats and criminals (see 11.2.8)
- Explains the consequences of inaccurate information on society and provides criteria to evaluate data sources including web sites: affiliation (who supports the source?); audience (level/who it's intended for); authority (author and credentials); content (organisation and working links); currency (is it up to date?); design (easy to navigate, visually pleasing, download speed?); objectivity (does it reflect preconceptions?)

**12.3.1** — *evaluates the effects of the use of computers and
information across a range of application areas.*
- Green Computing: identifies the negative environmental impact of computer use and provides solutions
- Explain how computers provide solutions to issues of global importance, such as: use of AI to detect patterns for terrorist activity, earthquakes, tsunami, social media propaganda; effects of digital communication and technology; availability of personal information vs misuse of personal information; right to access vs. right to privacy; big data — sources, accumulation, decision making; digital heritage (legal rights to data/applications, local and remote, upon death); social, political (e.g. fake news), environmental issues; privacy and information sharing e.g. Google Drive/Dropbox, movies/music; cyber bullying, illegal distribution of child pornography, harmful/explicit text or multimedia images; laws — Sexual Offences Act, Protection from Harassment Act, Electronic Communications and Transactions Act, Protection of Personal Information Act (POPI); the danger of errors in computers controlling equipment e.g. hospitals, motor cars, UAV; Internet of Things (IoT) — implications for security and privacy; deep and dark web — risks/dangers (why it should NOT be used), possible application in countries where access to social media/news is restricted; implications and effects of cryptocurrencies
