Lesson time ranges

How long each lesson takes a pupil to read and finish, worked out from every lesson's own content: its words, code listings, videos and each exercise, timed for three pupils. A lesson here is often a unit of several school periods, not one sitting.

Thandi, quick

Grade 11, reads about 230 words a minute, skims code, rarely needs a second try. The low end of each range.

Lerato, typical

About 160 words a minute, a second try on a quarter of the questions. The middle mark, and the periods column.

Sipho, slow reader

Grade 9, about 105 words a minute, rewatches parts of videos, needs a second try on nearly half the questions. The high end.

LessonTitle Quick – typical – slow

Periods: the typical pupil's time in 40-minute working periods (a 45-minute period less settling in). Bars share one scale within a course. Board sections count only one exam board; where a lesson has both, the quick time uses the shorter board and the slow time the longer. SQL lessons count one database.

How the times were worked out

  • Every block of every lesson was read from the lesson files: teaching text, study summaries, case studies and hints count as reading; code listings are read closely for their first 200 words and skimmed after that.
  • Videos use their real YouTube length (one, in AI lesson 3, could not be read and is taken as 5 minutes).
  • Each exercise has a time per item for each pupil, plus a share of second tries at 60% of the first go.
  • Written answers are timed by their marks: the first 10 marks at the full rate, the rest at half, because a long essay is planned once.
  • Two minutes (typical) are added per lesson for opening it, scrolling and the result reveal.
