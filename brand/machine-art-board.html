Inside the machine: pick a look

A shrinking ray goes wrong and the class ends up the size of a speck of dust, inside the school's broken computer. To get out, they have to fix it, one part per lesson: input and output, the CPU, memory and storage, binary, files, the internet. None of these six styles is used by another course. Pick a style and a mascot, or combine them.

Six styles

A Field notebook

The class keeps an explorer's notebook of the trip: ruled pages, typewritten notes, magnified circles of each part as if under a microscope, specimen labels and sketched arrows. Calm greens on paper. Each lesson is a new page of the expedition log.

B Retro sci-fi poster

1960s "fantastic voyage" posters: a tiny ship flying through giant circuits, bold slab capitals, halftone dots, teal and burnt orange on cream paper. Exciting and cinematic; each lesson opener is a new poster for the next stop on the trip.

C Circuit-city map

The motherboard is a city seen from above: the CPU is downtown, RAM is a row of flats, storage is the warehouse district, and copper traces are the roads. Tourist-map labels and a "you are here" pin that moves each lesson. Dark board green and copper gold.

D Blueprint

Engineering blueprints: white line drawings on blue grid paper, parts pulled apart in exploded views, handwritten callouts and a drawing number in the corner. Clear and precise - ideal for showing what sits inside what.

exploded viewswhite on bluecalloutsprecise

E Storybook watercolour

A gentle picture book: soft watercolour washes, glowing paths, tiny figures of the class walking through huge, friendly parts. Rose, sky and sand colours. Each lesson is a chapter with its own illustrated opening page. The warmest of the six.

picture bookwatercolourchaptersgentle

F Corkboard planning

The class plans its escape on a corkboard: felt-tip sticky notes, pinned photos, string and hand-drawn diagrams. Bright sticky-note colours on cork. Every lesson adds notes to the board until the whole computer is mapped.

Six mascots

1Dusty

A dust bunny who has lived inside the school computer for years. Knows every corner, sneezes when the fan starts, and is quietly the reason the computer overheats.

2Tardi

A tardigrade - a real microscopic "water bear" that can survive almost anything. Tiny, tough and calm, it is the class's guide at their new size. A true factoid in every lesson.

3Volt

A spark of electricity who races round the circuits at top speed and can't sit still. Brilliant for showing how signals travel - and for getting lost on the way to the CPU.

4Zero and One

Twins: round, sleepy Zero (off) and tall, bouncy One (on). Everything the computer knows is made of them - so they turn up in every lesson, and star in the binary one.

5Antenna

A tiny repair robot, the size of an ant, built to fix computers from the inside. Practical and a little bossy: it carries the toolkit and the checklist for each part.

6Dr Ndlovu

The science teacher whose shrink ray went wrong. She talks to the class over a crackly radio from outside the computer - full of facts, very sorry, and working on the un-shrink button.

Dr Ndlovu works well as the voice from outside, with one of the others as the guide inside: Tardi or Dusty for a calm guide, Volt for a fast one, Zero and One for a pair like Tag and Gat.

What the art must explain clearly: what sits inside a computer and how the parts connect, input to process to output, RAM against storage, binary as switches, file sizes compared, and a message travelling the internet in packets. Each style above can draw these; the field notebook and the blueprint are the clearest for diagrams, and the poster and storybook the most fun.

Real photos too: the lessons should also show real parts (a CPU, a RAM stick, an SSD) from the CAT VM or free photo libraries, next to the drawings.
