Four styles for the SQL course

A · THE FIELD NOTEBOOK

Pencil and watercolour

  • A ranger's notebook: hand-ruled tables, watercolour washes behind the row that matters, pencil maps and animal sketches.
  • Warm and calm, and close to Chris's hand-drawn feel.
  • SQL sits on luggage tags clipped to the page, so code always reads as code.
B · THE OPS ROOM

Tactical screens

  • The anti-poaching control room: dark screens, a terminal-green table, contour maps, an amber alert row.
  • Feels like real SQL work: query, rows, time taken. The console look matches the SQL runner.
  • The most exciting look, but the least hand-made. It needs care to stay readable in print.
C · THE LINO-CUT POSTER

Black, ochre and red earth

  • Bold carved shapes and flat colour, like a South African print or a conservation poster.
  • Very distinct from every other course, and it photographs well for thumbnails.
  • Simple shapes only, so detailed diagrams (JOINs) need care.
D · TRAIL-CAMERA EVIDENCE

Night-vision photos and printouts

  • Grainy green trail-camera shots with timestamps, beside query results printed and marked with highlighter and marker.
  • The data feels like real evidence, and timestamps teach dates and times for free.
  • Closest to the safety course's evidence feel, without copying its case file.

Three quick options for the social media course

The same subject in each: the like button as a slot machine, the film's idea that apps are built to keep you scrolling.

My lean: D (trail-camera evidence) for SQL, because timestamps and printed results are the data pupils query, with Oxi the oxpecker as the mascot and Thandeka as the ranger in the story. For social media, 2 (the slot machine), because its picture is the film's main idea.
