Club website: pick an art style

The course: pupils build a web page for a school club in HTML, one element a lesson. Each panel shows the same club page - the Robotics Club - drawn in a different style. None of these looks is used by another course.

ARiso club flyer

Two-colour risograph print: pink and blue ink, halftone dots, slightly off-register, on cream paper - like the flyers clubs pin up at school. The page's parts are labelled with their tags in the margin.

Rubik Mono Onepink + blue inkSVG drawn

BPaper cut-out

The page built from coloured paper pieces, scissors and tape - every HTML element is a piece you cut out and stick on. Warm, crafty, and it makes "a page is built from parts" visible.

Frauncesconstruction paperSVG drawn

CWireframe sketch

The designer's sketchbook: black felt-tip wireframes, a yellow highlight, blue notes. Real web designers plan pages exactly like this - it teaches planning before typing.

Gochi Handfelt-tip + yellowSVG drawn

DBold flat web

Modern flat illustration: chunky rounded shapes, navy with coral and sunflower, browser windows with three dots. Clean, current, close to the real web the pupils use.

Bricolage Grotesquenavy · coral · sunflowerSVG drawn

Mascot candidates

The guide who builds the page with the pupils, drawn in whichever style is chosen.

Weaver the weaver bird

South Africa's master builder: weaves a nest from separate strands, just as a page is built from separate elements. Warm and local.

Kaya the chameleon

Changes colour on demand - perfect for the lesson on colour and style. Slow, careful, misses nothing.

Tag and Gat

An opening and a closing angle bracket - always together, because every tag opened must be closed.

Boxy

The Robotics Club's cardboard robot - the club whose website the pupils build. Clumsy, enthusiastic, held together with tape.

Four more styles

EChalkboard noticeboard

The club's classroom: a green chalkboard with the page sketched in chalk, and sticky notes pinned round it with tag reminders. School-familiar and friendly - good for "rules to remember".

chalk + sticky notesGochi HandSVG drawn

FComic book

Bold black ink, flat primary colours, panels and speech bubbles. Each lesson's story told as a short comic strip - and the page grows panel by panel.

Rubik Mono Oneink + primariesSVG drawn

GStreet stencil

Spray paint and stencils on a concrete wall: bold black shapes, neon green and pink spray, drips and overspray. Grown-up for Grade 8 - the web as a wall anyone can put their message on.

spray + stencilconcrete greySVG drawn

HIsometric blocks

3D building blocks in isometric view: each HTML element is a block, and nesting is stacking one block on another. Shows the structure of a page better than any other style.

isometric 3Dviolet · amber · tealSVG drawn

Four more mascots

Webster the spider

Spins the World Wide Web, one thread (one tag) at a time. The pun does half the teaching: a website really is a web.

Hadi the hadeda

South Africa's loudest bird, who announces everything at full volume - perfect for headings (h1 is the loudest). Funny and very local.

Mika the meerkat

Always on lookout, scanning for mistakes: an unclosed tag, a missing alt text. The class's helpful checker.

Rolo the dung beetle

Small, strong and never gives up - builds something big one push at a time. A funny underdog pupils will remember.

Code and the preview

Whatever the style, the HTML box and its live preview stay as the site draws them - the art lives in the figures, the margin doodles and the "what the page should look like" pictures.

The "page the club wants" pictures can either be real screenshots of the model page in a browser, or drawn in the chosen style.
