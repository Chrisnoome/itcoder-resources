# Online safety course art style: the case file, Sniff and The Phisher

Chris, 9 October 2026: the Grade 8 online safety course (General Computing) gets its own art style,
planned before the course. He chose the **detective case file**, and from the board
(https://claude.ai/artifact/9WBmTyesaZsrCVMRC1xBuJ, source `brand/safety-art-board.html`) picked
**style A, the incident board, with style D's findings form**, and **Sniff the bloodhound as the
hero with The Phisher as the recurring villain**.

The other courses keep the blue pen (content-voice-and-pedagogy.md §5b); CAT keeps the marker and
highlighter (cat-art-style.md). Only the online safety course uses this.

## The look: the incident board

Every lesson is a case. The pupil is the detective; each figure is a page of the case file.

- **Ground:** a kraft manila folder (`#d6b277`, tab `#c99f62`) with a typed tab label:
  `CASE 01 · THE LOCKED ACCOUNT`. One folder per figure.
- **Evidence:** the thing being examined (a message, a screen, a login page, an app's permissions)
  is a pinned Polaroid on `#fbf8f0`, rotated 2-4°, with a handwritten caption under it
  ("Exhibit A · received 07:42").
- **Clues:** each point the figure teaches is an index card (`#f6efdc`, rotated about 1-2°) headed
  `CLUE 1: WHO SENT IT?` with one or two short lines under it. At most three or four cards.
- **Red string:** red (`#b3141c`, stroke 2-2.5 at 680 wide) runs from a pushpin on each card to the
  exact spot on the evidence it explains. This is what keeps the figure a clear labelled diagram:
  every label is tied to its place. Strings never cross if a different card order avoids it.
- **Stamp:** at most one rubber stamp per figure, red, rotated 8-14°, slightly faded: `SUSPECT`,
  `SCAM`, `CLEARED`, `SAFE`.
- **Type:** typed labels and card text in **Special Elite**; handwritten captions in **Caveat**;
  the evidence's own text (an SMS, a URL) in **Courier Prime**; stamps in **Black Ops One**.
  Ink `#2a2116`.

## The findings form (from style D)

The course's repeated routine (check the sender, the tone, the link - and later checks as lessons
add them) is drawn as a **findings form**: a typed table of CHECK / WHAT WE FOUND / FLAG, with a red
box-and-cross for a red flag and a verdict stamp ("3 OF 3 RED FLAGS · VERDICT: SCAM"). Use it
wherever a lesson applies the routine, so pupils see the same checklist every time. It sits on the
folder like a sheet of paper, in the same type.

## Sniff

The hero: a bloodhound with long droopy ears, a big nose, a red collar and a gold tag, drawn in
the folder's ink with flat warm browns. He sniffs out scams ("something smells phishy").

- **Where:** margin doodles (one-line joke captions, as in §5b), and now and then in a figure,
  nose to the one clue that matters, with a short speech bubble. Never more than once per figure,
  never covering the evidence.
- **Poses** to draw as jokes need them: sniffing (nose down, scent lines), pointing (nose and tail
  out straight), alarmed (ears up), proud (holding a `CLEARED` stamp).
- **Voice:** short, cheerful, never sarcastic about the pupil.

## The Phisher

The recurring villain: a shadowy figure in a wide-brimmed hat, white slit eyes and grin, holding a
fishing rod with a bait on the hook (an envelope, a "You've WON!" card, a fake login). Each lesson's
trick is his. He is caught, foiled or unmasked; he never wins, and he is never drawn as scary.
He appears in doodles and openers, not inside the clue figures.

## Models

The board's style A (the scam SMS with three clues and a SUSPECT stamp), style D's findings form,
the mascot sketches, and the password line-up (passwords as suspects in front of a height chart;
the taller, the longer to crack - redraw it on the folder in this style). Draw new figures to match.
