Grade 11 English: pick a look

The course frame stays the same all year: lesson openers, the year map, margin doodles, the asides' look, badges, rank emblems and the mascot. Grade 11 is the deepening year (Macbeth, Frankenstein or the class's own novel, the unseen poem, the literary essay, film study for the IEB), so these looks are a step more grown-up than Grade 10. Pupils' own world stays in every scene: group chats, short-video apps, games and memes, all made up, with no newspapers, no TV news and no real brands. Each frame below avoids every look already used by another course, and the Grade 10 and Grade 12 directions.

The course frame: six options

A Carved in ink

A linocut print: cream paper #efe4cc, one black ink #1a1714 and a vermilion second block #c23b22. Carved white gouge marks cut through the solid shapes, and hatching stands in for shading. Titles in Alfa Slab One, notes in Spectral italic. Asides are a ruled box with a red tab, and quotations sit on a solid black "carved" banner. Year map: the print run. 100 notches in four blocks; each lesson printed inks its notch, and today's notch is red.

B Director's cut

Every lesson is a shoot for the class's video reel on a made-up short-video app. Pencil storyboard frames on off-white #f6f5f0, graphite #2a2d33 lines, cue-tape yellow #ffcc29 for arrows and notes, and a red #e5322d REC dot. Titles in Oswald capitals; shot notes in Courier Prime, like a script. Asides are yellow director's notes. Perfect for IEB film study, and it turns a poem or a scene into "shots". Year map: the editing timeline. Four clips (terms) of 25 frames; the played part is yellow and a red playhead marks today.

C Ink and screentone

Black-and-white manga ink on warm white #fffdf8: brush outlines in #111, dotted screentone for shading, focus lines that zoom in on the moment that matters, and one accent only, signal red #ff2d55, for sound effects and "now". Titles and SFX in Dela Gothic One. Asides are narration boxes with a black header. Each lesson is a chapter in a made-up webcomic app. Year map: four volume spines of 25 chapters; read chapters are inked, and today is red.

D Backstage

The school stage from the wings: near-black #16121d, velvet red #8c1c2b, spotlight gold #ffe8a6 and gaffer-tape cream #ece7dc. Each lesson's key idea stands in the spotlight. Titles in Limelight, like a playbill; notes handwritten in Kalam on tape strips, which are also the asides ("CUE 14"). Built for Macbeth, orals and reading aloud, and pupils film their speeches on their phones. Year map: the dressing-room mirror. Four mirrors (Acts I to IV) ringed with 25 bulbs each; bulbs light up as lessons finish.

E The sketchbook

An open spiral sketchbook: cream page #fbf6ea, soft-edged watercolour washes (indigo #3f5d8f, sap green #7fae7a, rose #d9788d, ochre #e2b04a) and pencil #5b5750 notes. Titles in Fraunces italic; annotations handwritten in Caveat, with words circled and underlined the way you annotate a poem. Asides are cards held by washi tape. Calm and reflective, made for poetry and the creative essay. Year map: four seasons. A wash strip per term (summer, autumn, winter, spring) that gets painted in, lesson by lesson; today is pencil-circled.

F Level map

A bright mobile puzzle-game world (made up, not any real game): sea teal #27b9cf, sand #ffe0a3, grass #5fc06a, level purple #6a4cf2 and star gold #ffc531. Each skill is an island to clear: the seven summary points are gems on the path. Titles in Lilita One on a ribbon; body in Nunito ExtraBold. Asides are tooltip bubbles. Flat glossy shapes, no pixels, no hand-made paper. Year map: four worlds (terms) of 25 levels; cleared levels turn gold and a purple pin marks today.

Three mascots

Quill, the secretary bird

Stamps on waffle the way secretary birds stamp on snakes. Nags about clichés, padding and "very unique": cut it, then cut it again. Its crest is a row of pen nibs.

Folio, the pangolin

Armoured in pages, and curls into a ball at any claim without a quotation. Nags about evidence in essays and contextual answers: "Point, quote, explain. Where's your quote?"

Shade, the chameleon

Changes colour for every audience. Nags about register, tone and format in transactional writing: "You wrote a eulogy like a voice note. Who is reading this?"

Pick one, or a pair. Quill and Folio work well as a double act (one cuts, one proves). Shade could also be the guide just for the transactional and orals lessons. Like Hadi in maths lit, the mascot is drawn in the chosen frame's style, so a linocut Folio looks carved and a manga Folio looks inked.

Three sets of level names (nine ranks)

1 · The writer's path
  1. Scribbler
  2. Note-taker
  3. Drafter
  4. Reviser
  5. Wordsmith
  6. Essayist
  7. Critic
  8. Author
  9. Laureate

Plain and aspirational: from first scribbles to a laureate. It follows the writing process the course teaches (draft, revise, argue), and it works with any frame.

2 · The company
  1. Groundling
  2. Stagehand
  3. Prompter
  4. Understudy
  5. Chorus
  6. Player
  7. Lead
  8. Director
  9. Bard

Shakespeare's theatre, from the cheap standing crowd to the Bard. Each rank comes with a factoid (a groundling paid a penny to stand). Best with D (Backstage) or B (Director's cut).

3 · Ink to print
  1. Inkblot
  2. Doodle
  3. Footnote
  4. Margin note
  5. Paragraph
  6. Chapter
  7. Manuscript
  8. First edition
  9. Classic

A piece of writing growing up, from a blot to a classic that people still study. A bit funny at the bottom ("Footnote") and proud at the top. Best with A (Carved in ink) or E (The sketchbook).

What the art must do

The year map
  • About 100 lessons in four terms, drawn as the chosen frame's own metaphor: a print run, an edit timeline, volumes, mirrors, seasons or worlds.
  • Every lesson opener shows the map with today marked in the accent colour, so pupils see how far they've come.
  • IEB-only and CAPS-only lessons show a small board badge on their slot.
Margin doodles
  • The joke is drawn, and the caption is the joke: "Carved it backwards. Very on-theme." It is never labelled "Joke".
  • Doodles poke fun at the set texts (Banquo, the creature, Lady Macbeth's hand-washing) and at the pupils' own world (word counts, phone takes, no signal).
  • Small, in the frame's style, and placed beside the text, never on top of it.
Asides and factoids
  • Asides have one fixed look per frame (a tabbed box, a director's note, a narration box, a tape strip, a taped card or a tooltip). They use the word "aside" because it is also a drama term.
  • Factoids are about writers and their worlds (Shelley at 18, groundlings paying a penny), always checked and never invented.
  • Scenes stay in the pupils' media: group chats, short-video apps, games and memes, all made up. No newspapers, no TV news and no real brands.
Badges and rank emblems
  • Nine rank emblems for the chosen level names, plus unit badges (Macbeth, the novel, poetry, the essay, summary, orals, film study for the IEB).
  • All in the frame's style and palette. The course icon, badges and emblems go through ComfyUI; openers, doodles and the year map are SVG drawn by a script.
  • Set texts are only quoted when they are public domain or a short extract, as the plan sets out.

How to choose: pick one frame (A to F), one mascot (or a pair) and one set of level names. The chosen style then gets written up as brand/eng11-art-style.md, like mlit10-art-style.md.
