Tuck-shop tycoon: pick a look

The class runs the school tuck shop for a term: a price list, the day's takings, profit, best sellers, a chart, and a fundraiser budget at the end. Every Grade 7/8 course needs its own art style, and none of these six is used by another course. Pick a style and a mascot, or combine two, as you did for the club website.

Six styles

A Till slip

Everything looks like it came out of the till: thermal receipts, dashed lines, typewriter numbers, barcodes and a red price-gun sticker for the important bits. Black ink on paper, with one shop red. Lesson openers are a receipt that grows each week.

B Sticker bomb

Snacks drawn like stickers: fat black outlines, flat candy colours, hard offset shadows and starbursts. Loud, fun and very Grade 7. Numbers sit on sticker labels, and every lesson adds a sticker to the shop counter.

C Enamel shop sign

Old general-dealer signs: rounded enamel plates, double borders, slab lettering, rivets and a little rust. Bottle green, brick red and mustard on a cream wall. Calm, warm and grown-up, with plenty of room for the numbers.

D Board-game tycoon

The term is a board game: each lesson is a square on the board, prices sit on the squares, play money and dice in the margins, and a pawn moves round as the class finishes lessons. Bright primary colours with dark green outlines.

E Linocut market print

Two-colour block prints, like a market poster: black and one burnt orange on kraft paper, chunky cut shapes, carved lines and a slightly rough edge. A spaza-stall feel that is clearly South African without any brands.

F Clay snacks

Soft, squishy shapes, as if modelled in plasticine: rounded, gently shaded, pastel colours on a light lilac table. Friendly and calm; the numbers stay crisp beside the soft objects. Close to the ComfyUI pictures, so the two can mix.

Six mascots

1Randy the R5

A chatty five-rand coin who knows the price of everything and keeps asking "and the profit?". Good at money; bad at waiting.

2Chips

A cheeky packet of chips, the shop's best seller and very proud of it. Always checking the sales column to see if it is still number one.

3Till

The shop's old cash register: grumpy, exact, and never wrong about a total. Rings "ka-ching!" when a formula is right and huffs at typed-in numbers.

4Sista Koek

A sticky, syrupy koeksister who runs the shop's Friday bake sale. Warm and encouraging, a bit of a show-off about her plaits, and very local.

5Calc

A pocket calculator who is jealous of the spreadsheet: "I can add too!" - until the prices change and Calc has to start all over again. The joke teaches why formulas beat typed answers.

6Mielie

A laid-back mielie from the braai stand next to the tuck shop. Slow to panic, good at sharing out, and the one who says "let's check the numbers first".

Mascots can be a pair, like Tag and Gat: one who rushes and one who checks. Randy and Till, or Chips and Calc, work well that way.

The spreadsheet in the lesson

What the art style changes: the lesson openers, the step pictures, the side notes, factoids and jokes, the level emblems and the badges. The spreadsheet itself stays plain, like Excel.

What it can't mark: formatting (lesson 6) and charts (lesson 7). Those lessons use spot-the-problem pictures and questions, with the formulas in the block, and an optional Excel task in the lab.

Level names come after the style (shop-themed, for example Shop Helper up to Tuck-shop Tycoon).
