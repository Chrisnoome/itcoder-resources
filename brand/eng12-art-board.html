Grade 12 English: pick a look

Matric is the last year, and the stakes are high, so the look should feel adult. Pick one course frame: it gives the lesson openers, the year map, margin doodles, asides, badges and rank emblems. Each sample opens a real lesson from the plan. Any story or scene is set in the pupils' own media: group chats, short-video apps, games and memes, all made up. There are no newspapers, no TV news and no real brands. Every look here has been checked against the other courses and the Grade 10 and 11 boards.

Six course frames

A The writers' room

Typed manuscript pages: cream paper #f3ecdc with faint ruled lines, typewriter-ribbon black #1a1714, and an editor's red pencil #c0392b that strikes through and corrects. Titles are in Playfair Display, and the body and captions are in Special Elite. The texture is coffee rings, ink blots and pinned index cards, which also serve as the asides. Year map: the manuscript is "Draft 34 of 100", with the four terms as typewriter tab stops and the last tab marked FINALS. The ribbon turns red as the year is used up.

B Final season

Matric as the last season of a series on a made-up streaming app. The ground is black #0b0b0f with panels in #16161c, a signal red #e5333b and a countdown amber #ffb000. Titles are in Bebas Neue capitals and the body is in Inter Tight. Lessons are episodes, asides are "Previously on..." lower thirds, and factoids are "Behind the scenes". Year map: an episode guide, S12 E01 to E100, with a red watch bar, plus a live countdown chip to the finals. The season finale is the exam.

C The gallery

Each lesson is an exhibit in a quiet gallery. The walls are off-white #f4f2ec, the text charcoal #222222, the frames gilt #b89b5e, and there is one vermilion accent #e4572e. Titles are in DM Serif Display, quotations in Alegreya italic, and labels in small spaced Inter Tight capitals. The critic's quotation hangs as wall text, and asides are museum labels with a vermilion edge. The texture is flat, calm and expensive. Year map: a floor plan of four rooms (one per term), with numbered exhibits, a "you are here" dot and a Final Hall for the exam.

D Karoo night sky

The clear sky over the Karoo. The ground is midnight navy, from #0b1430 to #1d2d63, with starlight #e9edf8, gold #f5d27a for lines and highlights, and a koppie silhouette #060a1a along the bottom. Titles are in Cormorant Garamond, and labels are in Josefin Sans with wide letter-spacing. Poems and essays are drawn as constellations: the lines are stars and the volta is the brightest. Asides sit in double gold rules. Year map: four constellations, one per term, with about 100 stars that light up as lessons are done. The finals are the Southern Cross.

E Stained glass

The big themes (jealousy, ambition, guilt, freedom) as cathedral glass. The lead lines are #1c1c1c, and the jewel colours are ruby #b3122e, sapphire #1e4fa3, emerald #138a5a, amber #e8a33d and parchment #f3ead6. Titles are in Cinzel capitals and the body is in Alegreya. Each set work gets its own window, built from symbols such as the green eye, the skull and the portrait. Asides are single amber panes. Year map: a rose window of four quarters, one per term, whose panes fill with colour lesson by lesson. The centre boss lights up at the finals.

F Premiere poster

Each set work and skill gets a film one-sheet, as if the class were premiering it. The colours are a deep teal #0e3a43, a hot orange #f2803a and a cream #f6e7cf, with screen-print silhouettes and one big circle. Titles are in Oswald capitals and taglines are in Playfair Display italic. A billing block credits the pupil ("Written by you"), festival laurels mark badges, and asides are ticket stubs. Year map: four film reels, one per term, filled frame by frame. The final reel ends at PREMIERE, which is the exam night. It also covers film study for the IEB.

Three mascots

Quill, the Cape porcupine

Bristles at any claim made without a quotation. Nags: "Where's your evidence? Quote it, then explain it."

Tonie, the flap-necked chameleon

Changes colour to suit the audience. Nags about register and tone: "That's a blog voice. This is an open letter to a minister."

Marge, the praying mantis

A critic in reading glasses who stays perfectly still until she finds a plot summary, then strikes. Nags: "I know what happens. Tell me what it means."

What the art must do

Year map. It covers about 100 lessons in four terms and ends in the finals, so the last stop is always the exam (a finale, a final hall, the Southern Cross, a premiere). Every lesson opener shows the map with the current lesson marked. The term breaks have to be clear, and the run-in to the finals (exam practice in Term 4) should feel like a countdown, not a cliff.

Margin doodles. The joke is drawn and the caption is the joke, as with Yorick left on read, the unskippable ghost and the rooster-coop group chat above. There is never a "Joke" label. Doodles mix the set works with the pupils' own media: group chats, short-video apps, games and memes, all made up. There are no newspapers, no TV news and no real brands.

Asides and factoids. Asides define a term or give exam advice in one or two lines, in the frame's own box (index card, lower third, museum label, gold rule, amber pane, ticket stub). Factoids are small true facts about a writer, a word or a play, such as the fact that "Hamlet" is Shakespeare's longest play. They have their own label so they never look like an aside.

Badges and rank emblems. There are nine rank emblems, one per level name, plus badges for the main skills: summary, comprehension, visual literacy, language, poetry, literary essay, set works, creative, transactional, orals and film. They are drawn in the chosen frame's style, with the same palette and lettering. The course icon, badges and emblems go to the ComfyUI queue in that style.

Adult, not cute. These are matric pupils. The mascot can be funny, but the frame should look like something a 17-year-old would screenshot, not something they'd be embarrassed by.
