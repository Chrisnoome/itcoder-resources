Pick a look for Bolt's maze

Bolt, the robot from Station Kestrel, is lost in the station's maintenance tunnels. Pupils program it with blocks to get it home. The course is set in the world pupils actually play in: a puzzle game, level by level, with stars and a level map. Kestrel is pixel art, so this course needs a look of its own. Bolt stays recognisable (boxy head, one big orange eye, two antennae) in whichever style is picked.

Four looks

A Puzzle-game levels

Soft pastel isometric tiles floating in warm light, like a calm mobile puzzle game. Each lesson is a level with up to three stars. Command blocks are rounded "sweets" in orange (moves) and violet (loops and ifs).

B Neon tunnel run

The tunnels are dark, so everything glows: cyan walls, a pink exit portal, Bolt's orange eye. Feels like a speed-run game, with a tunnel number, a move counter and a timer on screen.

C Cardboard maze, stop-motion

The maze is built on a desk out of cereal-box card and masking tape, like a stop-motion video. Bolt is a paper cut-out on a lolly stick; arrows are felt pen; plans are on sticky notes. It suits lesson 1, where pupils program each other on paper first.

D Top-down adventure game

Chunky stone tiles seen from above, mossy walls and a torch glow round Bolt, like a classic top-down adventure game. On screen: hearts, a battery counter and a tunnel name ("The Damp Bit", "The Loop-de-Loop").

A sidekick for Bolt

Bolt is the hero you program. The sidekick makes the jokes and causes the trouble the lessons need (a wrong step to debug, a maze that changes).

Glitch

A small, cheerful bug who lives in the tunnels and swaps Bolt's commands round when nobody is looking. Every debugging task is "what did Glitch change?" The real meaning of "bug" comes for free.

Commander Naledi, on the radio

The station commander from the Grade 9 course talks Bolt home from the bridge and sets each tunnel's task. Links the two courses, so Grade 7s meet the station early.

Spanner

A fussy little maintenance drone who knows every tunnel and checks Bolt's programs against a clipboard: "Seven commands? I could do it in four." Good for the shortest-path and loop lessons.

What the art has to do, whichever look wins

  • A tunnel map that fills in lesson by lesson (one tunnel a lesson, seven in all).
  • Bolt in every pose the lessons need: walking, turning, bumping into a wall, cheering at the exit, going round in circles.
  • The robot block's maze recoloured to match (walls, floor, exit, batteries), so the activity looks like part of the game.
  • Margin drawings and drawn jokes in the same style, nine levels of rank emblems and achievement badges, a course icon.
