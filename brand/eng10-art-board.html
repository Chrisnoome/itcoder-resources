Grade 10 English: pick a look

About 100 lessons in four terms: comprehension, summary, adverts and cartoons, language in context, poetry and the unseen poem, the first literary essay, Shakespeare, a novel, creative and transactional writing, orals, and film study for the IEB. Grade 10 is where pupils first meet FET exams, so the frame has to feel grown-up without being stiff. Every scene is set in the pupils' own media: group chats, short-video apps, games, memes, streams and voice notes. All apps, accounts and products are made up. Pick one frame, one mascot and one set of level names.

Six course frames

A Cut-and-paste zine

The course is a photocopied zine the class makes about the media it reads. Off-white paper #f4f1ea with a halftone dot texture, black ink #141414, hot pink #ff3d8b and riso yellow #ffe14d. Titles are ransom-note tiles in Archivo Black; notes are typed in Courier Prime. Torn edges, washi tape and screenshots taped in at an angle. Year map: four stapled issues (one per term), with the current page counted on the cover.

Three mascots

Quill, the secretary bird

Born with pens in its crest and a clerk's name. Nags about comma splices, formats (where is the date? the sign-off?) and capital letters on proper nouns. Stamps a long leg when a run-on sentence goes past.

Ratel, the honey badger

A fearless editor with a red pencil. Nags about word counts ("90 means 90"), waffle, repeated points in a summary and lifted sentences. Doesn't care how pretty your sentence is if it's over the limit.

Kaya, the chameleon

Changes colour with the tone of the text: green when it's friendly, orange when it's sarcastic, grey when it's formal. Nags about register (no "hey guys" in a formal letter), tone words, and claims with no quotation behind them: "Where does it SAY that?"

Three sets of level names

1 · Draft to classic
  1. Blank Page
  2. First Line
  3. Rough Draft
  4. Second Draft
  5. Proofreader
  6. Editor's Pick
  7. First Edition
  8. Bestseller

A piece of writing growing up, from nothing to the shelf. Plain, ties into the writing process the IEB wants, and fits any frame.

2 · The punctuation ladder
  1. Full Stop
  2. Comma
  3. Question Mark
  4. Apostrophe
  5. Dash
  6. Colon
  7. Semicolon
  8. Ellipsis…
  9. Interrobang‽

Each mark is harder to use well than the last. The emblems draw themselves (one big mark per rank), and the top rank is a joke pupils will want.

3 · From reader to voice
  1. Skimmer
  2. Reader
  3. Note-taker
  4. Quoter
  5. Wordsmith
  6. Storyteller
  7. Essayist
  8. Critic
  9. Voice

What the pupil becomes, not what the page becomes: reading, then quoting, then arguing, then a voice of their own. Pairs best with the open-mic or podcast frame.

What the art must do

Year map. About 100 lessons over four terms (roughly 28 / 28 / 30 / 14). Every lesson opener shows where the pupil is: the term (issue, night, reel, album page, season or region) and the lesson number out of 100. Finished parts look finished: filled, punched or inked in.

Margin doodles. The joke is drawn and the caption is the joke: the asterisk "always there, never invited", the waffle you cut, the d20 Mercutio rolls. Never a label that says "Joke". One per lesson, in the frame's own style, often with the mascot.

Asides and factoids. Each frame has its own aside container (cut-out, napkin, clay slab, index card, show notes, scroll). Factoids use the same box with a small number badge, e.g. "Shakespeare invented or first recorded about 1 700 English words".

Badges and rank emblems. A course icon, badges per strand (poetry, essay, summary, visual literacy, orals, film) and nine rank emblems for the chosen level names, all drawn in the frame's lettering and colours. Emblems go through ComfyUI in the style; doodles and openers are SVG by script.

The pupils' media. Texts to read are posts, sponsored clips, voice notes, group-chat threads, game chat and stream comments, all invented. Adverts are sponsored posts in a made-up app; cartoons are memes or comic strips. Never newspapers, TV news or real brands. Set texts are quoted in short extracts only.

Kept clear of other courses. None of these frames reuses sign-painter streets, group chat + scanner, comic panels, board-game tycoon, circuit city, pixel space station, neon slot machine, ops room, case file, marker/highlighter or the handmade adventure game, nor the Grade 11 and 12 directions on the sibling boards.
