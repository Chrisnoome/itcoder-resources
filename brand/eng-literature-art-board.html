English Home Language · Grades 10-12 · IEB and CAPS · literature strand only

Literature: every work gets its own look

Chris's direction: "lit appropriate to the time, setting, topic and tone of the work / poem". So there is no course-wide literature frame. Hamlet looks like a woodcut of Elsinore in winter; Life of Pi looks like the Pacific at sunset. What stays the same is the skeleton of a literature lesson, so a pupil always knows where the quotation, the questions and the essay topic are. The language and writing strands and the mascots are on a separate board.

1 · The literature system

The skeleton: the same in every unit

Always the same

  • Placement. Title bottom-left on the opener (display face, then author · date · setting on one small line); the 8-step progress strip top-right.
  • Quotation card. A thick left rule in the work's accent; label "QUOTATION · act/chapter"; the line; speaker and place in mono; then one context question below a dashed rule.
  • Context-question box. A dashed inner border, numbered questions, marks in [brackets] on the right. CAPS contextual and IEB essay prompts use the same box.
  • Essay-topic card. The critic's statement, the task, and a word-count pill (CAPS 400-450, IEB about 600).
  • Body text, mono labels and the lesson chrome (buttons, gates, Jev feedback) never change.

Changes with each work

  • Palette: 4-6 colours, used on the opener, the quotation card, the box borders and the progress strip.
  • Display typeface: one Google font per work, used for titles and quotations only (never for body text).
  • Texture: woodcut grain, plaster, laid paper, gouache, glass.
  • Motifs: the work's own symbols, used in margins, dividers and the progress strip.
  • Illustration approach: the art tradition of the work's own time and place.

How a work's look is chosen

  • Period first. Borrow the art of the time it was written or set (Elizabethan woodcut, Puritan broadside, Romantic sublime, Beardsley, 1930s modernism).
  • Then place. Draw the real landscape and buildings: Kronborg's copper spires, Verona's swallowtail merlons, Kabul's hillside houses and walls.
  • Then symbols. One or two central symbols the essays turn on: the handkerchief, the candle, the portrait, the coop.
  • Tone sets the light. Tragedy goes dark and low-key; satire goes flat and clean; a survival story goes saturated.
  • Respect. Take patterns from real traditions (kolam, girih tilework, Persian miniature, Venetian Gothic). No caricatured people, and no costume or poverty clichés. Main characters are shown by symbols or silhouettes, not drawn faces.

2 · Unit openers: ten works

Each opener keeps the skeleton: title bottom-left, progress strip top-right (here at step 3 of 8). Quotations are short lines from public-domain texts. Works still in copyright (Miller, Martel, Adiga, Hosseini) show only a placeholder card, because the pupil quotes from their own copy.

HamletGr 12 · IEB and CAPS · drama (Shakespeare)

Quotation · Act 1 sc 4
"Something is rotten in the state of Denmark."
Marcellus, on the battlements, as Hamlet follows the Ghost
Why is it a guard, not a prince, who says this? [3]
Palette
#14161c#2b3440#9fb3c8#e9ecef#4f7a6b#c9a227
Type
UnifrakturCook (blackletter) for titles; IM Fell English for quotations, a face cut from 17th-century type.
Texture
White-line woodcut: carved hatching, rough edges, light grain on black.
Motifs
Kronborg's copper spires, the ghost in armour, snow on the battlements, a crown and a skull for dividers; gold is kept for the crown only.
Illustration
Black block with white carved lines, like a 1600s broadsheet woodcut. Night scenes, low moon.
Why
Tragedy in a court in mourning: black, frost and one cold gold. Woodcut is the picture-making of Shakespeare's own London.

OthelloGr 12 · CAPS · drama (Shakespeare)

Quotation · Act 3 sc 3
"O, beware, my lord, of jealousy; / It is the green-eyed monster…"
Iago to Othello, while planting the very jealousy he warns against
Explain the dramatic irony in this warning. [3]
Palette
#16232b#1f5c63#9b2d20#c8a24a#ecdcc6#6fd06a
Type
Cinzel Decorative for titles (Roman capitals, as on Venetian inscriptions); Cinzel for quotations.
Texture
Stone and brick: the Doge's Palace pink lozenge pattern, light grain.
Motifs
The ogee arch with striped voussoirs (Venice's Gothic arch and the striped arches of Moorish Spain share a root); quatrefoils; eight-pointed stars; the strawberry-spotted handkerchief; green used for jealousy only.
Illustration
A view through a window: Venice is the frame, the stormy sea to Cyprus is the scene. Othello himself is never drawn. Use symbols, not a face.
Why
A play about seeing and being made to see: everything is framed and watched, and the small green glints are Iago.

The CrucibleGr 12 · CAPS · drama

Quotation · Act 4
[A short extract, one to three lines, quoted from the pupil's own copy. Miller's play is in copyright.]
Speaker · act · moment
What does this line show about reputation in Salem? [3]
Palette
#efe6d2#1c1a17#7a1f1a#e8b04a
Type
IM Fell English SC for titles and IM Fell English italic for quotations: the Fell types were cut in the late 1600s, the time of the trials.
Texture
Laid paper with grain; ink that bites unevenly (the rough filter); double printer's rules.
Motifs
The meeting house, the gallows on the hill, a single candle, a quill, a signed confession, the forest edge. Red appears only as broadside red (dates, a wax drip).
Illustration
A two-colour woodcut broadside, like the printed sermons and "wonders" sold in 1690s Boston. Austere, hard-edged, no gradients except the candle's glow.
Why
Puritan plainness and public print: the play is about what gets written down and signed. The candle carries the light-and-dark imagery.

Pi · chapter · moment
How does Pi's tone change once Richard Parker is aboard? [3]
Palette
#0b4f6c#20a4b8#e0577b#f4a259#f2c14e#f26b21

Yatra One: a Latin face drawn by an Indian type studio alongside its Devanagari, with a brush-cut warmth.

Flat gouache layers with a fine paper grain; no outlines.
Motifs
A Tamil kolam border (Pondicherry's doorstep patterns) on Part 1, worn away in Part 2; the lifeboat, the tarpaulin, the raft on its rope, flying fish, the sun and the stars; the zoo gate for Part 1.

The Picture of Dorian GrayGr 12 · CAPS · novel

Quotation · Chapter 2
"The only way to get rid of a temptation is to yield to it."
Lord Henry to Dorian, in Basil's studio garden
Why is this witty line dangerous to Dorian? [3]
Palette
#0d0d0d#f7f4ec#b8913a#e6c977#5a4520
Type
Cormorant Garamond italic: the fine, high-contrast book type of the 1890s aesthetic presses.
Texture
Flat black and white, no tones; dotted borders; the only texture is a faint grain on the black.
Motifs
The gilded frame (the one colour), lilies, peacock feathers, the yellow book, the locked schoolroom; the portrait's decay creeps further up the frame as the unit's progress strip fills.
Illustration
Aubrey Beardsley's line: big black masses, whiplash curves, fine dotted lines, lots of empty white.
Why
Beardsley illustrated Wilde's Salome; this is the decadent look of the novel's own decade, beautiful on the surface and rotting in a corner.

Brave New WorldGr 12 · IEB · novel

Quotation · Chapter 1
"COMMUNITY, IDENTITY, STABILITY."
The World State's motto, over the Hatchery door
Which of the three words costs the citizens most? [3]
Palette
#f4f7f6#bfe3d6#f5c6cf#cdc4ea#2b3a55#e86a6a
Type
Poiret One: thin, geometric 1930s Art Deco capitals.
Texture
None, on purpose. A lab grid, clean vectors, barely any grain. Only the Savage Reservation units get dust and texture.
Motifs
The Ford "T", rows of identical flasks and caste letters, conveyors, helicopters, the sunburst, soma tablets, the scent organ.
Illustration
Clinical modernism: symmetric, repeated, pastel. Things are cloned with <use>, so every flask really is identical.
Why
A satire on comfort and control: it should look pleasant and sterile at once, with Huxley's own decade's design on top.

The White TigerGr 12 · IEB · novel

Quotation · The Fourth Night
[A short extract, one to three lines, quoted from the pupil's own copy. Adiga's novel is in copyright.]
Balram · night · moment
What does the rooster coop stand for, and who keeps it locked? [4]
Palette
#0f1020#1b2b4e#3fd0d4#f2a541#c7362f#f3efe6
Type
Teko: a condensed face from an Indian type foundry, used on Indian film and street posters.
Texture
Night photography: deep blacks, glowing sodium lamps and glass, a little film grain.
Motifs
The rear-view mirror (Balram watching his masters and himself), the rooster coop, the Darkness and the Light split, the white tiger's blue eyes, the red bag, the chandelier.
Illustration
The chauffeur's point of view, through the windscreen, every time. Village scenes are drawn as quietly as the city ones. No poverty spectacle, and real Bangalore and Delhi architecture.
Why
A dark satire narrated at night by a driver: the frame is his seat and the mirror is his conscience.

Romeo and JulietGr 10 · library · drama (public domain, full text)

Quotation · Prologue
"A pair of star-cross'd lovers take their life"
The Chorus, before the first scene
Why tell the audience the ending before the play begins? [3]
Palette
#efe3cc#3d5a8a#d8a84e#c46a4a#d9a08b#6e7b4a
Type
Marcellus: flared Roman capitals, like the lettering painted on Italian church walls.
Texture
Fresco plaster: a soft mottle, hairline cracks, and patches where the paint has fallen away.
Motifs
A Giotto-blue sky with gold stars (the Scrovegni Chapel is 80 km away in Padua); two crossing star trails; the Torre dei Lamberti; swallowtail merlons; the balcony; cypresses; the Ponte Pietra; daggers and a vial for the late acts.
Illustration
Early-Renaissance fresco: flat, warm, slightly naïve perspective, framed by a painted terracotta border.
Why
Verona's own walls and painting, with the "star-cross'd" idea painted into the sky.

FrankensteinGr 11 · library and IEB 2028-29 · novel (public domain)

Quotation · Chapter 20
"Beware; for I am fearless, and therefore powerful."
The creature to Victor, after the second creature is destroyed
Who has the power in this scene, and how has it shifted? [3]
Palette
#151a25#3b4758#5b6b80#8fb7cc#dbe7f0#fff4c2
Type
Playfair Display italic: a high-contrast didone, the Bodoni type of Shelley's own decade.
Texture
Oil-painting depth with grain; ice in smooth gradients; storm light.
Motifs
The oak blasted by lightning (Victor's boyhood, ch. 2), the Mer de Glace, a Rückenfigur on a rock, the creature far off on the ice, the Arctic for the frame story; galvanic sparks, a candle and a Leyden jar for the laboratory units.
Illustration
Caspar David Friedrich's sublime (his Wanderer above the Sea of Fog is also from 1818): huge nature, tiny people.
Why
A Romantic novel about ambition against nature. The creature is never drawn up close; he is always a distant figure, which keeps him human and unknown.

A Thousand Splendid SunsGr 10-12 · library · novel (in copyright)

Quotation · Part 1
[A short extract, one to three lines, quoted from the pupil's own copy. Hosseini's novel is in copyright.]
Speaker · chapter · moment
How do Mariam's surroundings shape what she believes she deserves? [4]
Palette
#1f3f8f#2aa7a1#e0a12b#a3243b#c97a5a#6c5a8e#f4ead5
Type
Amiri: a Latin face drawn to match a classical Naskh Arabic, warm and bookish.
Texture
Gouache on burnished paper with a fine grain; a gold rule round every scene, as in a manuscript page.
Motifs
Girih and eight-pointed-star tilework (from Herat's Friday Mosque); many small suns (the title comes from Saib Tabrizi's poem about Kabul); the old city wall on the ridge; hillside houses; the pomegranate tree; Herat's minarets for Mariam's early chapters.
Illustration
Persian miniature (the Herat school of Bihzad): high viewpoint, stacked coloured rocks, patterned borders. War and violence are never shown; scenes show homes, gardens and the city.
Why
A story of two women told with tenderness. Afghanistan's own court art gives the dignity, and the bright suns behind the walls carry the hope.

3 · Poems: each one gets its own card

Poems are shorter units, so each gets a 320 × 200 panel rather than a full opener. The skeleton still holds: title bottom-left, then the poem's line card. Lines are quoted only where the poem is safely public domain.

Ozymandias

P. B. SHELLEY · 1818 · IEB CLUSTERS
"Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair!"

Engraving hatch on sun-baked ochre, Roman capitals. The look is grand, but it is falling into the sand.

The Sun Rising

JOHN DONNE · 1633 · IEB CLUSTERS
"Busy old fool, unruly Sun,"

A sepia emblem-book engraving, the metaphysical poets' own picture-language. The cross sun peers in at the bed curtains.

Namaqualand after Rain

WILLIAM PLOMER · IEB CLUSTERS
Title and author only. Plomer died in 1973, so the poem is probably public domain in SA now, but that needs checking before any line is shown.

Flat, bright, almost naïve colour: a riot after drought. The granite koppies and daisy carpet are the real Namaqua landscape.

Fern Hill

DYLAN THOMAS · 1945 · CAPS
Title and author only. Thomas died in 1953, so it is probably public domain in SA, but it is still in copyright in the UK and US.

Soft watercolour washes in green and gold, with a white farm and apple boughs: childhood remembered in a glow, with the moon of the later stanzas.

Sonnet 130

WILLIAM SHAKESPEARE · 1609 · CAPS
"My mistress' eyes are nothing like the sun;"

Elizabethan emblem roundels, each Petrarchan cliché struck out in ink, then a plain heart. The joke and the love are both drawn.

The Shipwreck

EMILY DICKINSON · CAPS
"Glee! The great storm is over!"

Pencil on a hand-stitched fascicle page, with Dickinson's dashes drawn in the margin: quiet, private, joy and grief on one page.

4 · Watch for

  • Copyright. Miller, Martel, Adiga and Hosseini get placeholder quotation cards. In the lesson, a short extract (1-3 lines) goes in from the pupil's own copy. Plomer and Thomas are title-only here until we check them. Huxley's motto is three words.
  • Check "The Shipwreck". I have taken it to be Dickinson's "Glee! The great storm is over!", the poem anthologies print under that title. Confirm against the CAPS anthology before the line is used.
  • People. Main characters are never drawn as faces: Othello, Balram, Mariam and Laila, and the creature appear only as symbols or distant silhouettes. That keeps clear of caricature and leaves pupils to picture them from the text.
  • Fonts. This board loads 16 families. A lesson page should load only its own work's display face, plus the shared body and mono fonts.
  • Dark mode. The work panels keep their own colours in both themes; that is the point. Only the board chrome switches.
