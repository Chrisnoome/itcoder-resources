Case files: the art style board

Every lesson is a case to crack: a suspicious message, a weak password, an app that knows too much. Four ways to draw the case file, all on the same subject (a scam SMS), then four candidate mascots and a second subject. Pick a style and a mascot; the style doc follows the CAT one.

Four case-file styles

A · MANILA FOLDER AND RED STRING

The incident board

  • Kraft folder, evidence as a pinned Polaroid, clues on index cards.
  • Red string joins each clue to the exact spot it explains, so the diagram still reads as a labelled figure.
  • Typewriter labels, one rubber stamp per figure (SUSPECT, CLEARED, SCAM).
B · FILM NOIR

Black ink, one red

  • Heavy black line, Venetian-blind shadows, everything grey except the clue, which is red.
  • The explanation comes as the detective's narration in black caption boxes.
  • Strongest mood of the four; the narration voice needs care to stay clear for Grade 8.
C · THE DETECTIVE'S NOTEBOOK

Pencil and red pencil

  • Lined notebook page, grey pencil sketches, clues circled and arrowed in red pencil.
  • Handwritten notes read like a pupil's own working, which suits "do this, observe, explain".
  • Quietest of the four. Closest to the blue-pen courses in feel, so it is the least distinct.
D · EVIDENCE BAG AND FORMS

Forensic lab

  • Crime-scene tape, sealed evidence bags with labels, findings recorded on a form.
  • The form is a checklist pupils can reuse: every scam gets the same three checks.
  • Cleanest for tables and comparisons; the least hand-drawn.

Mascot candidates

Drawn in plain ink here; the one you pick is redrawn in the chosen style. Like Clicky, it appears in margin doodles and at most once per figure.

A second subject: the password line-up

My lean: A (the incident board) for figures, with D's findings form borrowed for the three-check routine, and Sniff plus The Phisher as hero and villain. A is the most distinct from the blue pen and from CAT's marker, and the red string keeps every label tied to its spot.
