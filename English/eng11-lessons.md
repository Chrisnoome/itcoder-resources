# English Home Language Grade 11 (`eng11`): the lesson list - PLAN

**Grade 11 · school year 2027 · status: PLAN (11 October 2026).** No lessons are written yet.

**Summary.** This is one course for both boards. Lessons are tagged `shared`, `caps` or `ieb`.
Each board follows its own Grade 12 exam format and mark distribution, at Grade 11 lengths:
- **CAPS:** P1 70, P2 80 (poetry + novel + drama), P3 100, P4 orals 50.
- **IEB:** P1 100, P2 100 (Shakespeare essay + novel essay + transactional), SBA, orals.

The order follows the DBE Grade 11 ATP and the Grade 10-11 list of 10 formal tasks. IEB
pupils follow the same order, with film study, SBA-style extended writing, a CAT rehearsal
and IEB-format papers in the slots where CAPS pupils write CAPS tasks.

Literature is taught from a teacher-chosen library. The teacher picks one Shakespeare play
and one novel for a class. The 12-poem set is the default for everyone. The play is read in
Terms 1-2, so it can carry the mid-year P2. The novel is read in Term 3, where the ATP names
the novel. Poems run through the whole year.

A typical pupil sees **100 lessons (CAPS) or 104 (IEB)** of about 30 minutes, about 3 a week.
The course has 163 entries in all.

**Sources.** The `§` refs below point to the two digests:
- **C §** = `English/CAPS/caps-ehl-reference.md`.
- **I §** = `English/IEB/ieb-ehl-reference.md`.
- **ATP Tn Wn** = the Grade 11 ATP row (C §2).
- **EG** = the DBE exam guidelines (C §3).
- **App A-D** = the CAPS rubrics. **D5-D20** = the IEB SAG rubrics.

## How to read this list

- **Stream.**
  - `shared` lessons are for every pupil.
  - `caps` lessons are shown only to CAPS pupils, and `ieb` lessons only to IEB pupils
    (`'stream' => 'caps'/'ieb'` with a badge; platform.md, "Stream-only lessons").
- **Text.** A `text:<slug>` lesson appears only if the teacher has chosen that text for the
  class.
  - The two drama units are interchangeable, and so are the two novel units. Each unit has
    the same 13-slot shape (D1-D13, N1-N13).
  - In the term tables, a **slot row** (# shown as "—") marks where "your drama" or "your
    novel" lesson falls. The full units are listed under their own sub-headings.
- **Every lesson** has:
  - at least one gated, guided try-it;
  - margin extras: a Ratel (language) or Quill (writing) drawing with the joke as its
    caption, an aside, and a factoid.

  These are not listed per lesson. The art follows `brand/eng-art-style.md`: L1 for
  language, W1 for writing, each work's own look for literature, and the Gr 11 accent,
  teal `#0f8a7e`.
- **Marking.** Jev marks first, with Claude for the unsure cases.
  - **CAPS** uses App A-D and memo-style points.
  - **IEB** uses the literary-essay bands 7++ to 1, the transactional 10 + 10, and the global
    summary rubric, with SOLO descriptors for short answers.
  - Summary word counts are checked by code, so each board's cut-off rule is applied exactly.
- **Kids' media world.** Every practice text is set in teen media: posts, group chats,
  short-video ads, gamer blogs, streams, memes and made-up apps. There are no newspapers, no
  TV news and no real brands.
  - **The tension.** Both boards' real papers use op-eds, newspaper articles and real ad
    campaigns (C §4, I §3). Our texts copy their register and question types, not their
    sources. The full past-paper practice in Term 4 is where pupils meet the real format.
- **Grade 11 lengths:**

| | CAPS | IEB |
|---|---|---|
| Comprehension passage | 600-700 words | 600-700 words |
| Summary | source 320 words; output 80-90 words, at most 90 | source 320 words; output at most 90 words |
| Essay | 350-400 words | SBA extended writing 400-500 words (our scaling, see Open points) |
| Literary essay | novel/drama 350-400 words; poetry 200-250 words | about 500 words (our scaling) |
| Transactional | 180-200 words | about 250 words (D18) |
| Listening text | 300 words or a 2-minute clip | 300 words or a 2-minute clip |

| 7 | Character I: the protagonist | Character I | shared |

| 10 | Shakespeare's craft (soliloquy, dramatic irony, verse and prose) | Narrative craft (narrator, structure, setting) | shared |
| 11 | CAPS: contextual questions on two extracts / IEB: reading a critic's quotation (thesis and spectrum) | same | caps / ieb |
| 12 | CAPS: literary essay, 350-400 words (25) / IEB: critical essay, about 500 words (30) | same | caps / ieb |
| 13 | CAPS: unit task, P2 Section C (25) / IEB: unit task, a timed essay against a critic's quotation (30), which also counts as an SBA literature test | Section B / the IEB novel essay | caps / ieb |

Each unit therefore has **16 entries**: 10 shared, 3 CAPS and 3 IEB. A pupil sees 13 of them.

**Placement.**
- **Drama:** D1-D6 in Term 1 (weeks 2-11) and D7-D13 in Term 2 (weeks 1-9).
- **Novel:** N1-N13 in Term 3 (weeks 1-11).

1. **Order of the set texts.** The drama runs Terms 1-2 and the novel Term 3. This mirrors the
   Grade 12 outline (`english-courses-plan.md`) and puts the novel where the Gr 11 ATP names
   it (T3 W2-3). The ATP's own Grade 11 P2 genre is the novel. Novel-first would be the
   alternative. Please confirm.

   - CAPS: the drama takes both the essay and the extract question, as the real Grade 11 P2
     does with its one genre.
   - IEB: Shakespeare essay + transactional only (out of 70).

   Please confirm, or choose a second Shakespeare topic for IEB instead.
3. **IEB Grade 11 lengths are not in the SAG** (I §1.9 gives only the comprehension, summary
   and listening lengths). Our scaling:
   - literary essay about 500 words (Gr 12: about 600);
   - transactional about 250 words (the D18 figure);
   - extended writing 400-500 words (Gr 12: 500-600);
   - film task 800-1000 words (Gr 12: 1000-1200).

   change to a "writing process task" (I §0, §4) may reshape the two extended-writing pieces
   once the revised SAG arrives. The CAT theme changes every year, and our rehearsal copies
   CAT 2026 (poetry).
