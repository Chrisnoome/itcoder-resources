## Decisions so far

- **One course per grade, with board streams.** Shared skills lessons are for everyone.
  Board-only lessons use `'stream' => 'ieb'` / `'caps'` with a badge (platform.md,
  "Stream-only lessons").
- **Grades 10 and 11 copy the Grade 12 exam format and mark distribution** for each
  board. Only the text lengths scale down, using the CAPS/IEB table: comprehension
  500-600 / 600-700 / 700-800 words; summary source 280 / 320 / 350 words; summary
  output 80-90 words in every grade.
- **Set texts:** the pupil uses their own copy. Lessons quote only short extracts.
  Public-domain texts can be shown in full (see "Copyright" below).
- New-course musts apply to all three courses: progress gated, an art style of its
  own, level names of its own, and margin extras. Status `open`/`soon`, never draft.
  Jev marks first.

## What each board examines (Grade 12, applied to 10 and 11 too)

| | CAPS (DBE) | IEB |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Language in context, 70 marks, 2 h: comprehension 30 (prose 700-800 words plus a visual, one cross-text question); summary 10 (350 → at most 90 words; 7 points + 3 for language); advert 10; cartoon 10; prose editing 10 | Response to text, 100 marks, 3 h: comprehension 25 (ends with a cross-text question); summary 10 (2-3 sources, recast for a new audience, at most 90 words, global marking); seen poetry 15 (two poems compared); unseen poetry 15 (a new poem paired with a prescribed one); critical/visual literacy 25 (real ad campaigns); language 10 (cartoon or article) |
| **P2** | Literature, 80 marks, 2.5 h: poetry 30 (two of four seen-poem questions, one of them a 250-300-word essay, plus an unseen poem 10); novel 25 and drama 25, with one essay (400-450 words) and one contextual answer across the two | Writing, 100 marks, 3 h: Shakespeare essay 30 (compulsory, about 600 words, against a critic's quotation); novel essay 30 (one of two topics); transactional 2 × 20 (250-300 words; open letter, blog, speech, e-mail, eulogy, editorial, review) |
| **P3** | Writing, 100 marks, 3 h: essay 50 (400-450 words; 8 topics, 3 of them pictures); transactional 2 × 25 (180-200 words; category A letters/e-mail, B report/review/articles, C speech/dialogue/interview/obituary) | (none) |
| **Orals** | P4, 50 marks: listening 15, unprepared speech 15, prepared speech and reading aloud 10 + 10 | 100 marks: prepared speech 20, CAT speech 20, CAT conversation 20, extra conversation 10, listening 10, unprepared reading 10, prepared reading 10 |
| **SBA** | 10 tasks a year (CAPS ref §1.7) | Learner file: 2 extended-writing pieces of 500-600 words, the CAT (a poetry task in 2026), film study (two films, 1000-1200 words), 3+ tests, prelims. Being changed in 2026 to a "writing process task" (user group). Check with the IEB. |

## Copyright

South Africa protects literary works for the author's life + 50 years. The list
below has to be checked before anything is hosted in full.
- **Likely public domain in SA:**
  - Shakespeare, Wilde, Shelley (*Frankenstein*); Huxley (d. 1963), Orwell (d. 1950).
  - Poets: Dylan Thomas, Plath, Pound, Plomer, Dickinson, Wordsworth, Wilcox, Schreiner,
    Donne, Rossetti, Tennyson.
- **Protected:** Martel, Miller, Adiga, Galgut, Duffy, Ferlinghetti and most of the
  modern SA poets (and translations such as Jonker's and Senghor's).
- **Rule:** protected texts get short quoted extracts only. The pupil reads their own
  copy.

## How a course runs (all three grades)

About **72 lessons of about 30 minutes**, roughly 2 a week, in the order of the DBE ATP
terms. The IEB sets no terms, so IEB pupils follow the same order.

Each grade has the same **strands**, scaled by grade:

| Strand | Shared | CAPS stream | IEB stream |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading and comprehension: tone, diction, intention, cross-text | ✓ | 30-mark paper practice | 25-mark paper practice; quotation/definition "lens" questions |
| Summary | point-finding, own words, the word limit | single source, 7 points + language | 2-3 sources, recast for a new audience and format |
| Visual and critical literacy: adverts, campaigns, cartoons, comic strips | ✓ | Q3/Q4 (10 + 10) | Q5 campaign sets (25) |
| Language in context: grammar for its effect, editing | ✓ | Q5 editing (10) | Q6 (10) |
| Poetry: reading a poem, devices, the unseen-poem method | ✓ | seen poems (board anthology), poetry essay 250-300 words | *Clusters* poems, two-poem comparison |
| The literary essay: arguing a position | ✓ (Gr 10 introduces it) | 400-450 words, plus contextual questions | about 600 words against a critic's quotation |
| Shakespeare | ✓ (the play's unit) | contextual questions + essay | essay only |
| Novel | (per text) | contextual questions + essay | essay only, 2 topics |
| Drama (non-Shakespeare) | | ✓ (*The Crucible* in Gr 12) | |
| Creative essays | narrative, descriptive, reflective, argumentative, discursive | P3 essay 400-450 words, picture prompts | SBA extended writing 500-600 words, process drafts |
| Transactional writing | format, register, audience | 180-200 words, categories A-C | 250-300 words: open letter, blog, eulogy, editorial |
| Orals | speech craft, reading aloud, listening, conversation | P4 tasks | 7 oral tasks; CAT speech and conversation |
| Film study (the "4th genre") | | | ✓ cinematography terms, two films, a 1000-1200-word task |
| Exam practice | | P1/P2/P3 papers in the 2023-2025 format | P1/P2 papers in the 2023-2025 format |

### Grade 12 outline (Grades 10 and 11 mirror it with their own texts and lengths)

- **Term 1 (about 20 lessons):** orientation and baseline; the comprehension toolkit;
  the creative essay (types, planning, picture prompts); transactional writing I; the
  unseen-poem method; summary; Shakespeare part 1; a Term 1 test (in each board's
  format).
- **Term 2 (about 20):** Shakespeare part 2 and its literary essay; seen poetry part 1
  (per board); visual literacy; language in context and editing; orals (prepared
  speech, reading aloud); mid-year paper practice.
- **Term 3 (about 22):** the novel (per board and text); drama (CAPS) / film study
  (IEB); seen poetry part 2; transactional writing II; trial paper practice.
- **Term 4 (about 10):** full past-paper practice in each board's format, and revision.

### Marking

- Written answers are marked by **Jev first**, with Claude when unsure. Essays and
  transactional pieces use each board's rubric:
  - CAPS rubrics: App A-D.
  - IEB rubrics: literary-essay bands 7++ to 1, transactional 10 + 10, global summary.
- Short answers are marked against memo-style conditions (CAPS points; IEB generic
  descriptors by mark value).
- Summaries are word-counted by code, so the board's cut-off rule is exact.
- Orals: the course coaches and practises. The pupil records themselves, and the
  teacher marks the formal oral. **Still to check:** whether the platform can take an
  audio upload.

## Art direction (Chris, 11 October 2026, after the first style boards)

The first boards offered one frame per course
([Gr 10](https://claude.ai/artifact/183vPL4WUvWJ8PM7gQJzQC),
[Gr 11](https://claude.ai/artifact/Sp7bofvVHz2K2pjRs1Z2Va),
[Gr 12](https://claude.ai/artifact/L77GEMR3hXoYRyNbwRf5Rf)). Chris rejected the idea of
a single frame: "don't think it should be monolithic - each part or section of the
course should be independent - language similar, writing similar, lit appropriate to
the time, setting, topic and tone of the work / poem". Mascots: "less basic - better
svg".

- **Language sections** (comprehension, summary, visual literacy, language in context,
  editing) share one look.
- **Writing sections** (essays, transactional, process writing) share another look.
- **Literature:** each work or poem gets art that fits its own period, setting, topic
  and tone. *Hamlet* looks like Elsinore, *Life of Pi* like the Pacific, and *Brave New
  World* like a sterile future. There is no course-wide literature frame.
- **Mascots** need richer, better-drawn SVG.
- This replaces the "one art style per course" rule for the English courses.
- **Chosen (11 October 2026):** see [../brand/eng-art-style.md](../brand/eng-art-style.md).
  - Language sections use **L1 Tracing overlay**, writing sections use **W1 Drafting
    table** (together the "Studio" family). The grade accents are cobalt, teal and
    violet.
  - Every literature work gets its own look, as on the literature board.
  - The mascots are **Ratel** (language) and **Quill** (writing).
  - The level names are **Draft to classic**.
