## 1. SAGs (Subject Assessment Guidelines)

### 1.1 Means of assessment: 400 marks
| Component | Time | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Paper I: Response to text (Reading & Viewing; Language) | 3 h | 100 |
| Paper II: Writing (Writing & Presenting; Language), with literature | 3 h | 100 |
| SBA (continuous assessment, learner file) | – | 100 |
| Oral (Listening & Speaking), internal and externally moderated | – | 100 |

### 1.2 Aims ("D.21 Purpose of the English Home Language curriculum")
The aims are given as bullets. Wording condensed:
- Broaden and deepen GET-phase language competencies, "including the abstract language skills required for academic learning across the curriculum, and the aesthetic appreciation and enjoyment of texts", so learners can listen, speak, read/view and write/present with confidence. This is the basis for life-long learning.
- "Use language appropriately in real-life contexts, taking into account audience, purpose and context."
- "Express and justify their own ideas, views and emotions confidently in order to become independent and analytical thinkers."
- "Use language and their imagination to represent and explore human experience", reflecting on their own lives and alternative worldviews.
- Use language to access and manage information. "Information literacy is a vital skill".
- "Use language as a tool for critical and creative thinking" (knowledge is socially constructed).
- "Express reasoned opinions on ethical issues and values": human rights, children, women, the disabled, the aged, race, culture, ideology, class, belief systems, gender, HIV/AIDS, freedom of expression, censorship, the environment.
- "Interact critically with a wide range of texts", recognising and challenging "perspectives, values and power relations that are embedded in texts".
- "Recognise the unequal status of different languages and language varieties" and assert language rights in a multilingual society.

### 1.3 Content and approach (D.22 "Content and contexts")
- "Text" has "the widest possible meaning, including all oral, written, visual, audio, audio-visual and multi-media forms". Texts are "the main source of 'content' and 'context'".
- The texts used should expose learners to: socio-cultural/historical settings; challenging themes and ethical issues relevant to SA learners; "a wide range of points of view"; models of spoken and written language; "analysis of stereotypes, bias and generalisations"; "persuasive and manipulative language"; "power relations within and between languages"; audience, purpose and context with mood, tone and register; features of a wide range of texts including literary ones; "visual and audio-visual elements"; style and stylistic devices / figurative language.
- **Text-based approach**: explores how texts work, so learners become "competent, confident and critical readers, writers, viewers and designers of texts". Grammar and vocabulary are taught "as choices in texts and in terms of their effects, not in an isolated way". Learners need a "meta-language".
- **Communicative approach**: lots of exposure and practice, so that "learners read by doing a great deal of reading and learn to write by doing much writing".
- **Texts used, Gr 10–12**: drama, novel, poetry, folklore, short stories, film study, essays, biographies, autobiographies. Also "transactional, reference, creative, visual, audio, audio-visual and multi-media texts".
- **Texts produced, Gr 10–12** ("A selection should be produced"):
  - Transactional: advertisements, brochures, CV, dialogues, editorials, e-mails, faxes, formal/informal letters to the press, formal letters (application, request, complaint, sympathy, invitation), friendly letters, invitation cards, magazine articles, memoranda, minutes and agendas, newspaper articles, obituaries, postcards, reports (formal/informal), reviews, SMS.
  - Creative: narrative, descriptive, reflective, discursive, expository, argumentative compositions; responses to literature.
  - Reference/informational: directions, instructions, mind-maps, notes, paraphrases, research projects, summaries.
  - Oral/visual/multimedia: advertisements, dialogues, flyers, formal/informal speeches, interviews, posters, presentations with graphic/sound effects, research projects.
  - Enrichment (non-compulsory): dramatisations, storytelling, radio/TV news and drama, panel discussions, own stories/poems/plays, cartoons, comic strips, jokes, signs.
- The four strands map onto assessment like this: Language is tested in P1 (in context) and P2. Literature is in P1 (poetry), P2 (Shakespeare and novel essays) and the SBA (film as "4th genre"). Writing is in P2 (transactional) and the SBA (extended writing, CAT essay). Oral is the separate 100-mark component. Visual literacy is in P1 Q5 (adverts, cartoons) and in film study.

### 1.4 Paper I: Response to text [100], 3 hours
"This paper focuses on the ability to make meaning of texts. Language and grammar questions will therefore be focused on the function of, and reasons for, using certain grammatical forms or language conventions across a range of texts."
| Q | Content | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Comprehension: "a comprehension passage of between 700 and 800 words". May include "current newspaper articles, advertisements or cartoons". Functional language and punctuation may feature. "Learners may be required to answer questions based on more than one text." | 25 |
| 2 | Summary of "a 350 word text which requires abstracting and recasting". The instructions set formality, register and format. Passage is different from the comprehension. | 10 |
| 3 | Contextual questions on at least **2 prescribed poems** ("may be linked through a similar theme or visual") | 15 |
| 4 | **Unseen poem**: "only one question set on contemporary verse". May involve more than one text, "an extract from another prescribed poem or a visual". | 15 |
| 5 & 6 | A selection from: propaganda and advertising; critical literacy; visual literacy; dictionary skills; grammar in context | 25 |
| (6) | Editing skills ("correction of sentences, use of appropriate punctuation, register, synthesis"). "The final question may be less than 10 marks since editing skills may be set elsewhere." | 10 |

### 1.5 Paper II: Writing [100], 3 hours
"The questions will focus on what style is deemed appropriate to a given context and why."
- **Section A: Literature [60]**
  - Q1 Shakespeare [30], compulsory. A literary essay: "the ability to state and defend a position in an essay of approximately 600 words".
  - Novel [30]: "Candidates must study one of the two prescribed novels." There are two optional essay topics per novel and candidates answer one. "Essay topics on each work may incorporate a quotation from the novel, or be rooted in a critic's comment." About 600 words.
- **Section B: Transactional writing [40] = 2 × 20.** Formats: "Editorial; Blog; Obituary; Eulogy; Articles – newspaper and magazines; Newspaper Column; E-mails and letters – formal; editor; application, open; Speeches; Reviews". Prompts are "based on a response to a given text/ photograph/ advertisement/ literature studied". Q6 is compulsory, then Q7 or Q8 (both from a single text). The focus is "an appropriate personal response to the prompt, and to choose the correct format, style and register". "The body of each piece should be between 250 and 300 words".

### 1.6 SBA / learner file [100] (Grade 12, pre-2026-change version)
- General: the file is "a collection of the year's work" showing "all the skills of the Grade 12 curriculum have been covered". It needs a learner "declaration of authenticity". Plagiarism rules must be known in advance. Tasks must require the learner's "own perspective". Regional moderation runs 15 Sept–15 Oct. National moderation submission is by 31 Oct (another passage says 7 Nov). The CAT is "written under controlled conditions".

| Section | Requirement | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Extended writing (creative) | "Two pieces of extended writing of 500 – 600 words ... must be distinctly different in terms of style or genre. Literary Essays cannot be included." Process writing: drafts included, "A maximum of two drafts", mark only the final draft. "Essays must be marked out of a minimum of 30 marks." "No short stories please." Reports, literary essays and adverts are not allowed. | 2 × 45 = 90 |
| 2 Common Assessment Task (CAT) | Theme sent to schools "in the March delivery". Includes an essay "written under controlled conditions", a conversation task and a prepared speaking task (both used in the oral). Set and moderated by cluster; marked to IEB guidelines. | 50 |
| 3 Literature: 4th genre (film) | "Schools must study any two films". Outcomes: explain cinematographic techniques (colour, subtitle, composition, dialogue, music, sound, lighting, editing, framing, shot styles, camera techniques/movement, foregrounding, backgrounding) in meaning-making; summarise the film's stance on a theme and how the audience is positioned; compare own perspective; analyse and evaluate messages and design/performance choices. "Total length ... between 1000 and 1200 words". One task or 2–3 smaller ones. "Inter-textuality is strongly encouraged". "Should not be studied in the same manner" as examined genres. Films must be reported to the IEB by 28 Feb (form D1). | 60 |
| 4 Tests (at least three) | From: literature (prescribed works; literary essays encouraged; contextual questions only if they need "the prescribed text in its entirety"); comprehension/advertising/critical language/poetry; 4th genre ("both thematic and cinematographic"). Checklist: "summative, at least 30 minutes in length, of a demanding standard and worth a minimum of 25 marks"; "1 test must be from the 4th genre"; "No study guides, exemplars, old examination papers or textbook questions"; "no multiple choice – no one word answers"; no open-book tests; no transactional tasks; no peer assessment. Allowed pairings from June/mini-prelim: comprehension + summary; seen + unseen poetry; visual analysis + language; long essay; mini-essay. | 60 |
| 5 Preparatory/Trials exams | Full scripts of both papers. They "must reflect the final examination layout with regard to mark breakdown; number of choices etc." Design grids required. | 40 |
| Total | coversheet: 300 ÷ 3 = 100 | 100 |

- Presentation: dividers, no plastic sleeves or flip files, blue or black ink, controlled-condition pieces marked, "no credit will be given for decorated files". Fourth-genre tasks: "recall of plot and characterisation is not sufficient"; author-biography research is "invitation to plagiarism"; "Empathy-based /personal opinion transactional tasks that do not explicitly engage with the concerns of the novel/play/film are not acceptable."
- Assessment approach: "Global assessment ... first indication" (place the piece in a level from its first and last paragraphs, read the whole, then apply the rubric). Guard against personal, religious or ideological bias. "Creativity" is called problematical, so judge linguistic competence and clarity of intention. The teacher's final % should reflect "ability, aptitude and application ... not merely be an aggregate".

### 1.7 Oral [100]
| Task | Marks |
|---|---|
| Prepared speech | 20 |
| CAT speech | 20 |
| CAT conversation with listening | 20 |
| Additional conversation | 10 |
| Listening comprehension | 10 |
| Unprepared reading | 10 |
| Prepared reading | 10 |
- Prepared speech: a meaningful topic with substance, formal register, "no longer than a maximum of 5 minutes", "talk to their audience, not at them", "only cue cards", "Reading from a written text should be heavily penalised". PowerPoint is allowed but is an aid only. Learners selected for moderation must deliver a prepared speech.
- Prepared reading: passage "two-thirds to three-quarters of a page", mature. Introduction must give title, author and motivation. Eye contact, pace, pause, pitch. Dialogue is useful.
- Unprepared reading: a demanding passage; "a minute or two" of silent reading first. Tests phrasing and sense units, not subtle tone.
- Listening comprehension: at least one, standardised across the department. Written texts ≤450 words, audio ≤5 min; may be several short texts on one theme. Process: listen → questions read → listen again → questions re-read; notes allowed. Must include fact/opinion, inference, general and specific detail, "at least one question which targets evaluation".
- Conversation: listen, sustain, don't dominate, answer directly. Topics can be current affairs or texts. Learners must show "additional reading (this cannot include prescribed works studied in other grades)". Each learner completes a profile sheet (D2).
- Moderation is online or a school visit, Sept to mid-Oct, using a rank-order list (D3) and a spreadsheet of all assessments.
- Rubrics: D5 prepared speech, D6 prepared reading, D7 unprepared reading. All use 7 levels (L7 80–100 ... L1 0–29). D8 conversation uses a 10-point scale with A+ to F descriptors.

### 1.8 Cognitive levels, rubrics and essay guidance
- **Cognitive weighting** (SBA checklist, for tests and prelims): "Blooms split: 20% Evaluation and Synthesis, 40% Analysis, 40% Recall, Comprehension, Application". Design/analysis grids are required (D.23 exemplar: skill, content, Bloom level, difficulty rating, marks). D.24 gives the adapted Bloom's taxonomy (knowledge → comprehension → application → analysis → evaluation → synthesis). The SAG gives no separate weighting for the final exam papers. The memos use SOLO-style descriptors (§3.5).
- Rubrics in the SAG: D15 personal writing (7 levels with % bands), D16 argumentative essay (introduction / quality of argument / structure, register), D17 reflective essay, D18 transactional (20 = 10 Purpose, audience & content + 10 Language & register; "approximate length of 250 words ... not prescriptive"; half marks allowed), D19 literary essay (30; levels 7++ to 1; "not a check-list"; about 600–650 words in the SAG version), D20 summary (10; global marking).
- **Summary rubric (D20)**: Very good 10–8½, Good 8–7, Average 6½–5½, Below average 5–4, Very weak 3–1. Exceeding the word limit caps the mark at 9/7/5½/4 by category. "Stop reading from 10 words over the word count". −1 (max −2) for no word count or "an inexcusably inaccurate word count". (The 2026 deck proposes removing half marks.)
- **Guidelines for the literary essay** (SAG): it assesses "interpretative ability" and the ability to "form an opinion". Topics should be "open-ended"; "A definitive reply ... should not be decided upon". "It is essential that the essay be introduced at grade 10 level". Length "between 2½ and 3½ pages". "The way in which a learner argues is as important as what the learner is arguing". Use short, integrated quotations; keep returning to the topic; analyse rather than mention incidents; formal register with no contractions; underline titles; "no sub-headings"; use transitions. "It is perfectly acceptable for learners to use the first person ... However, the use of the third person is also a legitimate route".
- **Film study guidelines**: learners must be "filmically literate" and able to decode camera, soundtrack, casting and editing for theme, mood and power relations. It is "not enough ... to describe a character". They should know shots and angles, editing, camera movement (panning, tracking, tilting, zooming), light and colour, casting, costume and make-up, soundtrack types (leitmotif, synchronous/non-synchronous, background), mise-en-scène, proxemics, and should "employ the terms index and icon (instead of literal/denotative and figurative/connotative)".

### 1.9 Grade 10 and 11 notes (all the SAG has)
- The literary essay "must be introduced at grade 10 level", with care to explain format and argument. Learners "will initially find the task a daunting one".
- The "texts used/produced" lists apply across Grades 10–12. A "wide selection of texts must be used in integrated teaching over the three-year period."
- D.26 Suggested length of texts ("taken from DBE CAPS documents"):

### 3.2 P2 (100 marks, 3 h): identical structure all three years
- **Section A Literature [60]**. Q1 Shakespeare, compulsory: a quotation from a critic, then a TOPIC ("Critically discuss / Argue to what extent ..."), about 600 words, a literary essay, "Do NOT provide a word count". The novels are Q2–Q3 (novel 1) and Q4–Q5 (novel 2); answer one. Each topic is quotation-based and usually requires "a minimum of TWO characters". The first topic of each novel uses the **same critic quotation** applied to both novels. All are **essays, with no contextual questions.**
  - 2023: *The Tempest* (Bowling, natural order and Prospero's leadership). *The Handmaid's Tale* (Fryer "subjective hope and cruel reality"; memory as resistance) / *The Theory of Flight* (Fryer; Okonkwo "triumph and defeat ... no new beginning").
  - 2024: *Othello* (Richmond: audience identifies with dominant Iago). *Handmaid's Tale* (Adamo "sense of closure"; Needham illegal relationships and male dominance) / *Theory of Flight* (Adamo closure; Knaggs on Genie's nature).
  - 2025: *Othello* (Harries: reality "twisted by perspective"). *Brave New World* (Shapland "ridiculous or horrific ... improved society"; Russell "freedom of mind to challenge propaganda") / *The White Tiger* (Shapland; Korte on resisting poverty and prejudice).
- **Section B Transactional [40]**. Q6 compulsory + Q7 or Q8, 20 each. "The body of your response should be approximately 250 to 300 words". "It is essential that the appropriate format is used." The stimulus is an article or extract.
  - 2023: Q6 blog on "woke AI" (Britannica definition + Fox News Musk article). Q7 open letter to the Roald Dahl Story Company CEO on censorship, or Q8 speech at assembly on writers' freedom of speech.
  - 2024: Q6 open letter to the Class of 2024 for Daily Maverick (stimulus "Hope on the Horizon"). Q7 email applying to be brand ambassador for Dapper Shooz, or Q8 first blog entry reviewing a SA product, place or experience.
  - 2025: Q6 open letter to the Pulitzer committee on Kendrick Lamar/hip-hop (NYT extract). Q7 email to Oxford Children's Dictionary editors (nature vs tech words, *The Lost Words*), or Q8 eulogy for your childhood.

### 3.3 Recurring patterns 2023–2025
- P1 order and marks never change: 25 / 10 / 15 / 15 / 25 / 10.
- **Intertextuality everywhere**: the comprehension ends with a second text (quote + tweet, song lyrics, a seen poem). The poetry questions end with a two-poem comparison, often through a supplied definition or quotation.
- **The unseen poetry question always pairs a genuinely unseen poem with a *Clusters* poem.** The SAG allows "an extract from another prescribed poem".
- The summary is always ≤90 words, one paragraph, multi-source (2–3 texts, including tweets and infographics), recast for a new audience and format (pamphlet, workbook, speech).
- Visual literacy uses real campaign adverts (mostly adsoftheworld.com), with language items embedded (voice, punctuation, sentence type). Q6 was a cartoon in 2023 and 2024 and a short article in 2025.
- Topics are contemporary and youth-facing: AI/ChatGPT, education, SASL, phones, democracy, censorship, hip-hop, Gen Z slang.
- P2 essays are always "to what extent / critically discuss" against a critic's quotation and require at least two characters. The open letter was the compulsory Q6 in 2024 and 2025. Choice formats included blog, email, speech and eulogy.

### 3.4 Marking approach (memos)
- All memos say examiners use the guidelines after a standardisation meeting, and that "The IEB will not enter into any discussions or correspondence about any marking guidelines".
- **P1**: "to be used in conjunction with the Generic Descriptors". Model answers come with bracketed conditions, for example: name a specific tone or positioning word or the mark is capped; discuss both texts or max 2; link a device to content, not just its function; "Credit valid alternative responses". Half marks are used. Descriptors by mark value:
  - 2 marks: "uni-structural thinking"
  - 3 marks: "multi-structural"
  - 4 marks: "relational thinking"
  - 5 marks: "extended abstract thinking"
- **Summary**: "Global marking, giving credit for concise and coherent sentences expressed in the candidate's own words". 5 categories (10–8½ ... 3–1). "Penalise from 94 words onwards by moving the mark to the bottom of the category". "Stop reading at 100 words". −1 for a missing or "inexcusably inaccurate" word count. Register is judged "for the format". (This differs from the SAG's D20 cut-off of 10 words over.)
- **P2 memos are rubric-only** (no content memo): "Assessment rubric: Shakespeare and novel literature essay (30 marks)", holistic, "The rubric is not a check-list (tick-box)", half marks allowed, about 600 words, "no penalty for length other than the development of the argument", mind-maps not credited. Mark bands:
  - 7++ = 30 ("full mark essay", "error free")
  - 7+ = 27–29 (90–99%)
  - 7 = 24–26
  - 6 = 21–23
  - 5 = 18–20 (multi-structural)
  - 4 = 15–17 (uni-structural)
  - 3 = 12–14
  - 2 = 9–11 (pre-structural)
  - 1 = 7–8 / 0–6
  - Criteria: knowledge, argument, thinking, structure.
- **Transactional rubric** = 10 Purpose, audience & content + 10 Language & register, marked independently (e.g. L7 + L5). Levels run from 7+ "impressive scintillating" to 1 "incoherent". Half marks are allowed.
