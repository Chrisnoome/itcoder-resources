## 1. CAPS policy

### 1.1 Time and skills (CAPS §2.6)
- 4.5 h/week over a 40-week year, planned in **2-week cycles of 9 h**. Gr 10–11 have 36 teaching weeks and 4 exam weeks. Gr 12 has 30 teaching weeks and 10 exam weeks. Timetable one continuous double period per week.
- Time per 2-week cycle:

| Skill | Hours | % |
|---|---|---|
| Listening & Speaking | 1 | 10 |
| Reading & Viewing (comprehension and literature) | 4 | 45 |
| Writing & Presenting | 4 | 45 |
| Language Structures & Conventions (LSC) | integrated into the three skills above | — |

- Approaches: text-based, communicative, integrated and process-oriented. Every 2 weeks learners should do one L&S exercise, a comprehension or summary, prescribed literature, an essay and/or a transactional piece, and LSC work. Homework is at least 3 h/week.
- Each learner needs an approved language textbook, the approved/prescribed **novel, drama and poetry**, a dictionary, and newspapers and magazines.

### 1.2 Listening & Speaking (CAPS §3.1)
- **Listening process:** pre-listening, during listening and post-listening. Three kinds of listening: for specific information; for critical analysis and evaluation; and for appreciation and interaction.
- **Speaking process:** plan, research and organise; then practise and present.
- **Oral text types with their features:** prepared speech; unprepared speech; interview (introduction, rapport, questions, summary, closure); introducing a speaker; vote of thanks; panel discussion (duties of the chair); informal discussion/conversation; debate (resolution, rebuttal, affirmative and negative speakers, procedure). The durations table below also lists dialogues, directions, storytelling and meetings. The ATPs also schedule reading aloud (unprepared), "meetings and meeting procedures" and informal conversation.
- **Listening comprehension texts:** about 200 / 300 / 400 words for Gr 10 / 11 / 12. Audio clips are 2 min (Gr 10–11) or 3 min (Gr 12), read at least twice, or set as a 30-minute test that includes the clip.
- **Suggested durations (minutes):**

| Oral text | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Conversations, debates, forum, group or panel discussions | 20–30 |
| Dialogues | 6–8 |
| Directions and instructions | 4–5 |
| Interviews | 10–15 |
| Introducing a speaker, vote of thanks | 4–5 |
| Prepared speech, report, review | 4–5 |
| Unprepared speech | 2–3 |
| Storytelling | 8–10 |
| Meetings and procedures | 10–15 |

### 1.3 Reading & Viewing (CAPS §3.2)
- **Reading process:** pre-reading (skim and scan text features and parts of a book, predict, key vocabulary); reading (comprehension strategies, notes and summaries); post-reading (lower- to higher-order questions, compare, evaluate, reproduce the genre).
- **Critical language awareness:** fact and opinion; direct and implied meaning; denotation and connotation; socio-political and cultural background; the effect of selection and omission; language and power; emotive and manipulative language, bias, prejudice, stereotyping, assumptions and arguments.
- **Visual texts:** persuasive techniques; how language and images shape values (sexist, racist or ageist stereotypes, especially in adverts); the effect of font type and size, headings and captions; cartoons and comic strips.
- **Vocabulary:** figures of speech and rhetorical devices, taught only as they occur in real texts. CAPS asks "why was it used?" rather than "name it". Also denotation and connotation; dictionary and thesaurus use; prefixes, suffixes and roots; context clues; polysemes; allusions; idioms and proverbs; word origins; confusables (homophones, homonyms, homographs); synonyms and antonyms; one word for a phrase; collocations.
- **Sentence and text structure:** conjunctions and transition words; abbreviations and acronyms; verb forms and mood; simple, compound, complex and compound-complex sentences; active and passive voice; direct and indirect speech; word order; concord, articles, infinitives, copulatives and prepositions; punctuation; paragraph and text organisation patterns.
- **Literary features:**
  - *Poetry:* literal and figurative meaning, mood, theme, imagery, figures of speech, diction, tone, sound devices, enjambment and so on.
  - *Drama:* plot and sub-plot, characterisation, theme, setting, mood and tone, ironic twist, stage directions, dialogue, monologue and soliloquy, dramatic irony.
  - *Novel:* plot, conflict, characterisation, narrator, theme, setting, mood, ironic twist, timeline.
  - *Short stories and film study* are **for enrichment only**. Film study covers cinematographic techniques.
- **Texts for integrated teaching:** literary genres (novel, drama, poetry; enrichment texts such as films, essays, biographies, folk tales, myths, speeches, songs and jokes); written information and media texts (dictionaries, articles, editorials, notices, obituaries, reviews, brochures, adverts); interpersonal and transactional texts (letters, diaries, invitations, emails, SMS and twitter, notes, reports, formal letters, minutes and agendas); and multimedia and visual texts (charts, graphs, posters, web pages, social networks, films, photos, cartoons, comic strips, graffiti, radio, recorded speeches).
- **Lengths of texts to read:**

| Text | Gr 10 | Gr 11 | Gr 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comprehension passage (words) | 500–600 | 600–700 | 700–800 |
| Summary source text (words) | 280 | 320 | 350 |
| Summary length (all grades) | 80–90 words | | |

### 1.4 Writing & Presenting (CAPS §3.3)
- **Process writing:** plan, draft, revise, edit, proofread and present.
- **LSC during writing:** register, style and voice; word choice; sentence construction; paragraph writing (topic sentence, cohesion and transition words); punctuation and spelling.
- **Essay types:** narrative, descriptive, argumentative, discursive, reflective, and the literary essay. CAPS defines them as follows:
  - *Argumentative:* one-sided.
  - *Discursive:* balanced, with the writer's view in the conclusion.
  - *Reflective:* personal thoughts, with no need to argue or conclude.
- **Transactional texts:**
  - *Formal letters:* application, editor/press, complaint, request, business, sympathy, thanks, congratulations.
  - *Other texts:* friendly/informal letter; agenda and minutes; speech; dialogue; interview; formal and informal report; review; newspaper article; magazine article; CV and covering letter; obituary.
  - *Email:* added in A19 and A20.
- **Lengths to produce (words):**

| Text | Gr 10 | Gr 11 | Gr 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Literary essay: poetry | 150–200 | 200–250 | 250–300 |
| Literary essay: novel/drama | 300–350 | 350–400 | 400–450 |
| Essay (narrative, descriptive, reflective, argumentative, discursive) | 300–350 | 350–400 | 400–450 |
| Transactional text, Gr 10–12 (content only) | 180–200 | | |

### 1.5 Language Structures & Conventions (CAPS §3.4 and Appendix 1, pp. 93–94)
- CAPS says not to teach LSC in "discrete, isolated lessons". LSC is taught through text analysis and the learners' own writing. Each term's plan has a cycle that focuses on LSC.
- **Appendix 1 checklist** (the ATPs refer to "pp 93–94"):
  - **Vocabulary:** synonyms, antonyms, paronyms, polysemes, homonyms, homophones, one word for a phrase; figures of speech (simile, metaphor, personification, oxymoron, metonymy, onomatopoeia, hyperbole, contrast, irony, sarcasm, anti-climax, symbol, euphemism, litotes, paradox, pun, understatement, synecdoche); idioms and proverbs; borrowed and new words (neologisms) and etymology; prefixes, roots and suffixes.
  - **Word classes:** nouns (abstract, concrete, common, proper, compound, collective, countable and uncountable, gerunds, gender, plural, diminutives); pronouns (indefinite, relative, demonstrative, possessive, personal, reflexive); verbs and modals (tenses including present perfect progressive; passive forms; transitive and intransitive; finite and non-finite; copulative; regular and irregular; phrasal; auxiliaries; subjunctive, imperative, conditional, indicative and potential moods); adjectives (comparative, superlative); adverbs (manner, place, time, frequency and so on); question forms; prepositions.
  - **Clauses and sentences:** simple, compound, complex and compound-complex sentences; relative and noun clauses; direct and indirect speech; word order; concord; active and passive voice; negation.
  - **Conjunctions and transitions by paragraph type:** chronological, cause-effect, procedure, compare/contrast, order of importance, spatial, generalisation, choice, classification, description, evaluation, definition, concluding.
  - **Other:** interjections and ideophones; exclamations; punctuation (hyphen, colon, semi-colon, apostrophe, quotation marks, parentheses, ellipses); spelling rules, abbreviations and acronyms; critical language awareness (as in §1.3).

### 1.6 Literature requirements per grade
- **CAPS** requires novel, drama and poetry. Poems: **Gr 10: 10, Gr 11: 10, Gr 12: 12**. CAPS says the "recommended literary genres are included in the National Literature Catalogue". It gives **no** number of novels or dramas per grade. It does **not** say in so many words that Gr 10–11 schools must choose from a DBE catalogue: the catalogue sentence is the only reference. A list of Gr 10–11 titles is **not in the sources**. The Gr 12 titles are prescribed by circular (§5).
- **Genres in the ATP** (as studied and examined in Paper 2):

| Grade | Genres in Paper 2 |
|---|---|
| Gr 10 | **Poetry + Drama** (no novel) |
| Gr 11 | **Poetry + Novel/folklore** (no drama) |
| Gr 12 | **Poetry + Novel + Drama** (all three) |

- **Folklore:** EG says African HL learners may take folklore instead of the novel. The Gr 11 English ATP still writes "Novel/folklore".
- **Paper 2 format, original CAPS (all grades):** poetry, both seen and unseen; Section B novel or Section C drama, with one essay and one contextual question.
- **Literature assignment (Task 8, Term 3, Gr 10–11):** Circular S7/2025 gives standard exemplars (folder Assessment/Lit-assignment-exemplars-2025/).
  - *Gr 10 and Gr 11 exemplars:* 35 marks, 2 weeks (week 1 research, week 2 writing), open book, at least 3 sources and a bibliography. Section A is a compulsory poetry essay (10). Then answer Section B (novel, 25) or Section C (drama, 25). The page also carries a "Research (20) ÷ 2 = /10" rubric line.
  - *Word counts:* Gr 10 asks for 150–200 poetry words in one place and 200–250 in another; novel/drama 300–350. Gr 11 asks for poetry 200–250 and novel/drama 350–400.
  - *Example texts:* Gr 10 uses *Mother to Mother*, *The Mark*, *Romeo and Juliet* and *The Lighthouse Keeper's Wife*. Gr 11 uses *Things Fall Apart*. These are example texts only, not prescribed lists.
  - *"Extra" Gr 11 options 1 and 2:* one 35-mark researched essay of 350–400 words on poetry, novel or drama, with an AI-use rule (verify against 2 sources).

## 3. Gr 12 exam guidelines (EG, DBE 2021, the latest edition)

EG still points to Circular E39 of 2016 for literature. Use E11 of 2024 instead (§5).

### Paper 1: Language in Context (70 marks, 2 h)
Suggested times: Section A 50 min, Section B 30 min, Section C 40 min.

- **Section A: Comprehension (30).**
  - Text A is prose of 700–800 words. Text B is a related visual or graphic text worth 6–10 marks.
  - Comparative questions on both texts: at most 4 marks.
  - At most 2 open-ended questions and only 1 multiple-choice question (4 options).
  - Spelling is not penalised unless it changes meaning. Yes/No, True/False and Fact/Opinion answers earn no marks without a reason.
- **Section B: Summary (10).**
  - Source text of about 350 words, not taken from the comprehension. One paragraph, at most 90 words, with the word count given in brackets.
  - Marking: 7 points (1 each) + 3 language marks.
  - Language marks when the candidate did not quote: 1–3 points → 1; 4–5 → 2; 6–7 → 3.
  - When the candidate quoted verbatim: 6–7 quotes → 0; 4–5 → at most 1; 2–3 → at most 2.
  - Beyond the word limit, stop reading at the last sentence within the limit. There is no penalty for a wrong or missing word count.
- **Section C: LSC in context (30).**
  - **Q3 Advertisement (10):** 8 marks on persuasive techniques, values and stereotypes, and font; 2 marks on vocabulary **or** sentence structure.
  - **Q4 Cartoon (10):** 8 marks on the same areas plus analysing the cartoon; 2 marks on whichever of vocabulary or sentence structure Q3 did **not** use.
  - **Q5 Prose (10):** a 150–200-word text; 5 marks vocabulary and language use, 5 marks sentence structures.
  - At most 2 open-ended questions in Section C (one on the advert, one on the cartoon) and at most 2 multiple-choice questions.

### Paper 2: Literature (80 marks, 2.5 h)
Answer 5 questions. Suggested times: Section A about 40 min, Section B about 55 min, Section C about 55 min.

- **Section A: Poetry (30).**
  - Q1 is an essay on a seen poem (250–300 words). Q2–4 are contextual. Answer **any two** of Q1–4 (10 each).
  - Q5 is a compulsory unseen poem, contextual (10).
- **Section B: Novel (25).** Q6–11, with an essay or a contextual question on each novel.
- **Section C: Drama (25).** Q12–15, with an essay or a contextual question on each drama.
- **Choice rule:** answer **one essay and one contextual** question across Sections B and C, never two of the same kind.
- **Lengths:** essays 400–450 words. Extracts are either one of 350–450 words or two of about 250 words each. In drama, speakers' names are not counted but stage directions are.
- **Marking:** if a candidate answers extra questions, mark the first ones. A short essay is not penalised further.
- **Folklore:** an option instead of the novel for African HLs only.

### Paper 3: Writing (100 marks, 3 h)
Plan before the answer. Suggested times: essay about 100 min, transactional about 2 × 40 min.

- **Section A: Essay (50).**
  - Eight topics, of which 2–3 are visual stimuli. Types: narrative, descriptive, reflective, discursive, argumentative. The topic must not prescribe the type.
  - Length 400–450 words.
  - Marks: content and planning 30 (60%); language, style and editing 15 (30%); structure 5 (10%).
- **Section B: Transactional (2 × 25).**
  - Six topics in three categories:
    - **Category A** (1–2 topics): letters (friendly; formal: request, application, business, complaint, sympathy, congratulations, thanks), letter to the press, CV and covering letter, e-mail.
    - **Category B** (1–3 topics): formal or informal report, review, newspaper article, magazine article, agenda and minutes (asked together).
    - **Category C** (1–3 topics): formal or informal speech, dialogue, written interview, obituary.
  - Length 180–200 words, content only. The type of text is given as a heading. Visuals are support material only.
  - Marks: content, planning and format 15 (60%); language, style and editing 10 (40%).
  - The rubric already carries the penalties: no extra penalties for length.

### Cognitive levels (Barrett's taxonomy, CAPS Table 3 and EG §7)

| Level | Type | Typical stems | Share of marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Literal | name, list, identify, describe | Levels 1+2: **40%** |
| 2 | Reorganisation | summarise, group, state similarities and differences | (with Level 1) |
| 3 | Inference | explain, what is the writer's intention, effect of an image | **40%** |
| 4 | Evaluation | do you think, is the argument valid, comment critically, justified? | Levels 4+5: **20%** |
| 5 | Appreciation | discuss your response, empathise, effectiveness of style or imagery | (with Level 4) |

These shares apply to every section and question. A literary essay requires discussing a statement "argumentatively or discursively", with a personal response.

## 4. NSC Nov 2023–2025 past papers: recurring patterns

### Paper 1 (the same skeleton every year)

**Q1, Comprehension (30).** Text A is an opinion or feature article. Text B is a related visual: an advert, or a cartoon in 2024. Questions 1.1–1.10 or so are on Text A, then 2–3 on Text B, then one on both texts.

| Year | Text A | Text B |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | "Listening instead of reading is not cheating" (audiobooks) | Audible advert |
| 2024 | "The era of instant gratification" | Cab-driver cartoon |
| 2025 | Youth "profit for purpose" entrepreneurship | MTN SME advert |

Recurring stems: why the writer uses a word, a repetition or a rhetorical question; irony or paradox; tone and its suitability; diction and the writer's attitude; one multiple-choice question; "Is the final paragraph an appropriate conclusion?"; whether the visual's portrayal fits the target audience; how both texts relate.

**Q2, Summary (10).** Seven points, at most 90 words, a fluent paragraph, in the candidate's own words. Topics: the benefits of silence (2023), why contentment beats happiness (2024), what makes sports fans (2025).

**Q3, Advert(s) (10).** 2023 had one advert (Levi's). 2024 and 2025 had two adverts, Texts D and E, with a comparison question. Questions cover image-text link, slogan purpose, persuasive or stylistic technique, depiction of people, a pun ("Lettuce do more"), and one or two grammar items (apostrophe, root word, rewriting a clause as a statement).

**Q4, Cartoon/comic strip (10).** Calvin and Hobbes (2023), a 10-frame strip (2024), Zits (2025). Questions cover attitude and tone, body language and visual cues, how the humour or satire works, plus grammar items: suffix, synonym, comparative, better verb, uses of the apostrophe.

**Q5, Prose editing text (10).** One-mark items: homophones; antonyms in context; concord and pronoun errors; tense; redundancy; malapropism; reported speech; punctuation (commas, dash, hyphen function); part of speech; Standard English for a phrasal verb; italics for a foreign phrase (*modus operandi*).

### Paper 2

**Poems set from the 12 prescribed:**

| Year | Q1 essay | Q2–4 contextual | Unseen (Q5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 'Sonnet 130' | 'Talk to the Peach Tree', 'The Shipwreck', 'Poem of Return' | 'The Abandoned Mine' |
| 2024 | 'Fern Hill' (extract) | 'This Winter Coming', 'Prayer to Masks', 'At a Funeral' | 'Farm Gate' |
| 2025 | 'Solitude' | 'It Is a Beauteous Evening, Calm and Free', 'Poem of Return', 'This Winter Coming' | 'The Trees' |

- **Poetry essay pattern:** a statement about the poem, then "critically discuss/discuss the extent to which you agree ... with close reference to diction/imagery/tone/structure", in 250–300 words.
- **Question numbering (all three years):** Q6–7 *The Picture of Dorian Gray*; Q8–9 *Life of Pi*; Q10–11 *Hamlet*; Q12–13 *Othello*; Q14–15 *The Crucible*.
- **Literary essays (25):** a contestable statement, then "critically discuss the extent to which you agree/the validity", in 400–450 words. In 2023 and 2025 the three drama essays shared one statement: 2023 "many characters are totally dishonourable and cannot be redeemed"; 2025 "deception and disguise", with a minimum of 2–3 characters named.
- **Contextual questions (25):** two extracts per text (A/B, C/D and so on), 9–10 sub-questions worth 2–3 marks. Typical stems: "place the extract in context", "explain the irony in light of later events", "what does this reveal about...", "discuss how these lines influence your response", and a closing Level 4/5 question: "to what extent do you agree...".

### Paper 3

**Q1 Essays.** Eight topics each year: 1.1–1.5 are words or quotations, and 1.6–1.8 are pictures (the candidate gives a title; there must be a clear link to the picture). Mixed prompt kinds every year:

| Kind of prompt | Examples |
|---|---|
| Evocative phrase | "The rhythm of city life", "A room full of mirrors", "In the rear-view mirror" |
| Debatable statement | "Abolishing plastic is an unrealistic expectation", "AI is a threat", "In the modern world, more is never enough" |
| Quotation, proverb or song lyric | — |
| Narrative opening line | "It was a loud and hearty laugh...", "It wasn't a 'bang'..." |

**Q2 Transactional.** Six topics each year; answer two.

| Year | Topics |
|---|---|
| 2023 | dialogue, formal letter (complaint to councillor), magazine article, speech, e-mail (thanks + feedback), formal report |
| 2024 | interview, book review, letter to the press, informal speech, e-mail (job application from an advert), magazine article |
| 2025 | e-mail (competition entry from an advert), speech (Africa Day), magazine article, formal letter (to principal), book review, dialogue |

Recurring: e-mail, magazine article and speech every year; dialogue or interview every year; a review in 2024 and 2025. Not set in 2023–25: obituary, CV, agenda and minutes, informal report, friendly letter.
