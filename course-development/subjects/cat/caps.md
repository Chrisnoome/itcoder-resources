`sources/cat/caps/CAPS_FET_CAT_Gr10-12.pdf` (the 2011 original, 60 pages)
and `sources/cat/caps/CAPS_CAT_Amendment_2024_Section3.pdf` (the 2024
amendment, 53 pages). Extracted with `pdftotext -layout`.

**The amendment replaces more than its title says.** It is filed on the DBE
site as "Amendment: CAT CAPS 2024 - Section 3", and `sources/cat/SOURCES.md`
records it that way (see [course-plan.md](course-plan.md) §6 note 2), but the
file itself runs from "SECTION 3 - OVERVIEW OF
CONTENT AND SCOPE PER TOPIC" through the **whole of Section 4 (assessment,
programme of assessment, Paper 1 and Paper 2 formats, PAT, cognitive and
difficulty levels)** and ends with a new Annexure A. So it replaces
**Sections 3 and 4** of the original, and everything in §2-§4 below is the
amendment's text, not the 2011 document's. Only §1 comes from the original.

**What this file is for:** a token-cheap reference to check CAPS wording,
term placement, weighting or exam format without re-opening the PDFs - the
CAPS twin of [sags.md](sags.md). It is a faithful, cleaned transcription of
the CAPS text, not a teaching document.

**Scope - what's in and what's left out:**

- **In:** Section 2 (what CAT is, aims, time allocation, resources) from the
  original; the whole of the 2024 Section 3 - the six topics' content and
  scope tables and the suggested teaching plan, every topic, Grades 10-12,
  term by term; and the whole of the 2024 Section 4.
- **Out:** Section 1 (the general CAPS preamble shared by every subject);
  the original Sections 3 and 4 (replaced); the new Annexure A (a glossary
  of 60 acronyms - CAT-specific ones are `POS`, `SOHO`, `BYOD`, `DLP`,
  `WiMAX`, `WYSIWIG` (sic), the rest are the usual); and the topic-overlap
  diagram on page 8 of the original, which is an image with no extractable
  text.

**Two numbering slips in the amendment**, kept here as the source has them:
its Internet Technologies section and its Information Management section are
**both numbered 3.4**, so Social Implications is 3.5, the teaching plan's
introduction is 3.6 and the plans themselves are 3.7.1-3.7.3. Section 3
therefore has no 3.7 heading of its own.

**How CAPS differs from the IEB SAGs, in one breath:** six topics, not four,
and no subtopic numbers - the content is a **suggested teaching plan per
grade per term**, so a place in CAPS is "Grade 10, Term 3, Internet
Technologies". Presentations are taught but **examined only in the PAT**;
databases and HTML start in Grade 11; the practical paper is
word processing + spreadsheet + database + HTML in one integrated,
scenario-based paper. Promotion weights and the theory paper's shape both
differ (see §4). Quoted wording is CAPS's own, cleaned of PDF artefacts
only; "±" in the source is written "~" here.

## 1. Introduction to Computer Applications Technology (original Section 2)

**What CAT is:** the study of the integrated components of a computer system
(hardware and software) and the practical techniques for their efficient use
and application to solve everyday problems. Solutions are designed, managed
and processed via **end-user applications** and communicated using
appropriate ICTs. ICTs are the combination of networks, hardware and
software, and the means of communication, collaboration and engagement, that
enable the processing, management and exchange of data, information and
knowledge.

**The six topics and their weighting (volume):**

| Topic | Sub-topics | Weighting |
|---|---|---|
| Solution Development | Word Processing, Spreadsheets, Databases, Fourth Application | **60%** |
| Systems Technologies | Concepts of Computing, Hardware, Software, Computer Management | 13% |
| Network Technologies | PANs, LANs and WLANs, WANs | 5% |
| Internet Technologies | Internet and World Wide Web, E-communications | 5% |
| Information Management | Find and Access Data and Information, Process Data and Information, Present Solution | 12% |
| Social Implications | Impact on Society, Legal and Ethical and Security Issues, Health and Ergonomic Issues, Environmental Issues | 5% |

**Topic links and overlap** (CAPS's own note): there is always overlap.
Solution development is enabled by systems technologies in the form of
application software; systems technologies allow electronic communication;
network technologies enable the Internet; information management overlaps
solution development and Internet technologies and is enabled by systems
technologies; all ICT activity is driven by human need, which gives rise to
social and ethical issues. Information Management, for instance, should be
taught with Internet Technologies and the application software from Solution
Development.

**Specific aims** - a learner will:
- use end-user software applications proficiently to produce solutions to problems within a defined scenario;
- understand the concepts of ICTs with regard to the technologies that make up a computing system;
- understand the various technologies, standards and protocols involved in the electronic transmission of data via a computer-based network;
- use the Internet and the WWW and understand the role that the Internet plays as part of the global information superhighway;
- find authentic and relevant information, process the information to draw conclusions, make decisions and communicate the findings in appropriate presentation media; and
- recognise the legal, ethical, environmental, social, security and health issues related to the use of ICTs and learn how to use ICTs responsibly.

**Time:** 4 hours a week. Grades 10 and 11: 35 teaching weeks + 5 of exams.
Grade 12: 28 teaching weeks + 12 of exams. Suggested teaching time per topic
(the original's table; the 2024 term plans below give their own hours, which
are what to use):

| Topic | Grade 10 | Grade 11 | Grade 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solution Development | 80 h / 20 wks | 86 h / 21.5 wks | 58 h / 14.5 wks |
| Systems Technologies | 22 h / 5.5 wks | 14 h / 3.5 wks | 16 h / 4 wks |
| Network Technologies | 6 h / 1.5 wks | 4 h / 1 wk | 6 h / 1.5 wks |
| Internet Technologies | 8 h / 2 wks | 10 h / 2.5 wks | 6 h / 1.5 wks |
| Information Management | 16 h / 4 wks | 18 h / 4.5 wks | 20 h / 5 wks |
| Social Implications | 8 h / 2 wks | 8 h / 2 wks | 6 h / 1.5 wks |
| **Total contact** | 140 h / 35 wks | 140 h / 35 wks | 112 h / 28 wks |
| Examination | 20 h / 5 wks | 20 h / 5 wks | 48 h / 12 wks |

**Resources:** the school pays. Learners work **individually on a computer
during contact time** and need Internet access. A business plan should cover:
entrance-level networked computers with a 4-5 year lifespan, one per learner
per period, **enough computers to write the practical exam in two sittings**;
Internet access; one high-speed printer per network; a data projector;
software (operating system, Office suite, security software); and a budget
for licensing, cartridges, paper, storage media, breakage and maintenance,
insurance, connectivity, and a sustainability plan to upgrade or replace
every 4-5 years.

## 2. Content and scope per topic (2024 Section 3, overview)

The amendment's six topic tables, laid out grade by grade. These are the
scope statements; §3 places the same content in terms.

### 2.1 Solution Development (§3.1)

Developing a computer-based solution with application packages to solve
problems represented by real-life scenarios. For CAT these are **end-user
applications**: word processing, spreadsheet, database and presentation.

**Grade 10**
- *Word Processing:* documents; basic file management; basic document layout; page layout; tables; view options; introduction to review and proofing functions; text and paragraph manipulation; graphics, shapes and diagrams; formatting and editing; introduction to integration; basic troubleshooting; lists and columns; existing styles; table of contents; basic referencing
- *Spreadsheets:* basic skills and core concepts; basic file management; cell reference; cell ranges and range names; basic calculations using basic operators; data types; workbooks and worksheets; formatting and editing; introduction to formulae and functions; introduction to calculations; basic functions; basic formulas; introduction to charts/graphs; types of graphs; problem-solving techniques (basic error indicators, basic troubleshooting)
- *Databases:* none in Grade 10
- *Other:* **Presentations** - slide design and layout elements; editing and formatting; text and lists; charts, illustrations and tables; slide manipulation; basic custom animation; slide show; basic integration techniques. **HTML (basic)** - what is HTML?; structure of a simple HTML page; HTML syntax; basic HTML tags; basic text and text formatting; attributes; basic troubleshooting

**Grade 11**
- *Word Processing:* file management; advanced document layout; customising; reviewing tools and proofing functions; electronic forms (legacy tools); mail merge; styles; sections; reference functions; editing - paste special; page layout design - themes; document layout (sections, section breaks, headers and footers); advanced page numbering; customise paragraphs; templates; integration techniques; troubleshooting
- *Spreadsheets:* advanced formulas and functions; **basic computational thinking (building blocks)**; conditional formatting; relational operators; print options; error indicators; extend graphs/charts; manipulate worksheets; import/export data; integration techniques; troubleshooting; subtotal feature
- *Databases:* basic skills and basic knowledge in working with tables, records, fields and data types; records manipulation; basic field properties; formatting and editing; create tables; create forms; basic queries; basic reports; report headers and footers; calculations in reports and queries; import/export data
- *Other:* creating forms using **online application software**; HTML/web design - tables, lists, images, attributes, comments, cite tag (PAT only), links, troubleshooting

**Grade 12**
- *Word Processing:* advanced file handling; customise templates; data sources; professional documents; use/reinforce word processing skills; integration techniques; import/export; troubleshooting; problem solving
- *Spreadsheets:* complex functions; text functions; date and time calculations; named ranges; advanced graphs/charts; validation of data; integration techniques; troubleshooting; problem solving
- *Databases:* advanced reports; changing the source of a report; advanced queries; grouping information; calculation fields in queries and reports; data validation techniques; troubleshooting; problem solving; integration techniques
- *Other:* HTML/web design (refer to the HTML tag sheet) - border attributes; table formatting; good webpage design

*Note (CAPS):* teach the techniques, tools and features so as to develop a
computer-based solution, as an end-user, within different scenarios.
Application packages share common features that are reinforced across
packages. Learners must also be taught the **underlying knowledge and
understanding** of the skills and applications, so the skills transfer to new
versions and to other, similar applications.

### 2.2 Systems Technologies (§3.2)

The physical and non-physical components of a computer system, connected as a
unit to perform input, processing, output, storage, communication and
transfer of data electronically.

**Grade 10**
- *Introduction to Concepts of Computing:* general model of a computing device; types of computing devices; role of computing devices and ICTs; introduction to data and information; GUI; file organisation and compressions
- *Hardware (systems unit):* definition of hardware; hardware components; ports and connectors; input and output; storage and media; peripherals; hardware configuration; scanning and reading devices; processing
- *Software:* definition of software; types of software; software components; definition and overview of system, application and utility software; categories of software; purpose and use of software; introduction to operating system concepts
- *Device Management:* introduction to using computing devices - working environment (GUI) and keyboarding; introduction to files and folders; file manager; print management; adding hardware

**Grade 11**
- *Concepts:* information processing cycle; role of ICTs; types of computing devices; computer categories
- *Hardware:* extend input, output, processing and storage; mobile technology; portable devices; devices for users with disability; alternative I/O and storage devices; interpret adverts; influence of hardware on software; input and output device application/use; basic troubleshooting
- *Software:* application software; software updates; software compatibility; system software; installing software; file management; web applications; software for users with disability; cloud-based applications and storage; system requirements
- *Device Management:* software installation; file management; system properties; basic troubleshooting; factors influencing performance

**Grade 12**
- *Concepts:* computer systems for different users/uses; reasons for using computers; case studies/scenarios regarding use of ICTs; use of computers in a variety of contexts
- *Hardware:* appropriate use of devices; buying decisions; productivity issues; factors influencing performance; case studies; new technology
- *Software:* productivity issues; role of operating system; utilities; buying decisions; human error potential; case studies; new technology; upgrades; file management - **extract a password-protected .exe file**
- *Device Management:* file properties, attributes and metadata; housekeeping; case studies; troubleshooting

*Note (CAPS):* deal with hardware and software at a **non-technical level**,
but enough that the learner can understand the concepts of a computer system,
know the functions and uses of its main components, select appropriate
software for a task, troubleshoot at an elementary level or pick the most
appropriate hardware for a scenario, know whether to upgrade or buy new, be
aware of new trends, know how technology benefits a user in context, and
understand managing and getting the most from a system. **Make a clear
distinction between the disadvantages and the limitations of a concept.**
Teach from familiar to unfamiliar contexts, integrated with Social
Implications and Internet and Network Technologies.

### 2.3 Network Technologies (§3.3)

Technologies that manage and distribute digital data from one point to
another, so people can share information whether they are in one place or
spread out.

**Grade 10** - what a network is; aims and objectives (facilitating
communication and sharing hardware, software, data and information); basic
security and privacy issues; examples of common networks - Internet, PAN
(including wearables), HAN, LAN; network devices (modem, router, switch);
communication channel/media including Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and NFC; pairing
devices; obtaining Internet access; ISP - definition and purpose.

**Grade 11** - LAN and WLAN; basic components of a network (overview and
concepts); basic network security (passwords, usernames, access rights);
connection (type, speed, usage).

**Grade 12** - WAN, with the Internet as the example; Internet services
(uses/purpose, advantages, disadvantages, limitations, examples) - instant
messaging, VoIP, file sharing, the concept of grid and cloud computing;
government Internet services and information; streaming vs downloading;
making informed buying decisions about Internet connection and access
(modem/router, connection types such as fibre and wireless, ISP and its
services, throttling and shaping, access points and coverage, transmission
speed in mbps, cap and bundle); the concept of broadband and bandwidth.

### 2.4 Internet Technologies (§3.4, first of two)

**Grade 10**
- *Internet and WWW:* what the Internet is; Internet addresses; describe the WWW; URL and URL shortener; web page, website, hyperlink; types of websites and what they offer (weblog/vlog, wiki, social network, educational, web application); browsers - what and why, examples, tabbed browsing; search engines - what and why, examples; downloading and uploading; explore and evaluate web pages for readability, navigation, consistency, layout and typography (linked to word processing documents and forms)
- *Electronic Communications:* what is e-communication and what is a communication device; applications that facilitate it (e-mail, web browser, instant messaging, text/picture/video messaging, online meetings); e-mail - taxonomy of e-mail addresses; register a web-based address; basic use of the Internet, e-mail and social media platforms; hyperlinks; netiquette rules; basic e-mailing (compose, send and receive, forward, reply, reply to all)

**Grade 11**
- *Internet and WWW:* Internet of Things (IoT); Fourth Industrial Revolution (4IR); uses of computer communications - social websites; overview of portable and mobile Internet access
- *Electronic Communications:* managing e-mail - folders, sort by, flag, prioritise, distribution lists, message rules, Cc and Bcc, attachments, address books

**Grade 12**
- *Electronic Communications:* video conferencing - advantages, disadvantages, good and bad practices; typical browser features (bookmarks, history and favourites, home page settings, blocking websites, caching, browser extensions - what and why, pop-up/ad blocker, toolbar extension - private browsing); uses of computer communications - blogs/vlogs, podcast/vodcast, wikis, GPS and geo-tagging, social networks; communication devices - smart phones, tablets, wearables

### 2.5 Information Management (§3.4, second of two)

The techniques and technologies of collecting, storing and processing data
into information that leads to knowledge and decision-making, and the tools
to communicate new knowledge and recommendations. **Most of this content can
be taught and reinforced through the PAT.**

**Grade 10**
- *Find and access:* data vs information; understanding the problem/task (role of questions and questioning, using questions); using a plan to solve the problem; types of information sources; information and data gathering - questionnaires/interviews, and one other source
- *Process:* information vs knowledge; sifting of information; find and access information and data; processing data; engaging with information; tools and techniques for manipulating information (make notes and summarise); ethical use of information; utilising data questions; data handling - the role of the spreadsheet and the database
- *Present:* knowledge vs insight/decision making; software tools to communicate findings; simple report; personal understandings; organise information into logical groupings and flow; findings/conclusions; copyright and plagiarism; acknowledgement

**Grade 11**
- *Find and access:* task definition in different contexts; questions and questioning (main question, other questions, quality control of questions); identifying appropriate sources; information and data gathering (add questions to the questionnaire, one other source); **the role of AI, including its ethical use**; quality control of information
- *Process:* extracting appropriate information; appropriate tools and techniques to process data and organise and manipulate information; processing data; adding data questions; data handling with the spreadsheet and the database; analysis of data and information - trends and patterns
- *Present:* appropriate software tools to communicate findings; report; interpreted knowledge and understanding; cohesive and logical organisation and flow of content and recommendations/conclusions; communication using supporting texts and graphics; copyright and plagiarism issues; appropriate referencing

**Grade 12**
- *Find and access:* reformulation of the problem/task into a complete statement; questions and questioning; new questions and discarding irrelevant ones as the investigation develops; identifying appropriate sources; gathering data (set a questionnaire, **two** other sources); advanced searching techniques; quality control of information - information evaluation and website evaluation; manage volumes of information
- *Process:* using a variety of information; using the most appropriate information and data; effective manipulation of information (rework and/or combine); processing data correctly into useful information using a variety of tools and techniques; use the correct software; formulating data questions; data handling with the spreadsheet and the database; meaningful analysis - trends and patterns
- *Present:* the best software tools used in an integrated fashion; comprehensive report; interpreted knowledge and new understanding; logical grouping and flow supported by text and graphics; cohesive and logical recommendations/conclusions; complete referencing; quality control of the report - a clear link between the original task, the discussion and the conclusion

### 2.6 Social Implications (§3.5)

**Most of Social Implications should be dealt with integrated into the other
topics; its scheduled time may be added to those topics.**

**Grade 10**
- *Impact on society:* ICTs in everyday life; ICT influence on life and lifestyles; economic reasons for using computing devices; communication etiquette; safe Internet and e-mail use
- *Legal, ethical and security:* ethical use of computing devices; basic security concepts; software piracy; intellectual property; licensing; e-mail threats, issues and remedies; computer criminals
- *Health and ergonomics:* ergonomics to promote health and well-being
- *Environmental:* green computing issues; environmental issues relating to the use of ICTs

**Grade 11**
- *Impact on society:* enhancing accessibility; computer and human error; impact of ICTs in the workplace and employment practices; social engineering tricks; online safety and protection issues; protecting oneself when online
- *Legal, ethical and security:* hardware theft and vandalism; ethical use of networks; unauthorised access; network safety and security; privacy issues; information accuracy; data protection; computing device misuse; currency of protective software; verification and validation; authorising permission; **AI ethical use and academic integrity**
- *Health and ergonomics:* factors that influence health; health risks
- *Environmental:* power settings and savings; protection against power failures

**Grade 12**
- *Impact on society:* distributed computing power; impact and use of social networking sites and technologies; information overload; different scenarios and case studies; making recommendations; **impact of technology on the global community**
- *Legal, ethical and security:* computer criminals and crimes; fraud scams; Internet attacks; misuse of personal information; malware and safeguards; different scenarios and case studies; making recommendations; **impact of AI**
- *Health and ergonomics:* user-centred design in software; usability and functionality issues; interpret adverts; interpret scenarios; making recommendations; buying decisions
- *Environmental:* interpret scenarios; make recommendations; new issues

## 3. Suggested teaching plan (2024 Section 3.6-3.7)

The plan gives the **minimum content per term**. The sequence within a term
is not prescribed; teachers write their own work schedules. Sub-topics are
not stand-alone - teach them integrated, because the content overlaps.
Revise the specific technologies regularly to phase out old ones and add new,
and be aware of variations (USB, micro USB, USB-C): pupils need to understand
what a variation represents and how it is applied in an ICT context. Term
lengths vary from year to year, so adapt the plan annually.

### Grade 10

#### Grade 10, Term 1

**Systems Technologies: Introduction to Concepts of Computing** (theory,
~1/2 week / 2 h)
- What a computer is: a general model in relation to the information processing cycle - input, processing, output, storage, communication
- Types of computing devices (definition and examples only): multi-purpose - desktop (including all-in-one), laptop, tablet, server (including convertible/detachable, 2-in-1); **the concept of convergence**; dedicated devices - ATMs, cameras, printers, fitness trackers, factory robots, electronic appliances (embedded computers)
- Main components of a computer system: hardware and software; devices - input, output, storage, communication
- ICTs in everyday life: define ICT; a general model of an ICT system (convey, manipulate, store data); an example in a familiar context (a point-of-sale system, a cell phone system)
- Data and information: the difference and the importance of each; examples of their use in a familiar organisation such as the school

**Systems Technologies: Device Management** (practical, 2 weeks / 8 h)
- Start-up: switch on, log on (the concept of access control)
- The desktop (GUI): first looks, icons and shortcuts; Start button, task bar, My Documents, Recycle Bin, file manager (Windows Explorer); accessing apps
- Introduction to and layout of the keyboard; enhancing keyboarding skills
- Basic file operations: open, save, close, basic printing
- *Notes:* keyboarding drills - correct fingers on the correct keys; correct posture introduces ergonomics and health

**Systems Technologies: Device Management** (theory and practical, ~1/2 week / 2 h)
- Describe file organisation; drives, folders and files
- Types of files and extensions (association): archived/compressed, forms of text files, web pages; word processor, spreadsheet, database, presentation; graphics, movie, sound, animation; PDF
- File specification: drive, path, filename, extension
- File naming, conventions and properties - type and size
- File manager (This PC), hierarchy
- Organise, copy, rename, delete, restore, move, search, view and sort files and folders

**Systems Technologies: Hardware** (theory, ~1/2 week / 2 h)
- What hardware is; identify hardware components; identify ports and connectors (USB, HDMI); extend ports with a convertor/adapter
- Input: what it is; types - data (unprocessed text, numbers, images, video, audio) and instructions (programs, commands, user response); what an input device is; keyboard and mouse (cable, wireless)
- Output: what it is; types - text, graphics, audio, video; hard copy vs soft copy; monitors (size, quality) and printers (ink, laser, 3D: purpose)
- Storage: what storage, a storage device and storage media are; fixed (HDD, SSD) vs portable (external HDD/SSD, flash drives, memory cards); measuring capacity (KB, MB, GB, TB); robustness, volatility, speed, physical size
- Definition of peripheral devices; methods for connecting them (cabled and wireless)

**Systems Technologies: Software** (theory, ~1/2 week / 2 h)
- What software is; the concept of a GUI; typical components of a basic GUI for desktops/laptops, tablets and smartphones; minimising, restoring, resizing, moving and closing a window; system vs application software
- Application software (apps): what it is; Office suites; basic applications and features (calculator, clock, calendar, snipping tool/cropping/screenshot)
- System software: what it is; the operating system - basic function and purpose, typical GUI features; examples on computing devices (Windows, MacOS, Linux, Android, iOS)
- Basic security: the concept of authentication - username and verification (password, pattern, pin, face recognition)

**Social Implications** (theory, ~1/2 week / 2 h)
- Issues linked to this term's content: ergonomics, green computing (recycling, e-waste), health (posture), authentication
- Economic reasons for using computers: saving paper, labour, communication costs, efficiency, accuracy, reliability
- *Note:* teach social implications as they relate to hardware, software and applications

**Solution Development: Word Processing** (practical and theory, ~5 1/2 weeks / 22 h)
- First looks: ribbons, tabs, menus; structure/elements of a document - pages, paragraphs, lines/texts, objects
- File management: create, open, close, save, save as, print
- Select data with keyboard and/or mouse; enter, edit and delete text; special characters (symbols)
- Basic punctuation: one space after all punctuation, including full stops; formatting marks
- Formatting: font type, style, size, colour, highlight, effects; paragraph spacing (paragraph and lines within a paragraph), alignment, borders, shading, simple indents (increase and decrease); existing quick styles
- Editing: cut, copy, paste, find and replace; reviewing: proofing, spelling and grammar
- Autocorrect and basic typography: quotes, dashes
- Page layout: page setup, margins, orientation, size, page border; document layout: page numbers, page breaks; view options - print layout and preview
- Insert and manipulate illustrations and text: pictures, shapes, WordArt, basic SmartArt, screenshot/snipping, text box
- *Notes:* allocate time for keyboarding drills; GIGO; teach **all** font styles and effects in the Font dialog; illustration objects can include icons and 3D models; Shift for one capital, Caps Lock for consecutive capitals

**Assessment (PoA):** 1 theory test, before the end of Term 1 - on content
covered per the teaching plan, with **theory questions based on practical
content included**.

#### Grade 10, Term 2

**Systems Technologies: Hardware** (theory, ~1/2 week / 2 h) - extend
- Input: pointing devices (touch pad, touch screen, stylus, joystick); scanning and reading devices (scanning as part of a multifunction printer, handheld, smartphone camera; RFID, magnetic strip, bar code, QR code; OCR); video input - webcam; audio input - microphone including voice recognition; biometric input - fingerprint, iris, facial scanner
- Output: audio output (headsets, speakers); multifunction devices, data projector, visualizer
- Storage media and devices: memory cards (size matched to device, transfer speeds - basic); card readers (built-in, external multi-card)
- Processing: motherboard, CPU, memory (RAM, ROM) - what it is and what it is used for; measuring speed in GHz

**Systems Technologies: Software** (theory, ~1/2 week / 2 h) - extend
- Categories of software: proprietary and open-source (definition, advantages, disadvantages)
- Licensing and licensing agreements: end-user, site licence, Creative Commons
- System software: drivers - what is a driver?; auto configuration of devices; utility programs - what, purpose, generic examples

**Systems Technologies: Device Management** (practical and theory, ~1/2 week / 2 h)
- Creating shortcuts; adding new peripherals (printer, mouse); changing the default printer; basic printing and printer queue management; compressing/decompressing files and folders

**Network Technologies: Networks** (theory, ~1/2 week / 2 h)
- What a network is; aims and objectives - facilitating communication and sharing hardware, software, data and information; basic security and privacy issues; examples - Internet, PAN, HAN, LAN

**Social Implications** (theory, ~1/2 week / 2 h)
- Ethical use of the content covered (hardware, software, device management, networks); software piracy, licensing, copyright and intellectual property

**Solution Development: Word Processing** (practical and theory, ~3 weeks / 12 h)
- Paragraphs: bullets (pictures, symbols, font size and colour) and numbering (font size and colour); indents (first line, hanging); tabs (position, alignment, leader)
- Document and page layout: customising margins; headers and footers (simple edit and remove, automatic page numbers, alignment, own text); insert cover page
- Tables: insert, table tools, table design, table properties; design - table styles, borders, shading; layout - rows and columns, header rows; cells - size, distribution, merging, splitting; text alignment and direction; split, autofit, gridlines; working with data - sorting, convert to text; **working with formulae (sum and average)**
- View options: more than one document/window, zoom; draft and full-screen reading views
- *Note:* teach the mechanical skills inside the paradigm of **computational thinking**, with the underlying knowledge, so skills transfer to new versions and similar applications

**Solution Development: Spreadsheets** (practical and theory, ~2 weeks / 8 h)
- Overview of basic skills and core concepts; uses of a spreadsheet
- First looks: rows, columns, cells, sheets, workbook
- **Cell reference - the importance of using cell references rather than constant values** in cells and formulae; cell ranges and range names
- Basic calculations with +, -, *, /, order of precedence, brackets
- Data types: General, Number, Currency, Text, Date, Time; values and cell references
- Format cells: data type, borders, shading, alignment, wrapping, merge, text direction, split, Autofill
- Formatting rows, columns and sheets: size, insert, delete, hide, unhide, borders, styles
- Reinforce common concepts from the word processor: formatting and editing, page layout, illustrations, find and select, proofing
- File options: open, save, save as, new, print
- Formulae vs functions; basic functions - SUM, AVERAGE, COUNT, MIN, MAX
- Error indicators: `#######`, `#NAME!`, `#DIV/0!`, `#REF!`, `#VALUE!`, `#NUM!`

**Information Management** (practical and theory, ~1/2 week / 2 h)
- Data vs information; understand the problem/task - problem-solving steps; the role of questions and questioning in determining information needs
- Information sources and data-gathering tools with their advantages and disadvantages: electronic reference works (Wikipedia, Internet articles), printed media (books), surveys (questionnaires/interviews)
- *Notes:* introduction and management of the PAT. Understand the problem - state it in your own words; what must be done or found; what is known; what information is missing or needed; find information and data - where and how?

**Assessment (PoA):** a practical test during the term; mid-year examination
(1 practical + 1 theory paper).

*Note (CAPS):* application packages share common features (formatting,
editing, page layout, illustrations) which take longer with the first
application taught and less with each later one.

#### Grade 10, Term 3

**Network Technologies: Networks** (theory, ~1/2 week / 2 h)
- PAN and HAN: what they are, what they are used for, PAN vs HAN
- Network devices: modem, switch, router

- Impact and use of social networking sites and technologies: social media platforms; cyber profile/digital footprint; **crowdfunding**; fake news, misinformation and disinformation
- Legal and ethical issues of social media use: confidentiality/privacy; copyright, plagiarism and intellectual property; identity theft; misrepresentation/fake news; cybercrime and cyber-hacking; online harassment - defamation and cyberbullying
- Remote access (including VPN) creating opportunity for e-commuting, e-working, e-learning
- How technology can benefit or harm society; information overload

**Solution Development**
- *Spreadsheets* (~1 week / 4 h): reinforce Grades 10-11; identify the appropriate functions for a scenario and solve problems; more advanced combinations of functions and formulas; edit, format and change charts - working with the axes, minimum and maximum values, re-labelling axes, **stacked bar and column graphs using a graphic**, trendline; appropriate graphs for a given scenario
- *Word processing* (~1/2 week / 2 h): reinforce; documents using styles; page layout with advanced techniques; integration with other software including linking objects
- *Database* (~1/2 week / 2 h): reinforce; create a database for a given scenario; integration with other software
- *HTML/web design* (~1/2 week / 2 h): reinforce; applying good website/page design

**Information Management** (practical, ~2 weeks / 8 h) - PAT: reinforce
Information Management skills; use the information and data gathered to
present the solution

**Assessment (PoA):** prelim examination (1 practical + 1 theory paper). **PAT
Phase 3** completed before the prelim examination.

#### Grade 12, Term 4

**Consolidation of documents** (word processor, spreadsheet, database, web
page; practical and theory, ~1 1/2 weeks / 6 h) - as Grade 11 Term 4's
Documents section, across Grades 10-12 content.

**Consolidation of content using case studies - all topics** (practical and

knowing about **upgrading and integrating equipment with new products and

## 4. Assessment (2024 Section 4)

### 4.1 Informal and formal assessment

- **Informal (daily) assessment** - for learning: observation, discussion,
  practical demonstrations, learner-teacher conferences, informal classroom
  interaction, self- and peer-assessment. Not recorded, not used for
  promotion.
- **Formal assessment** - of learning: tests, examinations, practical tasks,
  alternative tasks; marked, recorded and moderated. **Assessment in CAT
  should encourage computational thinking practices** that integrate human
  thinking with the capabilities of ICTs and application packages.

**Formal assessment structure:**

| | During the year | PAT | End-of-year exam |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grades 10-11** | **SBA 40%**: 4 tests (one of them an alternative form of assessment, open-book test, case study or survey) + 1 mid-year exam | 20% | 40%: practical 20% + written 20%, 2.5-3 hours each |
| **Grade 12** | **SBA 25%**: 3 tests (one an alternative form of assessment, open-book test, case study or survey) + 2 exams (mid-year and trial) | 25% | 50%: practical 25% + written 25%, 3 hours each |

The practical exam covers Solution Development; the written exam covers
"theory aspects of all content, concepts and skills of all topics".

**Types of formal assessment:**

- **Project** - in CAT the project **is** the PAT: an information management
  project based on providing a solution to a specific scenario using the
  applications listed under Solution Development. It must make the learner
  plan, prepare, investigate, research or gather data; carry out the task to
  given criteria; produce a product such as a report with introduction, main
  body, conclusion and recommendations (possibly built from smaller products
  such as a planning document); demonstrate thinking and decision-making; and
  show some innovation and creativity. The teacher sets clear criteria, keeps
  the scope manageable, provides the resources, sets the time frame, compiles
  an assessment tool and monitors continuously.
- **Tests** - practical or written, with the programme balanced between the
  two. Not a series of small tests: substantial content, **45 to 60 minutes**.
  Open-book tests make learners find information and apply knowledge, not
  rewrite text: written ones want longer reflective, paragraph-type answers
  to a scenario, with reasons and supporting evidence; practical ones want a
  combination of procedures and techniques applied to new situations -
  integrated practical tasks that encourage computational thinking. Every
  test, open-book test and exam must reflect different cognitive levels.
  **For practical tests, information sheets with HTML tags and input mask
  characters are provided** for the web development and database questions.
- **Alternative assessment** - a **survey** or a **case study**, either of
  them optional in place of a test.
  - *Survey:* assesses Information Management and doubles as preparation for
    that part of the PAT. **Section 1** - create a questionnaire to gather
    responses from a given number of respondents for a given scenario, with
    the findings to be presented to a stated audience; done under controlled
    conditions and handed in at the end for marking. **Section 2** - present
    the questionnaire to respondents, process the responses in a spreadsheet
    as instructed, and submit the findings with the completed questionnaires
    by a stated date. The survey may also be an integrated task (practical
    and theory), with some questions assessing Information Management theory.
  - *Case study:* an in-depth, detailed investigation of a real-life
    situation and its contextual conditions, with data gathered from a
    variety of sources by several methods. **45 to 60 minutes.**

### 4.2 The PAT (20% in Grades 10-11, 25% in Grade 12)

The PAT assesses procedural skills and the learner's individual interaction
with data and information - how they process, manipulate and present it. The
information is finally presented in a number of documents, **in the four
application programs studied**. It covers:

- identifying, finding and accessing information/data;
- using, manipulating and processing the information and data gathered;
- presentation of solutions, findings and recommendations.

**Evidence of the process must be provided in a series of draft documents**,
and each task includes a declaration of authenticity. It runs in **three
phases** (the term plans place them: Grade 10 Phase 1 by the end of Term 3
and Phase 2 before the final exams; Grade 11 Phase 1 by the end of Term 2,
Phase 2 in Term 3, Phase 3 before the final exams; Grade 12 Phase 1 by the
end of Term 1, Phase 2 by the end of Term 2, Phase 3 before the prelim).

### 4.3 Paper 1 - practical (3 hours, 25%)

- A practically oriented paper on **Solution Development**: the application
  packages studied - word processing, spreadsheets and databases - **as well
  as creating a simple web page using HTML**. **Presentations are assessed
  only in the PAT, not in the examinations.**
- Skills are assessed **in an integrated manner based on contexts**. Problem
  solving and aspects of **file management** form part of the application
  questions.
- Each learner must have their own computer in the exam room; provision for
  **two sittings**.
- **Information sheets with HTML tags and input mask characters are provided
  with the paper.**
- **No large amounts of data entry** - data is retrieved from the data disk or
  imported from a text file, word processing document, database table or
  spreadsheet.

| Content area | Grades 11-12 | Grade 10 |
|---|---|---|
| Word processing | ~45 marks | ~40% |
| Spreadsheets | ~40 marks | ~30% |
| Databases | ~35 marks | - |
| Presentations | - | ~15% |
| Web development | ~15 marks | - |
| General (integration and applying techniques, knowledge and procedural skills to new situations) | ~15 marks | ~15% |

### 4.4 Paper 2 - written (3 hours, 25%)

All topics, including the **theory aspects of Solution Development** (the
application packages and file management). A section assesses understanding
of the technologies studied so as to make informed decisions in authentic
contexts, from choices of technology to its responsible use.

Grades 11-12 structure:

| Section | Question | Description | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 1-3 | Short questions on all topics: multiple choice, modified true/false, matching columns | ~25 |
| **B** | 4 | Systems Technologies | ~25 |
| | 5 | Internet and Network Technologies (Internet and WWW, e-communication, network technologies) | ~15 |
| | 6 | Information Management | ~10 |
| | 7 | Social Implications (impact of ICTs on society and health; social, legal, ethical, security and environmental issues) | ~10 |
| | 8 | Solution Development (the knowledge and understanding that supports the practical application of skills) | ~15 |
| **C** | 9 | Integrated scenario - one scenario integrating all topics; informed decision-making about technology selection and responsible implementation, **including 3-5 marks of solution development** where the scenario calls for it | ~25 |
| | 10 | Integrated scenario - the same again | ~25 |

The mark allocation per question differs for the Grade 10 paper, whose total
may differ.

**Note (CAPS):** the 50:50 assessment split between P1 and P2 (150 marks
each) is not the same thing as the **60:40 split of teaching time** - the
practical content needs more teaching and practice, but assessment gives the
two papers equal weight, because P2 also assesses the knowledge and
understanding behind the practical skills.

**Content to be covered:** because the content progresses conceptually across
the grades, **Grade 10-12 content and skills are assessed in the external
papers at the end of Grade 12**. New and emerging technologies are
accommodated as the curriculum is reviewed.

### 4.7 Cognitive demand and levels of difficulty

| Paper | Lower order (C1) | Middle order (C2) | Higher order (C3) |
|---|---|---|---|

| Level | Taxonomy | Description |
|---|---|---|
| L1/C1 | Knowledge, remembering | Recall of factual or process knowledge in isolation - one step, set of basic steps, instruction or process at a time; definitions in theory, single procedures in the application packages |
| L2/C2 | Understanding, applying | Combining isolatable bits, or showing understanding of steps and processes - translating from one form of representation to another (pictures, symbols, diagrams, screenshots, words or mathematical equations into, say, spreadsheet formulas); may include reproducing aspects of documents.
