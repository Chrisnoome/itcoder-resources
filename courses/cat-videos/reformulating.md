## cat12-22.1 Reformulating the problem (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#newQuestions` - the comment after `r22Log`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say when a question needs reformulating, rewrite a problem as a complete statement, and decide which sub-questions to keep, add or discard.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAT`, title "Your question was *wrong*. Good."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:50).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo's PAT question: "How can people save water?" Three weeks, 120 answers - and they are all about the school's toilets.
   > Board: a speech bubble with the question; a pile of answer slips with "toilets!" sticking out; Clicky scratching its head.
2. **Why first questions are guesses (0:50-1:40).** You write it before you know much. "Research" means to search again.
   > Board: the thinker doodle; highlighter on "search again".
3. **Five warning signs (1:40-3:00).** Too broad, too narrow, answers point elsewhere, data you cannot get, yes-or-no.
   > Board: five road signs, one example under each.
4. **A complete statement (3:00-5:00).** What, who and where, why it matters, what you will find out, the limits (scope). Thabo's steps 1-5 and his problem statement read slowly; then point to each of the five parts in it.
   > Board: the statement written out; each part highlighted in turn with a label in the margin.
5. **Keep, add, discard (5:00-6:20).** Thabo's six sub-questions sorted. Irrelevant = its answer would not change the conclusion. The question log.
   > Board: three bins labelled Keep, Add, Discard; question cards dropped into them.
6. **The warning (6:20-6:50).** Reformulating sharpens; it does not swap the topic. Check it against the task.
   > Board: the task sheet with "Every drop counts" highlighted.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-22.2 Taming the flood - folders, a source log and versions (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#versions` - the comment after `s22Habits`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** set up a PAT folder structure, keep a source log, sift many sources in two rounds, and keep track of versions.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAT`, title "PAT final *final* FINAL"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Four files: PAT final, PAT final 2, PAT NEW, PAT use this. Which one do you hand in?
   > Board: the final-final doodle; Clicky hovering over four identical icons.
2. **Information overload (0:40-1:10).** 47 bookmarks, 23 PDFs, 61 photos. Two habits prevent it.
   > Board: a paper avalanche; highlighter on "information overload".
3. **The folder structure (1:10-2:40).** One main folder, sub-folders by phase, numbers in front, plain names, not too deep. Set it up on day one.
   > Screen: in File Explorer in the VM, create `PAT 2026 - Thabo Dlamini` and its numbered sub-folders, as in the lesson.
4. **The source log (2:40-4:10).** One row per source: number, details, date accessed, the question it answers, quality. Its three jobs. The source number in every file name.
   > Board: the three-row log drawn as a spreadsheet; arrows from S02 to a PDF named "S02 Planned water cuts.pdf".
5. **Sifting in two rounds (4:10-5:40).** Skim: who and when, headings and summary, Ctrl+F, decide. Then read properly; notes marked with source and question, grouped by question. Big data: sort, filter, count.
   > Board: a sieve; sources dropping through or caught. Highlighter on "Ctrl+F".
6. **Version control (5:40-7:20).** Numbered or dated versions, never "final"; keep the drafts (evidence); Version History in File, Info; a backup somewhere else.
   > Screen: Word with a document saved in OneDrive - File, Info, Version History, open an earlier version, Restore.
7. **Sign-off.**
