# CAT 12 lesson 3: Recommending a system - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory12/recommending.php`. Two videos: the four kinds of
user with their recommendation cards, and recommending for a user with a
disability, with how to write the answer. Board in the CAT marker style with
Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow highlighter on any words on screen
being talked about. One short screen recording in the video VM (Windows 11
accessibility settings). Everything below is in the lesson text.

## cat12-03.1 Five users, five computers (about 9 min)

**Goes:** after section `#power` - the comment after `w3Kamo`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** name the five kinds of user, say what matters most to each, and fill in a recommendation card (device, CPU, RAM, storage, GPU, input, output, communication, extras) with a reason on every row.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "One laptop for *everyone*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Saturday morning, a computer shop in Centurion. Gogo, Mr Botha, Mr Mokoena, Kamo and Ayanda walk in. The salesman shows every one of them the same R15 000 laptop. For four of them, it is wrong.
   > Board: five figures in a row at a shop counter; one laptop with a price tag; Clicky looking from face to face.
2. **Five kinds of user (0:40-1:50).** Home, SOHO (Small Office / Home Office), mobile, power, and a user with a disability - typical work and what matters most. One person can be two kinds; the kind is where you start, not the answer.
   > Board: the five-row table; highlighter on "Small Office / Home Office".
3. **The recommendation card (1:50-2:30).** Device; CPU, RAM, storage, GPU; input; output; communication; extras. Every row needs a reason.
   > Board: an empty card drawn with its rows, Clicky holding a pen.
4. **The home user (2:30-3:40).** The Dlamini family: easy, affordable, shared (an account each), safe. Their card, row by row - and what they do not need (a graphics card).
   > Board: the card filled in for the family; a graphics card crossed out in red.
5. **The SOHO user (3:40-5:10).** Botha's Bakery: reliability and a good warranty, daily backups to an external drive and the cloud, security for customers' details, a UPS, a laser multifunction printer for 200 invoices a week. Why a desktop.
   > Board: the device-desktop doodle; the bakery's card; highlighter on "backup" and "UPS".
6. **The mobile user (5:10-6:50).** Mr Mokoena in the Karoo: light, the battery's Wh, LTE or a hotspot, an SSD for dirt roads, protection if stolen. What small costs: more computing power uses more battery and makes heat. A gaming laptop lasts two hours.
   > Board: the choose-watts drawing; the choose-thumbs doodle; the card.
7. **The power user (6:50-8:30).** Kamo's wedding videos: many cores, 32 to 64 GB, a graphics card with plenty of VRAM, a fast SSD plus big hard drives, two colour-accurate screens, a UPS. Why usually a desktop: power per rand, cooling, upgrades.
   > Board: the gpu-gamer doodle; Kamo's card; a three-hour export bar cut off by a power cut.
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-03.2 Recommending for a user with a disability (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#recommend` - the comment after `w3Deaf`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** recommend hardware and software for a person with a disability, step by step, and write any recommendation in the shape the exam asks for.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Two hardware, two *software*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Ayanda is blind. She is in Grade 11 and does her schoolwork in Word and on the Internet. What does her computer need - and what does it NOT need?
   > Board: Ayanda at a desk with a laptop and headphones; Clicky with a question mark.
2. **Step by step (0:30-1:40).** Start with what the person can do; find input and output that work; add software and settings (often built in, free); then an ordinary computer with enough RAM; let them try it. Assistive technology.
   > Board: five numbered steps; highlighter on "CAN do".
3. **The usual recommendations (1:40-3:40).** The Learn/Memorise table row by row: blind; poor eyesight; deaf or hard of hearing; little use of the hands or arms; dyslexia - hardware on the left, software on the right.
   > Board: the table built row by row; the hey-assistant doodle by the voice recognition line.
   > Screen: Settings, Accessibility - point at Narrator, Magnifier, Contrast themes and Captions in the list, without opening them. Then Windows key + Ctrl + L to show Live captions starting.
4. **Ayanda's card (3:40-4:50).** Laptop with a full-size keyboard; mid-range, 16 GB for the screen reader; SSD and cloud; braille display and headphones; a screen reader (NVDA is free) and an OCR app. What she does not need. The tablet as it comes from the shop: nothing to feel, nothing read aloud.
   > Board: Ayanda's card; a braille display drawn with raised dots; highlighter on "does NOT need".
5. **The four shapes of question (4:50-6:00).** Recommend and motivate; suggest a device for each row; TWO hardware and TWO software; Option A or B and name the device. The marks are for the reasons.
   > Board: four question cards, each with its answer frame.
6. **The formula and the mistakes (6:00-7:30).** Recommendation + specification + "so that" + what it does for this user - the three examples. Mistakes: a brand, the most powerful, a stock word, the wrong disability, describing instead of recommending.
   > Board: the formula in marker with "so that" highlighted; five mistakes on sticky notes, each crossed out.
7. **Sign-off.**
