# CAT 12 lesson 10: Internet services - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory12/netservices.php`. Two videos: streaming against
downloading, and cloud, grid and distributed computing told apart. Board in
the CAT marker style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow highlighter
on any words on screen being talked about. Everything below is in the lesson
text.

## cat12-10.1 Streaming or downloading? (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#streaming` - the comment after `rSvcGogoTaxi`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain the difference between streaming and downloading, say what buffering is, and choose one for a situation with a reason.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · INTERNET`, title "Stream it or *save* it?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo is streaming the Springboks on his phone at the bakery. 97% loaded - and it has been 97% since kick-off. Gogo, meanwhile, wants to watch a church service on a taxi ride with no signal. Same video, two different answers.
   > Board: the stream-buffering doodle; Clicky tapping its foot. Yellow highlighter on "97%".
2. **Downloading (0:40-1:50).** The whole file comes to your storage first. You wait; then you play it as often as you like, offline, with no more data. It needs space.
   > Board: a file drawn as a box sliding whole into a phone; highlighter on "whole file".
3. **Streaming (1:50-3:00).** Small pieces, played as they arrive and thrown away. Starts at once, needs almost no storage - but needs a connection the whole time, and watching again uses the data again. YouTube, Netflix, Showmax, Spotify, a live match.
   > Board: the stream-vs-download figure, built in two halves; highlighter on "plays as it arrives".
4. **Buffering (3:00-3:50).** The player keeps the next few seconds ready; when the connection is too slow, that store runs empty and the spinning circle appears - or the picture goes blurry instead.
   > Board: a small bucket filling and emptying under the video; highlighter on "buffering".
5. **The data it costs (3:50-4:40).** Music 50 to 150 MB an hour; low-quality video 300 MB to 1 GB; HD 3 GB; 4K 7 GB or more. Stream what you watch once; download on Wi-Fi what you watch again or where there is no signal.
   > Board: the data table drawn as four bars; highlighter on "HD 3 GB".
6. **Gogo's answer (4:40-5:30).** Download it at home on the Wi-Fi: the taxi route has no signal, and the home Wi-Fi uses none of her mobile data.
   > Board: Gogo's phone with a download arrow under a Wi-Fi sign, then a taxi with "no signal" crossed out.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-10.2 Cloud, grid and distributed computing (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#distributed` - the comment after `wSvcDistributedWorld`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** describe cloud computing and its three forms, tell grid computing from cloud computing, and explain distributed computing power and what it does for the world.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · INTERNET`, title "Cloud, grid or *a million* computers?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. In 2020 about a million volunteers lent their home computers to one project, and together they beat every supercomputer in the world. How?
   > Board: the ants-big-job doodle; Clicky carrying one crumb.
2. **Cloud computing (0:40-2:20).** Storage, software and processing power on a provider's servers, over the Internet, paid for as a service - like electricity from the wall. Three forms: cloud storage (OneDrive, Google Photos), cloud applications (Google Docs, Canva), processing power (an online shop renting servers for Black Friday).
   > Board: the cloud-what-it-is figure; a plug and a wall socket beside it; highlighter on "as a service".
3. **Cloud for a small business (2:20-3:10).** Mr Botha's old laptop: nothing to buy, anywhere, backups done for him - but a monthly fee, trust, outages, and no Internet means no work.
   > Board: a two-column list, advantages and disadvantages; highlighter on "No Internet, no work".
4. **Grid computing - CAPS (3:10-4:20).** Many organisations join their own computers over a network to work like one powerful computer: the five steps. Grid or cloud: share your own, or rent a provider's.
   > Board: five universities drawn as buildings joined by lines; the grid-or-cloud table; highlighter on "share" and "rent".
5. **Distributed computing power (4:20-6:00).** One huge job, split into pieces, sent to many computers, worked on at the same time, sent back, put together. Volunteers and organisations. Medicine, the SKA and MeerKAT, cheaper research, anyone can help; the costs - electricity, checking strangers' results.
   > Board: the bigp-distributed figure built step by step; highlighter on each step word.
6. **The exam trap (6:00-6:40).** Many computers working on one huge problem is distributed (or grid) computing - not cloud computing.
   > Board: the Note callout written large; highlighter on "not".
7. **Sign-off.**
