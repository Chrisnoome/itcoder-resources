# CAT 10 lesson 4: ICT systems around you - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory10/ictsystems.php`. Three videos, one per system.
Board in the CAT marker style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow
highlighter on any words on screen being talked about. No screen recordings
needed - the systems are drawn. Each step names its part of the information
processing cycle with a small label (I, P, O, S, C) as the lesson does.
Everything below is in the lesson text.

## cat10-04.1 Inside the till (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#till` - the comment after `m4TillCycle`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** define ICT and an ICT system, and walk a POS system through stock in, the till, a card payment and the reports, naming the part of the cycle at each step.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · ICT SYSTEMS`, title "Four seconds at the *till*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:45).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo buys a pie and a cooldrink. Beep, beep, he taps his card, APPROVED, a slip curls out. Four seconds. In those four seconds the till looked up two prices, took two items off the stock count, asked his bank for permission, and saved the sale for tonight's report.
   > Board: a till and a card machine; a big "4 seconds" stopwatch; Clicky riding the slip as it curls out.
2. **ICT and ICT systems (0:45-1:50).** ICT is Information and Communication Technology - all the technology we use to collect, store, process and communicate data. An ICT system is people, processes, forms and ICT equipment working together. At Botha's Bakery: Mr Botha and his staff; the slips and the daily report; cashing up at 18:00; the till, the scanner, the card machine and the till program.
   > Board: the ict-sum drawing; each of the four parts gets a Botha's Bakery example on a label.
3. **POS (1:50-2:20).** The till is part of a POS system - point of sale, the point where the sale happens. It sells, takes payment, keeps track of stock and reports.
   > Board: "POS" opened out into its three words; yellow highlighter on "point of sale".
4. **The stock arrives (2:20-3:20).** Each box is scanned at the back door; the scanner reads the barcode. The system finds the product's record or makes a new one, adds the items to the stock count, and keeps the selling price - VAT already in it - with the barcode.
   > Board: the pos-stock-in drawing; the stock count ticking up to 48.
5. **At the till (3:20-4:50).** Scan the pie - the scanner reads only the number (input). Look it up in the database: name and price (processing). Add the price to the total and show it (processing, then output). One pie off the stock count (storage). Every item scanned: the total (output).
   > Board: the barcode-lookup drawing; each step numbered with its I/P/O/S label in a highlighter swipe.
6. **Paying by card (4:50-6:10).** Tap or insert the card and type the PIN (input). The card machine sends the card details and amount to the bank (communication). The bank checks there is enough money (processing - at the bank). "Approved" comes back (communication). The money moves, and the slip prints (output).
   > Board: the pos-till-ipo drawing; a line from the card machine across to a bank, and back. Clicky running along the line.
7. **Reports (6:10-7:20).** At the end of the day: how many of each item sold, money in, profit, VAT owed to SARS, and what is running low. In a chain, every till sends its sales to head office, often the moment each sale happens.
   > Board: the pos-report-phone drawing; "bread (6 left)" gets the yellow highlighter.
8. **CAPS's three words (7:20-7:50).** Every ICT system conveys data (the card machine to the bank), manipulates it (adding up the total) and stores it (the sale saved for tonight).
   > Board: three words on a sticky note, an arrow from each to its part of the till.
9. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-04.2 The ATM, step by step (about 5 min)

**Goes:** after section `#atm` - the comment after `mw4AtmInputs`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** walk through drawing cash at an ATM, name the input, processing, output, storage and communication, and say which computer decides.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · ICT SYSTEMS`, title "Who says *no* at the ATM?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. It is the first of the month, and Gogo Dlamini is at the ATM in the wall at the mall.
   > Board: the atm-g1 picture (or the atm drawing) with a short queue; Gogo at the front.
2. **What an ATM is (0:30-1:10).** ATM: automated teller machine. A teller is the bank worker behind the counter. An ATM is a dedicated device, joined to the bank's computers by a network - for cash, balances and deposits at any hour.
   > Board: "ATM" opened out; yellow highlighter on "teller"; a line from the ATM to a bank building.
3. **Nine steps (1:10-3:40).** Card in or tapped (input). PIN (input). Withdraw, R500 (input). Card details, PIN and amount sent to the bank (communication). The bank checks the PIN and the balance, and decides (processing - at the bank). Approved comes back (communication). Card back first, so nobody leaves it behind (output). Five R100 notes (output). A slip, and R500 plus any fee comes off her balance at the bank (output and storage).
   > Board: the nine steps on a numbered list beside the ATM; each step's I/P/O/S/C label in a highlighter swipe. Clicky follows the data along the line to the bank and back.
4. **Who decides? (3:40-4:30).** The balance is stored at the bank, not in the ATM. The ATM collects the input, sends it on, and gives out what the bank allows. "Insufficient funds" was decided by the bank's computer - and that is why an ATM stops working when its connection to the bank goes down.
   > Board: the screen showing "Insufficient funds"; an arrow from the bank, not the ATM, with Clicky pointing at the bank: "it was them!"
5. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-04.3 How a voice note gets there (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#cellphone` - the comment after `dw4Billing`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** name the parts of the cell phone system, follow a message from one phone to another, and explain prepaid and contract billing.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · ICT SYSTEMS`, title "Gogo *sends* a voice note"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Your phone is only half of the system. The other half is the cell phone network - and you never see most of it.
   > Board: a phone on the left half of the board; the right half covered by a curtain that Clicky pulls open.
2. **The parts (0:30-2:20).** The phone sends and receives by radio. The SIM card tells the network who you are - your number and your account. The cell tower talks by radio to every phone in the area around it; that area is a cell, which is where the name cell phone comes from. The network's computers find the other phone, connect the call, pass data to the Internet, and record every call and MB. The billing system works out the cost.
   > Board: each part drawn in turn; the cell drawn as a circle round the tower, with "cell" in yellow highlighter.
3. **Follow the voice note (2:20-4:00).** Gogo records it and presses Send (input). Her phone sends it by radio to the nearest tower (communication). Along cables to the network's computers (communication). Over the Internet to WhatsApp's servers, which find Lerato's phone and send it on through her network (processing and communication). Lerato's nearest tower, then her phone plays it (output). Gogo's network takes the data off her bundle (processing and storage).
   > Board: a map with Gogo's phone, her tower, the network, a cloud for the Internet and WhatsApp's servers, Lerato's tower and phone; a sound wave travels along, each hop numbered.
4. **Prepaid (4:00-4:50).** Most South Africans are on prepaid. Buy airtime or data first; each call, SMS and MB comes off the balance while you use it - calls per second; at zero, the network stops your calls and data until you buy more.
   > Board: a balance on a sticky note counting down; the phone going quiet at R0.00.
5. **Contract (4:50-5:30).** On a contract you pay afterwards: the monthly fee, anything beyond the bundle, and VAT, on a bill by e-mail or in the app, paid by debit order.
   > Board: the cell-billing-flow drawing, the contract half highlighted.
6. **All three systems (5:30-6:00).** The till, the ATM and the cell phone system, each in input, processing, output, storage and communication.
   > Board: the lesson's Learn / Memorise table filled in column by column.
7. **Sign-off.**
