Lesson: `content/cattheory11/threats11.php`. Two videos: malware (how it gets
in and the signs), and power problems with what protects against them.
Board in the CAT marker style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow

## cat11-19.1 Malware - how it gets in, and the signs (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#malware` - the comment after `w19Signs`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** name the broad kinds of malware and what each does, list the common ways malware gets onto a device, and give signs that a device may be infected.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SECURITY`, title "How did *that* get in?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Ms Naidoo keeps a list on the lab wall: *Things that went wrong this year*. A deleted folder, a virus from a flash drive, a power surge, a stolen laptop, a flood. Only two of the five were criminals. Today: the ones that were.
   > Board: a sticky-note list with five lines; yellow highlighter on "a flash drive brought a virus".
2. **The four kinds again (0:40-2:00).** A quick recap from Grade 10, with what you notice: a virus hides in a file (files change or won't open); a worm spreads across a network by itself (many computers act up at once); spyware watches and reports (often nothing at all); ransomware locks files for money (a ransom note). A Trojan is how much of it gets in - it pretends to be useful.
   > Board: the four-row table drawn row by row; Clicky ticks each "what you notice" cell.
3. **Two more names (2:00-3:00).** A keylogger records every key you press - passwords, card numbers - and can be a tiny plug between a keyboard and a computer. Adware floods you with pop-ups, changes your home page, and comes bundled with "free" downloads.
   > Board: a keyboard with a small plug between it and the computer, labelled "keylogger"; a browser drowning in pop-ups.
4. **Six ways in (3:00-5:00).** One at a time: an e-mail attachment or link; infected downloads - cracked games, fake apps, fake "update" pop-ups; hacked websites and poisoned adverts; USB flash drives (the drive found in the car park); peer-to-peer file sharing - a street market where nobody checks the tables; being tricked into installing it.
   > Board: a laptop with six doors, each opening as it is named; the threats-usb-carpark flash drive labelled SALARIES at door 4.
5. **The signs (5:00-6:30).** Suddenly slow, fan roaring; pop-ups everywhere, even with the browser closed; home page or search engine changed by itself; programs you never installed; anti-malware switched off; files won't open or a ransom note; friends get messages "from you"; on a phone, data or battery gone fast.
   > Board: Clicky as a doctor with a stethoscope on a laptop; each sign written on a sticky note around it, highlighted as it is read.
6. **The keylogger question (6:30-7:00).** Lerato's 30-character passphrase does not help against a keylogger - it simply records 30 keys. That is why a second proof on her phone matters (lesson 21).
   > Board: a long passphrase scrolling into the keylogger plug; a phone with a tick beside it.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-19.2 When the power fails - cuts, surges, the UPS and the inverter (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the CAPS section `#powerFix` - the comment after the board section.
**The pupil can afterwards:** tell a power cut, a surge and a brownout apart, say what each does to a computer, and choose between a surge protector, a UPS, an inverter and a generator for a job.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SECURITY`, title "Load shedding *ate* my homework"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. The power came back after load shedding with a bang - and two computers in the Phumlani lab never started again. What happened?
   > Board: a row of computers; a lightning-bolt plug; two computers with crosses on their screens.
2. **Three things go wrong (0:30-2:30).** A power cut - everything off, unsaved work lost, a file being saved can be corrupted. A surge - a sudden spike, often when the power comes back or from lightning; it burns out power supplies, motherboards, routers and chargers. A brownout - a dip; lights go dim, computers restart or crash.
   > Board: a voltage line drawn three times - dropping to zero, spiking up, sagging down; each labelled and highlighted.
3. **What everyone can do (2:30-3:30).** Save often or use AutoSave; switch off and unplug at the wall during a cut, switch on a minute after the power returns; keep a backup, because one day a part will fail anyway.
   > Board: Clicky pulling a plug out of a wall socket; a floppy-disk save icon; a backup drive.
4. **The protection, from small to big (3:30-6:00).** A surge protector blocks spikes but does nothing in a cut (R250-R500). A UPS has a battery that takes over at once - minutes for a desktop, enough to save and shut down; a router UPS keeps the Wi-Fi on for hours. An inverter with big batteries runs a home or office for hours, often charged by solar panels. A generator runs on fuel, stands outside, and takes a few seconds to start - so offices still use a UPS for those seconds.
   > Board: the ups-inside drawing - mains charging the battery, the battery taking over; then the four-column table built column by column, prices highlighted.
5. **Which one? (6:00-6:40).** Ask what must keep working, and for how long. A UPS is for a safe shutdown; an inverter is for carrying on working. Mr Botha's till through four hours of load shedding needs an inverter.
   > Board: two sticky notes - "SAFE SHUTDOWN = UPS" and "KEEP WORKING = INVERTER"; Botha's Bakery till lit up in the dark.
6. **Sign-off.**
