## cat10-06.1 The keys that matter (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#specialKeys` (the comment is below the `q6ShiftCaps` question).
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what input is, name the kinds of keyboard, use Shift, Caps Lock, Backspace, Delete and the main shortcuts, and put their fingers on the home keys.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Why is Gogo *SHOUTING*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo Dlamini's first
   WhatsApp: GOOD MORNING MY CHILDREN I HAVE LEARNT TO TYPE. "I'm not
   shouting. That key with the little light got stuck on."
   > Board: a phone with the message in big capitals; Clicky covering his ears.
2. **What input is (0:40-1:40).** Data - text, numbers, images, video, audio -
   and instructions - programs, commands, user responses. An input device is
   the hardware that gets them in. The marks are the input; the keyboard is
   the input device.
   > Board: two baskets labelled DATA and INSTRUCTIONS; items drop into each.
3. **QWERTY and the kinds of keyboard (1:40-2:40).** Named after the top row,
   from 1870s typewriters. Standard (numeric keypad), laptop (Fn), ergonomic
   (split or curved, wrists straight), wireless, on-screen.
   > Board: a typewriter morphing into a keyboard; then five small keyboards, each with its one difference highlighted.
4. **Shift or Caps Lock (2:40-3:40).** Shift: hold it, one capital (and the top
   symbol - Shift + 2 is @). Caps Lock: on until you press it again; check it
   when a password is "wrong".
   > Screen: Notepad in the VM. Type "thabo" with Shift for the T; then Caps Lock on, type PHUMLANI, Caps Lock off; then Shift + 2 for @. The key presses shown as on-screen key captions with a yellow highlighter.
5. **Everyday keys (3:40-4:50).** Backspace deletes to the left, Delete to the
   right; Enter, Tab, Esc; arrows, Home and End.
   > Screen: Notepad - put the cursor in the middle of "computer"; press Backspace (the "p" goes), then Delete (the "u" goes); Home and End jump to the ends of the line.
6. **Shortcuts (4:50-5:50).** Modifier keys do nothing alone. Copy, step by
   step: select, hold Ctrl, press C, let go. Ctrl+X, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+S,
   Ctrl+P, Ctrl+A. Programs show the shortcuts in their menus - read the screen.
   > Screen: Word - select a sentence, Ctrl+C, click lower down, Ctrl+V; Ctrl+Z to undo it; then hover over the Copy button to show its tooltip with Ctrl+C.
7. **Typing properly (5:50-6:40).** Home keys A S D F and J K L ; with the bumps
   on F and J; thumbs on the space bar; reach and come back. Sit up, feet flat,
   wrists straight, screen at eye level, mouse close.
   > Board: two hands on the home row, fingers coloured by the keys they own; then a side view of a pupil at a desk with the four posture points ticked.
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-06.2 Pointing - mouse, touchpad and touch screen (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#touch` (the comment is below the `w6OnScreen` question).
**The pupil can afterwards:** use the five mouse actions, connect a wireless mouse, and say what a touchpad, joystick, stylus, trackball and touch screen are for.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Point, *click*, tap"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo wants to rename,
   copy or delete a photo - which click shows her every choice?
   > Board: Clicky as a giant pointer hovering over a photo file, a "?" above him.
2. **The mouse (0:30-1:20).** It moves the pointer. Underneath: a light and a
   sensor (optical). On top: left button, right button, scroll wheel.
   > Board: a mouse drawn from above and below, parts labelled one by one.
3. **Five actions (1:20-3:00).** Click selects; double-click opens; right-click
   shows a menu of what you can do with the item; drag and drop moves; scroll
   moves up and down. Right-click is the best way to learn a program.
   > Screen: File Explorer in the VM - click a photo (selected), double-click it (it opens; close it), right-click it (the menu: Rename, Copy, Delete - yellow highlighter), drag it onto a folder, scroll a long folder.
4. **Connecting a mouse (3:00-4:00).** Cable (USB - no battery); wireless with
   a tiny USB receiver; Bluetooth - pair it once: battery in and on, pairing
   button until the light flashes, open the laptop's Bluetooth settings, choose
   the mouse, Pair, test.
   > Board: three mice with their connections; then the five pairing steps as numbered sticky notes.
   > Screen: Windows Settings, Bluetooth & devices, Add device, Bluetooth - the list appears (stop before pairing anything real).
5. **Touchpad, joystick, stylus (4:00-5:10).** A pointing device moves the
   pointer or picks things on the screen. Touchpad: a laptop's mouse - slide,
   tap, two-finger tap to right-click, two fingers to scroll. Joystick: tilts
   to control movement - games, flying, and electric wheelchairs. Stylus: a pen
   for a touch screen - signing for a parcel; drawing on a drawing tablet,
   thicker when you press harder.
   > Board: the three devices drawn side by side, each with Clicky using it.
6. **The trackball - IEB (5:10-5:40).** It stays still while you roll the
   ball: little space, less wrist movement; clean it now and then.
   > Board: a trackball with a thumb rolling it; a small "IEB" tag.
7. **Touch screens (5:40-6:40).** Input and output in one. A tap is a click, a
   long press a right-click, a swipe scrolls, spread zooms in, pinch zooms out.
   Good: easy, nothing extra. Bad: the on-screen keyboard covers the screen,
   slow typing, less accurate fingers, smudges.
   > Board: the four gestures drawn finger by finger; a fat finger trying to hit a tiny button.
8. **Sign-off.**
