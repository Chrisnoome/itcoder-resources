## cat10-11.1 Hard drive, SSD, flash or cloud? (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#cloud` - the comment after `w11Cloud`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain storage vs memory, tell a device from a medium, describe each kind of storage and give an advantage and a disadvantage of each.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Where do your *files* live?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. The matric farewell. Thabo lifts his phone - "Storage almost full." By the time he has deleted enough videos, the moment is gone.
   > Board: a phone with the message; Clicky frantically throwing photos out of it.
2. **Storage vs memory (0:40-2:00).** Storage keeps files when the power is off; saving copies your work from RAM to storage. RAM is the kitchen counter - what you are cooking now, wiped at night. Storage is the cupboard - everything you own, for months. RAM is volatile; storage is non-volatile. The device reads and writes; the medium holds the data - the DVD drive and the DVD.
   > Board: a kitchen counter and a cupboard; a power plug pulled, and the counter wiped clean. Yellow highlighter on "volatile".
3. **Hard drives (2:00-3:20).** Metal disks spin 5 400 or 7 200 times a minute; a head floats closer than a hair and writes magnetic patterns. Cheapest per GB and huge, about R1 400 for 2 TB. But slow, noisy, and a bump while it works can make the head hit the disk.
   > Board: the hdd-anatomy drawing; a speck of dust drawn as a mountain in the head's path.
4. **SSDs (3:20-4:30).** Flash memory chips, no moving parts. Many times faster - a laptop starts in seconds - robust, silent, light. But more per GB. Almost every new laptop has one; 256 GB at the cheapest, 512 GB comfortable.
   > Board: a chip-covered board, nothing moving; Clicky dropping it and it bouncing, unharmed.
5. **Flash drives and memory cards (4:30-5:50).** A flash drive is flash memory with a USB plug - robust, easy to lose, and never plug in one you found. Memory cards: SD for cameras, microSD for phones and drones. Match the shape, the biggest card the device takes, and the speed - U3 or V30 for video. Read them with a card reader, built in or by USB.
   > Board: an SD card the size of a stamp, a microSD the size of a fingernail; a three-item checklist on a sticky note.
6. **CDs, DVDs and Blu-ray (5:50-6:40).** Read by a laser. CD 700 MB, DVD 4.7 GB, Blu-ray 25 GB. Cheap to make for films, music and games; small, slow, scratch easily, and most new laptops have no drive.
   > Board: three discs that look the same, with their capacities written under them; pink highlighter on Blu-ray.
7. **The cloud (6:40-7:40).** Files on a company's servers, reached over the Internet: Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud. Anywhere, safe if the laptop is stolen, easy to share, 15 or 5 GB free. But no Internet, no files; it uses data; more space costs about R40 a month for 100 GB.
   > Board: a cloud, with a little door; inside, rows of servers. Clicky: "It's just someone else's computer!"
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-11.2 KB, MB, GB, TB - how big is a file? (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#units` - the comment after `q11Fit`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** put the units in order, convert between them step by step, and explain why Windows shows a 1 TB drive as 931 GB.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Kids Make Great *Tea*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A gigabyte or a snakebite? Today you learn to tell them apart.
   > Board: Dolly Parton's line on a sticky note; Clicky holding a snake at arm's length.
2. **The units (0:30-2:00).** One letter is about one byte. Then each unit is 1 000 times the last: kilobyte, megabyte, gigabyte, terabyte. Kids Make Great Tea. A short e-mail is a few KB, a phone photo 2 to 5 MB, an hour of HD video 1 to 3 GB, a big external drive 1 to 4 TB.
   > Board: a staircase of four steps, each labelled, with an example sitting on each step. Yellow highlighter on the K, M, G and T.
3. **Converting (2:00-3:50).** Find both units; count the steps; going down to a smaller unit, multiply by 1 000 a step; going up, divide. 2 TB is 2 000 GB. 4 500 MB is 4.5 GB. 3 GB is 3 000 000 KB.
   > Board: Clicky walking down the staircase multiplying, and up it dividing; the three examples worked on sticky notes.
4. **How many photos? (3:50-4:40).** 64 GB is 64 000 MB; 64 000 ÷ 4 = 16 000 photos - in theory. In practice Android and the apps take a big share first.
   > Board: the sum; then a phone's storage bar, mostly filled with "system" and "apps".
5. **1 000 or 1 024 (4:40-5:30).** Windows counts in 1 024s, so a new 1 TB drive shows as about 931 GB. Nothing is missing. In a test either is usually accepted - always show your working.
   > Screen: in the VM, File Explorer > This PC; right-click a drive > Properties; highlight the capacity in bytes and in GB. (About 15 seconds.)
6. **Sign-off.**

(The IEB section on PB and EB is one sentence on the board in scene 2 - "after TB come PB and EB" - and is in the lesson's `#bigUnits`.)
