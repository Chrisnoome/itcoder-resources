## cat10-24.1 What the Internet is, and who connects you (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#isp` - the comment after `q24IspJob`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** define the Internet, say that nobody owns it, tell an IP address from a web or e-mail address, list what a device needs to get online, and say what an ISP is and does.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · INTERNET`, title "Your bundle ran out. The *Internet* didn't."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Sunday night, project due, and Thabo's data runs out. "The Internet is down!" Lerato's video is still playing. The Internet is fine - Thabo lost his way onto it.
   > Board: Thabo with a phone showing "No data", Lerato beside him with a playing video. Clicky with a "?".
2. **A network of networks (0:40-2:00).** A network: two or more devices connected so they can share. Your street joins a road, the road a highway, the highway other towns - nobody built all the roads, but they join up. The Internet: a worldwide network of networks - homes, schools, businesses, cellphone networks. Nobody owns it; each network belongs to someone.
   > Board: the internet-networks drawing, built network by network; yellow highlighter on "network of networks". Sticky note: "Nobody owns it".
3. **Addresses (2:00-2:50).** Every device has an IP address - a number such as 203.0.113.25. People use names: a web address (bestlessons.co.za) and an e-mail address (with an @). Like tapping "Gogo" in your contacts instead of typing her number.
   > Board: three cards - 203.0.113.25, bestlessons.co.za, thabo.dlamini@gmail.com - sorted into three boxes; pink highlighter on the @.
4. **What you need to get online (2:50-4:20).** The four things, as numbered steps: a device that can connect (Wi-Fi, a network port or a SIM); a connection (a cable or radio); connecting hardware where needed (the router); software (the operating system, a browser, apps). Then the worked example: Gogo's phone - and Mr Botha's laptop.
   > Board: a numbered list, each item drawn as it is said; Gogo's phone with ticks beside it.
5. **The ISP (4:20-5:40).** An Internet service provider connects you, for a fee - like the municipality bringing electricity to your house. It connects you, sells packages (speed and data), may add e-mail or web space, and runs a help desk. Your cellphone network is an ISP; a fibre ISP is another. At 20:00 the shared connection fills up like the N1 at rush hour.
   > Board: a house, a cable to an "ISP" building, the ISP to a cloud labelled "the Internet"; the rush-hour doodle for the 20:00 point. Yellow highlighter on "ISP".
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-24.2 Ways to connect, up and down, and the cloud (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#cloud` - the comment after `w24CloudSchool`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** describe fibre, ADSL, mobile data, an LTE router and satellite; say why Wi-Fi is not the Internet and what a hotspot is; tell downloading from uploading; say what the cloud is, with an advantage and a disadvantage.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · INTERNET`, title "Wi-Fi is *not* the Internet"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Full Wi-Fi bars at a café - and nothing loads. Why?
   > Board: a phone with full bars and a spinning wheel; Clicky tapping his foot.
2. **By cable (0:30-1:50).** Fibre: glass strands carrying flashes of light - fastest, steadiest, usually uncapped. ADSL: data over a copper telephone line - older, slower, being switched off. The cable ends in a box; the router plugs in.
   > Board: the home-internet drawing, street to router; yellow highlighter on "fibre", then on "ADSL" with "old" written beside it.
3. **Through the air (1:50-3:00).** Mobile data: a SIM, a cellphone tower, bundles - works almost anywhere, most expensive per gigabyte. An LTE or 5G router: a SIM in a box at home, Wi-Fi for the family. Satellite: a dish, for a farm with nothing else.
   > Board: a tower with radio waves to a phone and to a router in a house; a dish on a farmhouse roof.
4. **Wi-Fi and hotspots (3:00-4:10).** Wi-Fi is a short-range wireless network made by the router - it only reaches the router. The router needs its own connection. Back to the café: the café's link to its ISP is down. A hotspot shares a connection over Wi-Fi: a café, or your phone sharing its data (bundle and battery).
   > Board: phone -> router (Wi-Fi, ticked) -> ISP (cable, crossed out). Pink highlighter on "Wi-Fi is NOT the Internet".
5. **Down and up (4:10-5:10).** Downloading: data coming to you - installing an app, watching a video. Uploading: data going from you - posting a TikTok, saving to Google Drive. The Internet is "up there". A video call does both at once - which is why it eats data.
   > Board: a cloud above a phone; arrows down ("download") and up ("upload"); then a video call with both arrows.
6. **The cloud (5:10-6:40).** Someone else's computers - servers in data centres - used over the Internet. Cloud storage (Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud) and web apps (Google Docs, Microsoft 365). Saving to OneDrive at school, opening on the phone at home: save, upload, open, download. Good: any device, a backup, easy sharing. Bad: no Internet, no files; uses data; you trust the company.
   > Board: a building full of servers labelled "the cloud"; the four steps numbered; a two-column good/bad list, a highlighter swipe per point as it is said.
7. **Sign-off.**
