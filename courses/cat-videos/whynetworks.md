Lesson: `content/cattheory10/whynetworks.php`. Two short videos, one per main
idea. Board in the CAT marker style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md);
yellow highlighter on any words on screen being talked about. No screen

## cat10-21.1 What is a network? (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#share` - the comment after `g21Share`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a network is and what it is for, name the nodes on one, and sort shared things into hardware, software, and data and information.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "What is a *network*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:45).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Saturday morning at Botha's Bakery in Centurion. A customer taps her card, the machine thinks for a second, and beeps: approved. In that second it sent a message to the bank and got an answer. At the back, Mr Botha prints the order list from his laptop - with no cable to the printer. Both are networks at work.
   > Board: a bakery counter with a card machine; a dashed line from the machine off the edge of the board to a small bank. Clicky taps the card.
2. **What it is, what it is for, an example (0:45-2:00).** A network is two or more computers or other devices connected together - by cable, radio such as Wi-Fi, or both. What for: so they can communicate and share. Example: Mr Botha's laptop and printer on the bakery's Wi-Fi.
   > Board: three sticky notes appear in turn - WHAT IT IS / WHAT IT IS FOR / AN EXAMPLE. Yellow highlighter on "communicate" and "share".
3. **Nodes (2:00-3:00).** Every device on a network is a node: workstations (the computers people work at), servers (computers that do a job for the others - keep files, check log-ins), printers, laptops, tablets, phones. A cable is not a node; it only carries the data between them.
   > Board: a small network drawn piece by piece; each node gets a yellow dot as it is named; the cable gets a cross and "not a node".
4. **Communication (3:00-3:45).** The first aim. Ms Naidoo sends one message and every Grade 10 parent has it in seconds. Thabo video-calls his cousin in Durban for a little data.
   > Board: one phone sending arrows to many phones.
5. **Sharing - four things (3:45-5:15).** Hardware: thirty lab computers, one printer; one fibre line for every classroom. Software: a program installed once on the server. Data: the bakery's stock list, every pupil's marks. Information: a class average or a report - everyone opens the same, up-to-date copy. The aim in one sentence: communicate, and share hardware, software, data and information.
   > Board: four boxes - HARDWARE, SOFTWARE, DATA, INFORMATION - filled with small drawings. Pink highlighter on the one-sentence aim.
6. **Without and with (5:15-5:45).** Thirty computers with thirty printers and their ink, against one printer, one line, one place for every file.
   > Board: the before/after lab: the left half fades to the right.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-21.2 Networks - the good and the bad (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#applying` - the comment after `w21Home`, before the scenario.
**The pupil can afterwards:** give advantages and disadvantages of a network, explain the security and privacy problems, and pick the points that fit a situation.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "Worth the *risk*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Ms Naidoo's lab has 30 computers. How much does a network save on printing?
   > Board: thirty little computer squares in rows; Clicky with a calculator.
2. **The printers, worked out (0:30-1:30).** Without a network: thirty inkjet printers at about R1 500 each - about R45 000, and thirty lots of ink for ever. With a network: one laser printer at about R8 000. The saving: more than R35 000 before a single page.
   > Board: two columns of sums on sticky notes; yellow highlighter on "R35 000".
3. **The advantages (1:30-2:30).** Saves money on hardware; fast, cheap communication; everyone uses the same, up-to-date data; backups in one place every night; software installed once; control over who sees what.
   > Board: six ticks drawn down the page, one per line.
4. **Security (2:30-3:20).** A connected device can be attacked over the network. Criminals can try from anywhere; malware on one laptop can spread to every computer and to the server.
   > Board: one laptop with a bug; the bug hops along the cables to the others. Yellow highlighter on "security".
5. **Privacy (3:20-4:10).** Privacy is keeping information about yourself to yourself. A shared folder left open; a Wi-Fi with no password; messages kept on a company's servers.
   > Board: an open folder with eyes peeping in; a router with "no password" crossed in red.
6. **The rest (4:10-5:00).** Cost for a business; a network administrator to pay; downtime - the bakery's card machine can't reach the bank, so cash only; slow when it's busy, like the 140 GB game update in the middle of a video call.
   > Board: the network-down doodle redrawn: paper aeroplanes.
7. **Both sides, and applying it (5:00-5:45).** Security can be an advantage too: one administrator keeps everything updated and backed up. In a test, pick the points that fit the situation - "expensive" earns nothing for a home.
   > Board: the Learn / Memorise table, a row at a time; pink highlighter on the home row's "hardly any cost".
8. **Sign-off.**
