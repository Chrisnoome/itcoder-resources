# CAT 11 lesson 17: The Fourth Industrial Revolution, VR and AR - videos

Lesson file: `AIPascalCourse/content/cattheory11/fourir.php`. Board drawn in
the CAT marker style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow
highlighter on any words being talked about. Everything below is in the
lesson text.

The 4IR part of the lesson is a CAPS board section. The first video covers
it briefly, as the lesson does for everyone; say nothing about boards in the
video.

## cat11-17.1 VR or AR? (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#difference` (the comment `// VIDEO cat11-17.1`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what the 4IR is, define VR and AR, say what a VR headset has, and tell VR from AR in an example.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · 4IR`, title "Replace it, or *add* to it?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:50).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A dinosaur walks
   across Thabo's lounge carpet on his phone. In a mall in Centurion,
   Lerato's knees shake on a plank at the top of a virtual skyscraper.
   > Board: a phone with a dinosaur on a carpet; Lerato in a headset on a plank.
2. **Four industrial revolutions (0:50-2:20).** Steam (1760), electricity
   (1870), computers (1970), smart connected machines - the 4IR (2010s).
   Machines that sense, connect and decide. Its technologies: AI, the IoT,
   robots, big data and the cloud, 3D printing, drones, self-driving cars,
   VR and AR. New jobs, shrinking jobs, the digital divide.
   > Board: the iot-revolutions drawing as four steps; highlighter on "sense, connect and decide".
3. **Virtual reality (2:20-4:20).** A computer-made 3-D world that replaces
   the real one; immersed. The headset: a screen for each eye, lenses,
   motion sensors and cameras, sound; controllers. Standalone vs PC or
   console VR.
   > Board: the xr-headset drawing, part by part; the xr-bump drawing.
4. **Augmented reality (4:20-6:00).** Computer-made extras on a live view of
   the real world - "augment" means add to. The four steps on a phone. AR
   you have used: filters, Pokémon GO, Google Lens, furniture and paint apps,
   lines on sport broadcasts, arrows on the street.
   > Board: a phone pointed at a sign in French, the English drawn over it.
   > Note: AR cannot be screen-recorded in the VM - board only.
5. **The difference (6:00-7:20).** VR replaces, AR adds. The Learn / Memorise
   table: what you see, equipment, moving around, cost. Why walking
   directions must be AR. Mixed reality in one sentence.
   > Board: the xr-spectrum drawing; two big words, REPLACES and ADDS, with highlighter.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-17.2 What VR and AR are for - and their limits (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#prosCons` (the comment `// VIDEO cat11-17.2`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** give real-world uses of VR and AR with South African examples, and explain their advantages and limitations, including motion sickness.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · 4IR`, title "Crash the plane. *Nobody* gets hurt."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A new mine worker
   escapes a fire underground - in a headset, before she ever goes down.
   > Board: a miner in a headset; flames that are clearly drawn on a screen.
2. **Simulations and uses (0:30-3:00).** What a simulation is. The uses
   table: training (pilots, mine workers, nurses), education (a virtual trip
   to the Cradle of Humankind), health, shopping (try on glasses, a couch in
   your lounge), property, tourism, games and sport, repairs.
   > Board: a grid of small icons filling in, one per area, each tagged VR or AR.
3. **Advantages (3:00-4:00).** Safe practice; cheaper than the real thing;
   go where you cannot go; learn by doing; try before you buy; information
   where you need it.
   > Board: six sticky notes.
4. **Limitations (4:00-5:40).** Cost; motion sickness - eyes see movement the
   body does not feel; eye strain and headaches, minimum ages; can't see the
   room, isolation; AR distraction; battery two to three hours; not quite
   real; privacy.
   > Board: the xr-sick drawing; highlighter on "eyes see movement the body does not feel".
5. **Using VR safely (5:40-6:30).** The six steps: clear a space, set the
   boundary, short sessions, jump don't glide, someone watching, clean it.
   > Board: a lounge being cleared, a dotted boundary drawn on the floor.
6. **Sign-off.**
