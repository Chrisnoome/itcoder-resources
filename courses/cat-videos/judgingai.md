## cat11-27.1 Checking a source like a fact-checker (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#crosscheck` (the comment `// VIDEO cat11-27.1`, after `o27Cross`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** judge a source with the five criteria (coverage included), find out who is behind a website, and cross-check a fact against independent sources.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAT`, title "Five sites. *One* source."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A WhatsApp message:
   "From 1 March all taxi fares in Gauteng go up by R10! Please share." Is it
   true?
   > Board: a phone with the message; Clicky with a magnifying glass.
2. **Five criteria (0:40-2:20).** Authority (who created it?), accuracy (are
   the facts substantiated?), currency (up to date, still relevant?),
   objectivity (any bias?), coverage (how well does it cover the topic?).
   Coverage is new: a report on Cape Town taxis says little about Soweto.
   > Board: the Learn / Memorise table built row by row; highlighter on each criterion.
3. **Who is behind a website? (2:20-4:00).** Affiliation and audience. The
   address endings: .gov.za, .ac.za, .org.za, .co.za - a clue, not proof.
   The two-minute check: address, About us, author and date, who it is for,
   what it is for, one fact checked elsewhere.
   > Screen: in the VM, a browser on a .gov.za page; scroll to the bottom, open "About us", find the date.
4. **Cross-checking (4:00-6:20).** Pick the fact; find it in two or three
   other good sources; make sure they are independent (ten sites that copied
   one story are one source); go back to the original; decide, and note any
   disagreement. The worked example: two news sites have nothing, three
   Facebook pages copied the same words, the association's own statement
   says R2 on two long routes. Do not share the message.
   > Board: the photocopier drawing - one page in, five copies out.
5. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-27.2 AI as a source (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#aihonest` (the comment `// VIDEO cat11-27.2`, inside the CAPS board section; taught to everyone).
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what AI is, explain why a chatbot can be wrong while sounding sure, check an AI answer, and use and cite AI honestly.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAT`, title "The chatbot *sounded* sure"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. April 2026: a draft
   national AI policy is withdrawn two weeks after it was published - some
   of its references did not exist.
2. **What AI is (0:40-1:40).** Software that learns from data to make
   decisions or create outputs; code and trained models, not a thinking
   being. Chatbots bring search, writing, summarising and translating
   together - a convergent technology. Where it helps: understanding a topic,
   keywords, summarising a document you give it, patterns in your data,
   pointing to sources.
   > Board: a chatbot window with five arrows out to the five uses.
3. **When AI gets it wrong (1:40-3:40).** It predicts likely-sounding text,
   not checked text. Hallucinations (invented court cases, 2023 and 2025);
   misread sources (the rock a day, 2024); out of date; the wrong country
   (driving licence 16 in the USA - 17 and 18 here); bias; just as sure when
   wrong. The five criteria applied to an AI answer.
   > Board: the ai-confident drawing; highlighter on "likely-sounding".
4. **Checking an AI answer (3:40-5:30).** Open the sources it links to;
   check the facts that matter against a trusted source; cross-check; is it
   about South Africa and up to date?; use and cite the real source. The
   driving-licence example, and Thabo's made-up 68% "(Stats SA, 2021)".
   > Screen: in the VM, a chatbot answer with a link; open the link and show it says something else (use a prepared page - no live claims about a real product).
5. **Using AI honestly (5:30-7:20).** Academic integrity. Follow your
   school's rules; never hand in AI's words as your own; say how you used it;
   cite it; keep private things out. The Harvard pattern: OpenAI (2026)
   *ChatGPT* [AI chatbot] ... and the note on how it was used.
   > Board: the reference written out; highlighter on maker, year, tool and date accessed.
6. **Sign-off.**
