# CAT 12 lesson 21: Cryptocurrency, drones and what comes next - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory12/crypto.php`. Three videos: what crypto changes
(inside the IEB section, taught to all), drones at work, and chatbots. Board
in the CAT marker style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow
highlighter on any words on screen being talked about. No screen recordings
needed. Everything below is in the lesson text. Say once, as the lesson
does: this explains crypto; it is not advice to buy or sell it.

## cat12-21.1 What crypto changes - for people, business and the planet (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#cryptoSociety` - the comment after it, inside the IEB section.
**The pupil can afterwards:** give positive and negative impacts of cryptocurrency on people, businesses, society and the environment, and explain each.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · CRYPTO`, title "What does Bitcoin *change*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A customer at Botha's Bakery asks: "Can I pay in Bitcoin?" Before Mr Botha answers - what would it change?
   > Board: the bakery counter, a phone showing a Bitcoin logo; Clicky with a question mark.
2. **Recap in one breath (0:40-1:20).** Digital money, no bank in charge; the blockchain copied on thousands of computers; bought on a crypto exchange; legal, but not legal tender.
   > Board: the blockchain-stokvel drawing; highlighter on "not legal tender".
3. **People: the good side (1:20-2:30).** Sending money home to Harare in minutes; no bank account needed; open all the time; nobody can freeze a wallet you keep yourself.
   > Board: a map arrow Johannesburg to Harare, a clock showing minutes.
4. **People: the bad side (2:30-3:50).** Volatility (more than half lost, October 2025 to February 2026); no undo; no safety net (FTX, 2022); scams and Ponzi schemes (Mirror Trading International); tax to SARS. The scam warning signs.
   > Board: a price line diving; the crypto-lost-key doodle; highlighter on "guaranteed".
5. **Businesses (3:50-4:50).** The four steps of a Bitcoin payment through a payment app; good (new customers, no fake notes, new jobs) and bad (value drop if kept, few customers, ransomware paid in crypto).
   > Board: four numbered boxes, QR code to rands.
6. **Society and the planet (4:50-6:30).** Crime, but payments can be traced; the FSCA licence (October 2022) and customer checks (December 2022); crypto mining uses as much electricity as a medium-sized country, much of it coal, plus e-waste; inclusion for people banks do not serve. El Salvador, 2021 and 2025.
   > Board: the crypto-miner doodle beside a power station; highlighter on "FSCA".
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-21.2 Drones at work (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#droneRules` - the comment after `w21Inspect`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** define a drone (UAV), describe it as an IPO system, give uses, limitations and disadvantages, and state the SACAA rules.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · DRONES`, title "No pilot *on board*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A drone flies up and down the maize rows next to the highway. Who is flying it?
   > Board: a field from above, a quadcopter, Clicky waving from the ground.
2. **What a drone is (0:30-1:30).** No pilot on board; remote control or its own computer with GPS; UAV, unmanned aerial vehicle; a quadcopter from about R6 000. The Queen Bee and the name "drone".
   > Board: the quadcopter labelled - four propellers, camera, battery, computer; highlighter on "UAV".
3. **A drone as IPO (1:30-2:30).** Input: remote, GPS, sensors, camera. Processing: how fast to spin each propeller. Output: propellers, live video, the memory card.
   > Board: the three-column IPO table filled in one column at a time.
4. **Drones at work (2:30-4:00).** Farming, inspecting, mining and building, filming, anti-poaching, search and rescue, deliveries (Rwanda since 2016, Ghana since 2019). Why: safer, cheaper, quicker, better information.
   > Board: seven small icons around the drone; highlighter on "safer".
5. **Limitations vs disadvantages (4:00-5:20).** Limits: battery 20-40 minutes, weather, range, small loads. Disadvantages: privacy, safety, misuse, jobs, noise. The difference, said slowly.
   > Board: two columns, "cannot do" and "bad effect".
6. **The rules (5:20-6:00).** SACAA: licensed pilot and operator's certificate for work; not near airports; not over crowds; keep it in sight.
   > Board: four rules with ticks; highlighter on "SACAA".
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-21.3 Chatbots - helpful, or a wall? (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#chatbotLimits` - the comment after `w21BotLimit`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** define a chatbot, tell rule-based from AI chatbots, and give advantages for a business and problems for a customer.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · CHATBOTS`, title "Talking to a *robot*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. 22:00. Gogo Dlamini's card was declined. Her bank's WhatsApp number answers in two seconds - but who is answering?
   > Board: a phone with a chat; Gogo with her glasses on her nose.
2. **Rule-based chatbots (0:30-1:50).** A script: "Reply 1, 2 or 3"; keywords; prepared answers. Quick and reliable for what it was built for - stuck on anything else. ELIZA, 1966.
   > Board: the three-line chat with Gogo replying 2; highlighter on "Reply 2".
3. **AI chatbots (1:50-3:00).** Understands everyday language, writes its own answer; ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Claude, Vodacom's TOBi; voice assistants. The comparison table.
   > Board: the table built row by row.
4. **Why businesses use them (3:00-4:10).** 24 hours, no queue, cheaper, same answer every time, staff freed for hard problems.
   > Board: the robot-no-leave doodle.
5. **What goes wrong (4:10-5:30).** Loops with no way to a person; hallucinations; privacy - never a PIN, password or OTP; call-centre jobs; fake chatbots.
   > Board: the ai-confident doodle; highlighter on "hallucination".
6. **Sign-off.**
