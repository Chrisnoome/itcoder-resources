## cat10-09.1 Screen size and resolution - what the numbers on the box mean (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#resolution` - the comment after `w9Monitor`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say how screen size is measured, explain resolution and the main resolutions (HD, Full HD, 4K), explain why a bigger screen is not a sharper one, and say what aspect ratio and refresh rate mean.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Bigger is not *sharper*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A shop advert: "24-inch Full HD monitor, 1920 x 1080, 16:9, 60 Hz." Four numbers, and most people buying it know what one of them means. By the end of this video you will know all four.
   > Board: the advert on a sticky note; Clicky squinting at it with a magnifying glass. Yellow highlighter swipes each number in turn.
2. **Output first (0:40-1:30).** Output is the result of processing, in a form you can use. Four types: text, graphics, audio, video. A printout you can hold is hard copy; the same thing on a screen or in a file is soft copy - even a PDF, until somebody prints it.
   > Board: four quick icons (a line of text, a chart, a speaker, a play button); then a sheet of paper (hard copy, pink) beside a phone showing a PDF (soft copy, blue).
3. **Screen size (1:30-2:40).** Screen size is measured diagonally, corner to corner, in inches. Measure the picture, not the frame; divide the centimetres by 2.54. Thabo's laptop: 39.6 cm, so 15.6 inches. Phones 6 to 7, laptops 14 to 16, monitors 22 to 27, classroom TVs 55 to 75.
   > Board: a screen drawn; a tape measure stretched from top-left to bottom-right; the sum 39.6 ÷ 2.54 = 15.6 written out on a sticky note.
4. **Pixels and resolution (2:40-4:10).** Go close and the picture is made of tiny dots: pixels. Resolution is pixels across times pixels down. Full HD is 1920 x 1080 - about 2 million pixels. 4K is 3840 x 2160 - twice as wide, twice as high, four times as many. HD is 1280 x 720.
   > Board: a zoom into a screen until squares appear; Clicky stands on one square: "I'm a pixel!" Then the Full HD rectangle drawn, and four of them tiled into the 4K rectangle. Yellow highlighter on "across x down".
5. **Bigger is not sharper (4:10-5:20).** Two Full HD monitors, 22 and 32 inches, on one desk. Same pixels - so on the bigger one each pixel is bigger, and the picture looks blockier. Sitting further back hides the pixels; that is why a big TV looks fine from the couch.
   > Board: two screens, the same grid drawn on each - small squares on the small one, big squares on the big one. Then the before/after photo from the lesson (48 x 48 and 480 x 480 pixels) wiped across.
6. **The other words (5:20-6:20).** Aspect ratio is the shape: 16:9 wide, the old 4:3 almost square. Refresh rate is new pictures a second, in hertz: 60 Hz for schoolwork, 144 Hz or more for gaming. Brightness, and contrast ratio - how much brighter the white is than the black.
   > Board: a 16:9 and a 4:3 rectangle side by side; a flip-book running at two speeds for refresh rate.
7. **Buying one (6:20-6:50).** Lerato's list: size, at least Full HD, a port that matches her laptop - HDMI, or VGA on old screens - and the price, about R2 000 for 24 inches.
   > Board: the four-item list on a sticky note, ticked off.
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-09.2 Projectors - and how to fix "No Signal" (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#projectors` - the comment after `w9Projector`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a data projector is for, what to look for (lumens, resolution, ports, lamp), compare it with a big screen, and fix "No Signal" step by step.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "*No Signal?* Fix it in 5 steps"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. First period at Phumlani Secondary. Ms Naidoo plugs in her laptop, switches on the projector - and thirty pupils read the words "No Signal" on the wall.
   > Board: a classroom wall with "No Signal" in a box; Clicky with his head in his hands.
2. **What a projector is (0:30-1:30).** A data projector shines the computer's picture onto a wall or screen so a whole room can see it. Many projectors are DLP: a chip with millions of tiny mirrors, one per pixel, each tilting light to the wall or away. Further from the wall: bigger picture, dimmer picture.
   > Board: projector, beam, wall; a zoom into the chip showing tiny tilting mirrors. Yellow highlighter on "DLP".
3. **What to look for (1:30-2:40).** Brightness in lumens - about 3 000 or more for a classroom in daylight. Resolution - old ones 1024 x 768, new ones Full HD. Ports - HDMI on new ones, VGA on old ones, sometimes Wi-Fi. The lamp - it wears out and is expensive; LED and laser ones last much longer.
   > Board: a four-line sticky note; yellow highlighter on "lumens".
4. **Projector or big screen (2:40-3:40).** A projector gives a huge picture for a hall and costs R8 000 to R12 000. A big TV or an interactive flat panel stays bright in a sunny room and has no lamp to replace - but a 65-inch TV is about R12 000 and an interactive panel R40 000 or more.
   > Board: the lesson's two-column table drawn row by row; pink highlighter on each side's win.
5. **Fixing No Signal (3:40-5:30).** No Signal means the projector works but no picture reaches it. In order: check the cable at both ends; press Source on the remote until it shows the socket the cable is in; on the laptop press Windows key + P and choose Duplicate; if the sockets do not match, use an adapter such as USB-C to HDMI; still nothing - try another cable. Ms Naidoo's problem was step 2.
   > Board: the five steps appear as a numbered list, each ticked as it is shown.
   > Screen: in the VM, press Windows key + P; the Project panel opens on the right; click Duplicate. (About 10 seconds; crop to the panel.)
6. **Sign-off.**
