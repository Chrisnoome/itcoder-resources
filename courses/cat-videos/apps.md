## cat10-15.1 Which app for which job? (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the match `m15Jobs`, at the end of section `#choosing` (it
uses the worked example table there).
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what each office application is for, tell
a spreadsheet job from a database job, and choose the kind of software for a
job with a reason.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "Don't slice bread with a *whisk*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Mr Botha's list for
   Monday at the bakery: profit on each kind of bread, a letter to the
   school, a poster for the window, the customer who ordered 40 rolls, the
   flour order. Five jobs - five programs. You wouldn't slice bread with a
   whisk.
   > Board: the list on a sticky note; a whisk sawing at a loaf; Clicky
   > shaking his head.
2. **Application software and suites (0:40-1:40).** Software that does a job
   for you; app is short for application. An office suite: Microsoft 365,
   Google Workspace, LibreOffice - they look alike, share data, cost less
   together.
   > Board: a box labelled SUITE with four programs inside; an arrow copying
   > a chart from the spreadsheet into the word processor.
3. **Word processor (1:40-2:20).** Documents - letters, CVs, essays, reports.
   > Board: a letter page; yellow band on "documents".
4. **Spreadsheet (2:20-3:30).** Numbers in cells; formulas that recalculate.
   The taxi example: =SUM(B2:B4), type 300 over 250, the total jumps from
   500 to 550.
   > Board: the spreadsheet-sum drawing, redrawn; the 250 crossed out, 300
   > written, 500 changing to 550 with a yellow band.
5. **Presentation software (3:30-4:10).** Slides while someone talks; a few
   words and a strong picture; you do the talking.
   > Board: a slide crammed with text (death by PowerPoint) next to a slide
   > with one picture and three words.
6. **Database (4:10-5:10).** Many records of the same kind, found in seconds:
   a library's 4 000 books, a clinic's patients. Spreadsheet or database?
   Mainly calculating - spreadsheet. Mainly storing and finding many
   records - database.
   > Board: a card index exploding into a neat table; pink highlighter on
   > CALCULATING and STORING AND FINDING.
7. **Back to Mr Botha (5:10-6:40).** Walk the worked example: profit -
   spreadsheet; letter - word processor; poster - graphic design; the 40
   rolls - database; flour order - e-mail; business breakfast - presentation;
   invoices and VAT - accounting software. Name the kind, not only the brand,
   and give the reason.
   > Board: the Learn / Memorise table filled in row by row.
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-15.2 Screenshots - Print Screen and the Snipping Tool (about 5 min)

**Goes:** after the written question `w15SnipPrint` (end of section
`#basicApps`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** take a screenshot with the Snipping Tool, crop
and mark it up, and explain how it differs from Print Screen.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · WINDOWS`, title "Snip it, don't *photograph* it"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. An error message pops
   up on Thabo's screen. He takes a photo of the screen with his phone -
   skew, blurry, full of reflections. The computer can do far better itself.
   > Board: a wonky phone photo of a screen with a glare spot.
2. **What a screenshot is (0:30-1:00).** A picture of what is on the screen -
   to show an error, keep proof of a payment, or put a program into a
   document.
3. **Print Screen (1:00-1:50).** On older set-ups it copies the whole screen
   to the clipboard - nothing seems to happen; paste it with Ctrl + V into
   Paint or a document. On an up-to-date Windows 11 it opens the Snipping
   Tool instead.
   > Screen: VM, press PrtSc (show what this VM does), open Paint, Ctrl + V.
4. **The Snipping Tool, step by step (1:50-4:00).** Windows key + Shift + S;
   the screen dims; choose rectangle, free-form, window or full screen; drag
   around only the message; it is copied to the clipboard; click the pop-up
   to open it; mark it up with the pen or highlighter; crop away the edges;
   save it as a picture, or paste it into Word.
   > Screen: a harmless test message box in the VM; every step shown, with a
   > key-press overlay for Windows + Shift + S; yellow band on each mode
   > button as it is named.
5. **On a phone (4:00-4:30).** Power + volume down on most Android phones;
   side + volume up on an iPhone; saved in your photos, ready to crop.
   > Board: two phones with the buttons highlighted.
6. **The difference (4:30-4:50).** Print Screen: the whole screen, to the
   clipboard. Snipping Tool: you choose what, mark it up, save it.
   > Board: two columns, pink highlighter on the differences.
7. **Sign-off.**

> Note: record in the VM. Show only a test message box - no personal files.
