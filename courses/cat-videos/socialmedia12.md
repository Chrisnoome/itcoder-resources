## cat12-18.1 Fake news - spot it before you share it (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#fakenews` - the comment after `w18Spread`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** tell misinformation from disinformation, give reasons why fake news spreads fast, and check a story in six steps.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOCIAL MEDIA`, title "Schools closed *tomorrow*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo forwards "Schools closed tomorrow - taxi strike!" to the class group. It is not true, and half the class stays at home. Did Thabo lie?
   > Board: a phone with the message bubble; highlighter on "tomorrow"; Clicky with a question mark.
2. **Three names (0:40-2:00).** Fake news - false information made to look like news. Misinformation - false, and shared by someone who believes it (Thabo). Disinformation - false on purpose (whoever made it up). The same message can be both.
   > Board: two arrows from one message - a scammer's hat ("on purpose") and Thabo's face ("believes it"); highlighter on "dis-" and "mis-".
3. **Why it spreads (2:00-3:40).** Made to shock; one tap to a group of 200; feeds push reactions; bots share it thousands of times; we trust the sender; the echo chamber shows us what we already believe. Deepfakes make it harder still.
   > Board: the soc-echo drawing; a message multiplying across phones; highlighter on "echo chamber".
4. **What it can do (3:40-4:20).** Panic, harm to health, harm to a person's name, violence, elections.
   > Board: five small icons appearing in a row.
5. **Checking a story (4:20-6:20).** Stop; who says so - the real web address (news24-breaking.info is not News24); does anyone else say so (cross-check); when - old photos as "today", reverse image search; read past the headline; ask a fact-checker such as Africa Check. If unsure, do not forward; "Forwarded many times" is a warning.
   > Screen: a made-up message in a mock chat; then a search for the claim on a news site; then Google Lens on a storm photo showing it is years old (as in lesson 13's video).
   > Board: the six steps as a ladder; highlighter on "Stop".
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-18.2 What a post can do - your footprint, your reputation and bullying (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#footprint` - the comment after `w18Footprint`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain the cyber profile and who builds it, give reasons why cyberbullying can do more harm than bullying at school, say what a bystander should do, and recommend steps to manage a cyber profile.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOCIAL MEDIA`, title "The interviewer has a *printout*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Friday: Thabo's bursary interview. The interviewer has a printout of a post he made in Grade 9.
   > Board: the soc-interview doodle; highlighter on "Grade 9".
2. **Footprint and cyber profile (0:40-2:20).** Active and passive footprint (Grade 10). The cyber profile: the picture others build from everything online - partly made by other people: tags, screenshots, old accounts, news pages. Universities, bursary funders and employers search names.
   > Board: the footprint-trail drawing; then a silhouette built up from photo, comment and tag cards; highlighter on "cyber profile".
3. **Managing it (2:20-3:40).** Search your own name in a private window; private settings and tag control; ask friends to remove photos; delete old accounts; the gogo-stranger-boss test before posting; build the good part.
   > Screen: a private browser window, a search for a made-up name; an app's privacy settings page (a test account).
4. **Cyberbullying (3:40-5:20).** What it looks like; why it is often worse - follows you home, huge audience, screenshots, hiding behind fake names, joining in with one tap; what it can lead to.
   > Board: the troll-bridge doodle; five numbered reasons; highlighter on "follows you home".
5. **The bystander (5:20-6:30).** Don't like, laugh or share; report; message the person privately; tell an adult - Childline 116. The class-video story in one line: what the people in the group do decides how far it goes. The law is lesson 19.
   > Board: a group chat with laughing emojis being crossed out, one "Are you OK?" message instead.
6. **Sign-off.**
