# CAT 10 lesson 29: Threats online - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory10/threats.php`. Two videos: the malware, and the
tricks. Board in the CAT marker style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md);
yellow highlighter on any words on screen being talked about. No screen
recordings needed: the e-mails and SMSs are drawn on the board (a real inbox
would show real people's details). Everything below is in the lesson text.

## cat10-29.1 Malware - viruses, worms, Trojans and ransomware (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#ransomware` - the comment after `w29Ransom`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what malware is, tell a virus, a worm, a Trojan and spyware apart, say how malware gets in, and explain what ransomware does and why a backup must be unplugged.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SECURITY`, title "Not *every* bug is a virus"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. In 2025, South Africa's banks had two bank robberies. In the same year, their customers reported more than 110 000 crimes on banking apps and websites, and lost about R2.4 billion. Crime has moved online.
   > Board: a bank with a "2" on a sticky note; beside it a phone with "110 000" and "R2.4 billion". Yellow highlighter on the two numbers.
2. **Malware (0:40-1:20).** Malicious software: any program written to do harm. Most people call all of it viruses - but a virus is only one kind.
   > Board: "MAL + WARE" with the letters highlighted; a box labelled MALWARE with smaller boxes inside it.
3. **Virus (1:20-2:00).** Hides inside a program or file; spreads when the file is copied to another computer and opened - like a cold passed on by a handshake.
   > Board: the virus-worm-trojan drawing, the virus panel.
4. **Worm (2:00-2:40).** Copies itself across a network by itself - nobody clicks anything. It looks for computers with a security hole. Updates close the holes.
   > Board: the worm panel; the worm wriggles from computer to computer.
5. **Trojan (2:40-3:40).** The wooden horse: left outside Troy as a "gift", pulled inside, soldiers climb out at night. A Trojan pretends to be useful - a free game, a "cracked" program, an invoice - so you open it yourself.
   > Board: the horse at the gates; then a gift box marked FREE game! with a bug on the bow.
6. **Spyware, and how they get in (3:40-4:40).** Spyware secretly watches what you do and reports it. The ways in: unexpected attachments; downloads from anywhere but the official store; a found or borrowed flash drive; software that was never updated.
   > Board: four doors into a laptop, one per way in.
7. **Ransomware (4:40-6:20).** Step by step: it gets in (usually a Trojan); it scrambles your files and every drive it can reach; it demands payment in Bitcoin before the countdown ends. Don't pay: no guarantee, and it funds the next attack. A backup saves you - but only if it was unplugged.
   > Board: the ransomware-three-steps drawing, step by step; the plugged-in backup drive gets a padlock too, the unplugged one a big tick.
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-29.2 Spot the phish - phishing, spam and hoaxes (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#spam` - the comment after `g29SoftwareOrTrick`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what phishing, spam and a hoax are, check a message for the six warning signs, and say what to do with each.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SECURITY`, title "Don't take the *bait*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Why break into a computer when you can just ask its owner for the password?
   > Board: Clicky as a fish eyeing a hook baited with an envelope.
2. **Phishing (0:30-1:40).** A message that pretends to come from someone you trust, with a reason to act now and a link to a fake website. The "SARS refund" e-mail: click, enter your banking details, claim within 24 hours. SARS never sends links like that. It also comes by SMS, WhatsApp and phone call.
   > Board: a drawn e-mail from "SARS"; yellow highlighter on the link and "24 hours".
3. **The six signs (1:40-4:00).** One at a time, on Mr Botha's e-mail: 1. who sent it - security@mybank-alerts.co is not the bank; 2. is it rushing you - URGENT, 12 hours; 3. does it want a secret - a password, PIN, card details or OTP; 4. a link or attachment you didn't expect - point, don't click; 5. too good to be true; 6. general - "Dear Customer". If in doubt, open the app or type the address yourself.
   > Board: the mw29Phish e-mail drawn large; each sign circled in turn, numbered, with a yellow highlighter swipe.
4. **Spam (4:00-5:10).** Unwanted messages sent in bulk. It wastes time, uses data and storage, and often carries phishing and malware. Don't reply - that tells the spammer your address works. Mark it as spam, so the filter learns.
   > Board: an inbox overflowing with envelopes; a "Spam" folder swallowing them.
5. **Hoaxes (5:10-6:20).** A false warning people pass on because they believe it: "WhatsApp will start charging - forward to ten friends"; "delete this file, it's a virus" (it's part of Windows). They waste time, cause panic and make people ignore real warnings. Check before you forward.
   > Board: one message multiplying into ten, then a hundred; Clicky holding a "CHECK FIRST" sign.
6. **Sign-off.**
