# CAT 10 lesson 27: E-mail - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory10/email.php`. Two videos: your first e-mail, then
replying, attaching and netiquette. Board in the CAT marker style with
Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow highlighter on any words on screen
being talked about. Everything below is in the lesson text.

**Screens:** both videos show webmail in Edge in the video VM, signed in to
a **test account made for the course** (never Chris's or anyone's real
mailbox), sending only between two test accounts. Registering an account
needs a phone number, so the registering steps are drawn on the board, not
recorded. If no test account exists, draw the compose window on the board
(the identify-l29 drawing is a good start) - Chris's call.

## cat10-27.1 Your first e-mail, step by step (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#compose` - the comment after `t27Subject`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what e-communication and a communication device are; take an e-mail address apart; list the steps to register a web-based address and choose a professional one; compose and send an e-mail; name the mail folders.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · E-MAIL`, title "Would *you* read this e-mail?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A bursary application from cutiepie_xoxo2009@gmail.com, no subject, all capitals, a 40 MB photo of the CV. The student might be brilliant. Nobody will find out.
   > Board: the inbox line drawn with that address; Clicky wincing.
2. **E-communication (0:40-1:40).** Communicating with electronic devices over a network. A communication device sends and receives: a smartphone, laptop, tablet, smartwatch. The applications: e-mail, the web browser, IM, text/picture/video messaging, online meetings. E-mail works like the post office - it waits in the mailbox even if the phone is off.
   > Board: the five applications as icons around a phone; a little post office.
3. **The address (1:40-3:10).** user name @ domain name. m.naidoo@phumlani.co.za taken apart: m.naidoo (her mailbox), phumlani (the organisation), .co (the kind), .za (South Africa). No spaces, one @, a full domain; capitals don't matter.
   > Board: the email-address-parts drawing, then m.naidoo@phumlani.co.za written large with each part highlighted in turn.
4. **Your own web-based address (3:10-4:40).** School addresses close when you leave. Gmail or Outlook.com - any browser, yours for life. The six steps: Create account; name and birth date; choose the address; a strong password; a recovery number; read and agree. thabo.dlamini@gmail.com, not thabo_the_goat2010.
   > Board: the six steps numbered on a sticky note; the two addresses side by side, pink tick and cross.
5. **Compose and send (4:40-6:40).** Compose (Gmail) or New mail (Outlook); To; a short, clear subject; the body - greeting, message, sign-off with your name; attach; check; Send. E-mails are forever - angry? save a draft.
   > Screen: webmail in the VM - click Compose, type the test address in To, type the subject "CAT project - request for an extension", the body, then Send. Highlighter band on each box as it is filled.
6. **The folders (6:40-7:30).** Inbox (received), Sent, Drafts, Spam (Junk), Trash (Deleted items). Refresh to check now.
   > Screen: click each folder in the left-hand list; highlighter on each name.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-27.2 Reply, attach - and netiquette (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#netiquette` - the comment after `s27Netiquette`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** choose between Reply, Reply all and Forward; attach a file and keep it small (or send a link); apply the six netiquette rules; (IEB) use To, Cc and Bcc and say why Bcc protects a group.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · E-MAIL`, title "Reply all? *Think* first."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. One "Thanks!" to the whole school - then 400 people reply all to say stop replying all.
   > Board: the reply-all-storm doodle, envelopes multiplying.
2. **Reply, reply all, forward (0:30-2:00).** Reply - the sender only (RE:). Reply all - the sender and everyone on it; only when everyone needs it. Forward - on to someone new, with a note (FW:); check it is true and not private first.
   > Board: the email-reply-forward drawing, one arrow at a time.
   > Screen: in the test inbox, point at the Reply, Reply all and Forward buttons under a message; highlighter on each.
3. **Attachments (2:00-3:30).** A file sent with an e-mail - the paperclip. Steps: paperclip, choose the file, wait for the upload, check, send. Gmail allows 25 MB; big files are slow and cost the receiver data. Smaller: PDF, zip, a smaller photo - or a cloud link. Don't open attachments you didn't expect.
   > Screen: attach a small PDF with the paperclip; the file name appears under the message.
   > Board: a 90 MB video squeezed into an envelope, bursting; then a link instead.
4. **IEB: To, Cc and Bcc (3:30-4:40).** To - must act; Cc (carbon copy) - kept informed, addresses visible; Bcc (blind carbon copy) - addresses hidden, for groups who don't know each other. The address book; a mailing list.
   > Board: the email-to-cc-bcc drawing; the carbon-paper doodle for the name. Note: an "IEB" flag in the corner for this scene.
5. **Netiquette (4:40-6:40).** Text has no tone of voice. The six rules: spelling check, courteous, concise, no gossiping, reduce attachment size, no capitals. Then Thabo's e-mail fixed line by line: the whole class in To, no subject, HEY MAAM, capitals, SMS-speak, gossip about Sipho, a 45 MB video, "thx".
   > Board: the tone-missing drawing; then Thabo's e-mail written out, each mistake swiped with yellow highlighter and fixed beside it in marker. Sticky note: the six rules.
6. **Sign-off.**
