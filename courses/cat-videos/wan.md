## cat12-09.1 What a WAN is, and joining the branches (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after the written question `wWanLeasedVpn`, at the end of section
`#links` (the comment `// VIDEO cat12-09.1`), before `#carry`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** define a WAN (expanded and explained) and give
its purpose, role and uses; name the three parts every WAN has; say what a
WAN makes possible; compare a leased line with a VPN through the Internet
and choose one for a business.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "Two shops, *one* network"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:50).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Mr Botha opens a
   second bakery in Hatfield, 25 km away. The cake ordered in Centurion is
   unknown in Hatfield; the price changed in one shop is old in the other;
   he drives over with a flash drive at closing time.
   > Board: two little bakeries with a long road between them; Clicky
   > driving a bakkie with a flash drive on the back.
2. **What a WAN is (0:50-2:30).** Recap the LAN: one site, its own cables.
   WAN: wide area network - joins LANs far apart so they work as one.
   Definition, purpose, role, uses (the four-row table). When asked to
   define it: expand it and explain it.
   > Board: the two bakeries each drawn as a LAN in a dashed box; a line
   > joins them; yellow highlighter on "far apart" and "as one".
3. **Three things every WAN has (2:30-3:30).** A LAN at each site, a router
   at each site, long-distance links - rented, because nobody digs a
   trench to Hatfield.
   > Board: the proto-wan drawing built up site by site; the router boxes
   > highlighted.
4. **What it makes possible (3:30-4:40).** The bakery's before-and-after
   table: one order list, one price everywhere, sales arrive by themselves,
   calls between shops, one backup. Scaled up: one set of data, central
   systems, communication, control and reports, shared resources. The ATM
   that asks your own bank.
   > Board: the two-column table filling in; an ATM with an arrow to a bank
   > building far away.
5. **Leased line or VPN (4:40-7:20).** Leased line: a private road rented
   from a telecommunications company - always on, fixed speed, not shared,
   faults fixed fast, expensive, weeks to install. VPN: the armoured cash
   van on public roads - encrypted, uses the connections the shops have,
   cheap and quick, but only as good as each Internet connection; also lets
   one person work from home. The comparison table. The bakery's choice: a
   VPN.
   > Board: a private road with one bakery van; then a busy public road with
   > an armoured van among taxis; the comparison table, row by row, with
   > highlighter on Cost and Speed.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-09.2 The Internet, the biggest WAN (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the written question `wWanVsCompany`, at the end of section
`#internet` (the comment `// VIDEO cat12-09.2`), before `#compare`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what carries WAN data (fibre, microwave,
satellite) and choose one for a site; explain why the Internet is the
biggest WAN and how it differs from a company's WAN; describe South Africa's
undersea cables.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "The *biggest* network on Earth"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. You tap play on a
   video stored in Europe. How does it get to your phone in Soweto?
   > Board: a phone in Soweto, a server in Europe, a big question mark of
   > sea between them; Clicky in a little boat.
2. **What carries the data (0:40-2:40).** Fibre (fastest; slow and costly
   to lay), microwave (dishes on towers up to about 50 km apart; quick;
   rain), satellite (almost anywhere; slowest; delay; rain). A WAN mixes
   them: fibre between cities, microwave to the mine, satellite to the
   Karoo depot or the game lodge.
   > Board: the three-column table; a digger next to a fibre trench; two
   > towers with dishes; a farm with a dish pointing straight up.
3. **The Internet is a WAN (2:40-4:20).** It joins networks far apart over
   long-distance links owned by others, with routers passing the data on -
   across the whole world. Undersea cables. How it differs from a bank's
   WAN: nobody owns it, anyone may join through an ISP, it is public - so
   companies use a VPN across it. ARPANET's first message in 1969.
   > Board: a bank's private WAN drawn in one colour, the Internet as a
   > web of many colours; highlighter on "nobody owns it".
4. **South Africa's links to the world (4:20-6:00).** West coast (WACS and
   Equiano at Yzerfontein and Melkbosstrand), east coast (SEACOM at
   Mtunzini); fibre inland to Johannesburg's data centres; local copies of
   popular content. March 2024: a landslide under the sea, and the Internet
   slowed for days.
   > Board: the sa-cables map, each cable drawn in as it is named; a
   > landslide puff off West Africa.
5. **Sign-off.**
