# CAT 11 lesson 7: Software for users with disabilities - videos

Lesson file: `AIPascalCourse/content/cattheory11/assistive.php`. Two videos.
Everything said or shown here is also in the lesson text (anchors in the
tables below). Screen recordings in the VM (Windows 11, with sound recorded
from the VM for Narrator - keep its voice at normal speed, then show it
sped up for a few seconds).

## cat11-07.1 How a blind person uses a computer (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the reveal `r7AltText`, at the end of section `#seeing`
(before `#hearing`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what accessibility and assistive
technology are, explain how a screen reader is used and why alt text
matters, and choose between a screen reader, Magnifier, bigger text, high
contrast and text-to-speech.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "The screen is *dark*. He finished first."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Kagiso is blind.
   His screen is dark, his earphones are in, the laptop talks faster than
   Thabo can follow - and he finishes the exercise first. "You read it; I
   listen."
   > Board: two desks side by side; sound waves from Kagiso's laptop; Clicky
   > with a hand to its ear.
2. **Accessibility (0:40-1:40).** Accessibility: how easily everyone can
   use something. Assistive technology: hardware or software that helps.
   Four needs - seeing, hearing, moving, reading. Most of it is free and
   built in, and helps everyone at times (subtitles in a noisy room).
   > Board: the four-needs table drawn as four icons; yellow band on
   > "built in".
3. **A screen reader (1:40-3:30).** It reads everything aloud - text,
   buttons, menus. Narrator, NVDA (free), JAWS (paid), VoiceOver, TalkBack.
   Windows key + Ctrl + Enter; Tab from button to button; arrows through
   text; Enter to click; the voice sped up. It can drive a braille display
   (lesson 3).
   > Screen: VM - switch Narrator on, Tab through File Explorer's buttons
   > and a Word document with a heading, then switch it off. Key presses
   > shown as overlays.
4. **Alt text (3:30-4:30).** A screen reader cannot see a picture - it reads
   the alt text. Good ("Thabo's group holding the trophy...") and useless
   ("IMG_2041.jpg"). The alt attribute of img; View Alt Text in Word.
   > Screen: VM - Narrator reading a web page picture with and without alt
   > text (a local test page); Word's right-click, View Alt Text.
5. **Seeing a little (4:30-6:00).** Gogo can see, but not small text.
   Magnifier (Windows key + plus, Windows key + Esc); Text size; contrast
   themes; colour filters; a bigger pointer. Text-to-speech: Review, Read
   Aloud; Edge's Read aloud - for dyslexia too.
   > Screen: VM - Magnifier on and off; a contrast theme on and off; Read
   > Aloud in Word.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-07.2 The accessibility settings, step by step (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the dragwords `dw7Settings`, at the end of section
`#settings` (before the scenario).
**The pupil can afterwards:** give the features for users who cannot hear
well and who find typing or a mouse hard, find them in Settings,
Accessibility, and switch the main ones on with their shortcuts.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "It's *already* on your computer"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Sipho broke both
   wrists playing rugby. Six weeks, no typing. What now?
   > Board: Sipho with two casts; Clicky offering a microphone.
2. **Hearing (0:30-1:40).** Captions (CC); live captions (Windows key + Ctrl
   + L, and in Teams); flash the screen; mono audio; video calls for sign
   language.
   > Screen: VM - a video playing with live captions switched on.
3. **Speaking instead of typing (1:40-3:00).** Voice recognition (Grade
   10): dictation - Windows key + H, "full stop", "new line"; commands -
   Voice access: "Open Word", "Click File". Best in a quiet room with a good
   microphone.
   > Screen: VM - voice typing into Word.
4. **The on-screen keyboard (3:00-3:40).** Windows key + Ctrl + O; pressed
   with a mouse, a touch, a head pointer or the eyes (Eye control); word
   suggestions.
   > Screen: VM - the on-screen keyboard typing a word with clicks.
5. **Keyboard and mouse settings (3:40-5:20).** Sticky Keys (Shift five
   times - Ctrl, let go, C); Filter Keys (shaking hands); Toggle Keys; Mouse
   Keys (8 up, 2 down, 5 clicks); pointer speed, double-click speed, primary
   button, pointer size.
   > Board: a hand pressing Ctrl, lifting, pressing C - with Sticky Keys
   > holding Ctrl down for it. Screen: VM - Bluetooth & devices, Mouse.
6. **Where it all lives (5:20-6:40).** Settings, Accessibility (Windows 10:
   Ease of Access) - Vision, Hearing, Interaction. The worked example:
   bigger text for Gogo - Windows key + I, Accessibility, Text size, slider,
   Apply. Phones: Settings, Accessibility.
   > Screen: VM - exactly these steps; yellow highlighter on each group
   > heading. Board: the Learn / Memorise shortcut table.
7. **Sign-off.**
