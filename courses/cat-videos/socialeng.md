# CAT 11 lesson 20: Social engineering, phishing and pharming - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory11/socialeng.php`. Two videos: the tricks played on
people, and phishing's family with pharming and the SIM swap. Board in the
CAT marker style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow highlighter on
any words on screen being talked about. No screen recordings: the SMSs,
WhatsApp messages and websites are drawn on the board (a real inbox would
show real people's details). Everything below is in the lesson text.

## cat11-20.1 Hacking people - social engineering and its tricks (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#tricks` - the comment after `w20Pretext`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what social engineering is and why it works, and recognise shoulder surfing, dumpster diving, pretexting and baiting.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SECURITY`, title "They hacked the *person*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:50).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Sunday evening. Thabo gets a WhatsApp from his sister's number: "Sorry, I sent a six-digit code to your number by mistake - can you send it to me?" An SMS with a code arrives at the same moment. Nobody hacked his phone. Nobody needs to.
   > Board: Thabo's phone with the WhatsApp bubble and the SMS code; yellow highlighter on "send it to me".
2. **What social engineering is (0:50-1:50).** Tricking people into giving away information, money or access - or into doing something unsafe - instead of breaking into the computer. It attacks the one part of a system that can't be updated: the person. A password that takes a hundred years to crack is safe - until someone phones its owner and asks.
   > Board: a fortress of a computer with walls and locks; Clicky walking round the side and simply knocking on the person's door.
3. **Why it works (1:50-3:00).** Six levers: trust, authority, fear and urgency, greed, curiosity, helpfulness - one example each. When a message makes you feel rushed, scared or excited, stop and check.
   > Board: six sticky notes, one per lever, each with its example ("Your account closes in 24 hours", "You have won R50 000", "SALARIES"); highlighter on each lever word.
4. **Shoulder surfing and dumpster diving (3:00-4:30).** Watching someone type a PIN - at an ATM, in a taxi, with a hidden camera. Searching rubbish - statements, customer lists, a password on a sticky note, an old phone never wiped. Protect: cover the keypad; fingerprint in public; shred paper; factory-reset a phone before selling it - deleted files can be brought back.
   > Board: the compcrime-shoulder drawing; then a dustbin with papers spilling out, one highlighted "Password: ..."
5. **Pretexting and baiting (4:30-6:20).** Pretending to be someone you trust, with a story: "IT support" who needs your password tonight; the man in overalls who asks you to hold the door; "the principal" who needs a payment today. Check through a second channel - a number you already have. Real IT staff never ask for your password. Baiting: the flash drive labelled SALARIES in the car park, or a free copy of an expensive game. Never plug in a drive you found - hand it in.
   > Board: a phone call bubble "Hi, it's IT..." with a big red X over "password"; a flash drive on tarmac with a fishing hook through its label.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-20.2 Phishing, pharming and the SIM swap (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#identity` - the comment after `q20NoSignal`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** name smishing, vishing and spear phishing, explain the difference between phishing and pharming, describe a SIM swap step by step, and say what to do when the phone suddenly loses signal.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SECURITY`, title "Phishing or *pharming*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Grade 10 met phishing: a fake message that rushes you to a fake website. It has a whole family now - and one relative that doesn't even need you to click.
   > Board: a fishing rod with an envelope as bait; Clicky as a fish, suspicious.
2. **The family (0:30-2:30).** Smishing - by SMS ("Your parcel is waiting - pay the delivery fee"). Vishing - by voice, a call from "the bank's fraud department" that knows your name and ID number. Spear phishing - aimed at one person with their details ("Hi Lerato, here are the photos from Thandi's braai"). QR code phishing - a fake sticker on a parking machine. WhatsApp scams - "Hi Mum, new number".
   > Board: five little drawings in a row - an SMS bubble, a phone receiver, a spear, a QR code with a sticker peeling, a WhatsApp bubble - each named and highlighted.
3. **The fake bank official (2:30-3:40).** Step by step: a call about suspicious payments; "install AnyDesk to secure your account"; a real, legal app that lets him see and control your phone; "open your banking app"; he watches and moves the money. A bank never asks you to install an app, read out an OTP or move money to a "safe account".
   > Board: five numbered sticky notes; the last one red; then the three NEVERs in capitals.
4. **The SMS to check (3:40-4:30).** The parcel SMS from the lesson: FINAL NOTICE, within 24 hours, a strange link, and a request for card number, expiry date and the three digits on the back. He ordered nothing.
   > Board: the mw20Sms text drawn large; each of the four signs circled and highlighted in turn.
5. **Pharming (4:30-6:00).** You type the bank's correct address yourself - and still land on the fake site, because the information that leads the address to the website was changed. Like a phone book with the bank's number changed. Phishing needs you to click; pharming does not. Never click past a browser warning on a banking site.
   > Board: the phishing-vs-pharming drawing, the two roads to one fake site; Clicky typing the address correctly and still arriving at the fake door.
6. **Identity theft and the SIM swap (6:00-7:40).** Your details come from phishing, oversharing, fake competitions, data leaks, the dustbin. With them, a criminal pretends to be you. A SIM swap, step by step: details collected; a new SIM for your number; your phone shows "No service"; your OTPs go to the criminal; your money follows. The sign: no signal when everyone around you has it - phone your network and bank at once from another phone.
   > Board: the compcrime-simswap drawing, panel by panel; highlighter on "No service".
7. **Sign-off.**
