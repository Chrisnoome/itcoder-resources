## cat10-32.1 Set up your desk (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#neck` - the comment `// VIDEO cat10-32.1` after `w32Laptop` (the video covers `#ergonomics` to `#neck`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** set up a workstation in the right order, and name RSI, eye strain and text neck with how to prevent each.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HEALTH`, title "Your desk is *hurting* you"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo played games
   for three hours on Saturday. On Sunday his thumbs ached, his neck was stiff
   and his eyes felt full of sand - and he had lifted nothing heavier than a
   controller.
   > Board: Thabo hunched over a controller; three pain stars.
2. **Ergonomics (0:40-1:20).** Designing and arranging things to fit the
   body. A workstation is the whole place you work: desk, chair, screen,
   keyboard, mouse, light.
   > Board: the word, with ergon + nomos underneath. Highlighter on "fit the body".
3. **Five steps, chair first (1:20-3:40).** Chair: feet flat, thighs level,
   back supported. Screen: arm's length (50-70 cm), top at or just below eye
   level. Keyboard and mouse: close, elbow height, wrists straight. Light from
   the side, no glare. Move every 30-60 minutes.
   > Board: the ergonomic-desk drawing built up step by step, each step numbered.
   > Screen: optional - in the VM's webcam view, a laptop raised on books with a separate keyboard (only if a real setup is filmed).
4. **Laptops (3:40-4:20).** Screen joined to the keyboard: one of them is
   always wrong. Raise the laptop, use a separate keyboard and mouse.
5. **RSI (4:20-5:10).** Repetitive strain injury: pain from the same small
   movements for hours. Breaks, stretching, straight wrists, a light touch,
   shortcuts. Carpal tunnel syndrome: a nerve squeezed in the wrist - tingling
   thumb and fingers.
6. **Eye strain and 20-20-20 (5:10-6:00).** Every 20 minutes, look 6 metres
   away, for 20 seconds. Blink; bigger text; no glare. Blue light does not
   damage eyes.
   > Board: the twenty-rule drawing; three sticky notes "20 min / 6 m / 20 s".
7. **Text neck and sleep (6:00-6:40).** A 5 kg head bent over a phone strains
   the neck like 27 kg. Lift the phone. Screens away 30-60 minutes before bed.
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-32.2 Where old phones go (about 5 min)

**Goes:** after section `#ewaste` (the comment `// VIDEO cat10-32.2`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain the three ways computers harm the planet, give green-use habits including vampire power, and put reduce, reuse and recycle in order.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · GREEN`, title "Your old phone is a *gold mine*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Look in any kitchen
   drawer: three old phones. What happens to them?
   > Board: the green-drawer drawing; Clicky clinging to an old phone.
2. **Three places of harm (0:30-1:40).** Being made (mines, oil for plastic,
   transport - most of a phone's harm is done before you switch it on), being
   used (electricity, mostly from coal here), being thrown away.
   > Board: three boxes left to right with arrows.
3. **Green use (1:40-2:40).** Power settings, switch off at the wall, lower
   brightness, print less, buy efficient. Vampire power: the decoder on
   standby - 10 W, 20 hours a day, about R290 a year.
   > Board: the vampire-plug drawing; the sum worked in three lines.
4. **E-waste (2:40-3:40).** About 62 million tonnes in 2022, a fifth recycled.
   Poisons leak, batteries catch fire, gold and copper lost, it never rots.
   > Board: a rubbish dump; highlighter on "lead, mercury, cadmium".
5. **Reduce, reuse, recycle (3:40-4:40).** Keep it longer; pass it on or
   repurpose it; recycle at a drop-off point. Wipe your data first.
   > Board: the ewaste-life drawing.
6. **Sign-off.**
