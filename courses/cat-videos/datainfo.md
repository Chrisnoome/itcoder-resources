## cat10-03.1 Data or information? (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#school` - the comment after `w3SchoolOwn`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** define data and information, tell them apart, follow a number from data to information, and give examples from a school.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · BASICS`, title "73 *what*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo's CAT test comes back with one number at the top, in red: 73. Is that good? You can't tell. 73 what? Out of what?
   > Board: a test paper with a big red 73; Clicky staring at it with a "?" over his head.
2. **Data (0:40-1:40).** Data is raw facts and figures that have not been processed yet. On its own it means nothing. 73. 12 °C. Sipho. The number under a barcode. Every sale the bakery made today. Data is collected - typed, scanned, photographed, measured. It is the input.
   > Board: the examples scattered on sticky notes; "collected" with yellow highlighter.
3. **Information (1:40-2:20).** Information is data that has been processed, so that it means something to someone - organised, calculated, given context. It is the output. "The class average for the CAT test was 61%."
   > Board: an arrow from the scattered notes through a box marked "processing" to one neat sentence.
4. **Following the 73 (2:20-3:30).** Step 1: 73 - data. Step 2: Thabo got 73 for his CAT test - context, but out of what? Step 3: 73 out of 100 is 73% - processed. That is information. And then Ms Naidoo uses that 73% to work out his term mark - so it is data again.
   > Board: the info-73-steps drawing built step by step; yellow highlighter moves up the steps.
5. **Telling them apart (3:30-4:20).** Data is collected; information is produced. You can have data without information, but never information without data. Information from one process is often data for the next.
   > Board: the Learn / Memorise table, row by row.
6. **At school (4:20-5:30).** The register becomes each pupil's attendance percentage, for the principal and parents. One test's marks become the class average, for Ms Naidoo. The term's marks become the report. Fees paid become a list of who still owes. Why each matters: data, because everything starts with it - one wrong tick and every percentage is wrong; information, because people decide with it - nobody can see a pattern in 400 marks.
   > Board: three rows of the school table drawn as arrows, data on the left, information on the right.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-03.2 From information to a decision (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#useful` - the comment after the loyalty-card try-it.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain knowledge, follow information through knowledge to a decision, and check information for the four things that make it useful.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · BASICS`, title "From 73% to a *plan*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Information tells you what happened. Knowledge tells you what it means for you - so that you can do something about it.
   > Board: Clicky holding "73%" in one hand and a question mark in the other.
2. **Knowledge (0:30-1:00).** Knowledge is information put together with rules you have learnt and experience.
   > Board: "information + rules + experience = knowledge" on a sticky note.
3. **Thabo's bursary (1:00-2:10).** Information: Thabo got 73% for CAT. A rule he knows: his bursary needs 80%. Knowledge: he is 7 marks short. Decision: he asks Ms Naidoo for extra work and studies 30 minutes a day. Data becomes information, information becomes knowledge, and knowledge leads to a decision.
   > Board: the dikw-steps drawing climbed step by step (the top step called "decision" in the narration).
4. **Mr Botha's bread (2:10-3:10).** The till's report: brown bread sold out by 10:00 every day. From experience: customers who find no bread go next door - and buy their milk there too. Knowledge: he is losing customers, not only bread sales. Decision: bake 20 more loaves a day. The bigger the decision, the more good information you need.
   > Board: a bakery counter with an empty bread shelf; a customer walking to the shop next door.
5. **Four checks (3:10-4:50).** Good information is accurate - 73 typed in as 37 is not. Up to date and on time - a rain warning after the braai is useless. Complete - a trip letter with no time. Relevant - the rugby scores when you asked which taxi to take.
   > Board: four sticky notes, each with its word and its bad example; yellow highlighter on each word.
6. **When it fails (4:50-5:40).** A wrong dose in a prescription. An ambulance sent to an old address. A fake news story shared before anyone checks. And too much information is a problem too: a 200-page report where nobody can find the one number they need.
   > Board: a quick sketch for each; then a towering pile of paper with Clicky buried under it.
7. **Sign-off.**
