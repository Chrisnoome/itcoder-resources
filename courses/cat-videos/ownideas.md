## cat10-33.1 Copyright, piracy and plagiarism (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#plagiarism` (the comment `// VIDEO cat10-33.1`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain intellectual property and copyright, say why piracy is a problem, and tell plagiarism from breaking copyright.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOCIAL`, title "Found it on Google = *mine*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:45).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Lerato drew a
   smiling loaf for Botha's Bakery. A month later it was on another bakery in
   Pretoria East. Nobody asked her. She cannot carry it home - but it is hers.
   > Board: the smiling loaf twice, two shop signs.
2. **Intellectual property and copyright (0:45-2:00).** Creations of the mind
   belong to whoever made them. Copyright: the creator decides who may copy,
   share, sell or change it. Automatic; in South Africa a book stays protected
   until 50 years after the author dies; only the owner gives permission.
   > Board: three numbered sticky notes. Highlighter on "automatic".
3. **Found it on Google (2:00-2:40).** A search engine only shows where a
   picture is. Check the licence; always say whose it is.
4. **Piracy (2:40-4:00).** What it looks like (cracked games, one licence on
   five laptops, downloaded films). Why it matters: makers lose income, jobs
   lost, against the law, malware, no updates.
   > Board: the pirate-disc drawing; five numbered reasons.
5. **Licences in one breath (4:00-4:40).** You buy a licence, not the
   program. Proprietary, freeware, open source, Creative Commons - lesson 16
   has the rest.
6. **Plagiarism vs copyright (4:40-6:20).** Plagiarism is about honesty;
   copyright is about the law. Shakespeare copied as your own: plagiarism, no
   copyright broken. A photocopied textbook with the author's name: copyright
   broken, no plagiarism. The honest way, in four steps.
   > Board: two circles, "honesty" and "law", with the two examples placed in them.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-33.2 Your digital footprint (about 5 min)

**Goes:** after section `#citizenship` (the comment `// VIDEO cat10-33.2`, inside the CAPS section).
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain digital citizenship, tell an active from a passive footprint, and give steps to manage a footprint.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · CAPS`, title "Footprints that *never* wash off"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Walk on a wet beach
   and you leave footprints. Use the Internet and you leave data.
   > Board: the digital-footprints drawing.
2. **Digital citizenship (0:30-1:40).** A good citizen online: respect,
   safety and privacy, think before you share, honesty, balance.
3. **Active and passive (1:40-3:00).** Active: posts, photos, comments, likes,
   reviews. Passive: where your phone has been, what you searched, what you
   watched.
   > Board: two columns; Clicky sorts items into them. Highlighter on "on purpose".
4. **Why it matters (3:00-3:40).** Close to permanent - screenshots keep
   deleted posts. Bursary funders and employers look people up.
5. **Five steps (3:40-4:40).** Think before you post; private accounts;
   location sharing off; search your own name and close old accounts; build a
   good footprint.
6. **Sign-off.**
