## cat11-26.1 Checking your questions (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#formats` (the comment `// VIDEO cat11-26.1`, after `m26Shapes`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** sort research questions into the four kinds and levels, check a set for variety, and choose the right shape of closed question for what they want to work out.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAT`, title "Is your question *any good*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo sent ten
   questions to his class WhatsApp group. 38 answers in an hour - and "92%
   take a taxi". Ms Naidoo was not impressed: what does question 4 mean?
   > Board: a phone with a WhatsApp group, Clicky watching answers pour in.
2. **Quality control (0:40-1:30).** A builder checks every brick. You check
   every question: its type, its level, and the variety of the set.
   > Board: a wall of question-bricks; highlighter on "type", "level", "variety".
3. **Four kinds, four levels (1:30-3:30).** Level 1 explicit-fact (What?
   When? Where? Who? How many?), level 2 examine and explore (Why? How? - the
   IEB says explore and query), level 3 adjust, alter or predict (If? What
   if?), level 4 judgement (What would be best? What do you recommend?). One
   of Thabo's transport questions for each.
   > Board: four steps of a staircase, a question on each; highlighter on the question words.
4. **Variety and the five checks (3:30-4:45).** All four kinds; open and
   closed; more than one kind of source. Then each question: does it help
   answer the main question? kind and level? can I find the answer? clear? a
   repeat? The worked example: the car-colour question is dropped. Group the
   questions under headings (How pupils travel, Cost, Safety, What should
   change).
   > Board: Thabo's four-row table, the car-colour row crossed out.
5. **The shapes of a closed question (4:45-6:30).** Yes/no; one choice
   (option buttons, drop-down list); tick all that apply (tick boxes); rating
   scale (balanced, labelled, same direction - the Likert scale); a number.
   Choose the shape by what you want to work out: an average needs a number,
   not "Short / Medium / Long".
   > Screen: in the VM, a new Microsoft Form; add one question of each shape (Choice, Choice with multiple answers, Likert/Rating, Text with number restriction) - no typing of real data.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-26.2 The right questions to the right people (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#sampling` (the comment `// VIDEO cat11-26.2`, after `w26WhatsApp`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** spot leading, double, assuming and vague questions and fix them, put a questionnaire together in a sensible order, plan an interview, and explain a random sample.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAT`, title "Ask the *right* people"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A bad question
   still gets answers. That is the danger.
2. **The traps (0:30-2:30).** Leading ("Most pupils say taxis are
   dangerous. Do you agree?"), double ("cheap and safe"), assuming ("How much
   does your taxi cost?" - a third walk), vague ("often"), choices with gaps
   or overlaps (0-10 / 10-20), private questions. A fix for each, from the
   lesson.
   > Board: each bad question written, Clicky stamping it; the fix written under it; highlighter on the trap words.
3. **Building the questionnaire (2:30-4:00).** The eight steps: introduction;
   anonymous and voluntary; easy closed questions first; headings; send
   people only to the questions that fit them; open and private questions
   near the end; thank you; a pilot test on three to five people.
   > Board: a form drawn top to bottom, each part labelled as it is added.
4. **Planning an interview (4:00-5:30).** Mr Mokoena, the taxi association.
   Before: right person, ask politely, six to ten open questions, follow-ups.
   During: say who you are, ask before recording, listen, check facts ("R18
   a trip from Kliptown - is that right?"), thank him. After: write up the
   same day, send notes to check, reference it.
   > Board: three columns, Before / During / After.
5. **Who do you ask? (5:30-7:30).** The soup pot: population (all 600 pupils)
   and sample (the spoonful). A random sample: full list, 120 is 1 in 5,
   start by chance, every 5th name. A convenience sample (the WhatsApp group)
   is biased. More answers are more trustworthy; response rate 90 of 120 =
   75%.
   > Board: a pot of soup and a spoon; then a class list with every 5th name highlighted.
6. **Sign-off.**
