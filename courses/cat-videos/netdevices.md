## cat10-23.1 Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and NFC (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#nfc` - the comment after `t23Nfc`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a communication medium is, sort media into wired and wireless, and say what Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and NFC each are, how far each reaches and what each is for.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "Three kinds of *wireless*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo Dlamini has a new tablet, a Bluetooth speaker and a tap-to-pay bank card. "Three kinds of wireless," says Lerato, "and they don't work the same way."
   > Board: the three gadgets in a row; Clicky with a "?" over each.
2. **The medium (0:40-1:50).** Whatever carries the data is the communication medium - not the news media. Wired: a network cable carries electricity along copper, up to about 100 m; a fibre-optic cable carries flashes of light along glass, for kilometres. Wireless: radio waves through the air.
   > Board: the nh-cables drawing, a cable at a time; then wavy radio lines. Yellow highlighter on "medium".
3. **Wi-Fi (1:50-3:20).** Wireless networking by radio to a router or access point, over tens of metres. For joining laptops, phones, TVs and printers without cables. Thabo's phone joins the house Wi-Fi when he walks in. A name and a password - without one, the neighbours can join. Walls weaken it; a free public Wi-Fi is a hotspot.
   > Board: a house with Wi-Fi rings from the router, fading behind a wall; a padlock on the router.
4. **Bluetooth (3:20-4:40).** Short-range radio, about 10 m, very little battery. Accessories: earphones, a mouse, a smartwatch, the car radio. Gogo's tablet plays through her speaker. It does not give you the Internet. A computer without it can get a USB dongle.
   > Board: a 10 m dashed circle round the tablet and the speaker. Pink highlighter on "no Internet".
5. **NFC (4:40-6:00).** Radio over a few centimetres - the devices almost touch. Tap to pay, tap to open a gate, tap a phone on a speaker to start the connection. Why so short? Safer: a thief would have to hold a reader almost against your card, and you can't pay by accident from across the room.
   > Board: a card hovering over a card machine, "4 cm" marked; Clicky holding a ruler.
6. **The three in one picture (6:00-6:40).** NFC: centimetres, tap to pay. Bluetooth: about 10 m, earphones. Wi-Fi: tens of metres, the home or school network.
   > Board: three nested rings round one phone, labelled.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-23.2 Pairing a Bluetooth device (about 4 min)

**Goes:** after section `#pairing` - the comment after `q23Paired`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what pairing is and why it is done, pair a device step by step, and pair safely.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "Pair it in *six* steps"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. The first time you use a Bluetooth device, you have to introduce it to your phone. That is pairing.
   > Board: a tablet and a speaker shaking hands; Clicky as the referee.
2. **What and why (0:30-1:10).** Connecting two Bluetooth devices for the first time, so that they recognise each other. Afterwards they connect by themselves - and nobody else's phone can take over your speaker.
   > Board: sticky note "PAIRING = first-time introduction".
3. **Step by step (1:10-3:00).** Lerato pairs Gogo's speaker: 1. hold the speaker's Bluetooth button until the light flashes - pairing mode; 2. on the tablet, Settings, then Bluetooth, switch it on; 3. wait for the speaker's name in the list; 4. tap it; 5. if a code shows on both, check it matches, tap Pair; 6. "Connected" - play some music.
   > Board: the tablet's screens drawn one per step, with the step number in a circle; yellow highlighter on each thing to tap.
4. **Next time (3:00-3:20).** Gogo just switches the speaker on; the tablet connects by itself.
   > Board: the speaker's light goes solid; music notes.
5. **Pair safely (3:20-3:50).** Pair only with devices you know; say no to a request from a device you don't recognise; unpair ("forget") devices before you give away or sell a phone or earphones.
   > Board: a pop-up "Pair with UNKNOWN-DEVICE?" with a big cross on Pair.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-23.3 The boxes - modem, router, switch and access point (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#boxes` - the comment after the try-it.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a NIC, a modem, a router, a switch and a wireless access point each do, and why a home router is several devices in one.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "Five *boxes*, five jobs"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Five pieces of hardware turn a pile of computers into a network. One at a time.
   > Board: a pile of computers; Clicky with a toolbox.
2. **NIC (0:30-1:20).** In every device: the part that connects it to a network. Wired - a network port; wireless - a small radio for Wi-Fi. Usually built in.
   > Board: a laptop opened up; a tiny card with a port and an antenna. Yellow highlighter on "every device".
3. **Modem (1:20-2:10).** Connects to the line the Internet comes in on - a telephone line, or the mobile network with a SIM. It changes data into a signal the line can carry, and back. With fibre, the fibre box on the wall does this.
   > Board: a box at the wall socket; data squares turning into wavy lines.
4. **Router (2:10-3:00).** Joins one network to another - your HAN or LAN to the Internet - and sends each piece of data the right way.
   > Board: a box with two doors: one to the house, one to a globe.
5. **Switch (3:00-3:50).** A row of network ports; every computer's cable plugs in. Like a post office sorting letters, it sends each message only to the computer it is meant for.
   > Board: a switch with six cables; one envelope travels to one computer only.
6. **Access point (3:50-4:30).** Sends out the Wi-Fi so laptops, phones and tablets join without a cable. A school has one in each area.
   > Board: Wi-Fi rings from a box on a classroom ceiling.
7. **One box at home (4:30-5:30).** The home "router" is several in one: modem (or a port for the fibre box), router, a small switch, an access point - and a firewall that blocks data nobody asked for.
   > Board: the swiss-router joke - a Swiss Army knife whose blades are labelled. Pink highlighter on "several in one".
8. **Sign-off.**
