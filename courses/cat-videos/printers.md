## cat10-10.1 Inkjet, laser or 3D - how each one prints (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#printing3d` - the comment after `w10ThreeD`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain how an inkjet, a laser and a 3D printer put their output down, and give an advantage and a disadvantage of each.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Ink, *powder* or plastic?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Three printers. One sprays liquid. One melts powder. One does not print on paper at all. Today: how each one works, and what each is good at.
   > Board: three boxes drawn side by side, each with a question mark; Clicky lifting the lid of the first.
2. **Inkjet (0:40-2:20).** An inkjet sprays tiny drops of liquid ink through nozzles. For colour it mixes four inks: cyan, magenta, yellow and black - CMYK. Cheap to buy, from about R1 200; the best colour photos; small and quiet. But the ink is expensive, it is slow for long documents, the ink dries in the nozzles if the printer stands unused, and wet ink smudges. An ink tank printer is an inkjet with refillable tanks: about R3 500 to buy, very cheap to run.
   > Board: drops flying from a nozzle onto paper; four coloured drops labelled C, M, Y, K (yellow highlighter on each letter). A two-column good/bad sticky note.
3. **Laser (2:20-4:10).** No liquid ink: a powder called toner. Step 1, a laser draws the page onto a turning drum. Step 2, the toner sticks only where the laser touched. Step 3, the drum rolls the toner onto the paper. Step 4, hot rollers melt it in - that is why the page is warm. Fast, cheap per page, the sharpest text, and toner does not dry out. But it costs more to buy - about R2 500 for black and white, R5 000 for colour - and photos look worse.
   > Board: the lesson's laser-printer-drum drawing built up step by step, numbered 1-4; Clicky holding his hands over the warm page.
4. **3D printer (4:10-5:50).** A 3D printer makes a solid object. Design a 3D model on the computer; software cuts it into thin layers; the printer lays melted plastic one layer at a time until the object is complete; then you clean it up. Artificial hands, models of new products, a stove knob nobody sells any more, school projects, even concrete house walls. One object exactly to a design, with no factory - but slow, hours per object, and prints can fail.
   > Board: the printing-3d-layers drawing, one layer at a time; a little prosthetic hand appears at the end. Yellow highlighter on "layer by layer".
5. **Recap (5:50-6:30).** Inkjet: liquid ink, photos. Laser: toner and heat, lots of text, fast. 3D: plastic layers, objects.
   > Board: the three boxes from the hook, now filled in.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-10.2 What a printer really costs (about 5 min)

**Goes:** after section `#costs` - the comment after `w10CostToRun`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain purchase price and running cost, work out cost per page, and choose a printer for a job.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "The *cheap* printer is the expensive one"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Mr Botha's printer cost R900. Last year he spent R7 200 on ink - eight times the printer.
   > Board: a small printer with a R900 price tag; a pile of cartridges grows next to it with a R7 200 tag. Clicky clinging to a rand sign.
2. **Two prices (0:40-1:30).** The purchase price is paid once. The running cost - ink or toner, and paper - is paid again and again. Cheap printers, expensive ink: that is where the money is made.
   > Board: two price tags, "once" (pink) and "again and again" (yellow).
3. **Cost per page (1:30-3:00).** Three steps: the price of the cartridge, the pages it prints (the page yield, on the box), divide. Inkjet: R300 ÷ 200 = R1.50. Laser: R1 200 ÷ 1 600 = 75c. Ink tank: R150 ÷ 4 500 = about 3c.
   > Board: the lesson's three-row table written out, each division done on a sticky note.
4. **Mr Botha's year (3:00-4:00).** 400 pages a month is 4 800 a year. Inkjet: 4 800 x R1.50 = R7 200. Laser: 4 800 x 75c = R3 600. The laser costs R1 300 more to buy and saves R3 600 in the first year.
   > Board: the two sums side by side; pink highlighter on the saving.
5. **Choosing (4:00-4:50).** What will I print, how much, how often, how fast, and what can I spend - to buy and to run? Text and lots of it: laser. Photos: inkjet or ink tank. Objects: 3D. And before any of it: do I need to print at all?
   > Board: the five questions on a sticky note; the printer-paperless joke drawn in the corner.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-10.3 The print queue and the default printer (CAPS, about 4 min)

**Goes:** after the CAPS section `#printQueue` - the comment after it.
**The pupil can afterwards:** print with Ctrl + P, cancel a stuck document in the print queue, and change the default printer in Windows 11.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Printer *stuck*? Clear the queue"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thirty pupils, one printer, and one document stuck at the top of the queue. Nobody's work comes out until it goes.
   > Board: a queue of paper sheets waiting in line at a printer, the first one tangled; Clicky tapping his foot.
2. **Printing (0:30-1:20).** Ctrl + P; choose the printer; copies; pages, such as 2-4; colour or black, one side or both; check the preview; Print.
   > Screen: in the VM, a short Word document; Ctrl + P; point at each setting in turn; stop at the preview. (Do not print.)
3. **The print queue (1:20-2:40).** The print queue is the list of documents waiting to print, in order. To cancel a stuck one: Settings (Windows key + I), Bluetooth & devices, Printers & scanners, the printer, Open print queue, right-click the document, Cancel, Yes.
   > Screen: in the VM with a paused printer (so jobs wait), send two documents; walk the path; cancel the first.
4. **The default printer (2:40-3:40).** The printer the computer uses automatically. Settings, Bluetooth & devices, Printers & scanners; turn off "Let Windows manage my default printer"; click the printer; Set as default.
   > Screen: the same Settings page; the toggle turned off (yellow highlighter on its words); Set as default clicked. Needs two printers installed in the VM - Microsoft Print to PDF and a second dummy printer will do.
5. **Sign-off.**
