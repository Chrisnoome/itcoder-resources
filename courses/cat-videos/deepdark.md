## cat12-14.1 Deep web, dark web - what is really down there (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the IEB section "The dark web" - the comment after `w14DeepDark`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** tell the surface web, the deep web and the dark web apart, say why most of the Web is not indexed, explain how Tor hides a user, and give who uses the dark web for good and for crime.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · THE WEB`, title "Deep web is *not* dark web"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Ask a search engine for Lerato's bank balance, Thabo's WhatsApp messages or his Term 2 CAT mark. Nothing. All of it is online - so where is it?
   > Board: a search box with "Thabo's CAT mark" typed in, and an empty results page; Clicky shrugging.
2. **Surface and deep (0:40-2:00).** A crawler can only index what it can reach. The surface web is every indexed page; the deep web is everything else - and you use it every day.
   > Board: the deepweb-iceberg drawing, built from the top down; yellow highlighter on "indexed" and "not indexed".
3. **What keeps a page in the deep web (2:00-3:10).** A login (e-mail, banking, the school portal, SARS eFiling); a paywall; a page made only when you ask (flights on a date); a choice (an intranet, "do not index me"). Hidden mainly for privacy and security.
   > Board: four small drawings in a row - a padlock, a coin slot, a form, a closed door; highlighter on each word as it is said.
4. **Good and bad (3:10-4:00).** Privacy, quality research collections, paid services; against that, hard to find, paywalls widen the digital divide, no outside check.
   > Board: a two-column good/bad table, filled in line by line.
5. **The dark web (4:00-5:30).** A tiny part, hidden on purpose; special software (Tor); .onion addresses; nobody knows who runs or visits a site. How Tor hides you: three volunteer computers, three layers of encryption, each peels one off; the website sees only the last computer. Slow; legal - what you do with it may not be. A VPN is not the dark web.
   > Board: the deepweb-onion drawing, the three layers peeled one by one; a big "deep web ≠ dark web" in marker, highlighter on "≠".
6. **Good and crime (5:30-6:30).** For good: readers where news is blocked (the BBC's .onion site), whistleblowers, people escaping an abuser. For crime: illegal markets, stolen data, ransomware leak sites, malware for hire. The police work there too. Why not to go there: scams, malware, illegal content, the law, no protection.
   > Board: a sorting board with two columns, "for good" and "for crime"; then the deepweb-onion-tears doodle.
7. **Sign-off.**
