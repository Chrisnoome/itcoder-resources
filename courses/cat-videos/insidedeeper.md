## cat11-01.1 How the parts work together (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#together` - the comment before `o1OpenFile`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** give the four jobs (motherboard houses, CPU processes, RAM holds during processing, ROM stores start-up instructions) and follow a file from storage through RAM and the CPU to the screen and back.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Who *fetched* the photo?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo Dlamini taps a photo of her new grandson and it opens in under a second. "Who fetched it? Where was it sleeping? Who painted it on the screen?"
   > Board: Gogo's phone, the photo appearing; three question marks above it.
2. **The four jobs (0:40-2:00).** From Grade 10, in four lines to learn word for word: the motherboard houses the components; the CPU processes; RAM holds the data and instructions while they are processed; ROM stores the start-up instructions. RAM is volatile; ROM is non-volatile.
   > Board: four sticky notes, one per part; yellow highlighter on "houses", "processes", "holds", "start-up".
3. **The motherboard's job (2:00-3:20).** It does no calculating and keeps no files. It holds the parts (socket, slots, connectors), connects them with copper roads, carries power to them, and holds the ROM chip. It also decides which CPU and RAM fit, and how many cards and ports. Like a multi-plug - for power and data.
   > Board: a motherboard drawn as a town with roads; Clicky driving a data taxi; a multi-plug with a kettle and toaster.
4. **One photo, step by step (3:20-5:30).** Input: the tap. Find it: the CPU asks for the file in storage. Copy it into RAM - storage is far too slow to work from. Process it: the CPU unpacks the compressed photo into millions of dots. Draw it: the graphics processor paints it. Output: the photo appears. Save copies changes from RAM back to storage.
   > Board: the cpu-ram-storage drawing, arrows lighting up one at a time; a power plug pulled and RAM wiped.
5. **The cycle (5:30-6:30).** Input, processing, output, storage - and communication when she sends it on WhatsApp: the information processing cycle. RAM is the meeting place: everything the CPU works on goes into RAM first.
   > Board: the five words in a circle; yellow highlighter on "RAM is the meeting place".
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-01.2 Graphics cards and reading the advert (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#specs` - the comment after `w1Soldered`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain what a GPU does, tell integrated graphics from a graphics card, and read the processor, RAM and graphics lines of an advert.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Who *paints* the screen?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A game changes millions of dots sixty times a second. Far too much for the CPU alone - so who does it?
   > Board: a screen of dots, Clicky with a tiny paintbrush, sweating.
2. **The GPU (0:40-1:50).** A second processor just for pictures: the graphics processing unit. It works out every dot and draws the picture, doing thousands of small jobs at the same time.
   > Board: the gpu-gamer doodle; yellow highlighter on "GPU".
3. **Integrated graphics (1:50-3:00).** Built into the CPU's chip, no memory of its own - it borrows RAM (why 16 GB shows as 15.8). Costs nothing extra, little power and heat; fine for schoolwork, video and simple games. Most school laptops and all phones.
   > Board: the gpu-integrated-dedicated drawing, left half; an arrow borrowing a slice of RAM.
4. **A graphics card (3:00-4:30).** Video card or dedicated graphics: an expansion card in a long slot, with its own faster GPU, its own VRAM, its own fan, lots of power, and the screen plugged into its ports. For gamers, video editors, 3D architects, AI. It needs a driver; gamers update it often.
   > Board: the right half of the drawing; the expansion-card picture.
5. **Reading the advert (4:30-7:00).** Specifications - the specs. Cores and GHz are the CPU. "16 GB DDR5 (2 x 8 GB)" is RAM: how much, which type, two sticks. "Soldered" means no upgrade later - buy enough now. "Integrated" means no graphics card; "RTX 5060, 8 GB" is a graphics card with 8 GB of VRAM. Two GB numbers: the small one is RAM, the big one storage. Read the small print after the star.
   > Board: the choose-advert drawing; yellow highlighter on each line as it is read; a magnifying glass on the star.
6. **Sign-off.**
