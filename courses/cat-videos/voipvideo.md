## cat11-15.1 Taming your inbox (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#signature` (the comment `// VIDEO cat11-15.1`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** use folders, sort by, flags and importance; set up a message rule and a distribution list; write a work signature and an automatic reply.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · E-MAIL`, title "63 e-mails. *One* cake order."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Monday morning at
   Botha's Bakery: 63 unread e-mails, and somewhere in there a wedding cake
   order for Saturday. He found it on Friday.
   > Board: a long inbox list; Clicky digging through it; the folder-maze drawing.
2. **Four tools (0:40-2:40).** Folders (Gmail calls them labels); sort by
   date, sender, subject, size or importance; a flag means "I still have to
   do something"; high importance (the red !) - and why not to use it on
   everything.
   > Board: four sticky notes - Folder, Sort, Flag, Importance; highlighter on "still have to do something".
   > Screen: in the VM, Outlook - right-click Inbox, Create new folder, "Orders"; drag a message onto it; click the Sort by menu and choose From; click the flag on one message; open a new message and click High importance.
3. **Message rules (2:40-4:20).** A rule = a condition + an action, every
   time a message arrives. The worked example: everything from
   accounts@goldgrainflour.co.za into Suppliers, step by step.
   > Board: "IF from ... THEN move to ..." with the two halves highlighted in different colours.
   > Screen: in the VM, Outlook - right-click a message, Rules, Create rule, tick From, tick Move the item to folder, choose Suppliers, OK.
4. **Distribution lists (4:20-5:20).** One name for a whole group - Ms
   Naidoo's Grade 11 CAT list of 32. Change it once when a pupil leaves.
   Strangers in Bcc, so nobody can reply all.
   > Board: one name tag that opens into 32 little envelopes; the reply-all-storm drawing.
5. **Signature and automatic reply (5:20-6:30).** What goes in a work
   signature (name, position, business, phone, website - never an ID number
   or a home address). A good automatic reply says when you are back and whom
   to contact.
   > Board: Mr Botha's signature block, then his "I am away." reply with the two missing things added in highlighter.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-15.2 Calls and meetings over the Internet (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#goodPractice` (the comment `// VIDEO cat11-15.2`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain VoIP, give its advantages and disadvantages, say what a video conference needs, and spot good and bad practice in an online meeting.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · INTERNET`, title "Camera on. Feet *off* the desk."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo used to buy
   airtime for her Sunday call to Cape Town. Now Lerato has shown her the
   WhatsApp call button.
   > Board: Gogo with a phone; a stack of airtime vouchers crossed out.
2. **VoIP (0:40-2:30).** Voice over Internet Protocol: a call that travels as
   data. The four steps (microphone, digital data, over the Internet, back to
   sound). App-based VoIP and office VoIP phones. About 0.5 MB a minute.
   > Board: the four steps as arrows from Gogo's phone to her son's; highlighter on "as data".
3. **Good and bad of VoIP (2:30-3:30).** Cheap, distance doesn't matter,
   extras built in; no Internet or power - no call, breaks up at a busy taxi
   rank, uses data, same app on both sides.
   > Board: a two-column table filling in.
4. **Video conferencing (3:30-5:30).** A meeting of several people online.
   What it needs: device, camera, microphone and speakers (a headset), the
   app, a good connection - about 5 MB a minute, ten times voice. Who uses it.
   The advantages and disadvantages table.
   > Board: the webinar-crowd drawing; a data meter jumping from 0.5 to 5.
5. **Good and bad practice (5:30-7:30).** Before: test, charge, light in
   front, check behind you, join early. During: mute, headset, look at the
   camera, raise hand, dress for it, share one window. Running it: password or
   waiting room, agenda, ask before recording. Why the echo happens.
   > Board: the zoom-with-feet drawing; a two-column good/bad table; the echo loop drawn from speaker to microphone and back.
6. **Sign-off.**
