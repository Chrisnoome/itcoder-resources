## cat11-13.1 Accounts and access rights (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#rights` - the comment after `w13ReadOnly`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say why every user needs their own username and password; name a network's password rules; define unauthorised access and authentication; say what access rights are (no access, read only, change), set for groups, giving each user only the access they need.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "Who may *open* what?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Monday morning at Phumlani Secondary: half the Grade 11 projects are gone from the server. Nobody hacked in from the Internet. Someone used a teacher's password - from a sticky note under the staff-room keyboard.
   > Board: an empty folder; a sticky note under a keyboard; Clicky pointing at it, horrified.
2. **Your own account (0:40-2:00).** The username says who you claim to be - not secret. The password proves it. Each person needs their own: the network records who did what; each person gets only what they need; your files follow you to any lab. In the story, the record shows the teacher's account - not who was typing.
   > Board: a padlock (username) and a key (password), from the keys-padlocks joke.
3. **The rules and your part (2:00-3:10).** Network rules: a minimum length; locked after a few wrong tries; a new password if it may have leaked; the screen locks when idle. Your part: no sticky notes; never share; Windows key + L, log off; report a leak. A router has two passwords on a sticker - change both on day one.
   > Board: four rules as a list; Windows key + L drawn as two keys. Yellow highlighter on "never share".
4. **Unauthorised access and authentication (3:10-4:20).** Using a computer, network, account or data without permission: a friend's login, a neighbour's Wi-Fi, files you may not see. A crime under the Cybercrimes Act, and against the school's rules. Authentication checks who you are: something you know, have or are - two together are safer.
   > Board: the bouncer at a door with a list. Three icons: a brain (know), a phone (have), a fingerprint (are).
5. **Access rights (4:20-6:20).** What each user may see and do: no access, read only, change. Windows calls them permissions. Set for groups - Grade 11 pupils, teachers. The Phumlani table: own folder - change; class notes - read only; tests and memos - no access; pupils' marks - no access. The caretaker's keys and your locker key: only the access you need, so a mistake or a stolen password does little damage.
   > Board: the table filled in row by row; a key ring with one big master key and one small locker key.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-13.2 The rules you sign - AUPs and BYOD (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#byod` - the comment after `w13Byod`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a network administrator does for security; say what an acceptable use policy is, what goes in one and why a school needs one; use a network ethically; say what BYOD is, give its advantages and disadvantages, and the rules that go with it.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "Sign *here* first"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Phumlani is switching on Wi-Fi for pupils' own phones. Before Thabo gets the password, he and his mother must sign a page of rules.
   > Board: a page with a signature line; Thabo and his mother with pens.
2. **The administrator (0:30-1:40).** The person who looks after the network: creates and removes accounts, sets access rights and password rules, resets passwords after checking who asks, updates and anti-malware, backups, the firewall and the Wi-Fi, watches the records, the AUP, approves own devices. After Monday: restore from backup, change the password, check the record.
   > Board: Clicky in a hard hat, with a checklist.
3. **The AUP (1:40-3:30).** An acceptable use policy: the written rules for using the computers, network, Internet and data, signed before you get access. What goes in: what it is for; accounts and passwords; what is not allowed; installing and own devices; equipment; monitoring; consequences. Why: security, the law, cost, protecting pupils, fairness.
   > Board: the Phumlani AUP extract, each heading highlighted in yellow as it is named.
4. **Ethical use (3:30-4:20).** Respect privacy, even in a folder left open by mistake; respect others' work; don't waste what everyone shares; report a problem instead of using it.
   > Board: an open folder with a "report it" arrow to the teacher.
5. **BYOD (4:20-6:20).** Bring your own device: own phones, tablets and laptops on the school network. For: the school saves money, pupils know their devices, more devices for learning. Against: malware, school data on devices it cannot control, a slower Wi-Fi and data costs, pupils without devices left out, theft, distraction, many devices to support. The rules: register; update and anti-malware; screen lock; join only the separate pupils' Wi-Fi; accept filtering and monitoring; your own responsibility. Why a separate Wi-Fi: malware cannot spread to the school's computers, and pupils cannot reach marks and personal information.
   > Board: two Wi-Fi clouds, "school" and "pupils", with a wall between them.
6. **Sign-off.**
