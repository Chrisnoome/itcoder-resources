## cat10-02.1 Five kinds of multi-purpose device (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#multipurpose` - the comment after `dw2WhichDevice`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** name the five kinds of multi-purpose device, say what each is for with an example, and explain all-in-one and 2-in-1.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · BASICS`, title "Which *computer* is that?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:45).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. When Gogo Dlamini got married, her kitchen drawer held a camera, a torch, a radio, an alarm clock, a diary and a little book of phone numbers. Last year Lerato set up Gogo's first smartphone - and Gogo emptied the drawer.
   > Board: a drawer full of gadgets; a phone slides in and the gadgets fly out. Clicky peeks over the edge.
2. **Multi-purpose (0:45-1:20).** A computer can do many jobs, depending on the program it runs. Install a new app and it does a new job. A device like that is a multi-purpose device. There are five kinds to know.
   > Board: "multi-purpose device" with yellow highlighter; five empty boxes numbered 1-5.
3. **Desktop and all-in-one (1:20-2:20).** A desktop stays on a desk: separate screen, keyboard and mouse, and a case that holds the computer. For a full day of work in one place - like your school's computer lab. An all-in-one hides the computer behind the screen - no case. You see them at a doctor's reception, because they take so little space.
   > Board: a desktop drawn part by part; then an all-in-one, with Clicky pointing behind the screen: "it's in here!"
4. **Laptop and 2-in-1 (2:20-3:20).** A laptop is one folding case with a battery - for work you carry, like Lerato's laptop at every lecture. A 2-in-1 turns into a tablet: a convertible's screen folds right back; a detachable's screen comes off.
   > Board: a laptop; its screen folds back (convertible), then lifts off (detachable). Yellow highlighter on each word.
5. **Tablet and smartphone (3:20-4:20).** A tablet is one slab of touch screen, bigger than a phone, no keyboard of its own - the tablet a waiter sends your order with. A smartphone is a phone that runs apps. For most South Africans, it is their main computer - often their only one.
   > Board: a tablet and a phone side by side; Thabo's phone in his hand.
6. **Server (4:20-5:00).** A server provides a service to other computers over a network - shared files, e-mail, websites. The school's server keeps every pupil's marks and shares them with every teacher. Bigger still: supercomputers and mainframes, room-sized, for the weather forecast and a bank's card payments.
   > Board: one server in the middle with lines to four teachers' computers.
7. **Their software (5:00-5:40).** Each kind runs an operating system - the main program that runs the device. Windows, macOS or Linux on desktops and laptops; Android or iOS on tablets and phones; server versions of Windows or Linux on servers.
   > Board: a three-row table on a sticky note.
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-02.2 One device for many jobs, or one for one job (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#dedicated` - the comment after `pc2Dedicated`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain convergence with examples, give an advantage and a disadvantage of it, explain what a dedicated device is, and sort devices into the two groups.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · BASICS`, title "One job or *ten*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. How many gadgets does one smartphone replace? A phone, a camera and a video camera, a radio and a music player, a map and a GPS, a torch, an alarm clock, a diary, a bank card - and a computer. More than ten, in one pocket.
   > Board: the list written fast down a sticky note, ticked off one by one; Clicky counting on his fingers.
2. **Convergence (0:40-1:50).** Many technologies, and the jobs of many devices, coming together in one device. To converge means to come together, like roads meeting at one point. It happens because the parts got so small and cheap that one device can hold them all. A device like that is a convergence device.
   > Board: roads drawn meeting at one point, and the point becomes a phone. Yellow highlighter on "come together".
3. **Not only phones (1:50-2:30).** A smart TV: TV, Internet and apps. A 2-in-1: laptop and tablet. A multifunction printer: printer, scanner and copier. A smartwatch: watch, fitness tracker and notifications.
   > Board: four quick drawings, each with its jobs on small labels.
4. **Two sides (2:30-3:50).** Advantages: less to buy, carry and charge; cheaper than buying each one; everything works together - take a photo, send it a second later. Disadvantages: lose or break it and every job goes at once; a flat battery stops everything; each job is often done less well - a phone camera cannot zoom like a big camera. And a disadvantage is a real problem, not an advantage turned around.
   > Board: a two-column list, pink highlighter on the advantages, blue on the disadvantages; a phone with a flat battery icon and Clicky looking worried.
5. **Dedicated devices (3:50-5:30).** Many devices are built for one job, or a small set of jobs - you cannot install a game on them. An ATM gives out cash. A digital camera takes photos. A printer prints. A fitness tracker counts steps and measures your heart rate. A factory robot welds the same car door thousands of times a day. A microwave heats. Most have an embedded computer inside: the appliance does one job, the chip is the computer.
   > Board: the six devices drawn in a grid; each gets a one-word job label. The robot arm moves back and forth.
6. **Smart wear, and a device in two groups (5:30-6:30).** A fitness tracker is dedicated. A smartwatch runs apps, so it is closer to a small multi-purpose computer. A multifunction printer is a convergence device - but still dedicated: printing, scanning and copying are all it will ever do.
   > Board: tracker and smartwatch side by side; then the printer standing on the line between two circles labelled "convergence" and "dedicated".
7. **Recap (6:30-6:50).** Multi-purpose: many jobs, an operating system and the apps you choose. Dedicated: one job, one program built in at the factory.
   > Board: the Learn / Memorise table, row by row.
8. **Sign-off.**
