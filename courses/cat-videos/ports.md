## cat10-12.1 Every port on your laptop, named (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#network` - the comment after `hs12BackPorts`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** define a peripheral, tell a port from a connector, name and recognise the USB shapes, HDMI, VGA, the network port and the audio jack, and choose an adapter.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "What *plugs* in where?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo Dlamini's kitchen drawer: old chargers, a camera cable, two that fit nothing. Her new phone's cable does not fit her old phone.
   > Board: the cable-drawer drawing; Clicky tangled in a cable.
2. **Peripherals (0:40-1:40).** A peripheral is a device outside the system unit that connects to it - input, output or storage: keyboard, printer, flash drive. The CPU and RAM are inside, so they are not. Cabled, usually USB; or wireless - Bluetooth for devices close by, Wi-Fi for a shared printer.
   > Board: a system unit in the middle, peripherals around it on lines - solid lines for cables, dotted for wireless.
3. **Port and connector (1:40-2:20).** The port is the socket; the connector is the plug. A wall socket and a kettle's plug. A standard means every maker follows the same rules.
   > Board: a socket and a plug, labelled; yellow highlighter on "socket" and "plug".
4. **USB (2:20-4:20).** Universal Serial Bus: almost everything plugs in, and it carries power. A hub turns one port into several. USB 2 slow, USB 3 fast and often blue inside, USB4 fastest. Backwards compatible, at the slower speed. The shapes: USB-A (one way up), USB-B (printers), Mini and Micro USB (older cameras and phones), USB-C (small, oval, either way up). USB-C is only a shape - check what each port can do.
   > Board: the usb-connectors drawing, one shape at a time; Clicky turning a USB-A plug over three times.
5. **HDMI and VGA (4:20-5:40).** HDMI: picture and sound in one cable, digital, on every TV and new projector; three sizes. VGA: the old blue plug with 15 pins and screws - picture only, analogue, still on old projectors. DisplayPort: another digital port for monitors.
   > Board: an HDMI plug and a VGA plug side by side; a speaker icon crossed out next to VGA.
6. **Adapters (5:40-6:20).** Thabo's laptop: USB-C and HDMI. The hall projector: VGA only. Look at both ends, buy an adapter with one of each: HDMI to VGA, or USB-C to VGA - and plug the sound in separately. A dock adds many ports at once.
   > Board: the adapter-chain drawing; the four steps on a sticky note.
7. **Network and sound (6:20-6:50).** The network port - RJ-45, Ethernet - almost square, like a big telephone socket. The 3.5 mm audio jack: green for speakers, pink for the microphone.
   > Board: the pic-back-ports photo with Clicky pointing at each port in turn, a yellow highlighter ring on each.
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-12.2 Plug and play - adding a device, and ejecting a flash drive (about 5 min)

**Goes:** after section `#plugPlay` - the comment after `q12HotSwap`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain plug and play, a driver and hot-swapping, eject a flash drive safely, and add a wireless printer or a Bluetooth mouse in Windows 11.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Plug it in. *It just works.*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo plugs in a new mouse. A few seconds later, it works. He installed nothing. What happened?
   > Board: a mouse plug heading for a port; Clicky waiting with a stopwatch.
2. **Plug and play and drivers (0:30-1:40).** Plug and play: the computer sets a new device up by itself. Every device needs a driver - a small program that tells the operating system how to talk to it. The five steps: plug in; Windows notices; the device says what it is; Windows finds and installs the driver; ready.
   > Board: the printer-driver joke drawn (the OS and the printer speaking different languages, the driver in the middle); the five steps numbered.
   > Screen: in the VM, plug in a USB mouse (pass a USB device through to the VM) and catch the "Setting up a device" / ready message. If the VM cannot show it, leave this to the board.
3. **Hot-swapping and ejecting (1:40-3:00).** USB is hot-swappable: plug in and unplug with the computer on. But Windows may still be writing to a flash drive after the copy looks done. Close any open file; in File Explorer right-click the drive, Eject; wait for "safe to remove"; pull it out.
   > Board: the flash-yank drawing.
   > Screen: in the VM, a flash drive (or a virtual USB drive) in File Explorer; right-click > Eject; the message appears. Yellow highlighter on "Eject".
4. **Adding a printer or a Bluetooth mouse (3:00-4:30).** Switch it on - Bluetooth devices into pairing mode. Settings, Bluetooth & devices. A printer: Printers & scanners, Add device. A mouse: Add device, Bluetooth. Click it when it appears; wait for the driver; connected.
   > Screen: in the VM, Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Printers & scanners > Add device; show the search. (No real printer needed - stop at the search list.)
5. **Sign-off.**
