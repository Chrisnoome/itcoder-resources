## cat11-04.1 Memory, storage and the cloud as a service (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#sync` - the comment after `w4Sync`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** sort primary from secondary storage with one test, explain why a computer needs both, (CAPS) explain why cloud storage is SaaS, and explain why syncing is not a backup.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Is the cloud a *backup*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Mr Botha's laptop is clicking. Five years of invoices on it. "Where is your backup?" He points to a drive on the shelf - right next to the laptop, last used in 2023.
   > Board: the bakery office; a laptop with sound lines; a dusty drive on the shelf beside it.
2. **Primary and secondary (0:40-2:30).** The counter and the cupboard from Grade 10, with their proper names. One test: can the CPU work on it directly? Yes: primary storage - RAM, cache, ROM. No: secondary - hard drives, SSDs, flash drives, memory cards, discs, the cloud, copied into RAM first.
   > Board: two columns; Clicky carrying items across the line. Yellow highlighter on "directly".
3. **Why both (2:30-3:40).** RAM is fast but volatile and costly per GB - 8 or 16 GB. Storage is non-volatile, big and cheap - but far too slow to work from. Only RAM: everything gone at the first power cut. Only a drive: the CPU waits all day.
   > Board: the Learn / Memorise table drawn row by row.
4. **Cloud storage as a service - CAPS (3:40-5:30).** Full of drives, but you never buy or touch one: you get a service. SaaS - software as a service: used over the Internet, run on the provider's servers, looked after by the provider, paid by subscription or free. Renting a flat: the landlord fixes the roof. The provider replaces drives, keeps spare copies, guards and updates; you choose a plan, guard the password, decide who sees your files.
   > Board: the cloud-plug doodle; the data-centre picture; a two-column "provider / you" list. Note on screen: "CAPS".
5. **Syncing is not a backup (5:30-7:20).** Lerato saves into OneDrive; it goes to the cloud, then to her phone and tablet - the same latest version everywhere. But delete a folder, or let ransomware scramble files, and syncing copies the damage too. A backup is a separate copy that keeps the good version. Deleted files are kept for about 30 days - not a real backup.
   > Board: the sync-not-backup drawing, the red spreading from device to device.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-04.2 Choosing storage, reading adverts and fixing a full drive (about 9 min)

**Goes:** after section `#trouble` - the comment after `q4Clicking`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** choose storage and backup storage for a job with a reason, weigh the cloud's advantages and disadvantages for a person, read storage adverts and cloud plans, and free up space or respond to a failing drive.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Which *storage* for which job?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. There is no best storage - only the best storage for the job.
   > Board: six jobs on six sticky notes, Clicky holding up drives one by one.
2. **Six questions (0:30-2:00).** How much; must it travel; how fast; how tough; what can you spend; must others reach it? Worked: Windows - an SSD; ten years of photos - an external hard drive; a CAT project - a flash drive or the cloud; a 4K drone - a fast microSD; a group assignment - the cloud; a building site - an SSD.
   > Board: the six-job table drawn line by line.
3. **Backup storage (2:00-3:30).** Holds everything, separate, kept somewhere else, reliable, easy. External hard drive: cheap, fast to restore - but can burn with the computer. Cloud: somewhere else, automatic - Internet, slow first upload, a fee. Flash drive: few files, easily lost. DVDs: small. Best: an external drive AND the cloud.
   > Board: the shelf drive in flames beside the laptop; a cloud untouched above.
4. **Good and bad, for the person (3:30-5:00).** Anywhere; a copy elsewhere; sharing; working together; the provider looks after it - against: needs Internet and data; wrong sharing; trust the provider; endless fees; a weak password. A Karoo farm: Internet decides it. A Sandton office on fibre: a copy elsewhere and working together.
   > Board: the anyone-link doodle; a farm with one bar of signal, an office with a fibre cable.
5. **Reading adverts (5:00-6:40).** rpm means a hard drive; "up to" MB/s is the best case; U3 and V30 for video; a family plan shares one amount. R40 a month is R480 a year, so a R400 yearly plan saves R80. Cost per GB: about 70c for the hard drive, R1.80 for the SSD, R4 a year for the cloud. "Unlimited" has small print.
   > Board: the advert table, yellow highlighter on each line as it is read.
6. **When storage goes wrong (6:40-8:40).** This PC: a red bar means almost full; Settings, Storage shows what fills it. Keep a tenth free. Free space: empty the Recycle Bin, Disk Clean-up or Storage Sense, uninstall, move big files, keep files only in the cloud. Errors: drive Properties, Tools, Error checking, Check. A dying hard drive clicks, slows and damages files; an SSD just stops. Back up the important files first.
   > Board: the storage-full doodle.
   > Screen: in the VM, File Explorer This PC (highlight the bar); Settings, System, Storage; right-click C:, Properties, Tools, Check.
7. **Sign-off.**
