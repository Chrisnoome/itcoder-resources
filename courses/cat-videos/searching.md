# CAT 10 lesson 26: Browsing and searching well - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory10/searching.php`. Two videos: getting around in
the browser, then searching well. Board in the CAT marker style with Clicky
(brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow highlighter on any words on screen being
talked about. Everything below is in the lesson text.

**Screens:** both videos use Edge in the video VM (Windows, a clean profile,
no personal bookmarks or history; sign-in prompts dismissed). In 26.2 the
results pages change from day to day - record them on the day, and if the
search engine shows no "Sponsored" result or AI summary for that search,
cut to the board's drawing of the results page instead. Never search for
anything personal.

## cat10-26.1 Getting around in your browser (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the IEB section "Bookmarks, history and the home page" - the comment before section `#engines`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a browser is and name four; use the address bar, Back, Forward, Refresh and Home; open, switch and close tabs and give the advantages of tabbed browsing; (IEB) make a bookmark, open the history and set the home page.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · BROWSING`, title "23 windows? Try *tabs*."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo has 23 windows open and one useful page. Lerato closes 20 of them in ten seconds.
   > Board: a screen stacked with overlapping windows; Clicky buried under them.
2. **What a browser is (0:40-1:40).** Software that fetches web pages and shows them - the four steps: you type or click, it asks the web server, the page arrives, it shows it. Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox, Samsung Internet - all free. Keep it updated.
   > Board: a window frame labelled "the browser" with a page arriving through it; the four steps numbered.
3. **The parts of the window (1:40-3:20).** Address bar (type an address, or anything else to search), Back, Forward, Refresh, Home, tabs. Opening a page step by step: click the address bar, type bestlessons.co.za, Enter, click a link, Back.
   > Screen: Edge in the VM - click the address bar (highlighter on it), type bestlessons.co.za, Enter; click a link; click Back; click Refresh. Highlighter band on each button as it is named.
4. **Tabs (3:20-4:50).** Ctrl+T (or +) for a new tab; Ctrl+click a link to open it in a new tab; click a tab to switch; X or Ctrl+W to close. Why: many pages in one window, switch quickly, compare (three shops' prices), keep your place. Each tab uses memory. Ctrl+Shift+T brings back a tab closed by mistake.
   > Screen: three shop pages opened in tabs and switched between; one closed and brought back with Ctrl+Shift+T.
   > Board: a sticky note with the four advantages.
5. **IEB: bookmarks, history, home page (4:50-6:30).** A bookmark (Edge: favourite) - the star or Ctrl+D, choose a folder, Done. History - Ctrl+H, the pages you visited and when; clear it on a shared computer. The home page setting - the page the browser opens first (Chrome: On startup). Two kinds of "home page": a website's main page, and your browser's first page.
   > Screen: in Edge, star a page, open Ctrl+H, open Settings at the start-up page setting. Note: an "IEB" flag in the corner for this scene.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-26.2 Searching well (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the IEB section "Search operators" - the comment before the identify question.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a search engine is and name three; tell a browser from a search engine; read a results page (Sponsored adverts, an AI summary, ordinary results); choose good keywords; (IEB) use " ", -, site:, OR, AND and NOT.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SEARCHING`, title "Four words that find *anything*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo types "load shedding" and gets millions of results - mostly schedules. Lerato changes four words.
   > Board: a mountain of pages labelled "load shedding"; Clicky with a tiny needle.
2. **What a search engine is (0:30-1:40).** A website that finds web pages for you. Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo. How it is so fast: long before you search it has made an index - which words are on which pages - like the index of a textbook. A browser is NOT a search engine: you use the search engine inside the browser.
   > Board: a textbook index next to a list "braai: pages 3, 9..."; pink highlighter on "browser is NOT a search engine".
3. **The results page (1:40-3:00).** Adverts marked Sponsored come first - paid for, not best. An AI summary can be confidently wrong: open its sources. Then the ordinary results. A search engine does not check whether a page is true.
   > Screen: a search in Edge on Bing; highlighter band on "Sponsored", then on the AI summary's sources.
   > Board (if the screen shows no advert or summary): the searching-q-drawing with A, B and C.
4. **Keywords (3:00-5:00).** The five steps: picture the page; the important words; be specific; add the place; change words if the results are poor. Worked: "load shedding" -> "load shedding effect small businesses South Africa". Then Gogo's "money" -> "SASSA older persons grant payment dates December" - and check it on the .gov.za site.
   > Screen: the two searches typed one after the other; scroll the first results of each.
   > Board: the steps numbered on a sticky note.
5. **IEB: operators (5:00-6:30).** " " exact phrase; - leave out (no space); site: one kind of site (bursary site:gov.za); OR either word. AND and NOT give fewer results, OR more; in Google a space is AND and the minus is NOT.
   > Board: the four operators with their examples; three circles growing and shrinking for AND, OR, NOT. Note: an "IEB" flag in the corner for this scene.
6. **Sign-off.**
