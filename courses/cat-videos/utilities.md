Lesson: `content/cattheory12/utilities.php`. Three videos: housekeeping
(free space, defragmenting and TRIM), getting files back (File History and
restore points), and troubleshooting step by step. Board in the CAT marker

## cat12-05.1 Housekeeping: clean-up, defragmenting and TRIM (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#optimise` - the comment after `tUtTrim`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what each housekeeping utility does and why, switch on Storage Sense, and explain why a hard drive is defragmented and an SSD gets TRIM instead.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "Never *defrag* an SSD"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Two weeks before the trials, Thabo's laptop says Storage almost full, the project will not save, adverts pop up and the printer says Offline. Lerato: "We are not buying a new laptop. We are doing housekeeping."
   > Board: Thabo's laptop with four warning stickers; Clicky holding a mop and a toolbox.
2. **The toolkit (0:40-2:00).** Housekeeping - regular jobs, like cleaning a kitchen. The seven utilities, what and why: disk clean-up, defragmenter, backup, anti-malware, updater, scheduler, compression (with a password in 7-Zip).
   > Board: the toolbox doodle with seven labelled tools; the lesson's toolkit table row by row; highlighter on "what" and "why".
3. **A full drive - the recap (2:00-2:50).** Grade 11 did the steps; in one breath: keep about a tenth free; This PC shows each drive's bar; empty the bin, delete temporary files, uninstall, move big files off. Why it matters: slower (temporary files, virtual memory), updates fail, work will not save.
   > Board: a drive bar turning red; four quick ticks for the four steps. Highlighter on "a tenth".
4. **Storage Sense (2:50-4:30).** Disk Clean-up works only when someone remembers. Storage Sense does it by itself: Settings, System, Storage; switch it on; choose when (low space, or every day, week or month); choose what (temporary files, the Recycle Bin after 30 days, Downloads only if you choose). Cleanup recommendations. The advantage, and the one thing to be careful of.
   > Screen: Settings > System > Storage; Storage Sense switched on and its schedule opened; Cleanup recommendations. Highlighter on "Run Storage Sense" and the Recycle Bin setting.
5. **Defragmenting a hard drive - a recap (4:30-5:20).** Lesson 2 taught it; in short: deleted and growing files leave a hard drive saving files in pieces - fragmented; the arm jumps to every piece, one book from five shelves; defragmenting puts the pieces side by side. Faster, but no extra space: nothing is deleted.
   > Board: Clicky running between five shelves for one book, then the books side by side on one shelf. Highlighter on "no extra space".
6. **TRIM for an SSD (5:20-7:00).** No arm, so no gain; extra writing wears it out. An SSD must clear a block before writing; TRIM tells it which blocks are free; it clears them in its own time; saving stays fast. Defragment and Optimise Drives: the media type, Optimise does the right job, weekly.
   > Board: an SSD as a grid of blocks, Clicky with a broom clearing the TRIMmed ones. Screen: Defragment and Optimise Drives, the Media type column. Highlighter on "Solid state drive".
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-05.2 Getting your files back (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#restore` - the comment after `wUtBackupVsPoint`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** restore a file from File History step by step, and explain why a restore point is not a backup.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "Is System Restore a *backup*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Lerato saved an old draft over her essay. Zanele deleted the bakery's price list and emptied the bin. One of them can get it back.
   > Board: two sad faces with two files; Clicky holding a time machine dial.
2. **The backup utilities (0:30-1:40).** The plan is lesson 17 (3-2-1, automatic, off-site). The tools: File History every hour to an external drive with older versions; Windows Backup to OneDrive; phone backups at night; WhatsApp separately. Syncing is not a backup.
   > Board: the sync-not-backup doodle; three tool icons; highlighter on "older versions".
3. **Restoring from File History (1:40-3:40).** Plug in the drive; Restore your files with File History; the folder; back in time before the mistake; Restore; open and check. Test a restore now and then, while the originals are safe.
   > Screen: the six steps in the VM, with a test folder and an essay saved over on purpose. Highlighter on the arrows and the green Restore button.
4. **Restore points are not backups (3:40-5:20).** A snapshot of Windows' system files, settings and programs, made before updates and drivers. System Restore undoes a bad driver - it does not touch documents, so it cannot bring back Zanele's price list. Reset this PC is the last resort: programs removed, files kept if you choose - back up first.
   > Board: a camera photographing only the Windows logo and a gear, not the documents beside them. Screen: Create a restore point > System Restore (open it, do not run it); Settings > System > Recovery. Highlighter on "System Restore" and "Reset this PC".
5. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-05.3 Troubleshooting, step by step (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#problems` - the comment after `wUtNoDisplay`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** follow the seven-step troubleshooting method, tell a hardware problem from a software one, try the right first fixes for the common problems, and run a troubleshooter.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "No Signal? Check *this* first"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. The projector in the hall says No Signal, ten minutes before assembly. Clicking everything in sight is not a method. Here is one.
   > Board: a projector screen with "No Signal"; Clicky running in circles, then stopping with a clipboard.
2. **Seven steps (0:30-2:40).** Describe the problem exactly - the error, when it started, what changed. The simple things. Restart. One fix at a time, testing each. Built-in tools. Search the error or ask - back up first. Write down what fixed it. Like a doctor: questions first.
   > Board: the detective doodle; the seven steps as a numbered list; highlighter on "what changed?".
3. **Hardware or software? When to stop (2:40-3:30).** Every program or only one. When to stop: burning smell, smoke, a swollen battery, liquid inside, a clicking drive; warranty.
   > Board: a fork in the road labelled "every program" / "only one"; a red stop sign with the five danger signs.
4. **The common problems (3:30-6:30).** Row by row: No Signal (power, cable, input source, Windows + P); wrong resolution (Recommended); not responding (lesson 4); printer (paper, default, Offline, the print queue, troubleshooter); scanner (connection, driver, glass, lid, dpi); mouse or keyboard (batteries, pairing, another port, Device Manager); no Internet (flight mode, Wi-Fi, other devices, router, data); disk errors (back up, Error checking, a clicking drive); drive full.
   > Board: the lesson's problems table, row by row. Screen: Windows + P menu; Display > Resolution "(Recommended)"; Device Manager with no warnings; a drive's Properties > Tools > Check. Highlighter on each first fix.
5. **Troubleshooters (6:30-7:30).** A built-in wizard for one kind of problem: Settings, System, Troubleshoot, Other troubleshooters; Run; answer, follow, test. Some open in Get Help. Other wizards set up printers and networks.
   > Screen: Other troubleshooters; Run next to Printer (the VM's virtual printer). Highlighter on "Run".
6. **Sign-off.**
