## cat11-05.1 Installing software safely (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the written question `w5Custom`, at the end of section
`#steps` (before `#activation`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** name three safe places software comes from and
the ones to avoid, give the steps of installing a program, explain why only
an administrator can install, and choose between a full and a custom
installation - unticking the extras.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "Next, Next, Next... *oops*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo found the
   newest soccer game, "full version, free". Next, Next, Next, Finish. The
   game never started - but the next morning his browser had a new home
   page, a toolbar and pop-ups every few minutes.
   > Board: Thabo's laptop; pop-ups multiplying like rabbits; Clicky hiding
   > behind the Recycle Bin.
2. **Three safe sources (0:40-2:00).** An app store (checked, installs and
   updates for you - check the maker, downloads and reviews, read the
   permissions); the maker's own website (an .exe or .msi in Downloads -
   type the address yourself, beware fake Download buttons); a portable
   medium (a flash drive or disc - scan it first; for slow lines, no
   Internet, small bundles).
   > Board: three doors drawn in marker, each with a tick; yellow highlighter
   > on "app store", "maker's website", "flash drive".
3. **Never from these (2:00-2:50).** A "free full version" of paid software
   (pirated, often malware); a pop-up cleaner; a file from a stranger on
   WhatsApp; an APK from a website ("Install unknown apps" switches the
   store's checking off). An installer is a program - opening it says "yes,
   run this".
   > Board: four doors with a red cross each; the torch app asking for
   > contacts (priv-torch-app).
4. **Installing, step by step (2:50-4:20).** Double-click the installer;
   allow the changes (admin); agree to the EULA; full or custom; where it
   goes and the shortcuts; wait; finish and restart. The screens with Back
   and Next are a setup wizard.
   > Screen: VM - Downloads folder, double-click a free program's installer
   > (e.g. LibreOffice), the User Account Control prompt, the licence page,
   > Typical/Custom, Finish. Yellow highlighter on each button as it is
   > clicked.
5. **Administrator rights (4:20-5:10).** Installing changes the computer for
   everyone, so only an administrator may. School pupils have standard
   accounts: no games, no pirated software, and malware that tricks a pupil
   cannot change the whole computer.
   > Board: two keys on a key ring - "standard" opens programs, "admin"
   > opens the whole house.
6. **Full or custom (5:10-6:30).** Full: quick, the maker's choices, may
   add extras. Custom: choose the parts and the drive, untick extras - takes
   longer, you must know what each part does. The extras: a toolbar, a "PC
   cleaner", a new home page, already ticked.
   > Board: the install-extras drawing; Clicky unticking each extra box.
   > Note: the hotspot in the lesson uses the same SnapTidy 5 window - show
   > a similar window, not the answer.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-05.2 Updates, patches and uninstalling (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after the written question `w5Unused`, at the end of section
`#uninstalling` (before the IEB section `#driversIeb`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** tell a version, an update, a patch, a service
pack and an upgrade apart, say how updates arrive and why to install them
soon, and uninstall a program properly.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "Patch, pack or *upgrade*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. May 2017:
   WannaCry locks more than 200 000 computers in about 150 countries.
   Microsoft had sent the patch two months before. The computers it hit had
   simply not installed it.
   > Board: a world map with padlocks popping up; a calendar showing March
   > (patch) and May (attack).
2. **Versions (0:40-1:30).** WhatsApp 2.26.4: the first number for a big
   new version, the numbers after the dots for small fixes. Help, About - or
   File, Account in Office; the store page on a phone.
   > Board: the version number written large, each part labelled; yellow
   > band on the first number.
3. **The four words (1:30-3:00).** Update - any free fix or improvement.
   Patch - one small fix, usually a security hole (the patch on a torn
   school jersey). Service pack - a big bag of patches at once (Windows 7
   SP1; now monthly bundles). Upgrade - a new major version, often paid
   (Windows 10 to 11). An update fixes the version you have; an upgrade
   replaces it.
   > Board: the Learn / Memorise table built row by row; a jersey with one
   > patch, then a bag of patches.
4. **How updates arrive (3:00-3:50).** Windows Update (Settings, Windows
   Update, Check for updates); the store, at night on Wi-Fi; a program's own
   updater. Mark mobile data as metered. An update waiting for a restart does
   not protect you yet - "Remind me later" 400 times.
   > Screen: VM - Settings, Windows Update, Check for updates; then Network
   > & internet, the metered connection switch.
5. **Uninstalling (3:50-5:20).** Settings, Apps, Installed apps, the three
   dots, Uninstall, confirm. On a phone: press and hold, Uninstall / Remove
   App. Two wrong ways: deleting the shortcut (it only points), deleting the
   folder (leaves bits behind). Why uninstall: space, speed (programs that
   start with Windows), fewer unpatched holes.
   > Screen: VM - Installed apps, search, ... , Uninstall.
   > Board: the shortcut-bin drawing - the game did not even notice.
6. **Sign-off.**
