## cat10-31.1 Six reasons Mr Botha bought a computer (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#economic` (the comment `// VIDEO cat10-31.1` before `s31Economic`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** name the six economic reasons for using computers and give a bakery example of each.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOCIAL`, title "Why *every* shop has a computer"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Botha's Bakery in
   Centurion ran for twenty years on a cash box, a notebook and a landline.
   Then Mr Botha bought a till, a laptop and a WhatsApp business number. Why
   would a baker spend that money? Six reasons - and every business has the
   same six.
   > Board: the bakery counter with a cash box and a notebook; Clicky peers in.
2. **Saving paper (0:40-1:25).** Invoices by e-mail instead of printed and
   posted: no paper, ink or envelopes.
   > Board: a stack of paper invoices crossed out; an e-mail envelope icon. Highlighter on "Saving paper".
3. **Saving labour (1:25-2:10).** The till adds up every sale; nobody adds up
   slips after closing. Labour means the work people are paid to do.
   > Board: a tired person with a calculator at night, then the till doing it. Highlighter on "labour".
4. **Saving communication costs (2:10-2:50).** The price list goes out on
   WhatsApp for a little data, instead of phone calls and printed flyers.
   > Board: a phone bill vs a WhatsApp bubble.
5. **Efficiency (2:50-3:30).** Orders arrive overnight and are ready to bake
   at 05:00 - more work done in the same time, with less waste.
   > Board: a clock at 05:00, a tray of loaves.
6. **Accuracy and reliability (3:30-4:30).** The price comes from the till,
   not from memory - no adding mistakes. And the till does it the same way
   every time, at 06:00 and at 18:00. It does not get tired.
   > Board: two sticky notes, "Accuracy" and "Reliability"; Clicky cheering.
7. **The rule for answers (4:30-5:30).** "Cheaper" or "faster" alone earns
   nothing. Name the reason, then say what it saves in the business you were
   given. Worked once: Mr Botha e-mails his invoices - saving paper, because
   he no longer buys paper, ink and envelopes to print and post them.
   > Board: "faster" crossed out; "Saving paper - no paper, ink or envelopes" highlighted.
8. **Recap (5:30-6:00).** The six names, built into the Learn / Memorise table.
   > Board: the six-row table from the lesson.
9. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-31.2 Bitcoin in plain words (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#crypto` (the comment `// VIDEO cat10-31.2`, inside the IEB section).
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a cryptocurrency is, how a Bitcoin payment works in five steps, and give its advantages and disadvantages.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · IEB`, title "Money with *no bank*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:45).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. When Lerato sent
   Thabo R50, no coin moved. A number went down in her bank account and up
   in his - the bank kept the record. Now imagine money with no bank at all.
   > Board: two phones with R50 arrows; a bank building in the middle, then rubbed out.
2. **What it is (0:45-1:45).** Cryptocurrency: digital money that exists only
   as data, issued by no bank or government. A record of every payment is
   kept on thousands of computers that check one another. Bitcoin is the
   first and best-known, started in 2009 by someone using the name Satoshi
   Nakamoto - nobody knows for sure who.
   > Board: a ledger page copied onto many little computers. Highlighter on "only as data".
3. **A payment, step by step (1:45-3:15).** The five steps in the lesson:
   wallet with a secret key; choose the other wallet's address; the payment
   goes to the computers; they check you have it and add it to the record;
   the other wallet shows it.
   > Board: five numbered boxes drawn one per line; Clicky moves along them.
4. **The good side (3:15-3:55).** Send money anywhere, any hour, without a
   bank; no bank can freeze a wallet you keep yourself; no name needed for a
   wallet.
5. **The bad side (3:55-5:20).** The price swings wildly - it lost more than
   half between October 2025 and February 2026. No undo. Lose the key, lose
   the money. Criminals and fake investment schemes. Lots of electricity. Not
   legal tender in South Africa; since 2022 it counts as a financial product,
   so sellers here need a licence.
   > Board: the crypto-rollercoaster drawing; a lost key; "No undo" on a sticky note.
6. **Recap and a warning (5:20-5:50).** Know the risks before anyone tells you
   it is easy money. (Say plainly that this is not advice to buy or sell.)
7. **Sign-off.**
