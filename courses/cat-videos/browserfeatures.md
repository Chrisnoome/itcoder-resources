# CAT 12 lesson 12: Browser features worth knowing - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory12/browserfeatures.php`. Two videos: what your
browser remembers (cache and private browsing), and cookies, passwords,
extensions, pop-ups and blocking. Board in the CAT marker style with Clicky
(brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow highlighter on any words on screen being
talked about. Screen recordings in the VM, in Chrome, signed out of the
browser, with no personal accounts or history showing. Everything below is
in the lesson text.

## cat12-12.1 What your browser remembers (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#private` - the comment after `rBrowPrivateSchool`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** set the home page, explain caching with an advantage and a disadvantage, and say what private browsing does and does not do.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · BROWSERS`, title "Incognito is *not* invisible"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo borrows Lerato's laptop for one minute - and learns about her shoes, opens her bank, and finds his own surprise party. Her browser remembers.
   > Board: a laptop with three speech bubbles (shoes, a bank, a party hat); Clicky looking guilty. Highlighter on "remembers".
2. **The quick recap (0:40-1:50).** Bookmark (Edge: favourite) with Ctrl+D; history with Ctrl+H; the home page setting - and the two meanings of "home page".
   > Screen: press Ctrl+D on bestlessons.co.za; Ctrl+H; then Settings - On startup - Open a specific page - Add a new page.
3. **Caching (1:50-3:30).** The browser keeps copies of pictures and files; the next visit is faster and uses less data. The four steps. The bad side: storage, an old copy, others can find what you saw. Ctrl+F5.
   > Board: the kitchen-cache doodle; then a phone with a small box labelled cache; highlighter on "copies".
4. **The old test date (3:30-4:10).** Ms Naidoo changed the date, Thabo still sees the old one: his cache. Ctrl+F5 loads it fresh.
   > Board: two phones, two different dates; highlighter on the old one.
5. **Private browsing (4:10-6:10).** Incognito, InPrivate, Private Window; Ctrl+Shift+N. What it does: no history, no cookies, no form data, logged out. What it does not: hide you from the network, the ISP, or a site you log in to; delete downloads; stop malware. When it is useful.
   > Screen: Ctrl+Shift+N opens an Incognito window (the hat-and-glasses icon).
   > Board: the does / does not table, one row at a time; the incognito-disguise doodle; highlighter on "this browser, on this device".
6. **Can Ms Naidoo see? (6:10-6:40).** Yes - the school's network sees every site.
   > Board: a lab computer, a line through the school firewall; highlighter on "network".
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-12.2 Cookies, extensions, pop-ups and blocking (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#blocking` - the comment after `wBrowBlocking`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain what a cookie is and answer a banner sensibly, use saved passwords and sync safely, install an extension safely, deal with a scam pop-up, and say why and how websites are blocked.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · BROWSERS`, title "Why did those *shoes* follow me?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Lerato looked at running shoes once. Now they are on every website she opens.
   > Board: the same pair of shoes in three different web pages; Clicky pointing.
2. **Cookies (0:30-2:10).** A small text file a site asks the browser to keep, sent back on every visit - the hand stamp. Logins, carts, settings, adverts. The site's own cookies and tracking cookies. The banner: reject non-essential. Not a virus; a login cookie on a shared computer. (An IEB section - taught to everyone.)
   > Board: the web-hand-stamp doodle; then the cookie-set-and-track figure, top half then bottom half; highlighter on "Reject non-essential".
3. **Saved passwords and sync (2:10-3:40).** Good: strong, different passwords; not filled into a fake site. Risky: anyone at your browser logs in as you. Sync copies everything to every device you sign in on - never on a library computer. The four rules; clearing browsing data.
   > Screen: Ctrl+Shift+Delete, All time, the ticks, Delete data (on the VM's test profile).
4. **Extensions and plug-ins (3:40-5:20).** A small program that adds a feature; CAPS says extension, the IEB says plug-in. Examples: ad and pop-up blockers, a password manager, grammar, a translator, a toolbar extension. Old toolbars that changed your home page. Flash is gone. Installing safely: the store, the maker, the reviews, the permissions.
   > Screen: the Chrome Web Store page of a well-known grammar extension; the permissions box after Add (cancel - do not install).
   > Board: the web-plugin-graveyard doodle; highlighter on "read and change all your data".
5. **Pop-ups and ad blockers (5:20-6:50).** Useful, adverts, scams: "Your phone is infected!" - close the tab, never call. The built-in blocker and allowing one site. Ad blockers: faster, less data, less tracking - but free sites lose income.
   > Screen: the pop-up-blocked icon in the address bar, Always allow pop-ups from this site (on a harmless test page).
   > Board: Gogo's phone with the scam box; a big marker X over "Call now"; highlighter on "close the tab".
6. **Blocking websites (6:50-7:40).** Who blocks and why; an extension, parental controls, or the network - the network covers every device.
   > Board: a school, a home and a bakery, each with a "no entry" sign; highlighter on "every device".
7. **Sign-off.**
