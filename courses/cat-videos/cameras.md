## cat10-08.1 Cameras and webcams (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#webcam` (the comment is below the `w8Webcam` question).
**The pupil can afterwards:** name the kinds of digital camera, work out megapixels, choose optical over digital zoom, and keep a webcam private.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Zoom with your *feet*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo Dlamini's first
   video call: an ear, the ceiling, a nose. "Your face was very small."
   > Board: a phone screen showing a giant ear; Clicky tilting his head.
2. **How a digital camera works (0:40-1:40).** Light through the lens onto a
   sensor - a grid of millions of light-sensitive dots - saved as a file on a
   memory card or in the phone's storage. No film.
   > Board: a lens, light rays, a grid sensor, a file icon popping out.
3. **The kinds (1:40-2:40).** Phone (always with you; front and back cameras),
   compact, professional (swappable lenses, dim light, distance), video camera
   (longer recording, smooth zoom, better sound), security camera (CCTV, to a
   hard drive). Photos can be edited or made by AI: a photo is input, not proof.
   > Board: five cameras in a row, each with a one-word sticky note.
4. **Megapixels (2:40-3:50).** A pixel is one dot; a megapixel a million.
   Across times down: 4 000 x 3 000 = 12 000 000 = 12 megapixels. Full HD shows
   about 2 megapixels, so a 12 MP photo can be cropped and stay sharp.
   > Board: the grid with "4 000 across" and "3 000 down"; the sum written out with yellow highlighter.
5. **Optical vs digital zoom (3:50-5:00).** Optical: the lens brings the view
   closer, the whole sensor still captures it - sharp. Digital: enlarges pixels
   already taken - blocky; it always costs quality. Cheap phones: zoom with your feet.
   > Board: the lesson's camera-zoom drawing.
   > Screen: the lesson's before/after slider (ba-g10-zoom-digital / optical) dragged slowly across; yellow ring on the stepped letters R25.
6. **Webcams (5:00-5:50).** A small camera sending live video to the
   computer: calls, classes, streaming. Built in above a laptop's screen with a
   light beside it, or clipped on and plugged into USB. Look for Full HD /
   1080p. Usually has a microphone built in.
   > Board: a laptop with the webcam ringed; a clip-on webcam on a monitor.
7. **Keeping it private (5:50-6:40).** Malware can switch it on. Slide the
   privacy shutter; check which apps may use the camera; notice the light.
   > Screen: Windows Settings, Privacy & security, Camera - the list of apps with their switches (yellow highlighter on the list; nothing changed).
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-08.2 Your voice, your face, your fingerprint (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#biometrics` (the comment is below the `w8Biometric` question).
**The pupil can afterwards:** choose a microphone, explain voice recognition as dictation and as commands, voice type in Word, and compare the four biometric devices with one advantage and one disadvantage.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "You *are* the password"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo unlocks her phone
   with her thumb and sends a voice note. Not one key pressed.
   > Board: a thumb on a phone; a voice-note wave; Clicky giving a thumbs up (his own, small).
2. **Microphones (0:30-1:30).** Sound into data. Built in (phones, tablets,
   laptops - not most desktops); a headset - close to the mouth, clearer, less
   room noise; a USB microphone - best sound for podcasts and music.
   > Board: the three microphones; noise squiggles getting smaller as the mic gets closer to the mouth.
3. **Voice recognition (1:30-2:40).** Software that turns speech into text,
   used as typing (dictation) or as a command ("Call Lerato", "Set an alarm for
   six"). Voice assistants work this way.
   > Board: a mouth, an arrow to "text", then two arrows: one to a document (DICTATION), one to a phone ringing (COMMAND).
4. **Voice typing in Word (2:40-4:00).** Click where the text goes; Home tab,
   Dictate; speak, saying "full stop" (or "period") and "comma"; Dictate again
   to stop; proofread. Good for slow typists, people who cannot type, busy
   hands. Limits: it mishears (noise, accents, names), needs a decent
   microphone and a quiet place - always proofread.
   > Screen: Word in the VM - click in the page, Home, Dictate (yellow ring on the button), dictate one sentence with a full stop, stop, then fix one misheard word.
   > Note: the VM needs a microphone for this take, or record it on the host with the same document; if dictation is not available, show the button and the steps only.
5. **Know, have, are (4:00-4:40).** A password is something you know, a card
   something you have, a fingerprint something you are. Biometrics measures a
   part of your body unique to you; a biometric device does the measuring.
   > Board: three columns - a brain, a card, a thumb - pink highlighter on "ARE".
6. **Four scanners (4:40-6:00).** Fingerprint (ridges; phones, banks, Home
   Affairs, gates, clocking in); facial (distances between eyes, nose, mouth;
   phones, laptops, airports); iris (the coloured ring - CAPS); retina (blood
   vessels at the back of the eye, lit by a beam - IEB). Both read the eye: the
   iris from the front, the retina at the back.
   > Board: an eye drawn in cross-section, the iris ringed in yellow and the retina at the back in blue; a fingertip with ridges; a face with dotted distance lines.
7. **Good and bad (6:00-7:20).** Good: cannot be forgotten, lost or lent;
   quick. Bad: a stolen fingerprint cannot be changed like a password; fails
   with a cut, dirty or wet finger, glasses, poor light; costs money; the
   stored data must be kept safe. Botha's Bakery: a floury finger at four in
   the morning.
   > Board: a two-column sticky-note list; Clicky with a floury handprint on him.
8. **Sign-off.**
