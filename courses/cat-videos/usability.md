## cat11-18.1 Five things that make a website easy to use (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after the marked-words question in section `#layout` (the comment `// VIDEO cat11-18.1`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** tell functionality from usability, and judge a page's readability, navigation, consistency, layout and typography - and see the same rules in their own documents.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · WEB`, title "Same buses. *Lost* customer."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:50).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo tries to book a
   bus ticket to Durban: pale grey text over a photo, no Book button,
   "Error: invalid input", an empty form. Lerato books on another site in two
   minutes.
   > Board: two phones side by side, a messy page and a clean one; Clicky lost on the messy one.
2. **Functionality and usability (0:50-2:00).** Does it do the job, and how
   easily can people do it? User-centred design: who, what, where, test it.
   Visitors scan.
   > Board: the gui-push-door drawing; highlighter on "how easily".
3. **Readability (2:00-3:10).** The good/poor table; contrast - black on
   white vs grey on white; same rules in a Word report.
   > Board: the same sentence written three times - grey on white, over a busy photo, black on white; the caps-shout drawing.
4. **Navigation (3:10-4:40).** Menu in one place, clear labels, logo home,
   search, working links, no dead ends. Breadcrumbs (Hansel and Gretel). The
   three-click rule - as a guide.
   > Board: the site-tree-breadcrumbs drawing, with Clicky clicking down three levels; the click-maze drawing.
5. **Consistency (4:40-5:40).** Same menu, colours, fonts, buttons and words -
   "Basket" vs "Cart". What people expect from other sites. Styles in Word,
   the slide master in PowerPoint.
   > Screen: in the VM, Word - apply Heading 1 to three headings, then change the Heading 1 style once and watch all three change.
6. **Layout, typography and colour (5:40-7:20).** The main thing at the top,
   grouped, white space, no clutter. Two or three fonts, sizes that show
   importance. A few colours with meaning, never colour alone.
   > Board: a page with a pop-up covering the button, then the same page cleaned up with white space; "Sold out" in red with the words added.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-18.2 Forms, phones and everyone - judging a website step by step (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#checklist` (the comment `// VIDEO cat11-18.2`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what makes a good online form, explain responsive design, list what makes a website accessible, and judge a site with the ten-step checklist.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · WEB`, title "Ten links called *click here*."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Back to Gogo's empty
   form. What should it have done?
   > Board: the empty form with "Error: invalid input" in red.
2. **Online forms (0:30-2:30).** Labels, required boxes marked, the right
   control (calendar, drop-down list, option buttons, tick boxes), helpful
   error messages next to the box, keep what was typed, ask only what is
   needed, a button that says what it does. The same rules in Word and
   Access forms.
   > Board: the bad form turning into the good one, one fix at a time; the forms-pile drawing.
3. **On a phone (2:30-3:40).** Responsive design - columns become one
   column, the menu becomes one button. The table: one column, big buttons,
   small pictures, the number keypad.
   > Board: a laptop page sliding into a phone shape; the fat-finger drawing.
4. **A website for everyone (3:40-5:40).** Web accessibility; the screen
   reader can only read what is written in. Alt text, captions, contrast,
   not colour alone, keyboard only, links that say where they go ("click
   here" ten times), labels on boxes. Alt text and Check Accessibility in
   Word.
   > Screen: in the VM, Word - right-click a picture, View Alt Text, type a description; Review, Check Accessibility.
5. **Judging a website, step by step (5:40-7:30).** The ten steps; Thabo's
   worked example of the shoe shop - two things done well, two to fix, each
   with a reason. Usable is not the same as true.
   > Board: a checklist with ten boxes being ticked; the pretty-scam drawing.
6. **Sign-off.**
