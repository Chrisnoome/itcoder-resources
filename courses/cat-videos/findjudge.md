## cat10-35.1 Can you trust this website? (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#judging` (the comment `// VIDEO cat10-35.1`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** name kinds of source, tell primary from secondary, use Wikipedia well, and judge a source with five questions.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAT`, title "*Three* websites. Three answers."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:45).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. One site: phones
   lower marks by 20%. Another: they raise marks by 30%. A third sells a study
   app for R199 a month. They cannot all be right.
   > Board: three browser windows with the three claims.
2. **Kinds of source (0:45-1:50).** People, print, electronic. Primary
   (first-hand) and secondary (someone else's write-up). The advantages and
   disadvantages table.
   > Board: three columns; highlighter on "first-hand".
3. **Wikipedia (1:50-2:50).** A wiki anyone can edit: a good start, not the
   end. The four steps: read, scroll to References, open and check, use those.
   > Board: the wiki-edit drawing.
   > Screen: in the VM, a Wikipedia article - scroll to the References section, click one reference.
4. **Five questions (2:50-5:50).** Who wrote it (authority); when (currency);
   why (purpose - inform, sell, persuade, entertain); is it fair (no bias); is
   it true (accuracy - the same fact in two other good sources). Search order
   proves nothing.
   > Board: five sticky notes, one per question, each flipped to show its name.
5. **Back to the hook (5:50-6:40).** The R199 app page fails "why"; the
   20% page has no author and no date.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-35.2 Sifting and summarising (about 5 min)

**Goes:** after section `#notes` (the comment `// VIDEO cat10-35.2`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** judge a web page's design, sift information against criteria, and summarise a source in their own words.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAT`, title "40 pages. Keep *four*."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo has 40 pages
   of printouts. He will use four.
2. **Design (0:30-1:30).** Readability, navigation, consistency, layout,
   typography - and good design does not make a page true.
   > Board: the pretty-scam drawing.
3. **Sifting (1:30-2:50).** Like a sieve. The five steps: does it answer a
   question; good source; current enough; already have it; label it.
   > Board: a sieve with pages falling through; highlighter on "meets your criteria".
4. **Summarising (2:50-4:30).** The six steps, then the worked example: the
   Pillay paragraph becomes one sentence with (Pillay, 2025).
   > Board: the paragraph with two key sentences highlighted, then the one-line summary.
5. **Sign-off.**
