## cat11-22.1 Working from anywhere (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#remote` - the comment after `w22Society`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** tell a virtual office, e-commuting, a mobile office and decentralisation of labour apart, say what a person needs to work this way, and give advantages and disadvantages for the worker, the employer and society.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOCIAL`, title "The office is *a laptop*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Lerato answers customers' e-mails for an online shoe shop in Cape Town. She has never been to Cape Town. She works from her bedroom in Pretoria, her boss checks in on a video call every Monday, and she has met nobody from the shop in person.
   > Board: a map of South Africa; a laptop in Pretoria joined by a dotted line to a shop in Cape Town. Clicky rides along the line.
2. **What ICTs do at work (0:40-1:40).** Two jobs: communication - e-mail for the record, chat for quick questions, video calls instead of driving to a meeting - and collaboration: people working on the same piece of work, like Phumlani's four CAT teachers writing one exam paper in a shared document.
   > Board: two sticky notes, COMMUNICATE and COLLABORATE; under the second, four stick figures around one document. Yellow highlighter on "same piece of work".
3. **Four names (1:40-3:40).** A virtual office - CAPS says remote office - is a business with no central building. E-commuting (e-working) is one person working from home and "travelling" to the office over the Internet, which needs remote access. A mobile office moves with you: the assessor writing her report in her car. Decentralisation of labour spreads work to wherever the workers are - the Durban contact centre answering calls for London.
   > Board: four boxes drawn one by one, each with its picture (an empty office block crossed out; a bedroom desk; a car; a globe with arrows to Durban). Highlighter on each name as it is said.
4. **What you need (3:40-4:40).** A computer, a fast and reliable connection, backup power for a power cut, remote access to the files, video calls and chat, and a quiet place.
   > Board: a checklist with six ticks appearing; Clicky ticks the last one.
5. **Good and bad, for whom (4:40-6:30).** For the worker: no traffic or taxi money, flexible hours - but lonely, and home and work mix. For the employer: a cheaper office, hire from anywhere - but harder to train and supervise, and home networks are less secure. For society: less traffic and pollution - but the canteens and taxis near offices lose customers, and people without power or data are left out.
   > Board: the three-row table filled in row by row. Pink highlighter on "who": an answer must say who it is good or bad for.
6. **Sign-off (6:30-7:00).** Work done with information can move out of the building. Work done with your hands, or face to face, mostly cannot.

## cat11-22.2 The gig economy - your boss is an app (about 6 min)

**Goes:** inside the CAPS section, after `#gig` - the comment after `w22GigSides`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what the gig economy is, follow one gig step by step, and give advantages and disadvantages for the worker.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOCIAL`, title "Five *stars* or no work"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:35).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo's cousin Sipho has no boss and no fixed hours. He delivers food on his scooter, and every delivery comes from an app.
   > Board: a scooter with a food box; a phone above it with a "New order" pop-up. Clicky in a helmet.
2. **What a gig is (0:35-1:30).** A gig is one short, separate job. The gig economy is all the work done this way - found through an app or a website instead of a permanent job: e-hailing and delivery riders, cleaners booked through an app, freelance designers and programmers. "Gig" is musicians' slang from the 1920s for one paid performance.
   > Board: three columns - transport and deliveries, services at home, freelancers online. Yellow highlighter on "short, separate jobs".
3. **One delivery, step by step (1:30-3:00).** The customer orders and pays the app. The app picks a free, nearby rider with good ratings and sets the price. Sipho fetches the food and rides - on his own scooter, petrol and data. The app keeps its share, often 20% or more. The customer gives stars; too few, and the app can stop sending work.
   > Board: five numbered steps; a R100 note cut in two pieces (20 and 80) at step 4; five stars at step 5, two of them falling off.
4. **Decentralised, and new work (3:00-3:50).** It is decentralisation of labour taken as far as it goes - no office, no fixed team. And it creates jobs: a phone and a scooter, and you can start next week.
   > Board: dots spread over a city map, each a worker.
5. **Good and bad (3:50-5:30).** For the worker: easy to start, own hours, income when jobs are scarce - but uncertain income, no medical aid, pension, UIF or paid leave, own costs, danger on the roads. For the customer: quick and convenient - but prices jump when demand is high. For the company: pays only for work done - but court cases about workers' rights.
   > Board: the table filled in. Pink highlighter on "no UIF" and "own costs".
6. **Sign-off (5:30-6:00).** Your boss is an app, and your performance review is five little stars.
