## cat10-34.1 From a topic to questions (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#dataq` (the comment `// VIDEO cat10-34.1`, after `s34DataQ`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** understand a task with five questions, narrow a theme to a main question and sub-questions, and sort questions into the four kinds.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAT`, title "Two *billion* results. Now what?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:45).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Ms Naidoo's PAT
   theme: Technology and learning. Thabo typed it in - two billion results. A
   theme is an ocean; a good question is a cup.
   > Board: the bigdata-hose drawing; then a cup.
2. **Understand the task (0:45-2:15).** The five questions in order, worked
   for Thabo, ending in his task definition.
   > Board: five numbered lines filled in one at a time.
3. **Theme to main question (2:15-3:30).** Theme, topic, focus, main question.
   Thabo's main question. What makes a good one: not yes or no, narrow enough,
   answerable.
   > Board: a funnel with the four levels. Highlighter on the main question.
4. **Sub-questions (3:30-4:20).** Break the main question into parts; answer
   them all and you answer it. Thabo's six.
5. **Four kinds (4:20-6:20).** Fact (What? When? Where? Who? How many?),
   explore (Why? How?), predict (What if?), judgement (What would be best?) -
   one of Thabo's questions for each. Judgement questions become the
   conclusion.
   > Board: a four-step ladder, easiest at the bottom; Clicky climbs it.
6. **Data questions (6:20-6:50).** How many? Most popular? Least common? How
   many more than? Average? A spreadsheet answers them.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-34.2 A questionnaire that works (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#writing` (the comment `// VIDEO cat10-34.2`, after `w34Improve`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** tell a survey, a questionnaire and an interview apart, tell open from closed questions, and fix a bad questionnaire question.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAT`, title "Is this a *bad* question?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. "Don't you think
   phones are really useful for homework and studying, yes or no?" Spot three
   problems.
   > Board: the question on a sticky note; three red circles appear later.
2. **Survey, questionnaire, interview (0:30-1:50).** The whole job; the tool;
   one person with follow-ups. Advantages and disadvantages of each.
   > Board: the soc-interview drawing; a two-column table.
3. **Open and closed (1:50-2:50).** Fixed choices, easy to count; own words,
   more detail. A questionnaire is mostly closed.
4. **Six rules (2:50-4:40).** One thing per question; do not lead; cover
   every answer (the 0-1, 1-2, 2-3 hours mistake); plain words; ask only what
   you need; keep it short.
   > Screen: optional - Microsoft Forms in the VM, building one closed question ("How useful is your phone for doing homework?" with four choices). Click New Form, Add new, Choice, type the question and the four options.
5. **The fix (4:40-5:30).** The hook question rewritten, as in the lesson's
   reveal.
6. **Sign-off.**
