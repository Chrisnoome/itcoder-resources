Lesson: `content/cattheory12/capsp2.php` (CAPS only - the whole lesson is a
CAPS board section). Two videos: Section A, and one mark per fact. No screen
recordings - all board. Board in the CAT marker style with Clicky

## cat12-26.1 Section A without losing marks (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#sectionA` - the comment after `q26Not`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** describe how Paper 2 is built, answer the three Section A formats, and correct a modified true/false statement for the mark.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAPER 2`, title "*False* is not an answer"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Lerato knew the statement was false. She wrote "False". She got nothing.
   > Board: an answer book line "3.2 False", Clicky looking at an empty mark box.
2. **The paper (0:30-1:40).** Three hours, 150 marks, ten questions. Section A 25 (multiple choice 10, matching 10, true/false 5); Section B 75 (Systems 25, Internet and networks 15, Information Management 10, Social Implications 10, Solution Development 15); Section C 50 - two scenarios of 25. The cover's rules: number as the paper does, a new page per question.
   > Board: the ten questions as a bar; highlighter on each section total.
3. **Multiple choice (1:40-2:50).** Letter only. The bold NOT: circle it first. Worked: "Which ONE is NOT an input device?" - webcam, scanner, projector, microphone. Thabo picked the webcam; the answer is the projector.
   > Board: the four options; highlighter on NOT; a circle drawn round it; Clicky crosses out the three input devices.
4. **Matching (2:50-3:40).** Ten descriptions, twenty terms A-T; half are spares that look right - RAM beside ROM. Read all twenty, match the sure ones, cross them off.
   > Board: two columns, the spares drawn faintly; pairs joined with lines.
5. **Modified true/false (3:40-5:20).** The three steps. Worked: "A firewall makes a copy of your files ..." - False, backup. False alone: nothing. "Backup" alone: the mark. Adding "not": nothing.
   > Board: the statement with "firewall" underlined; "backup" written over it; three answer lines with a tick or a cross each.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-26.2 Paper 2 - one mark per fact (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#nothing` - the comment after `w26Fix`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** write a 2-mark answer with two different facts, and avoid the answers that earn nothing.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · PAPER 2`, title "Two marks, *two* facts"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Four answers to the same 2-mark question. One earns 2, one earns 1, two earn nothing. Which is which?
   > Board: four answer cards face down, Clicky about to turn them.
2. **What the memo does (0:40-2:20).** A list of facts, "any two". One mark per fact; two different facts; the same fact twice is one; only the first facts asked for are marked; a paragraph read as a whole; other words fine; no half marks. Nothing over 3 marks; most parts 2.
   > Board: a memo list with "any two" and ticks; highlighter on "any two".
3. **The worked example (2:20-3:40).** Cloud storage for the school's photos: the 2-mark answer, the 1-mark answer (one fact twice), "cheaper and faster" (0), "Use Google Drive" (0).
   > Board: the four cards turned over, a mark written on each.
4. **What earns nothing (3:40-6:00).** "Faster", "cheaper", "easier" on their own - give the reason (the SSD, e-mail and online-form examples). Brand names - word processor, not Microsoft Word. Repeating the question - the hotspot. Not about CAT - working from home.
   > Board: each bad answer crossed out, its better version written beneath; highlighter on "because" and "so" in the better versions.
5. **Command words (6:00-7:20).** Name, give, explain (what it is + what it does), define, why, suggest, motivate. Combined parts: two halves, two marks. Explaining a UPS: not the letters - the two facts.
   > Board: the command-word table built row by row; the exam-acronym drawing for the UPS.
6. **Sign-off.**
