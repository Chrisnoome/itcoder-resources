Lesson: `content/cattheory12/webvsinstalled.php`. Three videos: the
six-point comparison, recommending software for a scenario, and user-centred
design (CAPS). Board in the CAT marker style with Clicky
(brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow highlighter on any words on screen being
talked about. Everything below is in the lesson text.

## cat12-06.1 Web-based or installed - side by side (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#compare` - the comment after `wWebUpdatesDiff`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** tell a web-based from an installed application with two questions, and compare them on access, updates, storage, cost, the Internet and privacy.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "In the *browser* or on the laptop?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Mr Botha's accounting program: paid once, nine years old, works with no Internet, only at the bakery. Zanele wants an online one at R450 a month. Who is right? Both, about something.
   > Board: the bakery laptop on one side, a cloud with a phone and a home laptop on the other; Clicky on a see-saw between them.
2. **Two kinds - the recap (0:40-1:40).** Grade 10 and 11 met both. Installed (stand-alone): runs on the device, work on its drive - Excel in the lab, Photoshop, the bakery's accounting program. Web-based: runs on the company's servers, used through a browser, work online - Google Docs, Word for the web, Canva, online banking, SARS eFiling. Two questions: where does it run, where is my work? Grade 12 asks you to compare and recommend.
   > Board: the webapp-waiter doodle; the two questions written large; highlighter on "run" and "kept".
3. **Six points (1:40-5:20).** Access, updates, storage (15 GB free in a Google account), cost (once-off, subscription, adverts and data), the Internet (needed, slow, data), privacy and control. Then working together and features and power.
   > Board: the lesson's comparison table, one row at a time; Clicky ticking the winning side for each row; the cloud-someone-else doodle at the privacy row; highlighter on each row heading.
4. **Advantage, disadvantage or limitation (5:20-6:30).** "Needs the Internet" is a limitation; "during a power cut the router goes off, so the bakery cannot invoice" is the disadvantage. Never the same point turned around.
   > Board: three labelled boxes; the router with a lightning bolt; a crossed-out "mirror" answer. Highlighter on "for whom".
5. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-06.2 Recommending software for a scenario (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#choosing` - the comment after `wWebSchool`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** sort out the in-between cases, ask the six choosing questions, and write a recommendation with reasons from the scenario.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "\"Web apps are *modern*\" earns nothing"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Lerato's Word saves to OneDrive. Is she using a web app?
   > Board: Lerato's laptop with a Word window and an arrow to a cloud; a big question mark.
2. **The line is blurring (0:30-2:10).** Installed apps that save online (still installed); Microsoft 365 rented, checking in about once a month; Google Docs offline, set up before the connection goes; progressive web apps with their own icon; a banking app that is installed but needs the bank's servers. In a test, answer the question asked.
   > Board: five little cards placed on a line from "installed" to "web-based"; highlighter on "where does it run".
3. **Six questions (2:10-3:20).** Reliable Internet? Working together? Which devices? Power or special features? Cost and data? How private is the data - the POPI Act?
   > Board: the fork-road doodle; six numbered sticky notes; Clicky pinning them up.
4. **The worked recommendation (3:20-5:20).** Botha's Bakery: the facts; the recommendation - online accounting; three reasons from his situation; dealing with the downside - a UPS for the router, a strong password and two-step verification, a company that follows the POPI Act. What makes it good: a clear choice, his reasons, the disadvantage handled.
   > Board: the facts as a list; the recommendation in a box; each reason linked by an arrow to a fact; highlighter on "his situation".
5. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-06.3 User-centred design in a database form and a report (about 5 min, CAPS)

**Goes:** after the CAPS section `#ucd` - the comment after the BoardSection.
**The pupil can afterwards:** recall what user-centred design is, and suggest user-centred changes to a database form and to a document that presents information.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "Designed for *Zanele*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Zanele types bakery orders into a database form all day, while customers talk on the phone. Every badly placed field costs her a second - hundreds of times a day.
   > Board: Zanele with a headset, eyes darting around a messy form; Clicky holding a stopwatch.
2. **The recap (0:30-1:10).** Grade 11: who are the users, what do they do most, where and on what - test with real users, fix, test again. A website in one line: same menu, readable, works on a phone and little data, text for every picture. Grade 12: use it in a website, a database form and a document.
   > Board: a circle of four arrows; three labelled boxes - website, form, document; highlighter on "test with real users".
3. **A database form (1:10-3:20).** Fields in the order the information arrives; a Tab order to match; choose, do not type - combo box, check box, date picker; default values for the usual answer; a validation rule with a plain message; only the fields needed, clear labels, required ones marked.
   > Board: the gui-bad-form drawing turning into gui-good-form, one fix at a time; highlighter on each fix.
4. **A document (3:20-4:30).** Know the reader - the governing body, not a WhatsApp; headings with styles and a table of contents; charts instead of walls of numbers, the right chart; white space and the same fonts throughout.
   > Board: a wall-of-text page beside a page with headings and a chart; Clicky fanning itself in relief.
5. **Sign-off.**
