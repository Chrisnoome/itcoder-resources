## cat11-23.1 What AI is - and why it can be confidently wrong (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#chatbots` - the comment after `w23Confident`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what AI is, tell rules from learning from examples, follow a spam filter learning step by step, and explain how a chatbot answers and why it hallucinates.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOCIAL`, title "Wrong - and *sure* of it"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:45).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. It is ten at night and Thabo is stuck on his CAT homework. A chatbot explains it in five seconds - better than his notes. Then he asks it about his own school. It invents a principal and a swimming pool, just as confidently.
   > Board: a phone with two chat bubbles - one with a tick, one with a cross; both with the same smiley. Clicky looks from one to the other.
2. **What AI is (0:45-1:50).** AI is software that learns from data to make decisions or create things: recognising faces and speech, understanding and writing language, predicting, deciding, creating. It is code and a model - a huge set of numbers - running on ordinary computers. Not a robot with a brain.
   > Board: five small icons in a row (face, speech bubble, crystal ball, signpost, paintbrush). Highlighter on "learns from data".
3. **Rules or examples (1:50-3:10).** Write the rules - IF the mark is below 30 THEN "Fail" - when the rules are known. Or give thousands of examples and let the computer find the patterns: machine learning. Try writing rules for your best friend's face in any light. Nobody can; a computer given thousands of photos can learn it.
   > Board: the ai-rules-vs-examples drawing, left side then right side.
4. **A spam filter learns (3:10-4:40).** People mark millions of e-mails spam or not spam - the training data. The computer finds the patterns. What it learnt is kept as a model. A new e-mail arrives and the model predicts. "Not spam" is one more example. It predicts - it does not know; and it is only as good as its examples (GIGO).
   > Board: five numbered steps with an envelope moving down them. Pink highlighter on "predicts".
5. **Where you meet it (4:40-5:30).** Your feed, face unlock, the keyboard's next word, voice assistants, translation, map apps, your bank's "Did you make this payment?", the AI summary on a results page, chatbots.
   > Board: a phone surrounded by small labelled icons appearing one by one.
6. **How a chatbot answers (5:30-7:00).** It was trained on a huge amount of text and learnt which words usually follow which. You type a prompt. It predicts a likely first word, then the next, very fast, and stops when the answer seems complete. So it writes text that sounds likely - it does not look facts up. When it lacks the facts, it still writes something that sounds right: a made-up quote, a book that does not exist, a wrong name. That is a hallucination.
   > Board: a sentence building word by word on a sticky-note strip; the last word is a wrong name, circled in red marker. Yellow highlighter on "sounds likely".
7. **Sign-off (7:00-7:30).** Check anything that matters - a date, a number, a name, a source - against a source you trust.

## cat11-23.2 Using AI honestly at school (about 5 min)

**Goes:** after section `#honest` - the comment after `w23Rules`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what academic integrity is, explain why AI-written work handed in is plagiarism, and give the six steps for using AI honestly.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOCIAL`, title "Your work, *your* words"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Kagiso says: "A chatbot wrote my essay, but I changed ten words in every paragraph - so now it is mine." Is he right?
   > Board: an essay page with a few words crossed out and replaced in marker. Clicky holding a big question mark.
2. **Academic integrity (0:40-1:40).** Being honest in your schoolwork: the work you hand in is your own; you credit every source, AI included; you follow the rules for each task. AI's work handed in as your own is plagiarism - like copying a website. Changing a few words does not make it yours.
   > Board: three sticky notes; highlighter on "your own".
3. **Six steps (1:40-3:50).** Check the rules - your teacher decides, and in tests and exams it is usually not allowed. Use it to learn, not to skip learning: explanations, examples, quizzes - then answer yourself. Do the work yourself. Check its facts; it hallucinates. Say how you used it. Keep private things private: no names, ID numbers or marks.
   > Board: a numbered list built step by step; Clicky ticks each.
4. **Detectors and proof (3:50-4:30).** AI detectors are unreliable and accuse honest pupils too. The best proof the work is yours: you can explain every part of it.
   > Board: a detector dial wobbling; then a pupil explaining a page.
5. **Sign-off (4:30-5:00).** Use the chatbot to understand, close it, and write it yourself.
