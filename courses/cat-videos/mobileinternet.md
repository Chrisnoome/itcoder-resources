## cat11-14.1 Hotspots - public ones, and your own phone (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#otherWays` - the comment after `q14Pocket`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say which of a phone's radios get it online; give advantages and disadvantages of a public hotspot and stay safe on one; say what a personal hotspot and tethering are, compare the three ways to tether, switch a hotspot on step by step, and name its catches; say what a pocket router and a dongle are.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · INTERNET`, title "Share your *phone's* Internet"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Lerato is at Gogo's for the weekend with an assignment due. No Wi-Fi, no SIM in her laptop - but four bars on her phone. And at the mall, Thabo's phone offers "Mall_Free_WiFi" and "Mall Free WiFi". Which is real?
   > Board: Gogo's house with no router; a phone showing two almost identical Wi-Fi names; Clicky with a magnifying glass.
2. **Recap - the radios (0:40-1:40).** Mobile data: a SIM connects to the nearest cell tower; works almost anywhere with signal; pay for every MB. A hotspot shares a connection over Wi-Fi. NFC (centimetres) and Bluetooth (about 10 m) join two devices close by; Wi-Fi and cellular get you online.
   > Board: the mob-range drawing - rings from a phone. Yellow highlighter on "gets you online?".
3. **Public hotspots (1:40-3:30).** Wi-Fi a place shares: a mall, library, airport, Gautrain station. Free and open, a password at the counter, a login page with an SMS code, or a paid voucher. Good: saves your data, often faster, helps people with no data. Other side: strangers on the network, fake hotspots, slow when busy, limits, login pages want your details. Why the fake one is dangerous: anyone can name a Wi-Fi anything, and everything you send passes through the criminal's device.
   > Board: a food court with one router and a hundred phones; a criminal's laptop at the next table, named like the mall's Wi-Fi.
4. **Staying safe, step by step (3:30-4:30).** Check the exact name with staff; choose "Public network" on a laptop; https and the padlock; banking only on your own data; forget the network and switch off auto-connect; keep updated. (IEB: a VPN.)
   > Screen: in the VM, join a Wi-Fi from the taskbar and choose "Public network". Yellow highlighter on the choice.
5. **Your phone as a hotspot (4:30-6:40).** A personal hotspot: the phone makes its own Wi-Fi, with a name and password, and shares its mobile data - a small router. Tethering, three ways: Wi-Fi hotspot (several devices, drains the battery), USB (one device, the laptop charges the phone), Bluetooth (slow, little battery). CAPS: a phone as a router; IEB: a phone as a modem. Step by step: Settings, Network & internet (Connections), Hotspot & tethering; set the name and a strong password; switch on; join from the laptop; set the laptop's connection as metered; switch off when done. iPhone: Settings, Personal Hotspot. The catches: eats the bundle, battery and heat, shared speed, needs signal, a weak password lets neighbours in, some bundles forbid it.
   > Board: the phone's hotspot screens drawn one per step, step number in a circle; then the three-ways table.
   > Screen: in the VM, Settings > Network & internet > Wi-Fi > the network > Metered connection: On.
6. **Pocket routers and dongles (6:40-7:30).** A pocket router (MiFi): a SIM, a battery and a Wi-Fi hotspot for several devices - your phone keeps its battery and data. A dongle: a USB modem with a SIM for one laptop. The LTE or 5G router is the home version. Ms Naidoo's school trip: four laptops, one pocket router.
   > Board: the comms-mifi picture, redrawn; a dongle in a laptop's USB port.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-14.2 What the G means - 4G, LTE and 5G (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#fiveG` - the comment after `w14FiveG`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what each cellphone generation added; say that LTE is 4G; give the speeds of 3G, 4G and 5G roughly; compare 4G and 5G and explain why 5G is not everywhere yet.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · INTERNET`, title "What does the *G* mean?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Look next to your signal bars: 4G, LTE or 5G. The G means generation.
   > Board: a phone's status bar, drawn big; yellow highlighter on "LTE".
2. **The generations (0:30-2:00).** 1G, 1980s: calls on the move. 2G, 1990s: clearer calls and SMS - South Africa's first networks in 1994. 3G, 2000s: the first real mobile Internet and smartphones - now being switched off. 4G, 2010s: fast enough for video. 5G, 2020s: much faster, less delay, many more devices.
   > Board: a timeline with five stepping stones; Clicky hopping along.
3. **4G and LTE (2:00-2:50).** LTE, Long Term Evolution, is the technology 4G uses - "LTE" on the phone means 4G. LTE-A or 4G+ is a faster kind. An LTE router is a router with a SIM for 4G. Speeds: 3G a few Mbps; 4G about 10 to 50 Mbps; 5G often 100 Mbps or more. All wireless, cellular, paid with mobile data.
   > Board: "LTE = 4G" on a sticky note.
4. **What 5G does better (2:50-4:00).** Faster downloads; much less delay (lag) for games and video calls; many more devices at once - a full stadium, or thousands of smart meters (up to a million devices in a square kilometre); fibre-like speeds at home with a 5G router.
   > Board: a stadium full of phones, all still working.
5. **Why it is not everywhere (4:00-5:20).** Four steps: 5G's fastest radio waves reach less far; so each tower covers a smaller area; so many more towers are needed, at great cost; so the cities come first. And you need a 5G phone or router - a 4G phone under a 5G tower stays on 4G.
   > Board: a big 4G circle round one tower, beside many small 5G circles round many towers; then the Learn / Memorise table, row by row.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-14.3 Where your data goes - and how to save it (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#savingData` - the comment after `r14Laptop`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a cellular data service is; compare prepaid and contract; explain bundles, validity and out-of-bundle rates; name ways to pay less; say roughly how much data everyday things use; find what uses the data; save data step by step.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · INTERNET`, title "Where did my *data* go?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. It is the 20th, and Thabo's bundle is gone - again. Where did 2 GB go?
   > Board: an empty fuel gauge labelled "data"; Clicky turning out empty pockets.
2. **Paying for data (0:30-2:30).** A cellular data service: data a cellphone network sells; the network is your ISP. Prepaid: pay first, buy a data bundle; every bundle has a validity period and unused data expires. Contract: usually 24 months, a fixed monthly amount, extra data on the bill. Out of bundle: Thabo's network sells 1 GB for R89 and charges 49c per MB out of bundle - R490 for the same GB, more than five times as much. The networks must warn you and ask before charging it. Pay less: bigger bundles cost less per GB, night bundles, app bundles, zero-rated sites.
   > Board: two price tags, R89 and R490, for the same 1 GB. Yellow highlighter on "expires".
3. **Text is tiny, video is huge (2:30-4:00).** A text message: a few KB. A one-minute voice note: about 0.2 MB. A web page: 2 to 3 MB. An hour of music: 50 to 150 MB. An hour of video calling: about 300 MB. An hour of video: 300 to 500 MB low quality, 1.5 to 3 GB in HD. A big update: hundreds of MB to GB. Rough numbers - the pattern is what matters.
   > Board: bars of very different lengths, a tiny one for text and a huge one for HD video.
4. **The hidden users (4:00-5:00).** Autoplay videos; WhatsApp auto-download; app updates and backups on mobile data; background data; a laptop on the hotspot. Finding out: Settings, Network & internet (Connections), Data usage; the worst app at the top; switch off its background data. Thabo: 1.4 GB on autoplay videos. Lerato: 1.5 GB in one evening - Windows updates and cloud syncing; the fix is a metered connection and pausing syncing.
   > Board: the phone's Data usage screen drawn, apps listed by MB.
5. **Saving data, step by step (5:00-6:40).** Wi-Fi when you can; Data Saver (iPhone: Low Data Mode); autoplay off; WhatsApp auto-download off on mobile data; updates and backups on Wi-Fi only; lower video quality; download on Wi-Fi for later; a data warning; mobile data off when not needed. On a laptop on your hotspot: metered, and pause syncing.
   > Board: a numbered checklist, Clicky ticking each line.
6. **Sign-off.**
