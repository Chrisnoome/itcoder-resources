# CAT 12 lesson 15: Computer crime and the criminals - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory12/compcrime.php`. Two videos: Internet fraud
scams, and bots, zombies and DDoS. Board in the CAT marker style with Clicky
(brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow highlighter on any words on screen being
talked about. No screen recordings - no real scam site, phone number or
bank is shown; every message on the board is drawn and made up. Everything
below is in the lesson text.

## cat12-15.1 Scams - the hook in every one (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#scams` - the comment after `w15FakeShop`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** name the common Internet fraud scams, spot the five hooks they all use, and say what to check before paying or handing over goods.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · CYBERCRIME`, title "Spot the *hook*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo sells his old phone for R2 500. "Paid! My driver collects in ten minutes." The proof of payment looks perfect. Should he hand it over?
   > Board: a WhatsApp bubble with a drawn proof of payment; Clicky hovering over "ten minutes", yellow highlighter on it.
2. **Internet fraud (0:40-1:20).** Tricking people online into handing over money, goods or details - the computer crime most South Africans meet. Phishing steals your details; a scam goes straight for your money.
   > Board: the definition written out; highlighter on "money, goods or details".
3. **The common scams (1:20-4:30).** One at a time, with the giveaway: the fake online shop; fake proof of payment; job and task scams; investment scams; romance scams; prize and inheritance scams; changed bank details; fake voice calls made with AI (the Hong Kong deepfake call, 2024).
   > Board: eight cards dealt out one by one, each with a small drawing and its giveaway underlined; the cyber-hook doodle on the last.
4. **The five hooks (4:30-5:40).** Greed or hope, urgency, fear, trust, payment that cannot be reversed. A padlock does not make a shop honest.
   > Board: a fishing line with five hooks, each labelled; the padlock-thief doodle.
5. **Before you pay (5:40-7:00).** Stop; search the name with "scam"; check on a number you already have; pay by card or cash on delivery; selling - wait for the money in your own app. Back to Thabo: he waits, and the "buyer" vanishes.
   > Board: five numbered steps; then Thabo's phone, the banking app showing no payment, Clicky giving a thumbs up.
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-15.2 Bots, zombies and DDoS (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#bots` - the comment after `w15Zombie`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain a DoS and a DDoS attack, tell a bot, a zombie, a botnet and a bot herder apart, and say how to keep a device out of a botnet.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · CYBERCRIME`, title "Is your laptop a *zombie*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Botha's Bakery's phone rings a thousand times an hour - all pranks. The bread is fine. Nobody real can get through to order.
   > Board: a phone buried under a pile of ringing phones; Clicky covering its ears.
2. **Denial of service (0:30-1:40).** Flood a website with requests so real users cannot get in. Nothing stolen, nothing broken - the service is denied. From one computer, easy to block.
   > Board: one laptop sending arrows at a website; a hand blocks it; highlighter on "denied".
3. **DDoS (1:40-3:00).** Thousands of devices at once, all over the world; each sends a little; no one address to block. September 2019: South African ISPs flooded; a month later, banks - with ransom notes. Why: ransom, protest, rivals, distraction. Defence: a DDoS protection service.
   > Board: the threats-botnet drawing; highlighter on "distributed".
4. **How a computer becomes a zombie (3:00-4:40).** Malware gets on; the bot waits for orders; the device is a zombie; thousands make a botnet; the bot herder gives the order. Zombie = one device, botnet = the army; a chatbot is something else.
   > Board: the threats-zombie-army drawing built step by step; three labels with highlighter: zombie, botnet, bot herder.
5. **What botnets do, and the signs (4:40-5:50).** DDoS, spam, password guessing, crypto mining, rented out. Cameras and routers with factory passwords (Mirai, 2016). Signs: slow, fan running, data used up, spam "from" you.
   > Board: a camera with a sticky note "password: admin"; a fan spinning on a laptop.
6. **Keep out of the army (5:50-6:40).** Updates - router too; change factory passwords; anti-malware; official downloads. The owner is a victim twice. Emotet taken down, 2021.
   > Board: three ticks next to a router, a laptop and a camera.
7. **Sign-off.**
