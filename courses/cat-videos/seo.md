Lesson: `content/cattheory12/seo.php`. Three videos: the operators and
filters, searching with pictures, and SEO (an IEB section, taught to all).
Board in the CAT marker style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow

## cat12-13.1 Search like an expert - operators and filters (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#tools` - the comment after `s13Settings`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain why the first result is not always the best, use the eight operators, and filter results by date with Tools.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SEARCHING`, title "Find the *real* timetable"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo types "matric timetable" - and gets an advert for tutors, an old news story and a blurry photo from Facebook. Which one is real?
   > Board: a results page drawn as three cards; Clicky hovering, puzzled. Yellow highlighter on "Sponsored".
2. **Who comes first (0:40-2:00).** The crawler visits pages and follows links; the index; ranking. The four questions: does it match, is it trusted (links are votes), does it work well, who is asking. Sponsored = paid; organic = ranked by the search engine.
   > Board: the seo-crawl-index drawing, then seo-link-votes; highlighter on "organic" and "Sponsored".
3. **Why first is not always best (2:00-2:50).** It may be an advert; popular is not true; it may be old; your history shapes it; some sites use tricks.
   > Board: five short lines appear one by one beside the first result card.
4. **The four from Grade 10 (2:50-3:30).** " ", -, site:, OR - one example each.
   > Board: the four-row table; highlighter on each operator as it is said.
5. **Four more (3:30-5:00).** filetype:, intitle:, before: and after:, the star inside quotes. The two rules: no space after the colon or the minus; OR in capitals.
   > Screen: type `filetype:pdf matric timetable` and `"the * of the Springboks"`; highlighter on the operator each time.
6. **Building Thabo's search (5:00-6:20).** Step by step: matric exam timetable - add site:gov.za - add filetype:pdf - add after:2026-01-01. Watch the results shrink and turn official.
   > Screen: the search built word by word, results refreshing each time.
7. **Tools and settings (6:20-7:40).** The tabs (News); Tools - Any time - Past week; Verbatim; the Advanced Search page; SafeSearch; Ctrl+F on a long page.
   > Screen: Tools, Any time, Past week clicked in turn; the Advanced Search page shown for a few seconds; Ctrl+F on a long article.
8. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-13.2 Searching with pictures (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#images` - the comment after `r13NoMatch`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** tell searching for pictures from searching with a picture, use the usage rights filter, run a reverse image search and say what it can and cannot prove.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SEARCHING`, title "Is this photo *really* new?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A "breaking news" storm photo is going round. It was online in 2019.
   > Board: the fake-photo doodle; a calendar page saying 2019 with a yellow highlighter band.
2. **Searching FOR pictures (0:30-2:00).** Words in, pictures out: the Images tab. Filters: size, colour and type, time, usage rights - pictures you may reuse; still check the licence and give credit.
   > Screen: search `baobab tree`, Images tab, Tools, Size - Large, then Usage rights - Creative Commons licences.
3. **Searching WITH a picture (2:00-3:00).** Google Lens: name a plant, find a product, translate a sign.
   > Screen: Lens on a photo of a plant (a picture from the VM's own folder, no people).
4. **Reverse image search (3:00-4:40).** Where else is this picture? Steps: right-click, Search with Google Lens (or the camera icon on Google Images); find the image source / exact matches; look at the dates and sites. TinEye sorts oldest first. What it tells you: old or new, who took it, fake profiles, a bigger copy.
   > Screen: right-click a landmark photo on a news page, Search with Google Lens, exact matches.
   > Board: the search-reverse drawing.
5. **Its limits (4:40-5:40).** Only pictures in the index; edits may not match; AI pictures are new; no match does not prove it is real; uploading hands the photo over.
   > Board: a WhatsApp bubble with a photo and a big "No match ≠ real" in marker, highlighter on "≠".
6. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-13.3 SEO - how websites climb the list (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after the IEB section - the comment after `w13Stuffing`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what SEO is and why a business wants it, give white-hat improvements, and name the black-hat tricks and what happens to sites that use them.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SEO`, title "Page *one*, or nowhere"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Botha's Bakery is on page four for "bakery Centurion". Nobody goes to page four.
   > Board: the seo-page-two doodle; Clicky digging on page four.
2. **What SEO is (0:30-1:40).** Improving a website so that it appears higher in the search results, to get more visitors. Organic, not paid - paying is advertising. Why a business cares.
   > Board: the definition written out; yellow highlighter on "higher" and "more visitors".
3. **White hat (1:40-3:40).** Useful, updated content; the words people type; a clear title and description; alt text; small photos (fast); mobile-friendly; https; earned links; a business listing with reviews.
   > Board: the seo-snippet drawing; then a phone with the bakery's page, each improvement ticked.
4. **Black hat (3:40-5:00).** Keyword stuffing, hidden text, bought links and link farms, cloaking. Then: spotted, punished, dropped or removed, months to recover, visitors leave.
   > Board: the seo-stuffed doodle; the seo-cloak-linkfarm drawing; a lift going down.
5. **Sign-off.**
