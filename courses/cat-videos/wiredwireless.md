## cat11-12.1 Wired or wireless? Five ways to compare (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#choosing` - the comment after `w12Lab`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** compare wired and wireless connections by speed, range, security, cost and interference; name the causes of interference on Wi-Fi and on cables; choose a connection for a job and give reasons.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "Cable or *Wi-Fi*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo's online game freezes in his bedroom, on Wi-Fi, two walls from the router. Lerato plays the same game in the lounge on a cable, and hers never freezes. Same Internet - so why?
   > Board: a house cut-away, Thabo's room with a frozen screen, Lerato with a cable to the router; Clicky scratching his head.
2. **Quick recap (0:40-1:20).** Wired: a network cable (copper, about 100 m) or fibre (light in glass, kilometres). Wireless: radio waves - Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC; phones also use 4G and 5G.
   > Board: a cable and a fibre strand; radio waves from a router.
3. **Speed and range (1:20-2:50).** A cable gives each device its own full speed - 1 Gbps to each desk in a lab. Wi-Fi is shared - thirty pupils, one milkshake, one straw - and weaker with distance and walls. Range: cable about 100 m, fibre kilometres, but only where laid; Wi-Fi tens of metres, but you can move.
   > Board: thirty straws in one milkshake. Yellow highlighter on "shared".
4. **Security and cost (2:50-4:10).** To get at a cable, someone must plug in inside the building. Wi-Fi leaks into the passage and the street, so it needs a strong password and the router scrambles (encrypts) the data. Cables cost money to buy and install, and moving a desk can mean new cables; one access point serves a whole classroom.
   > Board: Wi-Fi rings spilling into the street, a stranger with a laptop; a padlock on the router.
5. **Interference (4:10-5:30).** Anything that disturbs a signal, so data is spoilt and sent again - slower. Wi-Fi: walls and floors (metal worst), distance, the microwave oven, Bluetooth, cordless phones, the neighbours' Wi-Fi. Copper cables: power cables and motors. Fibre: none. Fixing weak Wi-Fi in four steps: move the router to the middle, up high; away from the microwave; an access point or mesh Wi-Fi for far rooms; a cable for devices that never move.
   > Board: the wifi-microwave joke; then the four steps numbered.
6. **Choosing for the job (5:30-6:40).** Four questions: does it move? how far, through what? how much does steadiness matter? cost, and can cables be laid? Phumlani's new lab: 30 desktops, cables. The library: laptops and tablets that move, Wi-Fi with a password. Most networks use both. Back to Thabo: walls, distance, interference and sharing.
   > Board: the five-row Learn / Memorise table, filled in row by row.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-12.2 Mbps, Gbps and reading the advert (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#adverts` - the comment after `w12UpTo`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** read a speed in Mbps and Gbps; tell bits from bytes; tell download from upload speed; work out a download time; read an Internet advert and say what it does not tell you.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "What does *100 Mbps* mean?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Mr Botha has a flyer: fibre, up to 100 Mbps, R799 a month. Is 100 a lot? And what does "up to" mean?
   > Board: the flyer on the bakery counter; Clicky peering at the small star.
2. **Transmission speed (0:40-2:00).** How much data a connection moves each second, in bits per second. A bit is the smallest unit; 8 bits make a byte. Mbps - millions of bits a second; 1 Gbps = 1 000 Mbps. A 100 Mbps line moves ten times as much as 10 Mbps.
   > Board: the bandwidth-pipes drawing, a thin pipe and a fat one. Yellow highlighter on "1 Gbps = 1 000 Mbps".
3. **Watch the b (2:00-2:50).** Small b = bits, for speeds. Capital B = bytes, for file sizes and bundles. 50 Mbps is a speed; 50 MB is a file.
   > Board: a giant small "b" and a giant capital "B", each with an arrow to an example.
4. **Download and upload (2:50-3:40).** Download: data to you - pages, streaming. Upload: data from you - posting a video, your face in a video call. 50/25 Mbps: 50 down, 25 up. Rough speeds: Bluetooth a few Mbps, 4G 10 to 50, 5G often 100 or more, home fibre 25 Mbps to 1 Gbps, a lab cable 1 Gbps.
   > Board: two arrows, down and up, with the numbers.
5. **How long will it take (3:40-5:00).** Thabo's 2 GB update on a 20 Mbps line: 2 000 MB, times 8 = 16 000 megabits, divided by 20 = 800 seconds, about 13 minutes. Longer in real life: shared line, Wi-Fi in his room.
   > Board: the four steps worked one at a time; yellow highlighter on "times 8".
6. **Reading the advert (5:00-7:00).** Step by step: the speeds (100/50); "up to" is the best case, not a promise; fibre only where laid; the price with VAT and once-off costs; the free-to-use router is lent; month-to-month contract; uncapped. What it does not say: the speed in the back office on Wi-Fi; the line is shared by every device; busy evenings and slow websites; whether fibre has reached his street.
   > Board: the BrightLine advert, each line ticked as it is read; the small print highlighted.
7. **Sign-off.**
