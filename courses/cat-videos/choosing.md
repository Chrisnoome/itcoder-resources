# CAT 12 lesson 1: Choosing a computer for a job - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory12/choosing.php`. Two videos: comparing two adverts
line by line, and writing reasons that earn marks. Board in the CAT marker
style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow highlighter on any words on
screen being talked about. The adverts are the lesson's own (Laptop A to D,
made up, no shop named) - shown as the lesson's tables, not a real shop's
page. Everything below is in the lesson text.

## cat12-01.1 Two adverts, side by side (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#sideBySide` - the comment after `w1PoorBuy`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** ask the six questions about the user, read every line of an advert, and compare two adverts line by line for one person.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "R6 499 or R13 999?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo needs a laptop for CAT. One advert says R6 499 and "up to 11 hours battery!". The other is more than twice the price. His dad says: a laptop is a laptop. Is he right?
   > Board: two advert cards side by side; Clicky between them, shrugging. Yellow highlighter on the two prices.
2. **Start with the user (0:40-2:00).** Never start with the hardware. Six questions: who, what (tasks and software), where, how much (with the extras), how long, anything special. The goal: fit for purpose - not the cheapest, not the most powerful.
   > Board: the choose-steps drawing; the six questions written as a numbered list; highlighter on "fit for purpose".
3. **Every line of an advert (2:00-4:00).** The basic configuration: processor, RAM, storage. Then screen (HD is 1366 x 768, Full HD 1920 x 1080, IPS), battery in Wh, ports, operating system, weight, webcam, warranty, price. The three new words: eMMC, watt-hour, S mode. Robustness: an SSD or eMMC has no moving parts.
   > Board: the lesson's advert-lines table built row by row; highlighter on "soldered", "eMMC", "S mode" and "carry-in".
4. **Thabo's six answers (4:00-4:50).** CAT pupil; Word, Excel, Access and many tabs; home and school by taxi, power cuts at home; R14 000; four years; nothing special.
   > Board: six sticky notes, one per answer.
5. **Comparing, step by step (4:50-7:20).** Line them up; one line at a time - better for this person; mark the warning signs; add the extras; decide with a reason for each choice. Walk through Thabo's comparison table: processor, RAM, storage, screen, battery, ports, weight, warranty, price.
   > Board: the two adverts (the lesson's table) with Clicky ticking down the lines; each warning sign gets a red circle; Thabo's "what it means for him" column filled in line by line.
6. **The choice (7:20-7:50).** Laptop B. A wins on price and weight; B wins on every line that decides whether he can do his PAT for four years. A is a fine laptop - the wrong laptop for Thabo.
   > Board: a big tick on Laptop B, a small "fine for someone else" sticky note on A.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat12-01.2 Reasons that earn marks (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#match` - the comment after `w1Gogo`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** spot what an advert hides, and write a recommendation in which every reason names a specification and links it to the user.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "\"Faster\" earns *nothing*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. "Laptop B, because it is faster." Zero marks. Why?
   > Board: the sentence written in marker, a big red zero next to it.
2. **What the advert does not tell you (0:30-3:00).** Five clues: "up to" is the best case (bursts of GHz; battery hours measured playing a video, screen dimmed, Wi-Fi off); what is missing (no graphics line means integrated; no Wh often means a small battery; no storage type - ask); the small print (the star, soldered, S mode, carry-in, "free" Microsoft 365 for a year); what no advert can show (hinge, keyboard, brightness, heat and noise, service - read reviews); the total cost of ownership.
   > Board: the choose-up-to doodle; Clicky with a magnifying glass following a star (*) to the small print; highlighter on "up to".
3. **Weak and strong reasons (3:00-4:40).** A strong reason names a specification and says what it does for this user. The lesson's three pairs: RAM, SSD, battery. "Faster", "cheaper", "better", "easy to use" earn nothing on their own; use the advert's numbers.
   > Board: the weak/strong table, weak side crossed out in red, strong side highlighted.
4. **The most powerful is not always right (4:40-5:40).** Gogo and the gaming laptop: heavy, three times the price, power she will never use. Choose the most powerful only when the work needs it.
   > Board: Gogo on the couch with a huge gaming laptop; Clicky shaking its head.
5. **Worked examples (5:40-6:40).** Thabo - Laptop B; Gogo - Laptop A; Ms Naidoo with the projector - Laptop B for its HDMI port. A recommendation and its reason, each time.
   > Board: the three-row worked-examples table, one row at a time.
6. **Sign-off.**
