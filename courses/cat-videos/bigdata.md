## cat11-24.1 What big data is - and where it lives (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after section `#databases` - the comment after `w24Database`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what big data is, explain volume, velocity and variety with an example each, and say what a database and a data centre do with it.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOCIAL`, title "How did they *know*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo buys rooibos every Thursday and swipes her loyalty card. On Wednesday night: "R10 off rooibos this week, just for you." How did they know? They kept every till slip of hers for three years - and of millions of other customers.
   > Board: a till slip, then a pile of slips growing into a mountain. Clicky on top with a flag.
2. **What big data is (0:40-1:40).** Every card payment, every search and click, every phone's location, every smart meter reading. Together: collections of data so huge, so fast-growing and so mixed that ordinary programs on one computer cannot handle them. A spreadsheet on a laptop would not even open it.
   > Board: four streams (card, phone, meter, camera) pouring into one big tank. Highlighter on "ordinary programs cannot handle".
3. **Three Vs (1:40-3:20).** Volume - how much: years of till slips from every branch. Velocity - how fast: a bank checks each payment for fraud before it goes through. Variety - how many kinds: text, photos, video, voice notes, GPS, sensor readings, mixed. Some add value (worth keeping only if it leads to better decisions) and veracity (can it be trusted?).
   > Board: the bigdata-vs drawing built V by V; value and veracity added last, smaller, in a different colour with "some add".
4. **Not big data (3:20-3:50).** Ms Naidoo's 35 test marks in a spreadsheet: ordinary data.
   > Board: a tiny spreadsheet next to the big tank.
5. **Databases (3:50-5:00).** A database stores every record the same way, links records (the card number links slips to the customer), finds one record among billions in under a second, and answers questions. One swipe, step by step: the till scans; the sale goes to the database; it is linked to her card number; a program finds the pattern across millions of customers; the voucher goes out.
   > Board: five numbered steps with a till slip travelling down them; at step 3 a string ties the slip to a card.
6. **Data centres (5:00-5:40).** Big data is too big for one computer, so it is spread over thousands of servers in a data centre - with backup power, cooling and guards.
   > Board: rows of server racks; a small lightning bolt (backup power) and a fan.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat11-24.2 Your data, their business - benefits and privacy (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#privacy` - the comment after `s24Protect`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say who collects big data and why, give benefits for organisations and for people, and explain the privacy risks and what protects them.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOCIAL`, title "Free? You *are* the product"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo says TikTok is free. Lerato says: "You pay - just not with money."
   > Board: a phone with a price tag "R0"; Clicky pulling a long trail of small data cards out of the phone.
2. **Who collects it, and why (0:40-2:00).** Shops - what to stock, personal specials. Banks - fraud. Social media and search - adverts. Cellphone networks - where to build towers. Medical schemes - who is at risk, rewards for healthy living. Government - planning, tax fraud. All for better decisions, usually to make or save money.
   > Board: six small buildings in a ring, each with one word under it. Highlighter on "better decisions".
3. **Good for whom (2:00-3:10).** Organisations: better decisions, less waste, fraud caught, adverts that work. People: specials on what you buy, recommendations, an SMS before a thief spends more, a smartwatch warning, a way around the traffic jam.
   > Board: a two-column list; Gogo's rooibos voucher in the People column.
4. **You pay with data (3:10-3:50).** The app records what you watch, skip and like, and where you are; it builds a picture of you and sells advertisers the chance to reach you. If you are not paying for the product, you are the product.
   > Board: the trail of data cards from scene 1 being sold to a stick figure with a megaphone (an advertiser).
5. **The risks (3:50-5:40).** Customer profiling: a picture that shows your health, religion, money worries, when the house is empty - and decides your adverts, your price, even a loan. Data brokers sell your data on. "Anonymous" is not anonymous: where a phone sleeps and spends the day is usually enough - in a 2013 study, four places and times picked out 95% of 1.5 million people. Data breaches: Experian South Africa, 2020, 24 million people's details handed to a fraudster. Wrong data, wrong decisions - and the computer does not say why.
   > Board: five numbered warning signs; a map with four dots joined into a face at "anonymous". Pink highlighter on "profiling".
6. **What protects you (5:40-6:40).** The POPI Act from Grade 10: collect only what is needed, say why, use it only for that, keep it safe, report a theft. And you: location only "while using the app", check camera and microphone permissions, fill in only the required boxes, read before you tick, set accounts to private.
   > Board: a shield with "POPI" on it; then a numbered checklist that Clicky ticks.
7. **Sign-off.**
