# CAT 10 lesson 22: Kinds of network - PAN, HAN and LAN - videos

Lesson: `content/cattheory10/networkkinds.php`. Two videos: the three kinds
every pupil needs, and a short one for the IEB section. Board in the CAT
marker style with Clicky (brand/cat-art-style.md); yellow highlighter on any
words on screen being talked about. No screen recordings needed. Everything
below is in the lesson text.

## cat10-22.1 PAN, HAN and LAN (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#lan` - the comment after `w22Lan`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a PAN, a HAN and a LAN are, what each is for, give an example of each, tell a PAN from a HAN, and explain a limitation.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "PAN, HAN or *LAN*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. "I don't use networks," says Gogo Dlamini. "I just use my phone." Thabo counts: her Bluetooth earphones, the house Wi-Fi, the computer at the library. Three networks before lunch.
   > Board: Gogo with her phone; three numbered bubbles pop up around her.
2. **The AN names (0:40-1:00).** AN stands for area network. The letter in front says how big the area is.
   > Board: "_AN" on a sticky note; the blank flashes P, H, L.
3. **PAN (1:00-2:20).** The network around one person, about 10 metres, moving with you. It connects your own small devices to one main device, usually your phone, by Bluetooth. Thabo's phone, earphones and smartwatch: the earphones play the music (output), the watch sends his steps (input). Wearables: smartwatch, fitness band, wireless earphones.
   > Board: the pan-halo idea - a dashed circle round a stick-figure Thabo, 10 m marked; the circle moves when he walks.
4. **A limitation (2:20-3:10).** Thabo leaves his phone in his bag and walks across the field. The music stops. Nothing is broken: Bluetooth was designed to reach about 10 metres, to save battery. A restriction built into the design is a limitation. A disadvantage is any downside - a cost or a risk.
   > Board: Thabo walks out of the circle; the music notes drop. Yellow highlighter on "built into the design".
5. **HAN (3:10-4:30).** The network in one home, used by the whole household. It shares one Internet connection, and lets devices share with each other. Thabo's house: one router by the front door makes the Wi-Fi for four phones, a laptop, the smart TV, the printer and Gogo's tablet.
   > Board: a house cut-away; dashed Wi-Fi lines from the router to each device.
6. **PAN or HAN? (4:30-5:20).** Both can be in your house. A PAN belongs to one person and moves with them. A HAN belongs to the household and stays at home.
   > Board: the PAN circle walks out of the front door; the HAN stays behind. Pink highlighter on "one person" and "household".
7. **LAN (5:20-6:30).** One building or site of an organisation: Phumlani Secondary's lab, staff room, server and printers. It can have hundreds of computers. What makes it a LAN and not a HAN: an organisation, many devices and users, a server, log-ins and an administrator.
   > Board: a school building with a lab of desks cabled to a server in a locked room.
8. **The biggest of all (6:30-6:50).** The Internet joins millions of LANs and HANs around the world - a network of networks.
   > Board: the house and the school each joined by a line to a globe.
9. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-22.2 Bigger networks - MAN, WAN and GAN (about 4 min, IEB)

**Goes:** after the IEB section `#bigger` - the comment after the board section ends.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what a MAN, a WAN and a GAN cover, put PAN, LAN, MAN, WAN and GAN in order of size, and give an advantage, a disadvantage and a limitation of the Internet as a GAN.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · NETWORKS`, title "From your *pocket* to the planet"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A school is one site. What do you call a network that joins a whole city - or the world?
   > Board: the school from the last video, the board zooming out.
2. **MAN (0:30-1:10).** One city. The City of Johannesburg joining its offices, clinics and libraries.
   > Board: a map outline of Johannesburg with dots joined.
3. **WAN (1:10-1:50).** A long distance - a country. A bank joining its branches in every province.
   > Board: a map of South Africa; nine province dots joined.
4. **GAN (1:50-2:40).** The whole world. A mining company with offices in South Africa, Chile and Australia. The best example: the Internet. Some books call it the biggest WAN instead.
   > Board: a globe; Clicky clinging to it.
5. **In order (2:40-3:10).** PAN, LAN, MAN, WAN, GAN - smallest to largest.
   > Board: the network-sizes drawing, one ring at a time.
6. **The Internet as a GAN (3:10-3:50).** Advantage: reach anyone in seconds. Disadvantage: criminals can reach you too. Limitation: it only works where there is a connection.
   > Board: three columns; a different highlighter colour on each heading.
7. **Sign-off.**
