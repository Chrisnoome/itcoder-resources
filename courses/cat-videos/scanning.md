## cat10-07.1 Barcodes and QR codes (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after section `#qr` (the comment is below the `t7QrWords` question).
**The pupil can afterwards:** say why reading beats typing, explain how a till finds a price from a barcode, and say how a QR code differs and how to scan one safely.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "What's *in* a barcode?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Saturday: Thabo buys
   bread and milk. Beep - the bread. Beep - the loyalty card. Beep - the bank
   card. Nobody typed a single number.
   > Board: a till counter; three "BEEP" bursts in yellow highlighter.
2. **Reading instead of typing (0:40-1:40).** A reading device reads data
   straight from a code, card, tag or page. Faster (a split second against ten
   seconds of typing 13 digits), more accurate (no typing mistakes - no wrong
   prices), less work. Garbage in, garbage out: it keeps the garbage out.
   > Board: a tired cashier typing a 13-digit number, sweat drops; next to her, a scanner going beep.
3. **A barcode is only a number (1:40-3:30).** Stripes for a machine, digits
   underneath for people. It holds no price and no name. At the till: scan the
   number, look it up in the database, the database sends back the name and
   price, the price goes on the slip, the stock count goes down by one. So a
   price change means changing one line in the database, not new barcodes.
   > Board: the lesson's barcode-lookup drawing built step by step; Clicky points at the number with a speech bubble "that's ALL it holds". Pink highlighter on "R19.99" as it comes back from the database.
4. **What reads them (3:30-4:10).** A handheld scanner (the gun), the glass
   window in the counter, a phone camera. A torn or wet barcode will not scan,
   and the cashier types it - slowly, with mistakes.
   > Board: the three readers; a crumpled barcode with a sad face.
5. **QR codes (4:10-5:30).** Quick Response. A square of blocks, read across
   and down, so it holds much more: a web address, a Wi-Fi password, payment
   details. Paying with SnapScan or Zapper, posters, menus, tickets, linking
   WhatsApp to a computer. To open a link: camera, point, read the link, tap.
   > Board: a barcode and a QR code side by side, a yellow arrow across the barcode and arrows across and down the QR code; then a phone scanning a poster, steps 1-4 as sticky notes.
6. **The fake sticker (5:30-6:20).** Crooks stick their own QR codes over real
   ones. You cannot read a QR code with your eyes - check the link before you
   open it or pay.
   > Board: a parking meter with a second QR sticker peeling over the first; Clicky with a magnifying glass on the link.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-07.2 Cards, tags and scanners (about 8 min)

**Goes:** after section `#ocr` (the comment is below the `w7OcrCheck` question).
**The pupil can afterwards:** compare a magnetic stripe with a chip, describe a card payment, say what RFID is and where it is used, choose a scanner for a job, and explain OCR.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · HARDWARE`, title "Swipe, *insert*, tap"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. A car drives through
   the e-tag lane at a toll plaza. It does not stop. Nobody scans anything. The
   boom just opens. How?
   > Board: a toll boom lifting by itself; Clicky in the passenger seat, waving.
2. **The magnetic stripe (0:30-1:30).** The dark strip on the back holds data as
   tiny magnetic patterns; you swipe it. CAPS says strip, the IEB stripe.
   Loyalty, hotel key and gift cards, older bank cards. Easy to copy (skimming)
   and easily damaged by magnets and scratches.
   > Board: a card swiped through a reader; a crook's hidden reader on an ATM slot; a magnet with a zig-zag "zap".
3. **The chip (1:30-2:30).** A tiny computer on the card: it stores data and
   makes a new secret code for every payment, so a copy is useless. SIM cards
   and the Smart ID card have chips too.
   > Board: the gold chip enlarged; codes 1, 2, 3 each different, a crossed-out copy.
4. **A card payment (2:30-3:30).** The cashier types the amount; insert chip
   first; the chip makes its code; type your PIN; the machine sends the details
   to the bank; the bank checks PIN and balance and says approved; slip, card back.
   > Board: the seven steps as a numbered strip, each lit in yellow highlighter in turn.
5. **Tap to pay and RFID (3:30-5:10).** Tap: a tiny antenna in the card, a few
   centimetres, NFC. RFID: a tag (chip + antenna) with no battery; the reader's
   radio waves power it; it answers with its number, looked up in a database.
   No need to see it, works from metres, reads many at once. e-tags, pet
   microchips, gate tags, stock, parcels, library books. RFID or NFC: metres
   and one way, against centimetres and both ways.
   > Board: the lesson's rfid-induction drawing built step by step; then a two-column RFID / NFC comparison with pink highlighter on "metres" and "centimetres".
6. **Scanners (5:10-6:30).** A scanner makes a digital picture of a page.
   Flatbed (best quality), sheet-feed (many pages), handheld, mouse scanner,
   the scanner in a multifunction printer, and the smartphone camera with a
   scanning app.
   > Board: the six scanners in a grid; Clicky feeding a pile of pages into the sheet-feed one.
7. **OCR (6:30-7:40).** A scan is a picture - dots, not words. OCR finds the
   letters and turns them into text you can edit. Scan, choose "Recognise text",
   open in Word, proofread: 0 and O, 1 and l, rn and m. Also: copying text from
   a phone photo, number plates, sorting post, ID documents. OCR is software,
   not a device.
   > Board: the lesson's ocr-scan-text drawing; then "rn" and "m" side by side, Clicky squinting.
8. **Sign-off.**
