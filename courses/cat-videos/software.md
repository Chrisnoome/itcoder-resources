## cat10-13.1 Hardware, software and the three kinds (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after the sort question `g13Kinds`, at the end of section `#kinds`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what software is, give two differences
between hardware and software, and sort a program into system, application
or utility software.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "The half you *can't* touch"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Gogo Dlamini had two
   problems with her new phone this month. WhatsApp stopped opening - and an
   update fixed it. Then she dropped the phone and the screen cracked. "Just
   update it again," she said. Why won't that work?
   > Board: a phone drawn in marker; Clicky taps the WhatsApp icon; then a
   > crack zig-zags across the glass. Clicky puzzled ("?").
2. **What software is (0:40-1:50).** Pick up your phone - that's hardware.
   Now try to pick up WhatsApp. Software is a set of instructions that tells
   the hardware what to do. One set that does one job is a program. The
   kitchen and the recipe: same stove, vetkoek or curry, depending on the
   recipe.
   > Board: a stove, pots and spoons labelled HARDWARE; a recipe card on a
   > sticky note labelled SOFTWARE; yellow highlighter on "instructions".
3. **Hardware vs software (1:50-3:10).** Walk down the table: touch it or
   not; factories vs programmers; breaks and wears out vs bugs and wrong
   settings; repair or replace vs update; every copy costs money vs a copy
   costs almost nothing. Back to Gogo: the WhatsApp problem was software, the
   crack is hardware.
   > Board: the two-column table drawn row by row; each row gets a yellow
   > band as it is read; the two Gogo problems land in their columns.
4. **They need each other (3:10-3:50).** A recipe cooks nothing without a
   kitchen; a new laptop with no software shows only "nothing to start".
   > Board: the paperweight joke - a laptop holding down a pile of papers.
5. **The three kinds (3:50-5:30).** System software runs the computer
   (Windows, Android - lesson 14). Application software does a job for you
   (Word, a game, WhatsApp; every app). Utility software looks after the
   computer (antivirus, backup, clean-up, zip) - part of system software. The
   test: what is it for?
   > Board: three columns; Clicky drags six program cards into them: Windows
   > 11, Android, Word, a phone game, antivirus, a photo backup program.
   > Pink highlighter on the question "What is it FOR?".
6. **Recap (5:30-5:50).** Software: instructions you cannot touch. Three
   kinds. Ask what it is for.
7. **Sign-off.**

## cat10-13.2 Working with windows and the desktop (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the match `m13Desktop`, at the end of section `#desktop`
(before `#touchGui`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** name the parts of a window and of the Windows
desktop, and minimise, maximise, restore, resize, move and close a window.
**Thumbnail:** tag `CAT · SOFTWARE`, title "Minimise is *not* close"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Thabo minimised his
   essay an hour ago, and now he thinks it is gone. It isn't - and by the
   end of this video you will know exactly where it is.
   > Board: Clicky searching under a desk with a torch.
2. **WIMP (0:30-1:30).** A GUI - say "gooey" - is a graphical user interface:
   windows, icons, menus, pointer. You see your choices and point at them
   instead of remembering commands.
   > Board: the four letters W-I-M-P, each with a tiny drawing (a window, a
   > bin icon, a File menu dropping down, Clicky as the pointer).
3. **The parts of a window (1:30-2:30).** Title bar, minimise, maximise,
   close, the ribbon or menus, the scroll bar, the border.
   > Screen: VM, Word open with a short document, not maximised. Yellow
   > highlighter band on each part as it is named (title bar, _ , the square,
   > X, ribbon, scroll bar, border).
4. **Minimise and get it back (2:30-3:20).** Click _ ; the window disappears
   but is not closed; its taskbar icon has a line under it; click the icon
   and it comes back as it was.
   > Screen: click minimise; zoom on the Word icon on the taskbar; click it.
5. **Maximise and Restore Down (3:20-4:00).** Click the square; the window
   fills the screen; the button becomes Restore Down; click it to go back.
   Double-clicking the title bar does the same.
   > Screen: click maximise, hover to show the "Restore Down" tooltip, click
   > it; then double-click the title bar twice.
6. **Resize and move (4:00-4:50).** Point at an edge or corner - the double
   arrow - and drag. A maximised window cannot be resized: restore it first.
   Drag the title bar to move the window.
   > Screen: drag the bottom-right corner; drag the title bar across.
7. **Close (4:50-5:20).** Click X; if the work is not saved, read the
   question before you click - Don't Save loses the work since your last
   save.
   > Screen: type a word, click X, the save prompt appears; yellow band on
   > "Don't Save"; click Cancel.
8. **The desktop and taskbar (5:20-6:40).** Background, icons, shortcuts (the
   little arrow - deleting one deletes only the shortcut), the Recycle Bin
   (double-click it, right-click a file > Restore). The taskbar: the Start button and Start menu,
   the search box (type "calc"), open programs with a line under them,
   pinned programs (right-click > Pin to taskbar), the notification area
   (clock, Wi-Fi, volume, battery).
   > Screen: the desktop with a shortcut icon (zoom on the arrow); open the
   > Recycle Bin and right-click > Restore on a test file; open Start; type
   > "calc" in the search box; right-click Calculator > Pin to taskbar; zoom
   > on the notification area.
9. **Recap (6:40-6:55).** Minimise hides, close stops. Thabo's essay was on
   the taskbar all along.
10. **Sign-off.**

> Note: record in the VM (screens-vm-first), Windows 11, a clean desktop with
> one shortcut and a test file in the Recycle Bin. No real names on screen.
