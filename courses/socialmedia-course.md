## The film

*The Social Dilemma* (Netflix, 2020, directed by Jeff Orlowski, 94 minutes): former tech
insiders on how social media is designed to hold attention, and what that does to people and
society, with a dramatised family story running through it. Shown in class by Chris from the
school's account; the lessons never embed or copy it - they give **timestamps, "watch for"
prompts and questions** between parts. Check the age rating against the school's policy
before term (to confirm).

## Lessons

1. **Who pays for free?** - before the film: how social media makes money (attention,
   adverts, data - links to the safety course's Case 05), the "if you're not paying, you're
   the product" idea, a screen-time guess the pupils check on their own phones at home. Watch
   part 1 (first ~25 minutes). Questions after.
2. **The film, part 2** - watch-for prompts (persuasive design: notifications, infinite
   scroll, likes, "pull to refresh" as a slot machine), pause points with quick questions.
3. **The film, part 3** - algorithms and recommendations, rabbit holes, misinformation; quick
   questions; a try-it showing how a feed ranks posts by predicted engagement.
4. **The film, part 4 and the end** - the effect on mental health and society; the
   insiders' advice; a class discussion block (structured, like the AI course's debate).
5. **Is the film fair?** - critical thinking: what it shows well, what it dramatises, who
   made it and why, what critics and the platforms said; separating evidence from opinion.
6. **My feed, my rules** - the pupil's own audit (time, notifications, who they follow), the
   settings that help (notifications off, time limits, grayscale, chronological feeds), and a
   personal plan; the final written reflection (about 20 marks, banded rubric).

Each lesson keeps the usual shape: prose in Chris's voice, popups, questions (Jev first),
margin doodles; the film parts add a `video`-like "watch now" block with the timestamps
(Netflix, not YouTube - a new block type, or a prose box).
