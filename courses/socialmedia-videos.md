# Grade 9 social media - video plans for the video chat

Chris, 9 October 2026: video plans for the Grade 9 courses. Course: `socialmedia`
(`AIPascalCourse/content/socialmedia/`, plan [socialmedia-course.md](socialmedia-course.md)).
The film itself (*The Social Dilemma*, Netflix) is shown in class and is **never** used,
quoted at length or shown in these videos - they teach the ideas around it.

**5 videos, about 28 minutes in all.** Each **Goes** line is the place in the lesson (add a
`// VIDEO sm-NN.n` comment there when you start it).

### sm-01.1 If it's free, you are the product (about 5 min)

1. **Hook.** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. How much did you pay for your apps this year?
   Zero. The companies are among the richest in the world. So who pays?
   > Board: a phone with "R0" on a price tag; a pile of gold coins beside it with a "?".
2. **Who pays.** Advertisers pay to show you adverts - so they are the customers, and your
   attention is what's being sold. The attention economy: every app races for your time.
   > Board: the sm-product figure built arrow by arrow - YOU, the FREE APP collecting what you
   > watch, pause on, where you are, who you know; ADVERTISERS paying R.
3. **Why time matters.** More minutes, more adverts; more data, better-aimed adverts. So the app
   is built - carefully - to keep you there. That's what the film is about.
4. **Before the film.** Watch for the insiders: how they describe their jobs, and how they feel
   about them now. Sign-off.

### sm-02.1 The slot machine in your pocket (about 6 min)

1. **Hook.** Pull down on your feed. The wheel spins. Maybe something new... maybe not. Where
   else do people pull a lever and wait?
2. **Persuasive design.** Apps are tested on millions of people to find what keeps them there.
3. **The machine, part by part.** Lever - pull to refresh; reels - endless scrolling, no bottom;
   jackpot - likes; coins - notifications; the next spin - autoplay.
   > Board: the sm-slot-machine figure built part by part, each label glowing as it is named.
4. **Why "maybe" hooks you.** Unpredictable rewards keep people (and animals) checking longer
   than rewards that always come; dopamine makes you want to repeat what felt good. Nobody is
   weak for feeling the pull - it was designed to be felt.
   > Board: two machines - one that always pays (a yawn), one that pays sometimes (a hand
   > pulling again and again).
5. **Sign-off.**

### sm-03.1 How your feed is chosen (about 6 min)

1. **Hook.** Two pupils, same class, same app, same moment - completely different feeds.
2. **The algorithm.** Thousands of possible posts; one question for each: will this person stop
   and look? Engagement. Not: is it true, is it good for you, is it your best friend.
   > Board: the sm-feed-funnel figure - the posts pouring in, the funnel, the top six out.
3. **Ranked two ways.** Walk through the lesson's feedRanker: newest first versus "keep you
   scrolling" - the furious video, the gossip fight and the glow-up rise; the best friend and the
   test reminder sink.
   > Screen: the lesson's try-it on bestlessons.co.za in the VM, switching the setting.
4. **Side effects.** Rabbit holes (each step a little more extreme), filter bubbles (only your
   view), outrage spreads; MIT's 2018 Twitter study - false news spread further and faster,
   mostly shared by people. The question to ask: why am I being shown this, and what am I not
   being shown?
5. **Sign-off.**

### sm-05.1 Evidence or drama? Judging a documentary (about 5 min)

1. **Hook.** A film about manipulation uses dramatic music and an acted family. Unfair - or good
   storytelling?
2. **Evidence and drama.** Evidence you can check: real insiders, studies, numbers. Drama made to
   make you feel: the control room, the music, the acted family.
   > Board: the sm-fair-scales figure, items dropped onto each pan.
3. **Who speaks, who doesn't.** Mostly Silicon Valley insiders; actors as teens; little on the
   good side; made for Netflix, which also wants your attention.
4. **The other side.** Facebook's 2020 reply. Both sides gain something - check against
   independent evidence. The habit works for films, posts and WhatsApp forwards. Sign-off.

### sm-06.1 Switch the machine down: settings that help (about 6 min)

1. **Hook.** The engineers who built the machine keep their own phones on a short leash.
2. **The audit.** Time, pickups, notifications, how you feel after 20 minutes, who you follow -
   privately.
3. **The settings, shown.** Notifications only from people; app timers (Screen Time / Digital
   Wellbeing); autoplay off; a Following or chronological feed; grayscale; charge outside the
   bedroom.
   > Screen (VM, emulator or simulator, test account): Settings > Notifications; Digital
   > Wellbeing / Screen Time app limits; Accessibility > grayscale (colour filters). Check each
   > menu name in the VM first.
4. **Three rules.** Small and specific: "phone charges in the kitchen from 21:00" is a rule; "use
   my phone less" is a wish. Try them for two weeks. Sign-off.
