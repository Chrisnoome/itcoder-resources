shown here is also in the lesson text (anchors in the tables below).

## safety-01.1 The SMS that wants your money (about 6 min)

**Goes:** at the end of section `#clueRush`, after the markwords question `mw1RushLines`.
**The pupil can afterwards:** say what phishing is, and check a message's sender and tone.
**Thumbnail:** tag `SAFETY · CASE 01`, title "Your account is *LOCKED*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. 07:42, Monday, toast. The phone
   buzzes: "KasiBank: your account has been LOCKED. Verify within 24 hrs or lose access."
   Your thumb is moving to the link. Stop. Put the toast down. You're a detective now.
   > Board: the SMS pinned to the kraft folder as Exhibit A; the tab "CASE 01 · THE LOCKED
   > ACCOUNT" typed on.
2. **The bait and the hook (0:40-1:50).** How you catch a fish: tasty bait, wait for a bite.
   Scammers send bait to thousands; a few bite. That is phishing - said like fishing. By SMS,
   smishing. On the hook: your password, PIN, a code, your card number, or money. The bait
   always wears a name you trust - a bank, a shop, your mom.
   > Board: The Phisher with his rod, the SMS as bait on the hook; four index cards drop in:
   > PASSWORD, CODE, CARD, MONEY.
3. **Clue 1: who sent it? (1:50-3:10).** +27 63 555 0192 - an ordinary cell number anybody
   can get with a cheap SIM. Banks use a short code or their name. Red flag. But careful: a
   scammer can make a message show the bank's name. A wrong sender is a red flag; a
   right-looking sender proves nothing.
   > Board: Clue card 1 pinned, red string to the number. Sniff sniffing; a speech bubble
   > "something smells phishy". Then a second SMS showing "KasiBank" with a question mark
   > stamped over it.
4. **Clue 2: the rush (3:10-4:40).** Read it aloud. How does it feel? Scared, hurried. When
   you are scared or excited, the thinking part of your brain slows down. The four buttons:
   fear, a deadline, greed, kindness. A real bank doesn't threaten you by SMS - it will still
   be wrong in ten minutes, after you've checked. And a real bank never asks for your PIN,
   password or a code it sent you. Never.
   > Board: Clue card 2 and its string to "LOCKED" and "24 hrs"; the clock doodle ringing;
   > four buttons labelled FEAR, DEADLINE, GREED, KINDNESS.
5. **Sign-off (4:40-5:00).** Two clues down, one to go - the address, in the next video.

## safety-01.2 Read a web address like a detective (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after the match `m1WhoseAddress` (end of section `#clueAddress`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** find the domain name in any web address, spot a look-alike, and
say what to do with a suspect message.
**Thumbnail:** tag `SAFETY · CASE 01`, title "kasibank-verify.co is *not* your bank"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. The link in Exhibit A says
   kasibank-verify.co. It has "kasibank" in it. So it's the bank's... right? Wrong - and this
   is the clue most people miss.
2. **The shop next to Sandton City (0:30-1:20).** A shop called "Sandton City Bargains" has
   the same words, but it isn't Sandton City. Addresses work the same: the exact name, letter
   for letter.
   > Board: two shopfronts, one big, one tiny with the borrowed name.
3. **Finding the owner (1:20-2:50).** Every site has a domain name; only one owner can have
   it. Skip https://. Find the first single slash. The name just before it is the owner -
   ending in .co.za or .com. Walk through https://www.kasibank.co.za/login (green ring) and
   https://kasibank-verify.co/login (red ring).
   > Board: the address-parts figure built up: the two Polaroids, the rings, the clue card
   > "the owner's name ends right before the first /", the NOT THE BANK stamp.
4. **Look-alike tricks (2:50-4:00).** An extra word (kasibank-help.co.za), a swapped letter
   (kasibamk - did you spot the m?), a different ending (.co instead of .co.za), and the
   sneakiest: the real name at the start - kasibank.co.za.secure-login.net belongs to
   secure-login.net.
   > Board: four index cards, each with its trick; the last one with a long red ring around
   > "secure-login.net".
5. **The findings form (4:00-5:20).** Write it down: sender - a cell number; tone - fear and a
   deadline; link - a look-alike. Three of three. Verdict: scam. What to do: don't click,
   don't reply, don't phone a number in the message; check another way - the bank's app or
   the number on the back of your card; tell an adult, especially if you clicked; report and
   delete. Clicked already? Don't hide it.
   > Board: the findings form filled in row by row, each red box crossed; the stamp
   > "3 OF 3 RED FLAGS".
6. **Sign-off.** Your case load is waiting in the lesson.
