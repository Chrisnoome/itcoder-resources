# Safety lesson 3: Case 03 - The second lock - videos

Lesson file: `AIPascalCourse/content/safety/secondlock.php`. Two videos.

## safety-03.1 The second lock (about 5 min)

**Goes:** after the reveal `r3WhyCodeHelps` (end of section `#codes`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** name the three kinds of proof, explain two-step verification and
where the codes come from.
**Thumbnail:** tag `SAFETY · CASE 03`, title "He has your password. *So what?*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. The Phisher finally has your password.
   He types it in, presses Enter, grins - and the screen says "We sent a code to your phone."
   He doesn't have your phone. The door stays shut.
   > Board: The Phisher at a login screen; the code message appears; his grin drops.
2. **Know, have, are (0:40-2:00).** Something you know: a password, a PIN. Something you have:
   your phone, a bank card. Something you are: a fingerprint, your face. The ATM: card plus
   PIN. Two of the same kind don't count - a password plus your first pet's name is two things
   you know, and your pet is on your Instagram.
   > Board: the two-locks figure built up - the door, lock 1, lock 2, then the three cards
   > with their strings.
3. **Two-step verification (2:00-2:40).** Asking for two different kinds: two-step
   verification, two-factor authentication, 2FA.
4. **Where the code comes from (2:40-4:10).** An OTP - a one-time PIN, good once, for a few
   minutes. By SMS: easy, much better than nothing, with one weak spot (next video). From an
   authenticator app: a new code every 30 seconds, made on the phone, never sent over the
   network. As a tap: "Is this you trying to log in?" All three share one weakness: you. A
   code is a key - never read it out, type it in a chat or send it. A prompt you didn't start:
   tap No.
   > Board: three index cards SMS / APP / TAP; then a key with "482 913" engraved on it and
   > the stamp NEVER SHARE.
5. **Sign-off.**

## safety-03.2 Three tricks to steal your code (about 7 min)

**Goes:** after the select `s3SimSwapSigns` (end of section `#simSwap`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** recognise the "bank calls you" scam, the WhatsApp code scam and
a SIM swap, and say what to do about each.
**Thumbnail:** tag `SAFETY · CASE 03`, title "Just read me the *code*..."

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. The second lock works - so scammers
   work very hard to get you to open it for them. Three tricks.
2. **The bank that calls you (0:30-2:30).** Step by step: he has your password; he logs in;
   the bank sends you a code; your phone rings - "KasiBank fraud department, someone's taking
   R8 000, read me the code to cancel it." The code doesn't cancel anything: it approves his
   login. The bank's name on your screen can be faked - spoofing. Never read a code to a
   caller; hang up; phone the number on the back of your card, from a different phone if you
   can.
   > Board: four steps as four cards on a string; The Phisher on the phone ("Read me the
   > code..."); a caller-ID screen with "KasiBank" and a SPOOFED stamp.
3. **The code sent by mistake (2:30-4:30).** WhatsApp sends a code to your number because
   someone is setting up WhatsApp with your number on their phone. Then "Thabo" asks for it:
   "sent it to you by mistake". Codes don't get sent to the wrong number - it's yours, and it
   opens your WhatsApp. Thabo's account was probably taken first. Don't send it; phone Thabo;
   switch on WhatsApp's two-step verification PIN in its settings.
   > Board: the code-by-mistake figure - the SMS banner, the chat, the three clue cards, the
   > NEVER SEND stamp.
4. **The SIM swap (4:30-6:20).** He pretends to be you at a shop or on the phone and moves your
   number to his SIM. Now every SMS code goes to him. Clues: suddenly No service where it
   normally works; no calls or SMSes while people around you can; a SIM or password change you
   didn't ask for. Borrow a phone; an adult calls the network and the bank at once. This is
   why app codes are safer than SMS: they never travel over the network.
   > Board: the no-service doodle; a number "082..." flying from one SIM card to another.
5. **Switch it on (6:20-6:50).** Email first - it resets everything else - then WhatsApp, your
   password manager, games and social media. Backup codes on paper at home.
6. **Sign-off.**
