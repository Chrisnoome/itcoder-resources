Lesson file: `AIPascalCourse/content/safety/fakefriend.php`. Two videos. **Awareness level**
(Chris, 9 October 2026): red flags and who to tell; no dramatised abuse, nothing explicit.
Never show or voice the "fake friend" as menacing - the clues do the work. End both videos on
the help card.

## safety-07.1 The fake friend: four clues (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after the reveal `r7WhySecret` (end of section `#grooming`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** spot a fake profile, name the red flags of grooming, and know
that secrecy is the biggest one.
**Thumbnail:** tag `SAFETY · CASE 07`, title "Kayla, *14*?"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Zinhle gets a friend request: Kayla,
   14, likes the same singer, easy to talk to. A week of chats. Then: "let's move somewhere
   private", "you're so mature", "don't tell your mom", and airtime... for a photo.
2. **Hacking people (0:40-1:50).** Every trick in this course tricked a person, not a computer:
   social engineering. It uses good things - kindness, trust, wanting to belong. Buttons:
   trust, flattery, secrecy, pressure. A fake friend may spend weeks before asking.
   > Board: The Phisher holding up a smiling mask - "hi, I'm 14 too!".
3. **The four clues (1:50-3:40).** Anybody can make any profile, often with a stolen photo.
   Brand new, no mutual friends. Too much, too fast. Secrets - the biggest red flag. Gifts for
   pictures. Reverse image search: Kayla's photo on a model's account abroad - case closed.
   You don't need to be sure to stop: block, report, leave.
   > Board: the fake-profile figure, the four clue cards pinned one by one.
4. **Grooming (3:40-5:20).** Some adults pretend to be young to get close to children and
   harm them, cutting them off from the people who protect them: grooming. The signs: a
   special secret friend; "they don't understand you like I do"; gifts, then "you owe me";
   asking for photos or making you uncomfortable; wanting to meet alone; threats to share
   something. It is never your fault. Never meet an online-only contact without a parent.
   Why is secrecy the biggest flag? Because the adults around you would spot the rest.
   > Board: index cards only, calm and plain. Close on Sniff: "Tell someone you trust. Today."
5. **Help card and sign-off.** TRUSTED ADULT · REPORT · CHILDLINE 116.

## safety-07.2 Cyberbullying, bystanders and back-up (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after the written question `w7Zinhle` (end of section `#tellSomeone`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** respond to cyberbullying, help as a bystander, and tell a trusted
adult the right way.
**Thumbnail:** tag `SAFETY · CASE 07`, title "Call for *back-up*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Not every danger is a stranger.
2. **Cyberbullying (0:30-2:00).** Cruel messages, embarrassing photos shared, being kicked out
   of groups on purpose, rumours, fake accounts. It follows you home. If it's you: don't reply
   in anger; screenshot with names and dates; block and report; tell.
   > Board: a phone with a cracked-heart group chat icon; four action cards.
3. **The bystander (2:00-3:30).** Most people in a bullying chat are bystanders - with more
   power than they think. Don't laugh, like or forward; message the person to say you're on
   their side; report it. The Sipho example: support him and tell a teacher.
4. **Who to tell, and how (3:30-5:20).** Detectives call for back-up. A trusted adult - parent,
   guardian, aunt, teacher, coach, counsellor; if the first doesn't help, tell another. Show,
   don't just say: bring screenshots. Tell early - "something feels off" is enough. The Report
   button too. Childline 116, free, day or night. In danger right now: an adult phones the
   police on 10111. You won't be in trouble for telling.
   > Board: Sniff with "Tell someone you trust. Today."; the help card built up line by line.
5. **Help card and sign-off.**
