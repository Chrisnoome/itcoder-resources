# Safety lesson 5: Case 05 - What your apps know - videos

Lesson file: `AIPascalCourse/content/safety/apps.php`. Two videos.

## safety-05.1 Why does a torch want your contacts? (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after the quiz `q5LocationSetting` (end of section `#location`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** decide which permissions to allow, and set location sensibly.
**Thumbnail:** tag `SAFETY · CASE 05`, title "The *nosy* torch"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:40).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Load shedding. Bongani installs a free
   torch, SuperTorch, and taps Allow four times: location, contacts, microphone, photos.
2. **Permissions (0:40-2:20).** Your phone locks its private parts; an app needs a key - a
   permission. The detective's question: what does it need it for? A torch needs the flash. A
   map needs location while you use it. A video call needs camera and microphone. A torch,
   calculator or quiz needs none of the rest. Don't allow - and an app that won't work without
   keys it doesn't need is telling you what it's for. You can take keys back in Settings.
   > Board: the permissions figure - the phone with five switches, the three clue cards and
   > strings.
3. **Location (2:20-4:30).** One location is a dot. All day, every day: where you sleep (home),
   where you are 07:30 to 14:00 (school), Saturdays, when the house is empty. Never / while
   using / always - almost nothing needs always. Live-location sharing on purpose, then off.
   Check-ins: "at Rosebank Mall right now" also says "not at home".
   > Board: the location-trail doodle - home, school, gran - growing pin by pin.
4. **Take the keys back (4:30-5:40).** Where the switches are.
   > Screen (VM): Windows 11 Settings > Privacy & security > Location, then > Camera and
   > > Microphone, showing the per-app switches and "Let apps access your location". Say that
   > phones have the same thing under Settings > Privacy or Apps.
5. **Sign-off.**

## safety-05.2 If it's free, who pays? (about 6 min)

**Goes:** after the select `s5Popia` (end of section `#yourRights`).
**The pupil can afterwards:** explain how free apps are paid for with data, and name their
rights under POPIA.
**Thumbnail:** tag `SAFETY · CASE 05`, title "You are the *product*"

### Scenes

1. **Hook (0:00-0:30).** Hi, and welcome to BestLessons. Programmers, servers, electricity -
   apps cost a lot to build. So how is SuperTorch free?
   > Board: the free-cheese mousetrap doodle.
2. **You are the product (0:30-2:20).** The app collects information and uses or sells it for
   targeted adverts - a 13-year-old in Soweto who likes soccer boots is worth more to an
   advertiser than "somebody". Trackers and cookies follow you: look at sneakers once, see
   them for a week. Not all evil - adverts pay for things you enjoy - but it should be your
   choice: only needed permissions; Reject or Necessary only on cookie banners; skip "fun"
   quizzes that collect security-question answers (your elf name is your birth month plus your
   first pet!).
   > Board: a sneaker following a stick figure from site to site; a cookie banner with
   > "Reject" ringed in green.
3. **QuizMania (2:20-3:00).** A free game, no adverts you can see, wants contacts and location
   always. If you can't see who pays, it's often you, with your information.
4. **POPIA (3:00-5:00).** The Protection of Personal Information Act. A company must collect
   only what it needs, for a reason it tells you; keep it safe and tell you about a leak; not
   keep it too long; and get a parent's consent for anyone under 18. You may ask what it has,
   have it corrected or deleted, stop marketing, and complain to the Information Regulator.
   The law helps - but not with what you give away yourself.
   > Board: two columns of cards, THE COMPANY MUST and YOU MAY.
5. **The check-up (5:00-5:40).** Once a term: permissions, location, delete unused apps,
   private profiles, limit ad tracking.
6. **Sign-off.**
