# Course plan: Computer Applications Technology

Written 25 September 2026 from the four reference files in this folder -
[caps.md](caps.md), [sags.md](sags.md),
[caps-exam-analysis.md](caps-exam-analysis.md) and
[ieb-exam-analysis.md](ieb-exam-analysis.md) - against
`kit/platform.md`, `kit/content-voice-and-pedagogy.md` and
`kit/theory-course.md`. **Nothing is decided. This is a proposal and a list
of questions.**

**The short version.** CAT's theory paper is the best fit for BestLessons of
anything looked at so far - shorter answers than IT's, and close to half of
it machine-markable. CAT's practical paper is 25% of the promotion mark, it
is 180 minutes of doing things inside Word, Excel, Access and File Explorer,
and the platform cannot mark any of it. Overall fit: **partial**. The
recommendation is to build the theory courses in full, build one small new
engine for HTML and one for spreadsheet formulas, teach the rest of the
practical work as prose the pupil then practises in class, and leave the PAT
to guide lessons.

## 1. CAPS vs IEB

### 1.1 The same six topics, the same content statement

Both boards use the CAPS topic names, and the IEB says so outright: its
SAGs tells teachers the examined content is "as described in Curriculum and
Assessment Policy Statement (CAPS) Computer Applications Technology -
Section 3 ... (2011, Updated 2012)". The IEB then prints its own content
list (SAGs Appendices L and M) which goes further. **So the two syllabuses
share a spine, and almost every difference is the IEB adding depth or the
DBE's 2024 amendment adding something new.**

### 1.2 Topic by topic

**B** = both boards, at the same grade. **I** = IEB only or IEB earlier.
**C** = CAPS only or CAPS earlier.

**Roughly 80% of the content is common.** The IEB's extra is almost all
*depth inside a topic both boards teach* - which is exactly what a flagged,
collapsible section handles. The CAPS extra is narrower but newer: **AI,
POPI, the gig economy, BYOD**. The one structural difference is
**algorithms and IPO**, which the IEB teaches from Grade 10 and examines
every year, and CAPS does not have at all.

### 1.4 Recommendation: one course per grade, with board sections

**Three courses - CAT Grade 10, Grade 11, Grade 12 - each including the
grades below it, exactly as `kit/theory-course.md` does for IT Theory.**
Content one board alone examines sits in a flagged, collapsible
`BoardSection()` that starts open for that board's pupils and folded for the
other's, and whose questions count for that board and are optional for the
other.

Why, rather than separate CAPS and IEB courses:

- **About 80% of the content is shared**, and most of the rest is the IEB
  going deeper inside a topic both boards teach - a paragraph or two inside
  an existing lesson, not a lesson of its own. Separate courses would
  duplicate four fifths of the writing.
- **The machinery already exists.** `BoardSection()`, `includes`,
  `glossaryFrom`, per-board totals and decision 28's exception were all
  built on 25 September 2026 for the IT Theory courses.
- **A topic taught in different grades goes in the earlier grade** with a
  grade note, as Chris decided for IT Theory. For CAT that means: HTML in
  Grade 10 (CAPS's grade), NFC and pairing in Grade 10, VR and AR in
  Grade 11, cryptocurrency in Grade 10.
- The exception is **the two exam guides**, which must be separate lessons
  per board, because the papers' shapes, the answer style and the technique
  really do differ. That is the same pattern as the Pascal course's lessons
  24 and 25.

**The one place this gets awkward** is Grade 10 word processing versus
presentations versus HTML: CAPS teaches HTML in Grade 10 and the IEB does
not, while the IEB puts more weight on presentations. Recommendation: teach
both to everyone in Grade 10 and flag the HTML chapter's *questions* as CAPS
only, because HTML is a good online topic and the IEB pupil meets it in
Grade 11 anyway.

## 2. Fit with the platform

BestLessons is short, self-marking lessons of about 30 minutes' work in a
40-minute period (`kit/platform.md`). The question is what of CAT fits that.

### 2.1 What can be taught and auto-marked with the question types that exist

| What | How | Share of the subject |
|---|---|---|
| **The whole of Paper 2, both boards** | `quiz` (multiple choice), `typed` (give the term, missing word, true/false correction), `match` (matching columns, which already allows more options than rows and scores per line), `select` (choose TWO), `written` with a per-idea `markerRubric` (everything else), `multipart` for Section C's scenarios, `identify` for the IEB's picture-choice items, `gridtyped` for the IEB's "complete the table" | **25% of the promotion mark**, plus most of the SBA's theory tests |
| **The theory behind the practical** (CAPS Q8, IEB Q7 - 15 and 8-13 marks a paper) | Name the feature (`typed`), read a formula or query criterion or HTML tag (`typed`/`checkedcode`), explain an error (`written`), which feature does this (`quiz`) | ~10% of Paper 2 |
| **Spreadsheet formulas written out** | `typed` with `codeAnswer`, or `checkedcode` (always AI-checked). **This is how both memos already mark them** - "mark the questions from the formulae and not the values" (DBE), and the IEB literally asks for it on paper (2025 P2 Q7.5, "write out the entire function in full") | most of Paper 1's spreadsheet marks are *reachable this way*, if not in the exam's own setting |
| **Database query criteria** | a `gridtyped` shaped like the Access design grid - field, table, sort, show, criteria row, or row - marked per cell | the query half of Paper 1 Question 5 / Question 8 |
| **Reading a screenshot** (a spec sheet, a chart that is hard to read, a database field list, a conditional-formatting rule, an entry form to critique) | `identify`, or the stimulus of a `multipart` | every paper has 3-4 of these |
| **File extensions, paths, properties, units** | `typed`, `match`, `order` | the recall half of the IEB's Section A |

**About 45% of Paper 2's marks need no AI marker at all** on the DBE side -
Section A's 25 plus the many single-mark recall parts in Sections B and C -
and about the same on the IEB side. The rest is 1- and 2-mark written
answers, which `written` with a per-idea rubric already handles, and
**nothing in either theory paper is longer than 5 marks**, so no band rubric
is needed anywhere in this subject. That is a better fit than IT Theory,
where algorithms, class diagrams and normalisations run to 10 marks.

### 2.2 What needs a new engine or question type

Largest first. Sizes are relative, against the Pascal compile subsystem as
"large".

1. **A spreadsheet engine - large, and probably not worth it.** To mark
   "insert a function in cell D5 to determine the total monthly salary paid
   to South African lecturers who work full-time" the way the exam does, the
   pupil needs a grid to type into, a formula evaluator for about 60
   functions, and a per-step comparison with a model. That is the biggest
   single piece of work in this plan.
   **The cheap 80%: formula-as-text.** Show the data as an HTML table with
   row and column headings, ask for the formula, and mark the string -
   exactly what both memos do. No engine; a `typed` variant with a
   spreadsheet-aware AI checker (accept `MAX(H11:H65)` or
   `LARGE(H11:H65,1)`; accept a named range; ignore case). **Small.**
   Recommendation: build formula-as-text first and decide about a real grid
   after Grade 10 is written.

2. **An HTML block - medium, and the best value in this plan.** A code box
   the pupil edits, rendered live in a sandboxed `iframe` beside it, with
   the mark coming from a tag-and-attribute comparison plus the AI checker.
   No server, no compile queue, no sandbox - unlike Pascal, the browser is
   the runtime. Both boards examine a **fixed, printed tag list of about 20
   tags** (`kit`-style inserts are reproduced in [caps.md](caps.md) and
   [sags.md](sags.md)), so the answer space is small and closed. It covers
   **15-24 marks of every practical paper**, and it is the only part of
   Paper 1 the platform could mark exactly as the exam does.

3. **A "where is it?" screenshot type - medium.** A screenshot of a ribbon,
   a dialog or File Explorer, with clickable regions; the pupil clicks where
   the setting lives. It answers a real, recurring exam question ("where
   would you change the resolution", "show where to enable hidden files",
   "which tab has the Developer ribbon") and it is the only way to reach the
   *find it* half of the practical marks. It needs an image, a set of
   rectangles and a per-region verdict - no more machinery than `match`.
   **Screenshots go stale when the ribbon changes**, which is the real cost.

4. **A database design-grid type - small.** `gridtyped` probably does this
   already: a fixed grid of rows (Field, Table, Sort, Show, Criteria, or)
   and columns, marked per cell. Worth checking before building anything.

5. **A file-and-folder simulator - large, and not recommended.** The IEB's
   Section A is 16-20 marks of File Explorer. A fake File Explorer in the
   browser could be built; it would be a lot of work for one board's 9% of
   one paper, and it would drift out of date with Windows.

### 2.3 What cannot be done well online

- **Anything whose mark is a setting inside Word, Excel or Access.** "Line
  spacing exactly 20 pt with a 1 cm hanging indent"; the Slice (Dark) cover
  page; a 3 pt border on the left and right of the first page only; a report
  grouped by a field with `=Count(*)` in the group footer; a macro bound to
  Ctrl+J that inserts a signature line; the alternate row colour of a table.
  That is **roughly 70% of Paper 1 on both boards** - call it 17-18% of the
  promotion mark. It can be *taught* (prose, screenshots, a `reveal`, a
  "try it in Word now" instruction) and *questioned about* (which feature,
  which dialog, what would happen), but the doing has to happen in class on
  a real copy of Office.
- **The PAT** (25% of the promotion mark on both boards). It is a
  months-long project with real respondents, real data and a teacher's
  rubric. Guide lessons yes; marked work no. The platform's existing
  **task pre-check** (`taskreview`, platform.md) could check an uploaded
  phase document against the board's rubric as a guide - that machinery
  already exists for the IEB IT PAT.
- **Keyboarding.** CAPS Grade 10 Term 1 spends two weeks on a typing tutor.
  Out of scope, unless a typing game is wanted in Practice.

### 2.4 Overall fit rating: **partial**

| Component | Weight | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paper 2 - theory** | 25% | **good** | Short answers, no part over 5 marks, ~45% machine-markable, scenario questions that `multipart` was built for, and the IEB's own picture-choice items that `identify` already models |
| **SBA theory tests** | part of 25% | **good** | Same material |
| **Paper 1 - HTML** | ~4% | **good**, with one new block | A closed tag list and a browser runtime |
| **Paper 1 - spreadsheet formulas and database criteria** | ~10% | **partial** | Reachable as text, which is how the memos mark them; not in the exam's own setting |
| **Paper 1 - word processing, database design, file management** | ~11% | **poor** | The mark is a setting in an application the platform cannot run |
| **PAT** | 25% | **poor** | A project, not a lesson; a pre-check is possible |

**Verdict.** CAT is worth doing for its theory, which is a better fit than IT
Theory's. It is not worth pretending the practical paper can be delivered
online: a CAT course that claimed to cover Paper 1 would be misleading, and
Chris's own pupils would find it out in the exam. The honest shape is **a
theory course that also teaches the practical work properly, and says plainly
that the practical has to be practised in front of Word, Excel and Access**.

## 3. Proposed course outline

### 3.0 Chris's decisions (27 September 2026)

Taken after reading [it-theory-reuse.md](it-theory-reuse.md). They change the
shape of §3 and most of §4, and they answer questions 1, 8 and 13 in §5.

1. **Solution Development leaves the theory course and becomes five courses
   of its own** - Word, Excel, Access, PowerPoint and HTML, each a course,
   the way Pascal, Java, SQL and AI are separate from IT Theory.
2. **They carry many screenshots and simulations.**
3. **Pupils do the work in the real application, upload the document, and it
   is marked.** This is new to the platform - see §4.
4. **A PDF of a CAT textbook will be sourced and uploaded.** The course must
   **differ substantially from it and be rewritten in Chris's voice** - those
   chapters were written by other authors. Planning goes ahead without it.
5. **Grade 10 end to end first**, as IT Theory was built.
6. Still planning; nothing is to be built yet.

**Second round, same day** (answers to questions 2, 5, 6, 15, 21-23, 25, 27):

7. **Marking works as it does for IT** - fixed answers for the activities
   that support them, plus **Haiku 4.5** (`claude-haiku-4-5`) on an API call
   for written answers, now including uploaded documents. Each task carries a
   rubric, and marking is guided by that rubric and the examining body's
   guidelines drawn from the papers.
   **Extract first, then judge.** PHP pulls the values out of the Open XML
   (font, style, section break, the `<f>` formula) and the model judges those
   against the rubric line - the file itself never goes to the model. That
   keeps the call small and keeps Haiku sufficient: CAT has nothing over 3
   marks. **Escalate to Sonnet 5** (`claude-sonnet-5`, twice Haiku's price)
   only for whole-document judgements inspection cannot reach - is the layout
   sensible, is that the right chart - and the PAT pre-check. At roughly one
   call in ten that blends to about $1.10 / $5.50 per million tokens, so no
   change to subscriber pricing unless PAT pre-checks grow. Two limits worth
   knowing: Haiku 4.5 has a 200K context and does not take the `effort`
   parameter, so a raw document could not be sent to it even if we wanted to.
8. **CAPS and IEB share, as per IT Theory** - one course per grade with
   `BoardSection()`, and the same for the application courses, where the
   overlap is larger still (Word is Word; the only board splits are named
   features and HTML's grade). Per-board either way: the four exam guides,
   the two PAT lessons, and practical-paper technique.
9. **The IT Theory lessons are rewritten, not shared** - reuse only what can
   be reused. No `includes` between the two subjects; the glossary aside.
10. **Microsoft 365 (desktop) on Windows 11**, stated once per course.
    **Microsoft only**; Google equivalents are Good to Know, not examined.
11. **A CAT bundle** enrols all eight courses - not an `includes` cascade
    from the theory course. New machinery (§4 item 5).
12. **HTML's Grade 10 chapter is flagged CAPS-only**; IEB pupils are pointed
    back to it from Grade 11 as revision.
13. **PowerPoint stays its own course** - six lessons, Grade 10, PAT only.

**Third and fourth rounds, same day** (answers to questions 3, 4, 7, 9-12,
14, 19, 20, 24, 26 - every question in §5 is now answered):

14. **Access stays on `.accdb`, read with `mdbtools`.** `.mdb` was
    considered: mdbtools is more reliable on Jet than on ACE, and pupils
    could be told to save in it. It costs more than it buys. `.mdb` cannot

18. **Build the HTML block now.**
19. **Build the full spreadsheet grid with a formula evaluator** - the

20. **Plain-English chapter names with the board's own name as a subtitle** -

21. **Every lesson gets a `study` block and a PDF**, as Pascal does.
22. **Re-download the DBE's November 2023 Paper 2 memo** before anything
    relies on that year.
23. **Treat the IEB SAGs as the 2025 document**; do not query the cover date.
24. **Pricing is deferred.** The courses go up with **full free access for

## 4. Platform work needed

Largest first. The 27 September decisions (§3.0) add three items at the top
that were not in the previous draft, and they are the biggest pieces of work
in this plan.

1. **Upload and mark - the largest piece of work in this plan, and new to
   the platform.** Decision 3 asks for something BestLessons has never done:
   pupils do the task in the real application and hand in the file. Four
   parts, and they can be built in this order.

   *(a) The upload block - small.* A file input with an extension
   allow-list and a size cap, stored per pupil per lesson, shown again on
   return like every other finished block (platform.md, "Everything a pupil
   finished is shown again on return"). **Check `taskreview` first** - the
   PAT pre-check built for the IEB IT PAT already takes an uploaded
   document, so part of this may exist.

   *(b) Inspecting the file - medium, and the reason this is worth doing.*
   `.docx`, `.xlsx` and `.pptx` are **Office Open XML: a ZIP of XML parts**.
   PHP reads them with `ZipArchive` and `SimpleXML` - no external library,
   no shell-out, no queue. That makes the following markable exactly, and
   they are the settings §2.3 wrote off as impossible:
   - *Word:* the font, size and colour on a run; which style a paragraph
     uses; section breaks and each section's orientation and margins;
     header and footer text and fields; page-numbering format; table
     borders, shading and merged cells; a table-of-contents field; whether
     tracked changes are present; captions, bookmarks and hyperlinks.
   - *Excel:* **the formula in a cell** (the `<f>` element - which is what
     both memos mark, "mark the questions from the formulae and not the
     values"); number formats; conditional-formatting rules; named ranges;
     chart type and series; sheet names; frozen panes; filters.
   - *PowerPoint:* slide count, layout used, transitions and animations,
     speaker notes, a chart or table on a slide.
   - *HTML:* no ZIP at all - parse and compare, as §2.2 item 2 already says.

   *(c) Access is the exception - and needs a decision.* `.accdb` is a
   proprietary binary format, not Open XML, and PHP cannot read it.
   Either run **`mdbtools`** (`mdb-tables`, `mdb-schema`, `mdb-queries`) as
   a command on the server - it reads `.accdb` schema, tables and queries,
   and was used to read the boards' own data files for this analysis - or
   ask for an export, or mark Access through screenshots, the design-grid
   question and simulations only. **Decided (question 24): stay on `.accdb`
   with `mdbtools`** - see §3.0 decision 14 for why `.mdb` costs more than
   it buys, and for the test to run before the marker is built.

   *(d) What happens to the mark.* Auto-inspection, an AI second opinion on
   what inspection cannot see (is the layout sensible? is the chart the
   right one?), or Chris marking by hand with a queue and a dashboard.
   **Decided (question 23): inspect first, Haiku 4.5 against the rubric on
   what is extracted, Sonnet 5 only for whole-document judgement** (§3.0
   decision 7). **Pupil files are personal data** - retention, access and
   deletion need a line in the platform, in a subject whose own syllabus
   teaches POPI.

2. **Simulations - large.** Three tiers, and the plan should use the cheap
   two almost everywhere:
   - *Hotspot* - a screenshot with clickable regions; "click where you would
     change the line spacing". Small, no more machinery than `match`, and
     already proposed as §2.2 item 3.
   - *Guided sequence* - several hotspots making one task, with a hint on a
     wrong click ("that is the Home tab; the setting is under Layout").
     Medium, and the best value of the three.
   - *A working fake dialog* with real state - Paragraph, Page Setup, Access
     field properties. Large, and the first thing to break when the ribbon
     moves. Worth it for perhaps three dialogs, not as a general approach.

3. **Screenshots - the largest content cost, and the one that decays.**
   Microsoft 365 updates monthly and the ribbon moves. Needs a capture
   recipe at a fixed window size and zoom (the Pascal course's
   `tools/ui-screens/` is the model), a naming convention, a callout style,
   and a **termly** re-shoot list rather than the yearly one the theory
   courses use. Two things to settle before the first capture: which version
   is shown (decided: Microsoft 365 on Windows 11) and whether Microsoft's
   terms allow its UI to be redistributed in a paid course - **worth
   checking rather than assuming**.

4. **The eight courses, their index files and the shared glossary.** Eight
   `CourseIndex()` rows with `includes` and `glossaryFrom`, eight
   `content/` folders, a `cat` marking voice in `CourseMarkStyle()`, chapter
   headings, and about 180 lesson stubs. Most of this is a copy of what was
   built for `theory10|11|12` on 25 September 2026, so it is a day's work,
   not a week's. Grouping the eight is the bundle's job, not `includes`
   (item 5); `includes` stays for the theory grades' own cascade.
5. **The CAT bundle - new.** Decision 11: one thing a pupil joins that
   enrols all eight courses. The `includes` cascade built for IT Theory
   chains a grade to the grades below it; it does not group a subject's
   separate courses, so this is a new object, not a configuration. Needs a
   bundle row, the enrolment fan-out, one plan covering all eight, and a
   decision about what a pupil sees on the course list - eight cards or one.
6. **The HTML block** (§2.2 item 2) - an editable HTML box with a live
   sandboxed preview and a tag-and-attribute marker. The single most
   valuable new thing in this plan, and the only part of a practical paper
   the platform could mark the way the exam does. Needs: a block type, an
   `iframe` with `sandbox` and no network, a tag/attribute comparison, an AI
   second opinion, and the usual "show it again on return" behaviour
   (platform.md, "Everything a pupil finished is shown again on return").
   **Built 8 October 2026** (not yet committed or published): the `html`
   block type - checks with `'exact'` rules and Jev questions, Claude when
   Jev is unsure - documented in platform.md ("`html` blocks"), checked by
   `bin/check-html.php`, with a worked example in catpilot lesson 5 ("Fix
   Botha's Bakery's page").
7. **The spreadsheet grid and formula evaluator** (decision 19; **built
   and live 9 October 2026, 16cca01** - 876 cases match Excel) - **not**
   the cheap formula-as-text version §2.2 recommended; Chris chose the full
   grid. A grid the pupil types into, an evaluator for the ~58 functions the
   two syllabuses name, and a per-step comparison with a model answer. The
   largest single build here after upload-and-mark, and the one to schedule
   against the Excel course rather than Grade 10. Two things make it
   tractable: the function list is closed and printed in
   [caps.md](caps.md) and [sags.md](sags.md), and the same evaluator is what
   the `.xlsx` inspector needs to read a pupil's `<f>` element, so it is not
   two builds. Build the text-comparison path first as its fallback.
8. **A database design-grid question** (§2.2 item 4) - check whether
   `gridtyped` already does it; if so, no work, just a convention.
9. **PAT pre-checks for CAT** - `TaskDefinitions()` entries for the CAPS PAT
   phases and the IEB PAT phases, against each board's rubric. The machinery
   exists; it is two rubrics and a prompt each.
10. **Glossary and Practice** - `content/cat10/glossary.php`, started with
   the first lesson, and `PracticeHeroGlyphs()` for CAT (`.xlsx`, `=SUM`,
   `<br/>`, `RAM`).

## 6. Notes on the brief

*This section is here because CAT is the pilot. It is about `CLAUDE.md`,
not about CAT.*

**What worked.** The five-file shape is right. The `kit/` models are
detailed enough to copy without asking anything, and the two exam-analysis
models in particular ("counts, not impressions") set the standard the rest of
the work then met. Putting the findings in files and not in chat is right.
The instruction to write down a question with a recommendation and carry on
is right, and was used thirty-three times across this session.

**What was unclear, missing, or should change before the other nine run:**

1. **Say that one analysis file covers both papers.** The kit has four model
   files (`caps-practical-`, `caps-theory-`, `ieb-practical-`,
   `ieb-theory-`) for two output files, and the brief does not say how they
   map. For CAT each output file became Part One (practical) and Part Two
   (theory). Add a line: *"`caps-exam-analysis.md` covers every CAPS paper -
   both papers where the subject has two."* For Mathematics and History,
   where both papers are written, the same file will just have two parts of
   the same kind.

2. **Say what to do when a source is missing or wrong.** The DBE's 2023
   Paper 2 memo is not the 2023 Paper 2 memo, and the 2024 amendment
   replaces more than `sources/cat/SOURCES.md` says. "Be exact ... never
   fill a gap with a guess" covers the first half; it does not say *where*
   to record it. Add: *"Record a missing, wrong or mislabelled source at the
   top of the file that would have used it, and raise it as a question for
   Chris. Never edit `sources/`."* That is what was done here, but it was a
   judgement call.

3. **`kit/`'s internal links are dead** and nothing warns a reader. Files in
   the kit link to `open-items.md`, `sags-topic4-syllabus.md`,
   `sql-course.md`, `courses/pascal-course.md`, `../CAPS/past papers/` and
   a dozen more that were not copied. Either copy them, or add one line to
   `kit/README.md`: *"These are snapshots. Links to files not in this table
   point at AIResources and will not resolve here."*

4. **Say which parts of `kit/writing-style.md` apply to findings.** The
   brief says "Prose follows `kit/writing-style.md`", but that file is
   written for lesson prose a pupil reads - a quote at the top of each
   lesson, `Gloss()` popups, `Warning:` callouts, "never name the SAGs". A
   findings file names the SAGs in its title. Narrow it: *"South African
   English and spelling, the banned-word list, short sentences, hyphens not
   em dashes, 'pupils' never 'learners'. The lesson-specific rules (quotes,
   popups, callouts, not naming the SAGs) do not apply to findings."*

5. **Give a length expectation.** The kit models run 15-48 KB. Nothing says
   whether a subject with six papers should produce a file the size of one
   built from 23. A line - *"match the models' depth; a subject with fewer
   papers gets a shorter file, not a thinner one"* - would save a decision
   per subject.

6. **Say where the questions live.** The brief puts "Questions for Chris" in
   `course-plan.md`, but questions arose in all four other files (a missing
   memo, a contradictory implementation date, a mislabelled amendment).
   They were all collected into `course-plan.md` §5 and cross-referenced.
   Say so: *"Every question goes in `course-plan.md`, numbered, even if it
   came from another file; the other files link to it."*

7. **Say what "the PR gets the list of questions" means** when there are
   fourteen of them with options and recommendations. Repeating §5 in full
   would make a 150-line PR body. This PR lists the questions as one line
   each and links to the section. Worth writing down.

8. **Say what to do when a subject is a poor fit.** CAT turned out to be a
   *partial* fit, and about half its marks cannot be delivered online at
   all. The brief assumes every subject gets a course plan. Add: *"If the
   fit is poor, still write all five files - the analysis is worth having -
   but say so plainly in the fit rating and make the first question 'is this
   worth building?'"*. That is what was done.

9. **Mention the data files properly.** For CAT the practical data files are
   as informative as the papers: the database schemas, the deliberately
   broken HTML, the worksheet sizes and the folder structures say more about
   what is examined than the question text does. The brief's "Reading the
   sources" bullet lists the tools but treats data files as a footnote. For
   CAT, Physical Sciences and Geography (where data and inserts matter),
   raise them: *"Say what the data files contain and which skills they test"
   is already in the brief's file list for the analyses - make it a
   first-class step in 'Reading the sources' too."*

10. **Afrikaans is not only a fallback.** The DBE's CAT files are bilingual
    *inside* the data (`First name_Voornaam`, `Question/Vraag 1.4`,
    `Kursus`), so a session reading English-only sources still meets
    Afrikaans and needs to know it is normal and not a corrupted file. One
    line in `CLAUDE.md` would do.

11. **Say whether a subject may be split across sessions.** This one took a
    full session end to end. Nine more at that size is a lot of sessions,
    and a session that runs out of room mid-analysis has nowhere to say what
    is done. Suggest: *"If the work has to stop, commit what is finished and
    add a `PROGRESS.md` in `subjects/<subject>/` saying which files are done
    and what is left. Delete it when the five files are complete."*

12. **The subject order is by size, not by fit.** CAT was the right pilot -
    it is the closest subject to IT and it exposed the practical-paper
    problem early. But Mathematics and Physical Sciences next will raise a
    different problem (equations, diagrams and working-out, none of which
    the existing question types handle), and English Home Language a third
    (essays and unseen texts). It may be worth doing **one of each kind**
    next - Mathematics and English Home Language - before the remaining
    seven, so the platform work can be planned once rather than three times.
